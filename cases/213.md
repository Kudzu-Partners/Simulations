## Abstract

Universidad del Río was, in the case universe, a private LATAM mid-sized university founded in 1962 and based in a secondary-tier metropolitan area, with 14,200 students across 42 undergraduate programs, 18 master's programs and 6 executive-education programs, a 2025 reputation reading of 72/100 on the national ranking index, a 2025 operating margin of –3% and a 2025 cash balance of USD 48M — approximately eighteen months of runway at the current burn rate. The 2023–2025 cycle had compressed the institution across three pressures: applications had fallen 11% year-over-year in the 2025 admission window (the fourth consecutive decline), the national online-only-cohort market had grown 38% through the same period, and a Q4 2025 Ministry-of-Education briefing had telegraphed a 7% public-funding cut for the 2026–2027 academic biennium. The board had, in November 2025, appointed a new Rector with a three-year mandate: stabilise enrollment, rebuild operating margin, capture the digital-cohort shift and leave the institution structurally solvent through 2036. The case opens at 6:32 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Universidad del Río Rectorate as **Dr. Patricio Esquivel-Dávila**, a 54-year-old Rector, prepares the Year-1 portfolio commitment ahead of the 9:00 a.m. governing-board strategic-planning session.

4. Design the three-year higher-education archetype that couples a portfolio-prune discipline (read the student who has already decided not to apply), a reallocation discipline (capital goes to aid, not to buildings) and a digital-overtake discipline (the brochure no longer describes the institution) — anchored on the principle that institutions survive by reading demand rather than defending supply.
5. Articulate the governance and stakeholder-management implications: faculty union, Ministry of Education, board of trustees, accreditation body and alumni network — and frame the three-year narrative as a sequence of stakeholder commitments that the institutional narrative has to carry.

On the morning of **Wednesday, April 22, 2026**, **Dr. Patricio Esquivel-Dávila** arrived at the Universidad del Río Rectorate at 6:32 a.m., opened the three-year strategic-planning workbook his Vice-Rector for Academic Affairs and his Chief Financial Officer had finalised at 11:58 p.m. the night before, and re-read the sentence he had written in the margin at his first board briefing in November 2025: *"The future of the university was never in the brochure."*

---

## The Protagonist

Esquivel-Dávila, 54, had assumed the Rector role at Universidad del Río on November 15, 2025, after twenty-two years across faculty, dean and provost leadership at three LATAM universities — an engineering-school deanship at a Santiago-based research university, an associate-provostship at a Bogotá-based teaching-and-research university, and a provost role at a Buenos Aires-based comprehensive university that had, under his watch in 2018–2021, absorbed a 9% ministerial funding cut and had rebuilt operating margin from –4% to +3% through a 14-program portfolio prune and a hybrid-masters launch. The board had recruited him specifically on three capabilities: his documented discipline of closing undergraduate programs on an enrollment-and-placement basis rather than a faculty-tenure basis, his willingness to reallocate capex from facilities to financial aid against board-finance-committee default patterns, and his command of digital-cohort strategy that had, at the Buenos Aires provost role, built a 6,800-student online programme from a 180-student pilot in thirty months.

---

## The Decision

Esquivel-Dávila had to commit, by the 9:00 a.m. governing-board session, the Year-1 portfolio envelope and the directional intent for Years 2–3.

1. **Year 1 — Portfolio prune and hybrid launch.** Three archetypes. A prune-and-launch envelope (drop 8 underperforming UG programs, launch 3 hybrid masters, USD 4M LMS investment, tuition +3%). A defensive-hold envelope (protect all 42 UG programs, launch nothing, tuition +6%). A burn-down envelope (freeze capex, absorb the 7% funding cut with a 14-program closure). Working envelope: prune-and-launch.
2. **Year 2 — Reallocation under the ministerial cut.** Three vectors. A capital-reallocation envelope (USD 6M from facilities to financial aid, 4% faculty raise, exec-ed-partnership with a bank at 70/30). A faculty-first envelope (6% raise, no exec-ed partnership, no aid reallocation). A capex-defensive envelope (hold facilities, 2% faculty raise, hold aid). Working envelope: capital-reallocation.
3. **Year 3 — Digital-cohort overtake.** Three vectors. A digital-overtake envelope (AI-tutoring rollout, platform expansion, enrollment 15,800 with digital exceeding on-campus). A digital-hold envelope (maintain current digital stack, enrollment 14,200). A digital-retreat envelope (no AI tutoring, retreat from online cohorts). Working envelope: digital-overtake.

On Esquivel-Dávila's working model, a Prune-and-Launch / Capital-Reallocation / Digital-Overtake envelope delivered Year-3 Enrollment 15,800, Reputation 81/100, Operating Margin +5% and Cash USD 61M — with a composite Sustainable-University rating.

## Company Background

Universidad del Río had been founded in 1962 as a regional teaching university and had, across sixty-four years, built a 14,200-student mid-sized institution organised into seven faculties (Business, Engineering, Law, Health Sciences, Social Sciences, Arts and Humanities, and Education), a 520-faculty full-time academic corps, a 1,100-staff administrative footprint, a 280,000-m² main-campus real-estate position and a 42-UG / 18-master's / 6-exec-ed programme portfolio. The 2023–2025 cycle had compressed the institution on three dimensions. First, enrollment pressure: applications down 4%, 6% and 11% in the 2023, 2024 and 2025 admission windows. Second, cost pressure: faculty-union settlements at 3%, 4% and 5% through 2023–2025, and a facilities-capex cycle of USD 18M across three campus renovation projects. Third, revenue pressure: the 2025 ministerial funding line had absorbed inflationary erosion, and the Q4 2025 briefing had telegraphed the 7% cut.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2019–2025 LATAM mid-sized private-university literature had, across 34 documented three-year strategic cycles on 10,000-to-20,000-student institutions with declining enrollment and below-zero operating margins, produced three findings the three-year envelope had to respect. First, portfolio-prune-and-hybrid-launch envelopes had stabilised enrollment inside 24 months in 68% of documented cycles, against defensive-hold postures that had continued the enrollment decline in 72%. Second, capital-reallocation from facilities to financial aid had captured 180–360 bps of operating-margin improvement in 64% of documented cycles under ministerial-funding cuts above 5%, against facilities-defensive postures. Third, digital-cohort-overtake architectures — AI tutoring, hybrid-masters programmes and LMS investment at USD 2M–USD 6M — had delivered 24–42% digital-enrollment shares in 58% of documented cases on comparable institutions.

Empirically, the 2022–2025 literature had two additional findings. First, tuition-increase envelopes at below-inflation pricing had preserved enrollment retention 180–280 bps above at-inflation pricing in 72% of cases on private LATAM institutions with reputations in the 60–80 range. Second, exec-ed-partnership envelopes with banks, insurers or large-corporate training functions had delivered seat-cost-zero revenue-share economics of 60/40 to 80/20 in 64% of documented cases.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in November 2025, appointed Esquivel-Dávila. The Vice-Rector for Academic Affairs had, on December 10, delivered the 42-UG-programme enrollment-and-placement scatter. The Chief Financial Officer had, on January 15, delivered the cash-runway model and the ministerial-cut sensitivity. The Vice-Rector for Digital Transformation had, on February 5, delivered the LMS and AI-tutoring readiness briefing. The Faculty Union had, on March 12, delivered the 6% salary-raise demand. The Director of External Relations had, on March 28, delivered the Bank-partnership-exec-ed memorandum of understanding.

### What Esquivel-Dávila knew

The eight UG programmes the Vice-Rector had nominated for closure had 3-year enrollment compounding below –14%, 3-year placement rates below 54%, and 3-year faculty utilisation below 62% — the three closure criteria the board had pre-agreed in November. The three hybrid masters — Data Analytics, Healthcare Management and Sustainability Strategy — had market-demand signals of 2.4×, 1.8× and 2.1× current seat supply.

### What he did not know

Whether the Ministry would accept the hybrid-masters accreditation dossier inside twelve months; whether the Faculty Union would absorb the 4% raise without a strike in the September 2026 academic-year opening; whether the Bank-partnership exec-ed would ramp to a USD 3M-annual run-rate inside eighteen months; and whether the AI-tutoring rollout would clear the accreditation body's academic-integrity review for the September 2027 cohort.

### What others were saying

**The Vice-Rector for Academic Affairs**: *"Eight programmes. Phased over three semesters. Tenured faculty re-deployed to the hybrid masters and the executive education."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"Six million out of buildings into aid. That's the margin flip."*

**The Faculty-Union Chair**: *"Four percent is below inflation. Six is the number."*

**The Governing Board Chair**: *"Read the student who's already not applying."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Esquivel-Dávila had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Prune-and-Launch / Capital-Reallocation / Digital-Overtake:** the recommended envelope. Year-3 Enrollment 15,800. Reputation 81. Operating Margin +5%. Cash USD 61M. Sustainable-University rating. Recommended.
- **Option B — Defensive-Hold / Faculty-First / Digital-Hold:** the status-quo envelope. Year-3 Enrollment 12,800. Reputation 68. Operating Margin –8%. Cash USD 16M. Rejected — runway exhausts mid-Year-3.
- **Option C — Burn-Down / Capex-Defensive / Digital-Retreat:** the defensive envelope. Year-3 Enrollment 13,400. Reputation 62. Operating Margin –2%. Cash USD 28M. Rejected — reputation collapse and digital-cohort cession.

Esquivel-Dávila's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the year most correlated with the final Sustainable-University rating was Year 2 — the reallocation under the ministerial cut — because it was the year that decided whether the institution flipped operating margin under external pressure (Option A) or drifted into structural deficit (Options B and C).
