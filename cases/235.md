## Abstract

Al-Bayyina Advisory FZ-LLC was, in the case universe, a Dubai-headquartered Sharia-compliant structuring and advisory firm with an eighteen-person structuring team, a 2025 deal book of USD 8.4B across six core Sharia-compliant instrument families (Murabaha, Ijara, Istisna, Takaful, Mudaraba, Sukuk), and a Sharia Supervisory Board of three scholars that reviewed every deal before committee. The 2024–2025 cycle had left the firm at a product-diverse but discipline-pressured posture: the 2025 deal book had grown 36% year on year on a pipeline that increasingly required hybrid structures (Sukuk al-Istisna, Sukuk al-Ijara sale-and-lease-back), and the Sharia Supervisory Board had, in the Q4 2025 review, re-emphasised that no structure should drift into synthetic riba or excessive gharar under market-expediency pressure. The Q2 2026 context had compounded three pressures: a six-deal pipeline that had concentrated review bandwidth in a two-week window; a USD 2B sovereign Sukuk al-Ijara airport sale-and-lease-back whose economic profile resembled a conventional bond and therefore required a tight real-asset-ownership articulation; and a Takaful fund-surplus mechanism that had attracted regulatory scrutiny in a GCC jurisdiction across Q1 2026. The case opens at 7:32 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Al-Bayyina Advisory Dubai DIFC structuring floor as **Yusra Al-Fahim-Castellanos**, a 42-year-old Head of Structuring, prepares the Scenario-1 Murabaha home-purchase commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Scenario-1 Murabaha home-purchase envelope (USD 250k cost, 4% profit rate, 25-year amortisation; the bank owns the asset at the moment of sale, then resells at cost plus profit; fixed-dollar payments are profit, not interest) and articulate the ownership-moment requirement that differentiates Murabaha from a rebranded loan.
2. Analyse the Scenario-2 working-capital pick (Murabaha on inventory vs. Mudaraba profit-sharing vs. Salam forward-sale) and articulate why risk-to-cashflow matching — not product-family preference — is the advisory call.
3. Diagnose the Scenario-3 USD 500M infrastructure bridge (Istisna construction contract with staged payments vs. Sukuk al-Istisna tokenisation into tradeable certificates) and the cashflow-and-tradability trade-offs between the two forms.
4. Respond to the Scenario-4 Takaful commercial-insurance pool (participant donations rather than premiums, surplus returned at year end, no gharar because claims are fully documented), the Scenario-5 Mudaraba investment fund (70/30 profit split, losses fall entirely on the rab al-mal, skin-in-the-game designed) and the Scenario-6 USD 2B Sukuk al-Ijara (sovereign sells airport to investors, leases it back, 10-year, 5% profit rate; legal ownership passes, economic profile resembles a bond, asset is real).
5. Design the six-scenario Sharia-advisory archetype that couples a Murabaha ownership discipline, a Mudaraba-vs-Murabaha risk-matching read, an Istisna-vs-Sukuk-al-Istisna tradability toggle, a Takaful no-gharar posture, a Mudaraba skin-in-the-game architecture and a Sukuk al-Ijara real-asset articulation — anchored on the principle that Sharia-compliant finance earns when something real happens.

## The Protagonist

Al-Fahim-Castellanos, 42, had assumed the Head-of-Structuring role at Al-Bayyina on October 1, 2022, after nineteen years across Islamic-banking structuring, Sukuk origination and Sharia-compliant advisory leadership at a Kuwait City specialist, a Kuala Lumpur sovereign Sukuk house and a London Islamic-finance boutique. She had, in 2018–2021, led the structuring of a USD 3.2B sovereign Sukuk al-Ijara for a GCC sovereign that had become the reference transaction in the regional practitioner literature for real-asset-ownership discipline under market-expediency pressure. She had, in 2020, co-authored with two Sharia scholars a practitioner paper titled *Six Contracts, Six Economies* that the firm had subsequently adopted as its internal orientation document for every incoming structuring analyst. The Al-Bayyina management committee had recruited her on three capabilities: her documented discipline of refusing to sign off on any structure where the ownership-moment, risk-sharing or no-gharar test was not cleanly articulable; her willingness to escalate borderline cases to the Sharia Supervisory Board before market-expediency pressure could compress the review; and her command of the Sukuk market that the 2025–2027 sovereign-and-corporate pipeline required.

## The Decision

Al-Fahim-Castellanos had to commit, by the 9:30 a.m. Sharia Supervisory Board pre-review, the six-scenario advisory envelope.

1. **Scenario 1 — Home Purchase Murabaha.** A single envelope: USD 250k cost, 4% profit rate, 25-year amortisation; bank buys, takes title, resells at cost-plus; fixed-dollar payments; ownership-moment documented. Working envelope: accept.
2. **Scenario 2 — Working Capital.** Three options. Murabaha on inventory. Mudaraba profit-sharing. Salam forward-sale. Working envelope: Mudaraba on variable-cashflow counterparties, Murabaha on fixed-inventory counterparties, Salam on agricultural pre-finance.
3. **Scenario 3 — USD 500M Bridge.** Two options. Istisna construction contract with staged payments. Sukuk al-Istisna tokenised certificates. Working envelope: Sukuk al-Istisna on public-tradability requirements, Istisna on private-placement structures.
4. **Scenario 4 — Takaful.** A single envelope: mutual pool, participant donations, surplus returned at year end, no gharar because claims documented. Working envelope: accept.
5. **Scenario 5 — Investment Fund Mudaraba.** A single envelope: 70/30 profit split; losses on rab al-mal; mudarib's work is uncompensated on loss; skin-in-the-game architecture. Working envelope: accept.
6. **Scenario 6 — Sovereign Sukuk al-Ijara.** A single envelope: USD 2B airport sale-and-lease-back; 10-year; 5% profit rate; legal title passes to investors; lease payments flow to Sukuk holders; asset is real. Working envelope: accept.

On Al-Fahim-Castellanos's working model, an Accept / Mudaraba-or-Murabaha / Sukuk-or-Istisna / Accept / Accept / Accept envelope delivered a six-scenario advisory pipeline that the Sharia Supervisory Board could review inside the two-week window and that the firm's pipeline economics supported. A counterfactual "collapse Scenarios 3 and 6 into a single infrastructure-plus-sovereign hybrid" envelope would attract review-bandwidth and gharar concerns; a counterfactual "substitute a conventional-bond economic clone" envelope would fail the real-asset test.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2005–2025 Islamic-finance literature had, across 120 documented advisory-firm deal-book redesigns at GCC, Malaysia and London specialists, produced three findings the firm had to respect. First, hybrid-Sukuk structures (Sukuk al-Istisna, Sukuk al-Ijara) had grown from 8% of the global Sukuk-issuance market in 2010 to 38% by 2024, driven by the tradability-plus-real-asset combination that pure Istisna or pure Ijara structures could not deliver on their own. Second, Takaful fund-surplus mechanisms had become the single most-regulated dimension of GCC Sharia-compliant insurance, with four of the six GCC jurisdictions tightening surplus-return rules between 2022 and 2025. Third, Mudaraba investment funds had consistently produced skin-in-the-game architectures that reduced first-loss leakage by 0.8–1.4% of AUM on average versus conventional-fee-only alternatives.

Empirically, the 2020–2025 Sharia-advisory-firm literature had two further findings. First, Sharia Supervisory Board pre-review cadences of two weeks or longer had, in 78% of surveyed firms, correlated with a 42% reduction in post-close structure renegotiation volume. Second, the real-asset-ownership test had become the single most-cited refusal ground on Sukuk structures across the 2023–2025 GCC market.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Q1 2026 pipeline had accumulated six deals into a two-week Sharia Supervisory Board review window. The USD 2B sovereign Sukuk al-Ijara had required a tightened real-asset articulation. Q1 2026 GCC regulatory guidance had scrutinised the Takaful fund-surplus mechanism.

### What Al-Fahim-Castellanos knew

The ownership-moment requirement on Murabaha; the risk-to-cashflow matching on the working-capital pick; the Istisna-vs-Sukuk-al-Istisna tradability toggle; the Takaful no-gharar posture; the Mudaraba skin-in-the-game architecture; the Sukuk al-Ijara real-asset articulation.

### What she did not know

Whether the Sharia Supervisory Board would clear all six deals in the two-week window or request staged reviews; whether the sovereign Sukuk al-Ijara would attract conventional-bond-cloning pushback at scholar level; whether the Takaful surplus mechanism would require a GCC-jurisdiction carve-out; and whether the working-capital pick for a USD 180M counterparty would settle on Mudaraba or Murabaha after diligence.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Real-Estate Structuring**: *"Murabaha title-moment. Document the ownership second. The bank was the owner. Then the family is."*

**The Head of Sukuk Markets**: *"Istisna for private placement. Sukuk al-Istisna for tradable certificates. The trade-off is at the tradability line, not the economic line."*

**The Head of Takaful**: *"Participant donations, not premiums. Surplus back to participants. That is how gharar is designed out."*

**The Senior Sharia Scholar**: *"Six contracts. One doctrine. Finance that earns when something real happens."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Al-Fahim-Castellanos had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Accept / Mudaraba-or-Murabaha / Sukuk-or-Istisna / Accept / Accept / Accept:** the recommended envelope. Six-deal pipeline approvable inside the two-week review window. Real-asset and no-gharar tests cleanly articulable. Recommended.
- **Option B — Collapse Scenarios 3 and 6 into a hybrid infrastructure-sovereign:** the review-bandwidth-optimised envelope. Rejected — gharar concern, structure-renegotiation risk.
- **Option C — Substitute a conventional-bond economic clone on Scenario 6:** the market-expediency envelope. Rejected — real-asset-ownership test fails.

Her private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the scenario most correlated with the firm's reputational trajectory was Scenario 6 — the USD 2B sovereign Sukuk al-Ijara — because it was the scenario that decided whether Al-Bayyina committed to a real-asset-articulation posture that scholars would defend publicly (Option A), drifted into a bandwidth-economy hybrid that would attract renegotiation (Option B) or retreated into a market-expediency clone that would fail the doctrine test (Option C).
