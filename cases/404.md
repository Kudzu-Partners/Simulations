## Abstract

Pago Claro S.A. de C.V. was, in the case universe, a Mexico-City-headquartered B2B embedded-payments fintech founded in 2018 with MXN 312M in FY2025 revenue, a 15.4% EBITDA margin, 2,840 active merchant clients, MXN 8.4B in monthly transaction volume and an IFPE license from the CNBV under the 2018 Ley Fintech. In March 2026 the company had just closed a MXN 480M Series B against a board mandate to double revenue to MXN 624M by the end of 2027, and its 68 engineers across six independent squads had begun breaking down at scale: a Q1 2026 cross-squad incident had blocked MXN 8.7M of transactions across a six-hour outage, two squads had independently built duplicate notification systems at MXN 3.2M/year, the Squad-Gamma mobile SDK v4.0 was six months late with 34% test coverage and 127 open bugs, the CNBV had issued Circular 3/2026 demanding 38 engineer-months of mandatory compliance work inside six months, and an AI startup, DataLens AI, had tabled a MXN 180M acquisition offer. The case opens at 6:40 a.m. on Monday, March 16, 2026, at the leadership offsite in Valle de Bravo as **Sofía Mendoza**, the 38-year-old Chief Technology Officer, prepares the Round-1 scaling-framework decision against 346.8 net engineer-months of capacity. Twenty-one months separate her from the board's end-2027 2× revenue review.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Round-1 scaling-framework choice (a custom dependency-first hybrid against pure SAFe and the Spotify model) and diagnose when a contract-and-platform design dominates a prescriptive framework on the DORA-composite and velocity trajectory at 68 engineers — and why a framework alone does not resolve the Alpha→Gamma coordination failure.
2. Analyze the Round-2 Squad-Gamma turnaround (descope-to-v4.0-lite plus a Mariana rotation against reinforce-with-headcount and fixed-price outsourcing) and apply Brooks's Law and a Cost of Delay of MXN 6.8M/month to the SDK decision and the ~65% key-talent attrition risk.
3. Allocate engineering capacity across 346.8 net engineer-months in Round 3 under the CNBV 38-engineer-month mandate and a 16-engineer-month tech-debt remediation carrying a ~798% ROI, while respecting squad fungibility — recognising Squad Alpha as the binding constraint.
4. Evaluate the Round-4 DataLens AI decision (license-with-option against acquire and build-in-house) and price the data-provenance risk (a CNBV fine of up to MXN 15.6M plus a model-destruction order) against the MXN 116M IP premium embedded in the MXN 180M price.
5. Design a four-round sequence that protects cash toward the MXN 624M 2027 target, clears the CNBV deadline at 100% compliance, and retains key talent — translating each decision into both financial language (CoD, ROI, NPV) and engineering language (DORA, tech debt, Brooks's Law).

On the morning of **Monday, March 16, 2026**, **Sofía Mendoza** reached the lakeside conference room at the Valle de Bravo offsite at 6:40 a.m., opened the dependency map her Head of Engineering had finalised at 11:48 p.m. the night before, and re-read the line she had pinned to her laptop since leaving Stripe Mexico: *"Frameworks don't ship software; dependency contracts do."* Twenty-one months separated her from the board's end-2027 2× revenue review.

## The Protagonist

Sofía Mendoza, 38, had joined Pago Claro in 2022 as Chief Technology Officer after four years as an engineering lead at Stripe Mexico, where she had scaled a payments-infrastructure team from 20 to 90 engineers and had run the dependency-contract program that survived two regulatory cycles without a Sev-1 cross-team regression. Before Stripe she had spent six years as a backend engineer and tech lead across two Mexican neobanks. CEO Andrés Villanueva — a non-technical, serial fintech founder — had recruited her on two strengths: a record of scaling engineering organisations through the 50-to-100-engineer coordination wall without resorting to heavyweight process, and a refusal to let a regulatory deadline be absorbed by deprioritising the very tech debt that slowed the team down. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the CNBV compliance outcome, 20% to the revenue-run-rate trajectory and 10% to a defined DORA-composite floor.

## The Decision

Mendoza had to commit, by the March 20 board pre-read, the Round-1 framework choice and the directional intent for Rounds 2–4.

1. **Round 1 — El Modelo Ágil (scaling framework).** Three archetypes: a **custom dependency-first hybrid** (versioned Alpha↔Gamma API contracts, a one-day quarterly PI-planning ceremony, a permanent Platform Team extracted from Squad Zeta, and cross-squad chapters) projecting the strongest DORA-composite and velocity uplift; **pure SAFe** (full PI Planning, ARTs, portfolio management) — a strong compliance and enterprise fit but heavyweight for 68 engineers, and only defensible when funded above a MXN 4.2M implementation envelope; or the **Spotify model** (autonomous tribes/squads/chapters/guilds) — informal dependency management that does not structurally prevent the Alpha→Gamma incident. Working envelope: custom hybrid plus Platform Team, prioritising MTTR on the DORA scorecard.
2. **Round 2 — El Equipo en Crisis (Squad-Gamma turnaround).** Four vectors: **descope to v4.0-lite** (cut 40% of scope, ship in three months, MXN 0 incremental); **reinforce Gamma** (add three engineers plus an external tech lead, MXN 2.8M — exposed to Brooks's Law in weeks one through six); **rotate Mariana plus an interim PM** (MXN 1.1M); or **outsource to Nearshore Labs** (fixed-price MXN 8.4M, contractual June delivery, IP and skills risk). Working envelope: descope-to-v4.0-lite plus a Mariana rotation, against a Cost of Delay of MXN 6.8M/month and a ~65% attrition probability if her situation is framed as performance rather than rotation.
3. **Round 3 — La Deuda y la Regla (capacity allocation).** Against 346.8 net engineer-months over six months, demand is the CNBV mandate (38, non-negotiable), critical tech-debt remediation (16, ~798% ROI), SDK v4.0 completion (18, descoped), the board roadmap (60+) and BAU (28). On paper 160 of 346.8 fits — until squads are mapped to workstreams and Squad Alpha (AML + SPEI 3.0 + payment-core debt + BAU) surfaces as the binding constraint. Working envelope: fund regulatory to 100%, protect the 16-month tech-debt envelope, hold roadmap below the slack line.
4. **Round 4 — La Adquisición (DataLens AI).** Three vectors: **license-with-option** (MXN 2.8M/year, an option to acquire at MXN 120M in 18 months, plus a SHAP/LIME explainability layer for CNBV) — the lowest-risk path that also preserves the AML capacity relief; **acquire 100%** (MXN 180M, a 42.9× ARR multiple, MXN 116M of IP premium, and a data-provenance exposure of up to MXN 15.6M plus model destruction); or **build in-house** (too slow for the September AML deadline). Working envelope: license-with-option, contingent on a clean data-provenance opinion, independent accuracy validation and the explainability layer — while hiring two ML engineers regardless.

On Mendoza's working model, a Custom-Hybrid / Descope-plus-Rotate / Capacity-Balanced / License-with-Option envelope held cash above MXN 440M, carried the revenue run-rate onto the MXN 624M glide path, lifted the DORA composite and closed regulatory compliance at 100% with Mariana retained — the release-ready trajectory. A SAFe-Underfunded / Reinforce-Gamma / Over-Allocate / Acquire-Without-Diligence envelope drove cash below MXN 280M on the MXN 180M outlay and the MXN 15.6M provenance hit, with a compliance gap and a depressed composite. A Spotify / Outsource-v4.0 / Underfund-Regulatory / Build-In-House envelope forfeited the CNBV deadline and the revenue target while leaving the Alpha→Gamma coordination failure structurally unaddressed.

## Company Background

Pago Claro had been founded in Mexico City in 2018 as embedded B2B payments infrastructure for SMEs and mid-market companies — a payment gateway, a white-label digital wallet and electronic invoice financing (factoraje) — monetised on a 1.4–2.2% transaction fee, a MXN 2,800–18,000/month SaaS platform fee and a factoraje spread. By 2025 it carried 2,840 merchant clients (including 48 mid-market enterprises), MXN 8.4B in monthly volume growing 28% year-on-year, MXN 312M in revenue at a 15.4% EBITDA margin, and 124 employees of whom 68 were engineers across six squads — Alpha (core payments API), Beta (merchant dashboard), Gamma (mobile SDK), Delta (factoraje), Epsilon (data and analytics) and Zeta (infrastructure and security). Its proprietary Go/Kafka/PostgreSQL core ran at 99.94% uptime; its React merchant dashboard had not had a major refactor since 2021; its internal tech-debt score stood at 62/100. It held an IFPE license from the CNBV under the Ley Fintech and ran factoraje through a SOFOM ENR. The leadership bench paired CEO Andrés Villanueva (non-technical founder) with Mendoza, CPO Jorge Saavedra (ex-MercadoPago), CFO Claudia Ríos (ex-BBVA) and Head of Engineering Rodrigo Castellanos (internally promoted, 2024).

## Industry and Market Context

The Mexican fintech landscape in 2026 sat inside three structural features the envelope had to respect. First, the Ley Fintech (2018) and a CNBV-Banxico-SHCP supervisory stack had, through Circular 3/2026, mandated ML-based AML monitoring by September 30, 2026, SPEI 3.0 open-finance APIs by December 31, 2026, and biometric KYC by March 31, 2027 — a non-negotiable 38-engineer-month draw on a single six-month window. Second, the agile-at-scale evidence base offered no universal answer: SAFe (Leffingwell, 2011) was a prescriptive enterprise framework typically justified above 100 engineers, the Spotify model (Kniberg/Ivarsson, 2012) was a descriptive culture pattern partially abandoned by Spotify itself, and the Team Topologies platform-team pattern (Skelton/Pais, 2019) addressed precisely the duplicate-build and cognitive-load failures Pago Claro was exhibiting. Third, the competitive window was closing: the rival SDK from Bolt had launched in January 2026 and had already captured three Pago Claro prospects, placing the cost of further mobile delay at MXN 6.8M/month.

Empirically, the DORA research base (Forsgren/Humble/Kim, *Accelerate*, 2018; State of DevOps 2023) framed the scorecard against which any framework would be judged: Pago Claro's 2.3×/week deployment frequency, 8.4-day lead time, 12.1% change-failure rate and 4.2-hour MTTR sat well below elite benchmarks, and the literature held that elite performers were roughly twice as likely to exceed revenue and profitability goals — the bridge between the engineering decision and the MXN 624M mandate.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, on March 9, ratified the MXN 480M Series B and the 2× revenue mandate. The CNBV had, in February, published Circular 3/2026. Castellanos had, on March 4, delivered the 112-point critical tech-debt register and the 38-engineer-month regulatory estimate. The QA lead had, on March 6, published Squad Gamma's 34% coverage and 127-bug position. DataLens AI's founders had, on March 11, tabled the MXN 180M offer with a 30-day exclusivity. Mendoza had, on March 13, convened the Valle de Bravo offsite to close all four decisions before the March 20 board pre-read.

### What Mendoza knew

The team had modelled three four-round envelopes. A Custom-Hybrid / Descope-plus-Rotate / Capacity-Balanced / License-with-Option envelope projected cash above MXN 440M, compliance at 100% and a rising DORA composite. A SAFe-Underfunded / Reinforce / Over-Allocate / Acquire-Without-Diligence envelope projected cash below MXN 280M and a compliance gap. A Spotify / Outsource / Underfund-Regulatory / Build envelope projected a missed CNBV deadline.

### What she did not know

Whether Mariana Ruiz would accept a rotation to Squad Alpha or take the Google or Nubank offer; whether DataLens's training data — 24M transaction records from a regional bank — had been licensed cleanly, or whether the CNBV could later order the model destroyed; whether the first-time board would tolerate a roadmap held below the slack line for two quarters; and whether the CNBV would require model explainability that a black-box neural network could not provide without the MXN 600K SHAP/LIME layer.

### What others were saying

**CEO Andrés Villanueva**: *"The board wants 2× by 2027. Don't let process slow the roadmap."*

**CPO Jorge Saavedra**: *"SAFe. We're B2B and regulated — we need PI Planning and predictable trains."*

**Head of Engineering Rodrigo Castellanos**: *"Spotify and SAFe solve Spotify and SAFe's problems. Fix the dependency contracts first."*

**CFO Claudia Ríos**: *"Show me the tech-debt remediation on a P&L, and don't pay MXN 180M for data we can't audit."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Mendoza had narrowed the space to three end-state envelopes.

- **Option A — Custom-Hybrid + Platform Team / Descope-to-v4.0-lite + Rotate Mariana / Capacity-Balanced / License-with-Option:** the recommended envelope. Cash protected above MXN 440M; regulatory compliance 100%; DORA composite and velocity rising; Mariana retained; the strongest composite trajectory. Recommended.
- **Option B — SAFe-Underfunded / Reinforce Gamma / Over-Allocate / Acquire DataLens Without Diligence:** the pressure-response envelope. Cash below MXN 280M on the MXN 180M outlay plus a MXN 15.6M provenance hit; Brooks's-Law velocity loss in Gamma; compliance gap. Rejected.
- **Option C — Spotify / Outsource v4.0 / Underfund Regulatory / Build In-House:** the autonomy-denial envelope. CNBV deadline missed; revenue short of the glide path; the Alpha→Gamma coordination failure left structurally unaddressed. Rejected.

Mendoza's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final outcome was Round 3 — the capacity allocation that, by funding regulatory to 100% and protecting the 16-month tech-debt envelope at a ~798% ROI, converted the velocity recovered from debt remediation into the roadmap headroom the board's 2× mandate required, rather than burning cash on the MXN 180M acquisition to buy capacity that disciplined allocation already contained.
