## Abstract

ATLAS INDUSTRIAL was, in the case universe, a São Paulo-headquartered industrial-parts manufacturer serving pan-LATAM automotive, appliance and industrial-equipment original-equipment manufacturers, with USD 2.1B in 2025 revenue, a 2025 annual procurement spend of USD 1.2B across roughly 2,400 active suppliers and 92 category families, a 2025 EBITDA margin of 12%, a Supplier-Trust Index of 70 against the pan-LATAM industrial-procurement peer-benchmark band of 62–74, a 2025 supplier-retention reading of 100% on the Strategic-quadrant cohort, and a 2025 run-rate cost-savings reading of 3.1% against a pan-LATAM peer-benchmark of 4.6% and an internal 2026 target of 5% (USD 60M annualised). The 2023–2025 procurement operating model had been anchored on a relationship-driven negotiation discipline that had preserved supplier continuity but had left the AI-augmented-negotiation savings-window to competitors already deploying structured AI-negotiation agents. The Q1 2026 board had approved a USD 14M AI-deployment envelope against a Savings-Captured floor above USD 30M inside 2026, a Supplier-Trust floor above 62, a Supplier-Retention floor above 92%, an EBITDA-impact floor above +120 basis points, and a composite Procurement-AI Score above 72. The case opens at 5:46 a.m. on Tuesday, April 14, 2026, at Atlas Industrial's Vila Olímpia office as **Damián Fiorelli**, a 45-year-old Head of Procurement, prepares the Round-1 deployment-scope commitment. Seventy-six days separate him from the June 29 board procurement-and-AI review.

2. Analyze the Round-2 transparency-posture commitment (disclosed-AI with supplier-notification at RFP stage vs. concealed-AI running behind a human-negotiator interface) and recommend the disclosed envelope against the concealed envelope on the supplier-trust and retention basis.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 category-segmentation-gating commitment (exclude Strategic-quadrant and Bottleneck-quadrant categories from AI-negotiation, reserve for joint-roadmap human negotiation) and recommend the gated envelope against the ungated envelope on the supplier-continuity basis.

5. Design the Round-5 AI-human-oversight commitment (human-in-the-loop checkpoint at every AI-proposed offer above USD 5M and on every Strategic-quadrant exception vs. full-autonomy) and diagnose when AI-augmented negotiation becomes a durable procurement capability rather than a short-term cost tax.

## The Protagonist

Damián Fiorelli, 45, had assumed the Head-of-Procurement role at Atlas Industrial on November 3, 2025, from a Tier-1 European automotive OEM where he had, across nine years, led two AI-negotiation deployments and had closed a 2024 AI-augmented-procurement programme across a USD 2.4B spend envelope that had delivered 4.6% annualised savings while preserving Supplier-Trust above 68 and Supplier-Retention above 94%. Before that he had spent six years as a Head of Category Strategy at a Buenos Aires-based industrial holding. The Chief Executive Officer had recruited him specifically on two capabilities: his documented discipline of gating AI-negotiation to Leverage-quadrant categories and his willingness to disclose AI use to suppliers at RFP stage. His 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the Savings-Captured outcome and 20% to the Supplier-Retention floor.

## The Decision

Fiorelli had to commit, by the May 7 procurement-committee pre-review, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–5.

1. **Round 1 — AI-negotiation deployment scope.** Three archetypes: Leverage-quadrant-pilot (pilot across 18 Leverage-quadrant categories on USD 310M spend, savings-target 5.8%, Composite 74), full-portfolio (deploy across 92 categories on USD 1.2B — Composite 34, supplier-exodus risk), or Leverage-plus-Non-Critical (Leverage and 31 Non-Critical categories, USD 520M — Composite 62, partial risk envelope). Working envelope: Leverage-quadrant-pilot.
2. **Round 2 — Transparency posture.** Three archetypes: disclosed-AI (suppliers notified at RFP stage that AI-negotiation will run, written disclosure of algorithm class, feedback window, Supplier-Trust sustained, Composite 74), concealed-AI (AI runs behind human-negotiator interface — Composite 32, supplier-trust erosion and dark-pattern litigation risk), or partial-disclosure (disclosure that AI is in use without algorithm-class specification — Composite 54). Working envelope: disclosed-AI.
3. **Round 3 — Category-segmentation gating.** Three vectors: gated (exclude 14 Strategic-quadrant categories and 9 Bottleneck-quadrant categories from AI-negotiation, reserve for joint-roadmap human negotiation with quarterly review, Composite 76), ungated (AI-negotiation across all 92 categories — Composite 28, Strategic-supplier defection risk), or Strategic-only-gated (exclude Strategic only, run AI on Bottleneck — Composite 52, continuity risk on sole-source Bottleneck). Working envelope: gated.
4. **Round 4 — Supplier-relationship architecture.** Three vectors: joint-value-scorecard (quarterly supplier review on on-time delivery, quality-PPM, innovation-contribution and cost-of-quality alongside price, Supplier-Trust +12, Composite 78), price-only-take-rate (price as the dominant variable — Composite 32, BATNA erosion), or blended (price weighted 70%, value 30% — Composite 58). Working envelope: joint-value-scorecard.
5. **Round 5 — AI-human oversight.** Three vectors: human-in-the-loop (human checkpoint at every AI-proposed offer above USD 5M, at every Strategic-quadrant exception, and at every counter-offer >12% movement from the AI's first offer, Composite 80), full-autonomy (AI operates without checkpoints — Composite 34, concession-cascade and regulatory risk), or offline-review (weekly batch review without in-flight checkpoints — Composite 56). Working envelope: human-in-the-loop.

On Fiorelli's working model, a Leverage-Pilot / Disclosed / Gated / Joint-Value / Human-in-the-Loop envelope delivered Savings Captured USD 36M, Supplier Trust 70, Supplier Retention 96%, EBITDA +142 bps, Composite 76. A Full-Portfolio / Concealed / Ungated / Price-Only / Full-Autonomy envelope delivered Savings Captured USD 48M (short-term), Supplier Trust 38, Supplier Retention 72%, EBITDA +184 bps (Year 1), Composite 28 (regulatory and continuity triggers). A Leverage-Plus-Non-Critical / Partial-Disclosure / Strategic-Only-Gated / Blended / Offline-Review envelope delivered Composite 54.

## Company Background

Atlas Industrial had been founded in São Paulo in 1998 as a single-site industrial-fastener manufacturer and had, across twenty-eight years, grown into a pan-LATAM industrial-parts manufacturer with four production sites (São Paulo, Campinas, Monterrey and Córdoba), 2,400 active suppliers across 92 category families, and a client roster weighted toward pan-LATAM automotive and appliance OEM. The 2023–2025 procurement operating model had delivered run-rate savings of 3.1% but had exposed Atlas to a 1.5-point annualised savings-gap against peers already running AI-augmented negotiation across their Leverage-quadrant categories.

The Q1 2026 board dossier had identified three specific pressures the five-round envelope would inherit. First, the Q4 2025 Chief-Financial-Officer analysis had priced the 1.9-point savings-gap at USD 22M of annualised gross-profit forgone. Second, the Q1 2026 supplier-trust pulse had surfaced a cohort of 74 suppliers who had explicitly surveyed a preference for AI-negotiation with disclosure — a cohort that controlled 62% of the Leverage-quadrant spend. Third, the Q1 2026 competitive benchmarking had documented two pan-LATAM automotive OEM peers already running AI-negotiation on USD 600M–USD 900M of spend and had, on external reporting, delivered 5.2% and 4.8% cost-savings while preserving Supplier-Trust above 66.

## Industry and Market Context

The AI-augmented-procurement landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2022–2024 AI-negotiation evidence base had, across 34 industrial-procurement programmes, documented a savings differential of 2.2–3.4 points between disclosed-AI envelopes and concealed-AI envelopes measured at the twelve-month horizon — net of concealed-AI's higher Year-1 savings offset by supplier-retention erosion. Second, the 2020–2024 category-gating literature had, across 28 industrial procurement programmes, placed gated envelopes at a Supplier-Retention differential of 18–24 points on the Strategic-quadrant cohort against ungated envelopes. Third, the 2022–2024 human-in-the-loop literature had, across 22 AI-augmented-negotiation deployments, placed human-in-the-loop envelopes at a concession-cascade incident rate of 2% against 18% for full-autonomy deployments.

Empirically, the post-2022 AI-procurement literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, programmes that had disclosed AI-negotiation at RFP stage had, across a 2020–2024 sample of 28 programmes, preserved Supplier-Trust above 62 in 84% of cases against 18% for concealed-AI programmes. Second, programmes that had gated Strategic-quadrant categories out of AI-negotiation had sustained joint-roadmap-continuity above 88% in 82% of cases against 42% for ungated programmes.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the October 2025 procurement-review, approved the USD 14M envelope and the June 29 review calendar. The Director of Category Strategy had, on January 26, delivered the 92-category Kraljic-segmentation. The Head of Supplier Relations had, on February 9, delivered the supplier-trust baseline. The Chief Technology Officer had, on February 23, delivered the AI-negotiation vendor-selection short-list. The Chief Legal Officer had, on March 6, delivered the disclosure-framework analysis. The Chief Financial Officer had, on March 20, confirmed the USD 22M annualised gap estimate.

### What Fiorelli knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. A Leverage-Pilot / Disclosed / Gated / Joint-Value / Human-in-the-Loop Envelope projected Composite 76. A Full-Portfolio / Concealed / Ungated / Price-Only / Full-Autonomy Envelope projected Composite 28. A Leverage-Plus-Non-Critical / Partial-Disclosure / Strategic-Only-Gated / Blended / Offline-Review Envelope projected Composite 54.

### What he did not know

Whether the disclosed-AI posture would compress the short-term savings capture below the USD 30M floor inside the 18-category Leverage pilot; whether the joint-value scorecard would hold against the 2026 H2 automotive-OEM demand softening; whether the human-in-the-loop checkpoint architecture would meet the throughput requirements of the 2026 tender calendar; and whether the 74-supplier AI-positive cohort would sustain their preference under the first post-pilot tender cycle.

### What others were saying

**The Director of Category Strategy**: *"Leverage-quadrant pilot on eighteen categories. Full-portfolio is a cost-discipline story we cannot defend to the Strategic suppliers."*

**The Head of Supplier Relations**: *"Disclosed-AI. Concealed-AI will surface in the first negotiation post-mortem, and the Supplier-Trust reading will collapse."*

**The Chief Legal Officer**: *"Disclosure at RFP stage, written algorithm-class, feedback window. Anything less is a dark-pattern exposure."*

**The Chief Executive Officer**: *"In procurement, the cheapest quote is the one that ships next year. Ship the disclosed, gated, scorecard-anchored architecture."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Fiorelli had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Leverage-Pilot / Disclosed / Gated / Joint-Value / Human-in-the-Loop:** the recommended envelope. Savings USD 36M. Supplier Trust 70. Supplier Retention 96%. EBITDA +142 bps. Composite 76. Recommended.
- **Option B — Full-Portfolio / Concealed / Ungated / Price-Only / Full-Autonomy:** the Year-1-savings envelope. Composite 28. Rejected — Supplier-Trust and Retention collapse.
- **Option C — Leverage-Plus-Non-Critical / Partial-Disclosure / Strategic-Only-Gated / Blended / Offline-Review:** the partial envelope. Composite 54. Rejected — forfeits human-in-the-loop and joint-value architecture.

Fiorelli's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 2 — the disclosed-AI posture — because it was the round that turned AI-negotiation from a supplier-relationship risk into a supplier-relationship asset and made the gated / scorecard / oversight commitments credible.
