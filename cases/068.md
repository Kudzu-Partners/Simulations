## Abstract

Conecta was, in the case universe, a Bogotá-headquartered B2B services marketplace for Latin American small-and-medium-enterprise buyers, pre-committed at case opening with thirty-five service providers across accounting, legal, marketing, technology, and operations and 280 registered but inactive SME buyers. On Monday, February 16, 2026, the company closed a USD 1.8M seed round at a USD 9M post-money valuation, giving it eighteen months of runway at the planned USD 95K monthly burn. Carolina Espinoza, founder and CEO, had to commit the Round-1 platform-architecture package in the first ninety days — the chicken-and-egg configuration that would compound across six two-month rounds and twenty-four calendar months. The Platform Health Index composite — anchored at 32 on Day 1 on Match Rate, Liquidity, Cohort Retention, LTV-to-CAC, and Trust — would be the single metric the Series B investors in Round 6 would benchmark against. Six rounds would unfold; a Round-6 offer of USD 5M from a MercadoLibre ecosystem fund with a six-LATAM-market white-label requirement would close the case. The case opens at 6:34 a.m. on Tuesday, February 24, 2026, as Espinoza prepares the Round-1 package for the March 10 board.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Round-1 chicken-and-egg strategy — BD focus ratio toward providers, take-rate level, trust-infrastructure versus marketing investment — and diagnose the configuration that earns the first transaction before it advertises the second.
2. Analyze the Round-2 match-rate-versus-provider-count trade-off and recommend a quality-filtering protocol that moves match rate from Day-1 baseline to 40-plus percent.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 top-provider take-rate pressure and design a Preferred-Provider tier with SLA commitments that preserves the headline take rate.
4. Design the Round-4 expansion-path choice — geography, vertical, enterprise — and the Round-5 zero-take-rate competitive response that locks in top providers without racing to margin zero.
5. Recommend a complete Round-1 package and identify the Round-6 white-label-versus-independent-Series-B choice most likely to preserve the platform's technology moat.

On the morning of **Tuesday, February 24, 2026**, **Carolina Espinoza** sat at the long shared desk of the WeWork Chicó in Bogotá, where the Conecta four-person founding team was headquartered, and opened the Round-1 package draft her co-founder had finalized at 1:08 a.m. The package opened to three sliders: BD focus toward providers zero to one hundred, take rate four-to-eighteen percent, marketing-versus-trust-infrastructure allocation zero to one hundred. Fourteen days separated her from the March 10 board meeting — a three-person board comprising Espinoza, her co-founder, and the lead seed investor from a LATAM-focused venture fund.

---

## The Protagonist

Carolina Espinoza, 34, had founded Conecta in August 2025 with her co-founder, a former software-engineering lead at a LATAM unicorn. A graduate of Universidad de los Andes with an MBA from MIT Sloan, she had, before founding Conecta, spent four years at a Bogotá-based consulting firm building digital operations for SME clients, where she had observed — in her own 2024 founder-hypothesis memo — that the single most consistent pain point for the LATAM SME buyer was not finding the service provider but verifying them. The Conecta founder-hypothesis memo had run fourteen pages and had anchored on a single claim: "The marketplace will win if and only if it builds the trust infrastructure before it builds the transaction volume." The seed investor's November 2025 term sheet had ratified the claim in writing.

---

## The Decision

Espinoza had to commit, by 9:00 a.m. on Tuesday, March 10, the Round-1 platform-architecture package the three-person board would treat as the twenty-four-month operating envelope:

1. **BD-Focus Ratio** — what proportion of the four-person BD team's working hours (total 160 hours/week) to allocate to provider-side density versus buyer-side activation in the first two months.
2. **Take-Rate Level** — the Day-1 take rate across the five service categories, inside a four-to-eighteen-percent range the industry cohort had signalled.
3. **Marketing-versus-Trust-Infrastructure Allocation** — how to split the USD 220K Round-1 cash envelope between paid acquisition and trust infrastructure (review system, escrow, dispute resolution).

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across six rounds. A Round-1 package that set BD focus at sixty-five percent toward providers, take rate at eight percent, and allocated 75% of the USD 220K to trust infrastructure with 25% to selective buyer-activation marketing produced, on the internal platform-health model, a Round-2 Match-Rate move from 18% to 41% through quality-filtering, a Round-3 top-provider-preservation path with Preferred-Provider tier deployment, a Round-5 competitive-response that held sixteen of the top twenty providers against a zero-take-rate entrant, and a Round-6 Series B at USD 5M independent-raise with the Platform Health Index at 76.

## Company Background

Conecta had been incorporated in August 2025 as a Colombia-registered entity with a Delaware parent; the February 16, 2026 seed round had closed with participation from three LATAM-focused seed funds and two strategic angels from the LATAM SME-services sector. The pre-committed thirty-five provider panel had been assembled across November 2025 through January 2026 and had been distributed as eight accounting firms, seven legal practices, seven marketing agencies, seven technology consultancies, and six operations-and-logistics providers — all sub-twenty-employee firms serving a Bogotá SME market. The 280 registered buyers had been surfaced through the founders' personal networks and a targeted LinkedIn campaign; none had transacted on the platform before the February seed close.

The cash position at the case opening was USD 1.72M — the USD 1.8M seed less the USD 80K Q4 2025 pre-seed burn. The founding team was four people: Espinoza (CEO), her co-founder (CTO), a head of BD who had joined in January 2026, and a head of operations who had joined in February 2026. The USD 95K monthly burn projected eighteen months of runway without revenue; revenue in the Round-1 model was not assumed to begin materially until month four. The 2026 budget allocated USD 220K to Round-1 discretionary spend (paid acquisition plus trust infrastructure) and the balance to team and infrastructure.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM B2B marketplace landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Round-1 decision sat inside. First, three LATAM-focused marketplace competitors had, across 2023–2025, raised between USD 6M and USD 14M in Series A-and-extension rounds; two had deployed aggressive zero-take-rate or low-take-rate entry strategies into adjacent geographies (Mexico City, Lima); one had failed at month fourteen with a Platform Health Index below 20. Second, the post-2018 network-effect literature had converged on a finding: match rate — the percent of buyer requests resulting in completed transactions — was, across a 2018–2024 sample of thirty-one B2B marketplaces, the single most predictive Platform Health Index component, with a correlation coefficient of 0.78 against twenty-four-month survival. Third, the Colombian Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio had signalled, in late 2025, a marketplace-platform-liability framework that would formalize the dispute-resolution-and-escrow expectations that Conecta had already built into its architectural roadmap.

Empirically, the platform-scaling research had two robust findings the Round-1 package had to respect. First, BD allocation in the first ninety days biased toward the short-side of a two-sided marketplace (providers in a services marketplace, buyers in a product marketplace) had, across a 2018–2024 sample of fifty-eight B2B marketplaces, produced Year-1 match-rate trajectories averaging 44% against 22% for balanced-BD approaches. Second, take-rate disciplines that introduced Preferred-Provider tiers with SLA commitments rather than cutting the headline rate had, across a 2020–2024 sample of twenty-two marketplaces, preserved median gross-margin profiles 340 basis points above cohort peers at the same Round-4 scale point.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The founding team had, across November 2025 through January 2026, executed the provider-panel pre-commitment work that the seed-round investors had treated as the principal validation milestone. The February 16 close had been contingent on the panel existing. The co-founder had, in the first Board pre-read on February 18, flagged three strategic choices that were not yet locked: the BD ratio, the take-rate level, and the marketing-versus-trust split. The seed-investor Board member had requested the Round-1 package commitment by March 10.

### What Espinoza knew

The internal team had modeled three Round-1 packages. A Growth-Velocity Package (BD 50/50, take rate 6%, 70% marketing / 30% trust) projected Round-2 Match Rate 22%, Round-6 Platform Health 52, Series B probability 34%. A Structural Package (BD 65% providers, take rate 8%, 25% marketing / 75% trust) projected Round-2 Match Rate 41%, Round-6 Platform Health 76, Series B probability 84%. A Trust-First Package (BD 80% providers, take rate 10%, 15% marketing / 85% trust) projected Round-2 Match Rate 44%, Round-6 Platform Health 74, Series B probability 80% — at a Round-1 provider-attraction friction that the internal team flagged as a Month-2 risk.

### What she did not know

Whether the thirty-five pre-committed providers would transact in the first sixty days at the sixty-day-transaction-or-churn signal the internal team had modelled, or whether the trust-infrastructure investment would push the first-transaction timing to Day 75–90; whether the Mexico-City-competitor-entry-into-Bogotá that the competitive-intelligence team had placed in the Round-4 window would actually land on schedule or delay to Round 5; whether the Round-5 ProServicios zero-take-rate-plus-signing-bonus event would trigger at the modelled 42% probability, at a higher probability if ProServicios secured a Series A, or not at all; and whether the Round-6 MercadoLibre-ecosystem-fund white-label condition would be firm or negotiable.

### What others were saying

**The Co-founder and CTO**: *"Structural Package. Trust infrastructure first. The technology moat that matters is not feature parity — it is the review-escrow-dispute architecture that the next entrant has to rebuild."*

**The Head of Business Development**: *"Sixty-five percent BD toward providers in the first two months. Then pivot to 50/50 at Month Three once the match rate is above 35%. The buyer side gets activated by provider density, not by marketing spend."*

**The Lead Seed Investor**: *"Preferred-Provider tier in Round 3 is the single most important structural move. It will come up in Round 3 because the top three providers will ask for a take-rate cut in Round 3. You will want the tier ready in Round 2."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Espinoza had narrowed the space to three Round-1 packages.

- **Option A — Growth-Velocity:** BD 50/50, take rate 6%, 70% marketing / 30% trust. Round-6 Platform Health 52. Fastest initial visibility. Structurally weak on match rate.

- **Option B — Structural Package:** BD 65% providers, take rate 8%, 25% marketing / 75% trust. Round-6 Platform Health 76. Aligns with Co-founder and Lead Seed Investor positions.

- **Option C — Trust-First:** BD 80% providers, take rate 10%, 15% marketing / 85% trust. Round-6 Platform Health 74. Two-point composite drag with provider-attraction friction risk.

Espinoza's private view, not yet shared with the Head of BD, was that Option B was the right Round-1 package and that the single subsequent decision most correlated with the Round-6 Series B outcome was the Round-5 competitive-response — specifically, the twenty-four-month Preferred-Provider exclusivity agreement that locked in the top sixteen providers against the ProServicios zero-take-rate entry without matching the zero-rate signal.
