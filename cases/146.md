## Abstract

PLASTICOL MEDELLÍN was, in the case universe, a Colombian plastics-manufacturing plant operating on the industrial corridor of southern Medellín, with 620 unionised workers under a collective-bargaining agreement signed in 2024 that had expired five weeks before the case opening, 14 lost-time safety incidents reported in calendar year 2025 against an industry-peer-benchmark of 4–6, a union-management relationship index of 42, a plant-utilization rate of 74%, and a cost ceiling imposed by the Chief Financial Officer at a wage-envelope rise of 12% across the forthcoming two-year CBA. The 2023–2025 industrial-relations-operating model had been anchored on a minimise-concessions discipline that had delivered short-term cost restraint but had left safety-incidents drifting above benchmark and had eroded trust to a point where the Q4 2025 climate-survey had placed union-management relationship at the lowest reading in twelve years. The Q1 2026 plant-board had approved a four-round Director-of-Industrial-Relations-led negotiation mandate against a wage-envelope ceiling at or below 12%, a Safety-Incident ceiling below 6/year, a plant-utilization floor above 78%, and a Composite Labor-Relations Score above 68. The case opens at 6:18 a.m. on Monday, April 13, 2026, at the PlastiCol plant as **Carolina Restrepo**, a 46-year-old Director of Industrial Relations, prepares the Round-1 opening-position commitment. Forty-two days separate her from the May 25 CBA signature-or-strike deadline.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Round-1 opening-position commitment (union 18% / management 3% anchors) and diagnose when integrative anchoring dominates positional anchoring on the convergence-time and relationship-index trade-offs.
2. Analyze the Round-2 safety-investment-plus-joint-committee commitment (USD 1.1M safety infrastructure, joint committee with union seats, incident-projection 14 → 5) and recommend the safety-first envelope against the wages-first envelope on the relationship-index and settlement-velocity basis.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 production-flexibility-for-wage-step-up trade (union concedes flex scheduling and weekend premium; management concedes 11% wage step-up; plant utilization +8%) and recommend the integrative-trade envelope against the distributive-wage-only envelope on the composite basis.
4. Respond to the Round-4 relationship-preservation commitment (grievance panel with neutral chair, quarterly joint review, no-strike clause while panel is active) and recommend the relationship-architecture envelope against the transactional-CBA envelope on the twenty-four-month relationship-index basis.
5. Design a four-round collective-bargaining sequence that clears Wage-Envelope ≤ 12%, Safety-Incidents < 6/year, Plant-Utilization > 78%, and Composite > 68 under a six-week CBA expiry clock.

Es cómo van los próximos dos años."* Forty-two days separated her from the May 25 CBA signature-or-strike deadline.

## The Protagonist

Carolina Restrepo, 46, had assumed the Director-of-Industrial-Relations role at PlastiCol Medellín on October 1, 2024, after sixteen years inside a Tier-1 Colombian manufacturing group where she had, across four plant-level CBAs, closed three settlements inside the management cost-envelope without triggering a strike, and had, in 2021, led a safety-investment-anchored negotiation at a food-processing plant that delivered a 68% reduction in lost-time incidents alongside a wage settlement at the CFO's ceiling. Before that she had spent five years as a labor-lawyer. The plant-board had recruited her specifically on two capabilities: her documented preference for safety-investment-first sequencing and her willingness to walk into a negotiation with the budget-envelope written on the first page of her folder rather than the last. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the Wage-Envelope outcome and 25% to the Safety-Incident floor.

## The Decision

Restrepo had to commit, by the April 21 plant-board pre-review, the Round-1 opening-position envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–4.

1. **Round 1 — Opening positions.** Union tables 18% wage-rise over two years; management opens at 3%. Three archetypes: integrative-anchor-management-opening (open at 5% with explicit pairing to safety-and-flexibility conditions, Composite 72), positional-anchor (open at 3% without explicit conditions, Composite 56, longer convergence-time), or accommodate-anchor (open at 9% — Composite 48, budget-ceiling forfeit). Working envelope: integrative-anchor.
2. **Round 2 — Safety investment and joint committee.** Fourteen incidents × industry-peer-benchmark 4–6. Three archetypes: safety-first-envelope (USD 1.1M in safety infrastructure, joint committee with union seats, incidents projected 14 → 5, relationship-index +9 before wage discussion, Composite 78), wages-first (defer safety until after wage settlement — Composite 52, relationship erosion), or safety-lite (USD 300K, no joint committee — Composite 58, incident-projection flat). Working envelope: safety-first.
3. **Round 3 — Production-flexibility-for-wage-step-up trade.** Three vectors: integrative-trade (union concedes flex-scheduling, weekend shifts with premium pay, peak-season surge; management concedes 11% wage step-up over two years; plant-utilization +8%, Composite 78), distributive-wage-only (wage-only settlement at 12%, no flex — Composite 56, utilization flat), or no-flex-walk-away (no trade, strike risk — Composite 24, CBA breach). Working envelope: integrative-trade.
4. **Round 4 — Relationship preservation.** Three vectors: relationship-architecture (grievance panel with neutral chair, quarterly joint review of CBA in practice, no-strike clause while panel is active, relationship-index 42 → 71, Composite 78), transactional-CBA (standard CBA without architecture — Composite 58, twenty-four-month erosion risk), or management-only-committees (management steering only — Composite 48, relationship-index drift). Working envelope: relationship-architecture.

On Restrepo's working model, an Integrative-Anchor / Safety-First / Integrative-Trade / Relationship-Architecture envelope delivered Wage-Envelope 11%, Safety-Incidents 5/year, Plant-Utilization 82%, Composite 78. A Positional-Anchor / Wages-First / Distributive-Wage-Only / Transactional-CBA envelope delivered Wage-Envelope 12%, Safety-Incidents 13/year, Plant-Utilization 74%, Composite 46. An Accommodate-Anchor / Safety-Lite / No-Flex-Walk-Away / Management-Only envelope delivered strike-outcome with complete forfeit across all dimensions, Composite 18.

## Company Background

PlastiCol Medellín had been founded in 1984 as a single-line rigid-plastic moulding operation and had, across forty-two years, grown into a multi-line plastics-manufacturing plant serving FMCG, household-goods and industrial-packaging customers across Colombia, Ecuador and Peru, with 2025 revenue of USD 84M, 620 unionised workers and 42 non-union technical staff. The 2023–2025 industrial-relations-operating model had been anchored on a minimise-concessions discipline that had delivered short-term cost restraint inside the 2024 CBA but had imported a 2025 safety-incident reading of 14 against the industry-peer-benchmark of 4–6, a relationship-index reading of 42 against a plant-operating floor of 60, and a union-survey readout that had placed management-credibility at the lowest reading in twelve years.

The Q1 2026 plant-board dossier had identified three specific pressures the four-round envelope would inherit. First, the 2025 safety-incident reading had placed PlastiCol on a Ministry-of-Labor quarterly inspection watch-list, with a remediation-plan submission required by Q3 2026. Second, the parent-group's 2026 group-wide CBA-cost guidance had placed the PlastiCol wage-envelope ceiling at 12% against a union opening position that historically tabled at 1.6–1.8× the parent-group ceiling. Third, the Q4 2025 customer-on-time-delivery reading had dropped to 83% against a customer-contract floor of 92% — a pattern the Head of Plant Operations had tied to plant-utilization constraints on the weekend-shift schedule the 2024 CBA had foreclosed.

## Industry and Market Context

The Colombian plastics-manufacturing labor-relations landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2018–2024 integrative-bargaining evidence base had, across 48 Colombian and regional plant-level CBAs, documented a settlement-velocity differential of 22–28 days between integrative-anchor openings and positional-anchor openings. Second, the 2020–2024 safety-first-sequencing literature had, across 36 plant-level CBAs, placed safety-investment-first envelopes at a relationship-index differential of 14–18 points against wages-first envelopes at settlement. Third, the 2018–2024 relationship-architecture literature had, across 28 Colombian plants, placed grievance-panel-with-neutral-chair envelopes at a twenty-four-month relationship-index-preservation rate of 78% against 24% for transactional-CBA envelopes.

Empirically, the post-2022 industrial-relations literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, plants that had traded production-flexibility for wage-step-up had, across a 2019–2024 sample of 32 LATAM plants, preserved plant-utilization above 78% in 74% of cases against 28% for plants on distributive-wage-only settlements. Second, plants that had installed no-strike clauses tied to active grievance panels had, across the same sample, reduced work-stoppage days by 84% over the subsequent two-year CBA horizon.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The plant-board had, in the December 2025 board review, approved the negotiation mandate and the May 25 signature-or-strike deadline. The Head of Plant Operations had, on February 19, delivered the plant-utilization and weekend-shift flexibility case. The Safety Manager had, on February 26, delivered the fourteen-incident root-cause analysis and the USD 1.1M safety-investment proposal. The Chief Financial Officer had, on March 5, confirmed the 12% wage-envelope ceiling. The union had, on March 12, tabled its 18% opening position and its safety-committee demand. The Ministry-of-Labor inspection watch-list notification had landed on March 19.

### What Restrepo knew

The team had modelled three four-round envelopes. An Integrative-Anchor / Safety-First / Integrative-Trade / Relationship-Architecture Envelope projected Composite 78. A Positional-Anchor / Wages-First / Distributive / Transactional Envelope projected Composite 46. An Accommodate-Anchor / Safety-Lite / Walk-Away / Management-Only Envelope projected strike-outcome Composite 18.

### What she did not know

Whether the union-negotiating-committee would accept a 5% opening tied to safety-investment anchoring as a good-faith move or would read it as a procedural trick; whether the weekend-shift flexibility concession would hold across the twenty-four-month CBA horizon or would surface grievances inside the first quarter of the new agreement; whether the Ministry-of-Labor inspection of Q3 2026 would accept the joint-committee-with-14-to-5 trajectory as remediation-compliant; and whether the parent-group would hold the 12% ceiling if PlastiCol's plant-utilization trajectory surfaced above 82% inside the first six months of the CBA.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Plant Operations**: *"Integrative trade. Weekend shifts and the wage step-up travel together. Neither side is giving; both sides are moving."*

**The Safety Manager**: *"The one-point-one million is not a concession. It is a remediation-plan cost. The alternative is Ministry-of-Labor enforcement."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"Twelve percent is the ceiling. Safety is already budgeted. The flex-for-step-up trade is the only way the numbers work."*

**The Plant General Manager**: *"A CBA isn't a document. It's how the next two years go."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Restrepo had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Integrative-Anchor / Safety-First / Integrative-Trade / Relationship-Architecture:** the recommended envelope. Wage-Envelope 11%. Safety-Incidents 5/year. Plant-Utilization 82%. Composite 78. Recommended.
- **Option B — Positional-Anchor / Wages-First / Distributive-Wage-Only / Transactional-CBA:** the conventional envelope. Composite 46. Rejected — safety and utilization forfeits.
- **Option C — Accommodate-Anchor / Safety-Lite / No-Flex-Walk-Away / Management-Only:** the break-and-capitulate envelope. Composite 18. Rejected — strike outcome breaches CBA deadline.

Restrepo's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 2 — the safety-first sequencing that moved relationship-index by nine points before the wage number was discussed, which made the Round-3 integrative trade feasible.
