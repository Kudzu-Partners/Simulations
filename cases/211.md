## Abstract

Mercado Fresco S.A. was, in the case universe, a small fresh-produce distributor serving seventy-eight restaurant and hotel customers across a single LATAM metropolitan area, with nine employees, one rented cold-storage unit, one delivery route built around a single ageing van and a Q1 2026 opening balance sheet that totalled USD 63,000 on each side: Cash USD 25,000, Accounts Receivable USD 20,000, Inventory USD 16,000, Accounts Payable USD 10,000, Loan Payable USD 10,000 and Equity USD 40,000. The founder-owners had, after twenty-eight months of running the books on a cash-only spreadsheet that had priced three consecutive working-capital crunches as profit problems rather than collection problems, promoted their senior accountant, **Mariana Quispe-Roldán**, to Controller on January 3, 2026, with a single mandate: produce the first complete quarterly close, compliant with LATAM small-enterprise accounting norms, through a four-round accounting cycle that recorded six operating transactions, posted five non-cash adjustments, closed nominal accounts to equity and articulated the Income Statement, the Balance Sheet and the Cash Flow Statement from the opening trial balance. The case opens at 7:12 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Mercado Fresco back-office desk as Quispe-Roldán, 31, prepares the Round-1 six-transaction recording commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Record the Round-1 six-transaction operating cycle (cash sale USD 15K, credit sale USD 35K, inventory purchase on credit USD 25K, COGS USD 26K, AR collection USD 18K, AP payment USD 8K) and defend the ending Cash USD 50K, AR USD 37K and COGS USD 26K readings on the double-entry basis.
2. Apply the Round-2 capitalisation-vs.-expense discipline: identify the USD 36K delivery van as an asset (not an expense), split the acquisition into USD 12K cash and USD 24K financed, accrue USD 500 utilities, USD 160 interest and USD 2K prepaid rent, and post a second operating cycle (cash sale USD 10K / COGS USD 5K / wages USD 8K).
3. Post the Round-3 five non-cash adjustments (depreciation USD 600, insurance amortisation USD 200, accrued wages USD 8K, bad-debt provision USD 340, income-tax expense at 25% of pre-tax income) and articulate how each adjustment crosses the Income Statement, the Balance Sheet and the Cash Flow Statement.
4. Execute the Round-4 closing entries to clear revenue, expense and dividend accounts to Retained Earnings and produce the three statements with Net Income USD 8,400, Retained Earnings USD 28,400 and Net Change in Cash USD 13,000 — reconciling Net Income to Cash through the indirect-method Cash Flow Statement.
5. Diagnose the gap between Net Income USD 8,400 and Net Change in Cash USD 13,000 and explain to a non-accountant owner-operator why profit and cash diverge — the teaching payoff of the cycle.

Cash is a fact."*

## The Protagonist

Quispe-Roldán, 31, had joined Mercado Fresco in 2023 as the operator's first full-time bookkeeper after four years in the accounts-payable team of a regional food-service distributor and two years in a small-practice tax firm that had serviced fourteen sole-proprietor produce wholesalers. She had, in 2023–2025, cleared the operator's cash-basis back-office — invoice registration, AR ageing, AP scheduling, bank reconciliation and monthly tax filing — and had, across eighteen months, documented the three named working-capital crunches: the July 2024 cash squeeze that had priced a USD 14K credit-sale bunch as a profit overrun, the December 2024 inventory build that had priced a USD 11K unsold-stock position as a gross-margin collapse, and the April 2025 payables-delay that had priced a USD 9K supplier-payment gap as a liquidity event. The founder had, in December 2025, committed to the first accrual-basis quarterly close and had promoted Quispe-Roldán to Controller on the express condition that she could produce the three statements from first principles, close the nominal accounts to equity and explain the divergence of profit and cash to a non-accountant audience. Her personal discipline was a default-to-the-accounting-identity posture: every entry had to balance on the debit-and-credit side before it could be posted, and every number had to trace back to a source document before it could clear the trial balance.

## The Decision

Quispe-Roldán had to commit, by 5:00 p.m. on Friday, April 24, to the Q1 2026 closed books and the three printed statements.

1. **Round 1 — Six operating transactions.** The Q1 2026 cycle had opened with a USD 15K cash sale (at 60% gross margin), a USD 35K credit sale, a USD 25K inventory purchase on ninety-day credit, a USD 26K aggregate COGS charge, a USD 18K AR collection and a USD 8K AP payment. Working entries: Cash goes from USD 25K to USD 50K; AR goes from USD 20K to USD 37K; Inventory moves from USD 16K to USD 15K (16 + 25 − 26); AP moves from USD 10K to USD 27K (10 + 25 − 8); Revenue USD 50K; COGS USD 26K.
2. **Round 2 — Capitalisation discipline and second cycle.** Quispe-Roldán had to classify a USD 36K refrigerated van acquisition — USD 12K cash paid and USD 24K financed through a new three-year loan at 8% — as a capital asset (not as an operating expense); to accrue USD 500 utilities (expense and accrued liability), USD 160 of interest (expense and accrued liability), USD 2K of prepaid rent (asset); and to post a second small operating cycle (USD 10K cash sale, USD 5K COGS, USD 8K wage expense).
3. **Round 3 — Five non-cash adjustments.** USD 600 of quarterly depreciation on the van (straight-line fifteen years), USD 200 insurance-prepayment amortisation, USD 8K accrued wages (pay-day falls after quarter-end), USD 340 bad-debt provision (1% of AR plus credit sales), and income-tax expense at 25% of pre-tax income.
4. **Round 4 — Closing entries and three statements.** Close revenue and expense accounts to Retained Earnings; close any dividend to Retained Earnings; reconcile Net Income to Cash through the indirect-method Cash Flow Statement. Working readings: Net Income USD 8,400; Retained Earnings USD 28,400; Net Change in Cash USD 13,000.

On Quispe-Roldán's working model, the Q1 2026 three statements delivered a recognisable narrative: USD 50K gross cash position, USD 37K AR position, a capitalised and depreciating asset, a financed liability matched to the asset, USD 8,400 of Net Income and USD 13,000 of cash build. The gap between profit (USD 8,400) and cash (USD 13,000) was the teaching payoff — depreciation, the non-cash bad-debt provision, the working-capital swings in AR and AP, and the financing-inflow from the van loan all separated the two numbers.

## Company Background

Mercado Fresco S.A. had been founded in 2018 as a single-truck fruit-and-vegetable wholesaler and had, across seven years, built a USD 320K-quarterly-revenue produce distributor with nine employees, one leased cold-storage unit of 240 m², seventy-eight active restaurant and hotel customers, a single ageing delivery van and a founder-operated sales desk. The operator had, for twenty-eight months, run the books on a cash-only spreadsheet that had matched bank deposits to invoices and had skipped the accrual layer. The 2025 year had, across three named working-capital episodes, made the cash-only spreadsheet untenable: the operator had paid suppliers on time and collected late, had built inventory ahead of demand and had missed the tax-filing cut-off once. The Q1 2026 mandate was the first accrual-basis close.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2019–2025 small-LATAM-distributor accounting literature had, across 112 documented first-close cycles on USD 0.5M–USD 2M-revenue operators, produced three findings the cycle had to respect. First, cash-basis-only regimes had understated asset positions by 14–28% in 72% of documented cases and had priced working-capital episodes as profit problems in 64%. Second, capitalisation-discipline errors — expensing vehicles, equipment or software — had generated 180–360 bps of Net-Income overstatement reversals at the first audit review in 68% of cases. Third, closing-entry omissions had produced multi-period Retained-Earnings reconciliations that had absorbed 40–80 advisory hours per operator at the first external-accountant engagement in 58% of cases.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The founder had, in December 2025, engaged a regional small-practice external accountant to audit the opening balance sheet and had formalised the Q1 2026 close as the first accrual-basis cycle. The external accountant had, on January 20, signed the opening trial balance at USD 63,000. The van acquisition had closed on February 8. The AP-heavy inventory build had landed on March 1.

### What Quispe-Roldán knew

The six Round-1 transactions, the Round-2 van acquisition and second cycle, the five Round-3 adjustments and the closing-entry template had been pre-reviewed with the external accountant. The operator's bookkeeping-software migration to a double-entry package had gone live on March 15 and had cleared the trial balance on April 10.

### What she did not know

Whether the founder would accept the Net-Income-USD-8,400 reading once he saw that cash had climbed by USD 13,000 and would therefore ask "where is the missing USD 4,600?"; whether the bad-debt provision would survive the external-accountant review or be re-priced to 1.5% of AR plus credit sales; whether the depreciation-schedule would be accepted at straight-line fifteen years or re-priced to ten; and whether the tax authority would accept the Q1 2026 tax-expense accrual as a quarterly estimate or demand a full annual reconciliation.

### What others were saying

**The Founder**: *"Show me the three statements. And tell me, in one sentence, why the cash number and the profit number are different."*

**The External Accountant**: *"Capitalise the van. Depreciate it. Accrue the wages. Close the nominal accounts. If the three statements don't tie, the cycle isn't closed."*

**The Head Driver** (informally): *"Mariana, the van is new. It doesn't feel like an expense."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Quispe-Roldán had narrowed the close to three postures.

- **Option A — Full Accrual Cycle with Five Adjustments and Closing Entries:** the recommended posture. Net Income USD 8,400. Retained Earnings USD 28,400. Net Change in Cash USD 13,000. Three statements articulated. Recommended.
- **Option B — Cash-Basis-Only Close:** the shortcut. Reports cash movements; omits depreciation, bad-debt, accrued-wages and income-tax; overstates equity by USD 4,600. Rejected — fails the external-accountant engagement and the tax-filing standard.
- **Option C — Partial-Accrual Close (transactions + capitalisation, no Round-3 adjustments):** the compromise. Reports capitalisation correctly but omits depreciation, bad-debt and accrued-wages; overstates Net Income by USD 8,940 before tax. Rejected — partial articulation breaks the three-statement tie.

Her private view was that Option A was the right posture and that the round most correlated with the first-close defensibility was Round 3 — the five non-cash adjustments — because it was the round that separated the accrual-basis close from the cash-basis shortcut and that framed the teaching payoff of the cycle: the USD 4,600 gap between Net Income and Net Change in Cash.
