## Abstract

La Parrilla del Río S.A. was, in the case universe, a 52-year-old Argentine steakhouse chain founded in 1974 in San Telmo by the Moreno-Paternoster family and now operating four Buenos-Aires-corridor locations (Palermo flagship, San Telmo original, Belgrano, Recoleta) — the Palermo flagship at 130 covers, approximately ARS 1.8bn 2025 revenue (across the four locations approximately ARS 5.4bn), 96 employees at the flagship of which 36 are service waiters and 8 are parrilleros, operating margin at the Palermo flagship at 8.2% against 14.1% at the San Telmo original under the same brand and menu — a 5.9-percentage-point gap whose origin had not been resolved by three successive operations interventions since 2023. The throughput read 62 covers/hour against 82 theoretical maximum, the asado grill ran at 88% utilisation with 18-minute average wait for main course on Friday peak, waiter turnover was 64% annually against a 42% benchmark, and the 50-cover private-event room was used only for booked events three days per week. On **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 8:18 a.m., **Lic. Agustín Moreno-Paternoster**, a 39-year-old incoming Operations Director and third-generation family-member arriving on his seventy-fourth day in role, opened the service-operations diagnostic dashboard his sister-location operations counterpart from San Telmo had refreshed at 11:47 p.m. the previous evening and sat down to commit the three-round package for the 10:00 a.m. family-and-operations-committee session.

On the morning of **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 8:18 a.m., **Lic. Agustín Moreno-Paternoster** arrived at La Parrilla del Río's Palermo flagship on Jorge-Luis Borges 1984 on his seventy-fourth day in the Operations-Director role, greeted the two morning-shift kitchen-prep staff and the accounting office manager, and walked into the Operations office past the wall of four framed photographs (the 1974 San Telmo founding by the family patriarch; the 1989 first-supporter-ownership milestone; the 2004 Palermo flagship opening; the 2024 Belgrano and Recoleta expansion). On the desk sat the service-operations diagnostic dashboard his San-Telmo counterpart Valentina Moreno-Paternoster (his cousin) had refreshed at 11:47 p.m. The Friday-night VUT curve was highlighted. The 5.9-percentage-point margin gap versus San Telmo was on the second page, in red.

---

## The Protagonist

Moreno-Paternoster, 39, had been Operations Director at La Parrilla del Río since February 9, 2026 — seventy-four days into role at the case opening — had completed a Licenciatura en Administración at the Universidad de San Andrés in 2010 and an MBA at the IESE Business School in 2016, had spent five years at a Spanish-headquartered hotel-group operations function between 2017 and 2022, and had served as operations manager at the Belgrano La-Parrilla-del-Río location between 2023 and February 2026 before the flagship promotion. His service-operations instincts had been shaped by three formative exposures: the 2015 IESE service-operations elective taught on the Chase / Hopp-Spearman / Sasser / Heskett / Maister canon; the 2020 Barcelona hotel-group VUT-and-Little's-Law service redesign that had delivered +28% throughput on the restaurant-and-bar unit; and the 2024 Belgrano batching-of-event-space conversion that had reproduced the Benihana pattern at ARS 5,800-per-cover against a ARS 3,900 baseline.

---

## The Decision

Moreno-Paternoster had to commit, by the 10:00 a.m. family-and-operations-committee session, the three-round package.

1. **Round 1 — Chase / VUT / Little's Law.** Accept the service-operations diagnosis. Hire one additional parrillero for Friday-Saturday peak. Deploy the dedicated reset team. Implement parallel-processing reset. Target 78 covers/hour throughput.
2. **Round 2 — Benihana batching.** Convert the 50-cover private-event room into a communal-parrillada experience. Rework the menu to a fixed set-menu of ARS 6,100 per cover. Target party-size 7.0 and beverage-attach uplift.
3. **Round 3 — Service-profit chain and waiting psychology.** Reduce waiter workload to four tables per peak. Raise base pay 38% with a reduced tip-pooling ratio. Restructure the lobby to an aperitivo-plus-kitchen-view experience. Install honest time-to-table communication with rolling updates.

Commitments: the Round 1 VUT-and-Little's-Law intervention; the Round 2 Benihana-batching conversion; the Round 3 service-profit-chain and waiting-psychology redesign; and a one-page family-and-operations committee memo that frames the three-round commitment as a single 120-day Palermo-flagship margin-recovery arc.

The 1961–2025 service-operations literature produced three findings the simulation had to respect. ## The Situation

### How we got here

The 1974 San Telmo founding; the 1989 first-supporter-ownership milestone; the 2004 Palermo flagship opening; the 2019 first documented Palermo-versus-San-Telmo margin-gap reading at 3.2 percentage points; the 2021 first operations-intervention attempt that focused on menu-engineering and did not move the needle; the 2023 second operations-intervention attempt that focused on supplier consolidation and did not move the needle; the 2024 Belgrano and Recoleta expansion; the February 9, 2026 Moreno-Paternoster promotion; the 74-day diagnostic phase; and the April 23 family-and-operations committee session were the sequence.

### What Moreno-Paternoster knew

The 88%-grill-utilisation reading and the Kingman VUT-predicted 18-minute wait; the Little's-Law throughput gap and the 18-minute-reset-versus-8-minute-benchmark intervention; the 50-cover private-event-room underutilisation and the Sasser Benihana-batching conversion economics; the 64% waiter-turnover reading and the Heskett service-profit-chain remediation; the Maister lobby-redesign intervention; the Moreno-Paternoster family council's approval of the operational framework as a 120-day test; and the San Telmo benchmarks.

### What he did not know

Whether the additional Friday-Saturday parrillero hire would absorb the cultural weight of the existing two-parrillero team or would trigger union-council friction; whether the private-event-room regulars who had booked three weeks out would accept the conversion to communal parrillada; whether the waiter base-pay increase would actually reduce the 64% turnover to the projected 38% or would land at 48–52% under first-year turbulence; whether the lobby redesign would produce the 30%-perception-improvement Maister predicts at the Palermo demographic; and whether the 120-day test would preserve the no-service-deterioration constraint through the conversion period.

### What others were saying

**The family council (Moreno-Paternoster patriarch, 74, founder)**: *"Agustín, cincuenta y dos años de parrilla. La ves como proceso. Yo la vi como fuego. No te olvides del fuego."*

**The San Telmo counterpart (cousin, Valentina Moreno-Paternoster, 41)**: *"La diferencia no es ubicación ni clientela. Es operación. Lo mismo lo operamos mejor. Copialo."*

**The head parrillero (58, 32 years at Palermo)**: *"Si me traen un tercero, lo acepto. Si lo llaman tercero, no lo acepto. Lo llamamos ayudante y se queda."*

**The waiter-shift lead (34)**: *"Seis mesas no las trabajo bien. Cuatro mesas, sí. Y si el fijo sube, me quedo."*

**The regular private-event client (family-matriarch of a fiftieth-anniversary booking, 62)**: *"Si la parrillada es comunal, pago más. Si la sala no existe, me voy al Don Julio."*

**The 38,000-per-month Palermo-flagship covers (aggregate signal via the 2025 patron-satisfaction panel)**: *"El sabor está. La espera no."*

## Options Under Consideration

Moreno-Paternoster had narrowed the service-operations space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — Extra-parrillero + dedicated-reset-team + Benihana-batching conversion + waiter-redesign-plus-lobby-redesign (Recommended).** Respects the Chase-Hopp-Spearman-Little-Sasser-Heskett-Maister canon; reduces grill utilisation from 88% to 80% with a 7-minute wait; closes the reset gap from 18 to 9 minutes for 78 covers/hour throughput; converts the underutilised private-event room to a communal-parrillada with bill-per-cover +45%; reduces waiter turnover from 64% to 38%; drops the no-show rate from 13% to 4%. Projected 120-day Palermo margin uplift from 8.2% to 12.6%.
- **Option B — Price-up-the-menu + add-capex-for-a-second-grill + no-Benihana-conversion + no-waiter-redesign.** Rejected — ignores the VUT-hyperbolic-multiplier finding; adds capex that is not needed; leaves the event-space underutilised; preserves the waiter-turnover regime that drives service inconsistency.
- **Option C — Reduce-covers-to-100 + ultra-premium-positioning + no-event-space-conversion + waiter-headcount-cut.** Defensible on positioning grounds but violates the no-service-deterioration-during-conversion constraint, surrenders the volume base, and risks brand-consistency rupture versus the San Telmo original.

Moreno-Paternoster's private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the single decision most correlated with the 120-day margin-recovery outcome was the Round 1 VUT-plus-Little's-Law intervention, because the 2015–2025 panel had shown that service-operations-unoptimised root-cause readings correlated at 0.82 with persistent flagship-versus-benchmark margin gaps, and because his 2020 Barcelona hotel-group redesign had delivered +28% throughput under the same intervention pattern. The *Restaurante que No Para* frame sits at the advanced no-breaks-version service-operations tier in the 2026 Express catalogue, distinct from Bistro Meridiano (sim 043, restaurant-P&L frame) and Hotel Cielo F&B (sim 076, hotel-F&B frame) — 320 emphasises the VUT / Little's Law / Benihana / Heskett / Maister synthesis inside a LATAM flagship steakhouse.
