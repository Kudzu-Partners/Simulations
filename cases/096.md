## Abstract

Agencia Turismo Valdesol was, in the case universe, a destination management organization serving a LATAM coastal region, with a 2025 annual budget of USD 15M, 1.2 million visitors, an average spend of USD 387 per visitor, an annual destination revenue of USD 465M, an NPS of 45, a sustainability score of 30 out of 100, a community score of 50 out of 100, a commercialization score of 5 out of 100, and four undefined visitor segments (families, couples, adventure, cultural). On Monday, January 12, 2026, the Board approved a three-year strategic commitment inside which the Executive Director would commit brand-and-segment architecture in Year 1, experience-infrastructure investment in Year 2, and sustainability-and-commercialization investment in Year 3 — anchored on six watch metrics: visitors, average spend, NPS, sustainability, community, and a combined composite. Paula Zuloaga, a 52-year-old former regional Chief Heritage Officer promoted to DMO Executive Director on October 6, 2025, faced a Board mandate that the three-year trajectory had to lift average spend above USD 800, sustainability above 75, community above 80, close the UNESCO Biosphere bid inside Year 3, and close Year 3 on a composite above 85 — on a visitor count that the model deliberately compressed. The case opens at 5:44 a.m. on Monday, January 19, 2026, at the Valdesol DMO head-office on the coastal boardwalk, as Zuloaga prepares the Year-1 brand-and-segment commitment.

4. Design a three-year integrated sequence that pays for sustainability with brand-driven spend lift and pays for community with the commercialization-structure that converts price-per-visitor into local economic value.
5. Recommend a complete three-year sequence, identify the year most correlated with the Year-3 composite, and write the Board-facing paragraph the Year-1-to-Year-3 trajectory would anchor.

On the morning of **Monday, January 19, 2026**, **Paula Zuloaga** arrived at the Valdesol DMO head-office on Paseo Marítimo at 5:44 a.m., opened the Year-1 brand-and-segment sheet her Chief Strategy Officer and Chief Marketing Officer had finalized at 11:28 p.m. the night before, and re-read the sentence she had pinned to the top of the document in her December all-staff off-site: "Valdesol's beaches are loved. The question is who loves them and at what cost to who lives here." Twelve days separated her from the January 31 Board commitment.

---

## The Protagonist

Paula Zuloaga, 52, had joined the regional Secretariat of Culture in 2009 as a heritage-program coordinator, had been promoted to regional Chief Heritage Officer in 2018, and had been named DMO Executive Director on October 6, 2025. A graduate of the Universidad de Valdesol in cultural management with an MPA from Georgetown and a UNESCO-World-Heritage-site management certification, she had, in her Chief Heritage Officer tenure, led the 2022 coastal-cooperative artisan-market rehabilitation that had produced the first integrated artisan-tourism revenue line, designed the 2024 cultural-route pre-feasibility study that had identified three colonial-coffee-estate-plus-pre-Columbian-site corridors, and had, in 2025, co-authored the regional memorandum that had framed the DMO's transition from volume-tourism to value-tourism — the memorandum that earned her the October 2025 DMO appointment.

---

## The Decision

Zuloaga had to commit, by the January 31 Board commitment, the Year-1 sheet and the Year-2 and Year-3 intent:

1. **Year-1 Brand and Segment** — whether to commit a heritage-plus-coastal-culture positioning against the all-inclusive-beach competitor set, target couples-plus-cultural-adult (not families), a 40%-digital-25%-OTA-35%-PR marketing mix, USD 55M to cultural infrastructure, USD 28M to beach upgrades, USD 35M to gastronomy, a daily visitor cap of 5,800 (from uncapped), and a community share of tourism revenue at 12% (from 8%).
2. **Year-2 Experience Infrastructure** — whether to lift beach investment to USD 42M (reef-restoration led), gastronomy to USD 48M (chef-in-residence program plus eight certified restaurants in smaller towns), cultural to USD 60M, commercialization level from 5 to 25, tighten the daily cap to 5,500, commit USD 18M to Blue-Flag-plus-Biosphere certification, and commit USD 12M to two new cultural routes.
3. **Year-3 Sustainability and Commercialization** — whether to tighten the daily cap further to 5,200, lift community share to 16%, commit USD 14M to certification (post-Biosphere-award), USD 18M to adventure-plus-wellness expansion (shoulder-season demand), USD 22M to sustainability (waste-to-energy plus mobility), USD 8M to PR, and USD 6M to a slow-tourism booking platform run by the DMO.

The three sub-decisions would compound. On Zuloaga's working model, Year 1 delivered visitors at 1.35M (+12%), average spend at USD 512 (+32%), revenue at USD 691M, NPS at 53, sustainability at 42, community at 61, three cultural routes activated, and 14 international media outlets covering Valdesol for the first time. Year 2 delivered visitors held at 1.28M (deliberate), spend at USD 678, revenue at USD 868M, NPS at 62, sustainability at 58, community at 72, two Blue Flag beaches awarded, Biosphere bid submitted, and a top-3 LATAM gastronomy ranking. Year 3 delivered visitors at 1.22M (lower than start, deliberate), spend at USD 812 (more than doubled), revenue at USD 991M (+113% on a visitor count 3% below start), NPS at 71, sustainability at 78, community at 82, UNESCO Biosphere awarded, three Blue Flag beaches, and a composite of 88.

## Company Background

Agencia Turismo Valdesol had been established in 1998 as the regional destination marketing organization and had, across twenty-eight years, built the USD 15M-budget 1.2M-visitor coastal-DMO footprint the case studied. The 2025 destination revenue of USD 465M was distributed as accommodation 46%, food-and-beverage 24%, retail-and-artisan 12%, tours-and-activities 11%, and transportation 7%. The coastal region comprised 14 municipalities across 180 kilometers of coastline, with the historic colonial district of Puerto Valdesol as the primary tourism anchor, three colonial-coffee-estate cultural corridors in the piedmont, and seven beach-municipalities across the coastal range.

The Board's December 2025 strategy dossier had identified three specific pressures the three-year envelope would inherit. First, the 2025 carrying-capacity audit had, across the coastal region, documented four beach-municipality overcrowding episodes in peak season and a 62% resident-sentiment-negative reading on the Puerto Valdesol historic-district consultation — a pattern that framed the Year-1 visitor-cap-at-5,800 commitment. Second, the 2025 coral-reef ecological audit had documented a 38% reef-health compression across the 2018–2025 period — a pattern that framed the Year-2 reef-restoration USD 42M commitment. Third, the Q4 2025 national-press positioning audit had placed Valdesol in the all-inclusive-beach competitor set rather than in the cultural-heritage coastal set, with 88% of 2025 international earned-media references price-and-beach-centric — a pattern that framed the Year-1 brand repositioning.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM coastal DMO landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2020–2024 heritage-positioning evidence base had, across a commercial-summary sample of 18 LATAM destinations that had moved from volume-beach to heritage-plus-cultural positioning, documented an average-spend lift of 28–42% within 24 months at a visitor-volume hold or modest compression — a pattern that monetized the Year-1 repositioning. Second, the carrying-capacity-plus-Blue-Flag evidence base had, across a 2019–2024 sample of 12 LATAM coastal-protected-area initiatives, documented an NPS lift of 8–14 points and a community-sentiment lift of 12–20 points at destinations that had tightened caps below the pre-cap peak-season median — a pattern that anchored the Year-2 cap-tightening commitment. Third, the UNESCO Biosphere evidence base had, across a 2021–2024 sample of 7 LATAM Biosphere awards, produced an international-earned-media lift of 3–5x in Year 1 post-award and a shoulder-season-visitor-growth of 12–18% in Year 2 — a trajectory that anchored the Year-3 post-Biosphere commitments.

Empirically, the post-2020 destination-management literature had two robust findings the envelope had to respect. First, DMOs that had sequenced brand-and-segment discipline (Year 1) ahead of infrastructure-build (Year 2) ahead of sustainability-certification (Year 3) had, across a 2020–2024 sample of 19 LATAM coastal peers, closed the composite 1.9 times higher than DMOs that had sequenced infrastructure-first. Second, DMOs that had committed community share above 14% of tourism revenue through a three-year envelope had, across the same sample, preserved resident-sentiment-positive readings above 72% against a 48% median for community-share-below-10% peers.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Board had, in the December 2025 off-site, approved a three-year composite target of 85, a Year-3 sustainability floor of 75, a community floor of 80, and an average-spend floor of USD 800. The Chief Strategy Officer had, on January 5, circulated a Year-1 pre-reading that proposed the heritage-plus-couples-plus-cultural repositioning. The Chief Marketing Officer had, on January 8, signed off on the 35%-PR mix conditional on the cultural-route launch inside Q2 2026. The Chief Operations Officer had, on January 12, argued for a tighter Year-1 cap at 5,400 — a position Zuloaga had held under review pending the carrying-capacity calibration due before the Year-2 commitment.

### What Zuloaga knew

The team had modeled three three-year envelopes. A Brand-Infrastructure-Sustainability Envelope (Year-1 repositioning-plus-cap; Year-2 reef-plus-gastronomy-plus-certification; Year-3 Biosphere-plus-sustainability-plus-slow-tourism) projected a Year-3 composite of 88. A Volume-Growth Envelope (Year-1 no cap, family-segment push) projected a composite of 42 — community-sentiment collapse and no international earned-media shift. A Infrastructure-First Envelope (Year-1 USD 140M infrastructure without brand repositioning) projected a composite of 61 — infrastructure utilized but average-spend compressed and sustainability flat.

### What she did not know

Whether the UNESCO Biosphere review would award inside Year 3 or slip to early Year 4; whether the national-press positioning shift would persist into Year 2 post-Guardian-Condé-Nast coverage or reset; whether the chef-in-residence program would attract the tier-one international talent the commercial model assumed; and whether the slow-tourism booking platform would clear the four-municipality legal-and-commercial framework review inside Year 3.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Strategy Officer**: *"Year-1 brand-and-segment. Heritage-plus-couples-plus-cultural is the reposition. Families stay on the volume-beach competitors."*

**The Chief Marketing Officer**: *"35%-PR mix. Heritage is a PR story. Digital spend is downstream of earned media."*

**The Chief Operations Officer**: *"Tighter cap. Carrying capacity is lower than the consultation has documented."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Zuloaga had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Brand-Infrastructure-Sustainability:** Year-1 heritage-plus-couples-plus-cap; Year-2 reef-plus-gastronomy-plus-certification; Year-3 Biosphere-plus-sustainability-plus-slow-tourism. Year-3 composite 88. Aligns with CSO, CMO, COO. Recommended.

- **Option B — Volume-Growth:** Year-1 no cap, family-segment push. Year-3 composite 42. Community and sustainability floors not met. Rejected.

- **Option C — Infrastructure-First:** Year-1 USD 140M infrastructure without brand repositioning. Year-3 composite 61. Spend floor not met, Biosphere bid fails. Rejected.

Zuloaga's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single year most correlated with the Year-3 composite was Year 1 — the heritage-repositioning commitment, which shifted the international-earned-media pattern from beach to cultural and produced the spend-lift that funded every subsequent year.
