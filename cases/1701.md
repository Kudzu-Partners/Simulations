# Caribia Banca Digital: The Shift-Lead Seat

**Case Code:** ORG-2026-1701 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1701 — *Service Excellence Live* · **v1.0 · September 2026**

---

On Tuesday, March 3, 2026, at 09:14, the billing system at Caribia Banca Digital stopped accepting payment instructions. The mobile application still loaded and balances still displayed, but every transfer, scheduled payment and payroll batch returned the same notification: *your payment cannot be processed.* Daniela Restrepo-Ahumada, shift lead on the early Spanish and Creole-mix surface at the bank's Santo Domingo contact centre, was at the supervisor's desk, headset on, nobody in her ear. The board showed three calls on hold.

Tech-Ops acknowledged at 09:15 in one line — *investigating, no ETA* — and went quiet. Three of Restrepo-Ahumada's eleven agents had already asked the team channel what they should tell customers. She had ninety minutes, in three windows, to settle what her floor said, what her agents could promise, and what her manager and the bank's communications lead heard from her. Her manager, María Camila Vélez-Ortiz, would record the morning window by window against the bank's shift-lead assessment sheet.

## The Protagonist

Restrepo-Ahumada was twenty-nine and four months into the role. Until November she had been an agent on the same floor for two years, and a good one: the person the senior team paged when a dialect call was sliding. She inherited eleven agents — three senior, five mid-tenure, three hired within the previous four months — and a job description Caribia had formalised in 2023 around four duties: live-floor supervision and escalation, handling-time and satisfaction targets, fortnightly coaching, and a written handover to the incoming shift lead. She had supervised busy mornings. She had not supervised an outage.

## The Decision

Restrepo-Ahumada had to decide, three times in ninety minutes, what the floor would say and who would say it — and to keep deciding as the queue moved and the information changed.

The first of the three windows the sheet recorded (see **Exhibit 2**) was already open. Lina Suárez-Pinto, four months in on the Creole-mix surface, had put a six-line script of her own on the team channel, mostly accurate, promising the system back within the hour. What the second and third windows would bring, nobody on the floor yet knew.

## The Company and its Setting

Caribia Banca Digital, S.A., founded in 2018 and headquartered in Santo Domingo, was a Caribbean-region digital bank licensed in seven countries, serving roughly 2.4 million retail customers and 85,000 small and medium enterprises with approximately US$3.1 billion under management at end-2025. Its billing layer was a third-party integration; the failure left the core platform up and only payment initiation failing.

The contact centre ran about 180 agents across two shifts and three language surfaces, to targets of 4 minutes 20 seconds average handling time, at least 78% first-contact resolution, and at least 4.30 out of 5.00 on satisfaction (see **Exhibit 1**). Under the Dominican banking supervisor's 2024 continuity regulation the bank had four hours from detection to notify affected customers, which made the first script an agent read aloud part of the regulatory record.

## The Situation

What Restrepo-Ahumada could see at 09:14 was a queue board, a chat channel and a rolling satisfaction figure for her floor at 4.30 out of 5.00, exactly at target. She could not see a restoration time; nobody had one. At 09:26 Tech-Ops added a second line — working on it, an estimate when they had one, likely *in the hour* — which could be read as within sixty minutes or within the current hour.

If she did nothing, the shape of the morning was not in doubt. Inbound routing was unchanged; the queue would keep filling against agents who could not complete a payment. Eleven agents without a common script would produce close to eleven versions of the truth, and Suárez-Pinto's version, already circulating, put restoration inside the hour.

Vélez-Ortiz, who sat off the floor and had held the same seat in 2022, recorded five entries per window: each window carried its own score, the three summed to a total out of 100 that stood at 0 when the morning began, and the total resolved into a performance band. The queue count and the floor satisfaction figure sat beside them as conditions, not as entries.

## What Restrepo-Ahumada Controlled

In the first window she settled five things. The **holding statement** agents read on pickup: two paragraphs of acknowledgement, no time given; one paragraph with an approximate time; or one paragraph with a firm one, back before 10:00. The **promise boundary** for the second monitor: four written lines naming the may and the may-not; one spoken line leaving it to each agent's judgement; or one spoken briefing of the same ground. **Suárez-Pinto's script**: a private message within ninety seconds with the corrected wording; a correction in the team channel, where the floor would see it; or a correction once the queue came down. Her **first report upward**: one line to Vélez-Ortiz at 09:21, before being asked; several paragraphs at 09:35, once she had detail; or one when Vélez-Ortiz asked for it. And the **headset**: coach from the supervisor's desk through chat; or take calls beside her agents.

In the second window, five more. The **triage rule** for outage-day calls, one of them Sra. Beatriz Castaño-Mejía's — a fourteen-employee bakery, payroll due at 14:00: three written tracks, callback, manual workaround and Tech-Ops bridge, with her on the workaround; a callback for every caller, her included; or the track chosen call by call. The **recovery authority** Andrés Beltrán-Cruz carried into that call: a defined one, a manual slot before 16:00 and a waiver up to US$150; an open-ended one, whatever he judged it took; or a held one, nothing offered before she approved it. **How to coach the live call**: through the chat sidebar, letting her finish and mirroring her back before the offer; typing the offer out for him to read at the top; or taking the call and running it herself. The **language about Tech-Ops**: the bank's own voice, the technicians', or the third-party provider's. The **closing commitment**: manual processing at 13:30, a named agent, a callback at 13:00; contact from the bank once it knew more; or a call back from her that afternoon if she heard nothing.

In the third window, five again. The **reply to Tatiana Brugal-Henríquez**, Head of Communications, who wanted three quotable sentences within ten minutes: three naming the fault, the customer pattern and the next step; three asking for time until the floor settled; or three pitched for the public rather than the floor. By then José Luis Rentería, nine years on the floor, had told Suárez-Pinto within earshot of two others that the bank had logged three interruptions on this scale in twelve months (see **Exhibit 1**) and the floor had absorbed each one; Suárez-Pinto had gone quiet. The **message to Suárez-Pinto**: two lines within two minutes; two lines once the queue came down; or two lines in the team channel, answering Rentería where the floor could read them. The **script for the 14:35 conversation with Rentería**: asking what he would do differently next time, with a junior in earshot; telling him the remark was not one to make in front of a junior; or a conversation that left it unraised. The **handover note** for the late-shift lead: five bullets — what happened, the morning's pattern, the coaching plan, the open items, the Communications line; three bullets — what happened, the open items, the Communications line; or the same ground in one paragraph. And **which dimension of the service she had chosen to be best at and which worst**: truthful acknowledgement over speed of promise; speed of resolution over accuracy; or the three held level, none named either way.

## Closing

At 09:16 Restrepo-Ahumada pulled a blank document onto the left of her screen and the queue board onto the right. Four calls on hold. Tech-Ops had nothing. Suárez-Pinto's script was already on the channel, and the first thing any customer heard that morning would be whatever she typed next.
