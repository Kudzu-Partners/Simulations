## Abstract

Hotel Mirador was, in the case universe, a 180-room full-service hospitality property located in the capital's business-and-convention district, owned by a LATAM mid-market hospitality group whose Board had, in November 2025, approved a three-year USD 420,000 digital-transformation envelope. On Monday, January 5, 2026, the property's digital-maturity index stood at 2 on a five-point framework, stakeholder coverage spanned four of five functional groups (operations, finance, IT, HR — revenue-management pending), and the technology stack included a 2011-vintage PMS nearing end-of-life, a cybersecurity posture rated below the sector-baseline by the group's 2024 external audit, and no CRM or AI capability. Twelve technology-investment tiles — PMS upgrade, cybersecurity, staff training, CRM, AI personalization, guest mobile app, revenue management system, cloud migration, IoT in-room, chatbot, check-in kiosks, website overhaul — carried costs, ROI projections, risk ratings, and prerequisite dependencies. Sebastián Navarro, appointed Digital Transformation Lead on October 15, 2025 after three years as the group's Director of IT Operations, faced three annual Board reviews, a Year-2 AI-plus-app-plus-RMS unlock window dependent on Year-1 foundation investments, and a Year-3 coverage-completion round. The case opens at 6:22 a.m. on Tuesday, January 13, 2026, as Navarro prepares the Year-1 foundation-stack commitment for the January 22 Board review.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Year-1 portfolio-sequencing choice between a foundation-first stack (PMS, cybersecurity, CRM, staff training) and an AI-first alternative, and diagnose when each dominates on the three-year composite.
2. Analyze the prerequisite-dependency structure of the twelve-tile portfolio and recommend a sequence that maximizes the Year-3 tile-unlock rate.
3. Diagnose the Year-1 investment in staff training and cybersecurity — neither of which produces Year-1 measurable ROI — and defend the sequencing against a Board member's AI-first proposal.
4. Design a Year-2 allocation of USD 200,000 across AI personalization, guest mobile app, and revenue management system that captures the unlocked dependencies from Year-1.
5. Recommend a complete Year-1 commitment and identify the Year-3 tile most likely to convert a Year-2 unlock cycle into a Year-3 composite outcome.

On the morning of **Tuesday, January 13, 2026**, **Sebastián Navarro** sat in the small back-office corner of Hotel Mirador on Avenida del Convento at 6:22 a.m. and opened the Year-1 digital-transformation workbook his IT analyst had finalized at 10:47 p.m. Monday. Nine days separated him from the Board review. The twelve-tile investment board on his wall carried three color rows — Year 1 candidates in blue, Year 2 dependencies in amber, Year 3 completion in gray — with PMS at the top of Year 1 and the AI personalization tile locked behind a small padlock icon labeled "requires CRM."

---

## The Protagonist

Sebastián Navarro, 39, had joined the hospitality group in 2019 as Senior IT Manager on the flagship property and had been promoted to Director of IT Operations in 2022, a role in which he had, across 2022–2024, led the group-wide network and cybersecurity refresh that had raised the group's external-audit score from 58 to 76 on a 100-point framework. A graduate of the Tecnológico de Monterrey with an executive program in digital transformation at MIT Sloan, he had, in his Director role, co-authored the group's 2024 digital-maturity framework that the Board had adopted as the operating measurement for the Hotel Mirador transformation. The General Manager's October written mandate had been: "The Board wants a three-year transformation, not a three-month press release. If the Year-1 tiles make Year-2 possible, you are doing it right."

---

## The Decision

Navarro had to commit, by 9:00 a.m. on Thursday, January 22, the Year-1 foundation-stack commitment the Board would treat as the three-year USD 420K operating envelope:

1. **Foundation Stack** — whether to commit Year-1 to PMS upgrade (USD 75K, low risk, prerequisite for four other tiles), cybersecurity (USD 50K, medium risk), staff training (USD 15K, low risk, highest maturity multiplier), and CRM (USD 40K, low risk, prerequisite for AI personalization).
2. **Budget Envelope and Contingency** — whether to deploy USD 180K in Year-1 and roll USD 60K to Year-2, or to front-load Year-1 to USD 240K and pre-order the Year-2 AI tile.
3. **Board-Facing Narrative and Stakeholder Coverage** — whether to present the sequencing rationale with the explicit defense of "no Year-1 AI line," whether to commit to four-of-five stakeholder coverage in Year-1 (operations, finance, IT, HR) and flag revenue-management as Year-2 unlock, and whether to absorb the USD 12K internal-change-management cost in Year-1.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across three Board-review rounds. A Year-1 commitment that deployed USD 180K across PMS, cybersecurity, staff training, and CRM, rolled USD 60K to Year-2, and delivered four-of-five stakeholder coverage produced, on the internal model, a Year-1 digital-maturity move from 2 to 3, Year-1 cumulative ROI of USD 128K, Year-2 unlocks on AI personalization, guest app, and RMS, Year-3 coverage completion with all twelve tiles purchased or consciously deferred, and a Year-3 composite of 87 out of 100.

## Company Background

Hotel Mirador had been opened in 1994 as the flagship property of the LATAM mid-market hospitality group and had, across thirty-two years, operated as a full-service 180-room business-and-convention property with a 2025 RevPAR of USD 118, an ADR of USD 168, and an occupancy of 70%. 2025 total revenue was USD 22.6M, of which rooms 64%, F&B 28%, meetings and conferences 8%. The property's technology stack, assembled over 2011–2023, carried four known constraints the Year-1 commitment would address: the PMS was a 2011 on-premises installation nearing end-of-life and vendor-end-of-support by June 2027; the cybersecurity posture was rated below sector-baseline by the group's 2024 external audit; there was no CRM capability beyond the PMS-embedded guest-profile module; and the direct-booking share had, since 2021, stalled at 36% of demand against a peer-boutique benchmark of 48%.

The group's Board, in its November 2025 approval, had carried two explicit requirements: a three-year cumulative ROI above 140% of invested capital, and a digital-maturity move to at least 4 on the five-point framework by the end of Year-3. One Board member — the group's independent Director with a tech-industry background — had, in the November 22 Board discussion, flagged an AI-first alternative that the independent Director described as "the AI-personalization tile in Year-1, defer PMS to Year-2." Navarro's Year-1 commitment would have to defend against that alternative in the January 22 Board review.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM mid-market hospitality technology landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Year-1 decision sat inside. First, the post-2023 hospitality PMS cycle had consolidated around cloud-native architectures with direct integration to CRM, RMS, guest-app, and channel-management systems; properties on legacy on-premises PMS were, in 2026 peer benchmarking, running digital-maturity scores 1.2–1.8 points below cloud-PMS operators, with the gap concentrated in guest-experience and revenue-management dimensions. Second, the hospitality AI-personalization market had matured enough that Year-1 vendor claims of 12–18% upsell uplift could be documented, but only where a CRM foundation of at least twelve months of clean guest-profile data was in place — properties that had launched AI without the data foundation had produced measured upsell uplifts below 4% in three-quarters of 2023–2024 launches. Third, the hospitality-cybersecurity environment had, since 2023, included three documented LATAM ransomware events on mid-market properties whose cyber investment had been deferred in favor of guest-facing technology; two of the three events had produced recovery costs above USD 4M on balance sheets that had skipped a USD 50K cybersecurity baseline investment.

Empirically, the post-2020 digital-transformation research had two robust findings the Year-1 commitment had to respect. First, foundation-first sequencing (PMS + CRM + cybersecurity + staff training in Year-1; AI + guest app + RMS in Year-2) had, across a 2020–2024 sample of 74 hospitality digital-transformation programs, produced three-year cumulative ROI of 138% on average against 62% for AI-first sequencing, driven primarily by the AI tile's measured uplift dependency on CRM data depth. Second, staff-training investment of USD 12K–USD 18K in Year-1 had, across the same sample, produced a digital-maturity multiplier of 1.22 — the single highest dollar-for-dollar maturity lever in the twelve-tile taxonomy.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Board had, in the November 2025 approval, committed the USD 420K three-year envelope and established the three review cycles (January, February Year-2, February Year-3). The independent Director had, in the November 22 Board discussion, flagged the AI-first alternative. The group's Chief Financial Officer had, on December 15, circulated a Year-1 budget pre-reading requesting a front-loaded deployment to accelerate Year-2 AI unlocks. The General Manager had, on January 6, presented three Year-1 commitments modeled against the cumulative-ROI and digital-maturity targets.

### What Navarro knew

The team had modeled three Year-1 commitments. A Foundation-First Commitment (PMS USD 75K, cybersecurity USD 50K, staff training USD 15K, CRM USD 40K, USD 60K roll-forward) projected Year-1 maturity 2 → 3, Year-1 cumulative ROI USD 128K, Year-3 composite 87. An AI-First Commitment (AI personalization USD 65K front-loaded, guest app USD 35K, cybersecurity USD 50K, CRM USD 40K, USD 30K roll-forward) projected Year-1 maturity 2 → 2.8, Year-1 cumulative ROI USD 72K, Year-3 composite 71 — driven by the measured AI-uplift shortfall under a shallow CRM data base. A Minimum-Viable Commitment (PMS USD 75K, cybersecurity USD 50K, USD 55K roll-forward) projected Year-1 maturity 2 → 2.6, Year-1 cumulative ROI USD 68K, Year-3 composite 72 — disciplined but under-invested.

### What he did not know

Whether the independent Director's AI-first alternative would return as a formal Board challenge at the January 22 review (internal read: likely); whether the PMS vendor's end-of-support timeline would slip from June 2027 to December 2027 (mid-December 2025 vendor conversation had hinted at a possible slip); whether the group's external cybersecurity auditor would accept the USD 50K baseline investment as sufficient for the 2026 audit cycle (last audit commentary had referenced a preferred USD 65K investment); and whether the CRM platform short-listed by the IT team would deliver the AI-data-readiness certification the Year-2 personalization tile required.

### What others were saying

**The General Manager**: *"Foundation-First. The Board asked for a three-year transformation. The PMS upgrade is the tile everything else sits on. Do not front-load AI on a PMS that will be end-of-life inside the Year-2 window."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"Do not roll more than USD 60K to Year-2. A Year-2 unlock window needs cash for the AI and RMS tiles, but the roll-forward should not become a piggy bank for a Year-2 overrun."*

**The Independent Director**: *"AI-first. The market is moving. A twelve-month PMS delay is a cost; a twelve-month AI delay is a relevance loss."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Navarro had narrowed the space to three Year-1 commitments.

- **Option A — Foundation-First:** PMS, cybersecurity, staff training, CRM; USD 180K deployed, USD 60K roll-forward. Year-1 maturity 2 → 3, Year-3 composite 87. Aligns with General Manager and CFO. Challenges the Independent Director.

- **Option B — AI-First:** AI personalization, guest app, cybersecurity, CRM; USD 190K deployed. Year-1 maturity 2 → 2.8, Year-3 composite 71. Aligns with Independent Director. Exposed to the AI-uplift shortfall under shallow CRM data.

- **Option C — Minimum-Viable:** PMS, cybersecurity only; USD 125K deployed, USD 55K roll-forward. Year-1 maturity 2 → 2.6, Year-3 composite 72. Disciplined. Under-invested.

Navarro's private view, not yet shared with the Board, was that Option A was the right Year-1 commitment and that the single subsequent decision most correlated with the Year-3 composite was the Year-2 USD 200K deployment — specifically, AI personalization at USD 65K (unlocked because CRM is there), guest app at USD 35K (unlocked because PMS is upgraded), revenue management system at USD 45K (unlocked because PMS is upgraded), and USD 55K rolled forward as contingency for the AI-integration complexity.
