# Norandic Iberia: Three Decisions by Friday

**Case Code:** ORG-2026-1704 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1704 — *Inclusive Manager: DEI in Daily Decisions* · **v1.0 · September 2026**

---

At 8:14 on Monday 4 May 2026, Ana Torres-Mancebo opened her laptop at Norandic Iberia's head office in Aranda de Duero and found the weekend had left her three things: a promotion calibration panel on Wednesday, a hiring decision to be answered by Tuesday evening, and three return-to-office accommodation files to be signed by Friday. None was an emergency on its own. All three carried a date.

By Friday end-of-day Ana had to have run the panel, given the Operations Director an answer on his preferred candidate, and put the three accommodation decisions into writing. She had five working days and no additional authority.

## The Protagonist

Ana Torres-Mancebo, thirty-four, was HR Business Partner for the Iberia unit's commercial and engineering populations — roughly 640 of its 1,950 employees, across Aranda, Vitoria and a small Madrid office. She had joined six years earlier and held the partner seat for three. She reported to Lucía Pinheiro-Castaño, the unit's HR Director, who had told her on her first day that the job was not to be the conscience of the business unit but to install the disciplines that made decisions hold up when the inspections came.

## The Decision

In each of the week's three situations the substance belonged to somebody else: the promotion ratings to the panel, the plant hire to the Operations Director, the accommodations to three line managers who had already taken positions. What belonged to Ana was the procedure — what she wrote, what structure she put around each decision before it was taken, and whom she told once it was.

## The Company and its Setting

Norandic Industries AB was a Stockholm-listed industrial coatings group: €4.1 billion of revenue in FY2025, 18,000 employees. Its Iberia unit ran €500 million and 1,950 employees from three plants: Aranda de Duero, Setúbal and Vitoria.

Three obligations framed the week (see **Exhibit 1**). The unit's Equality Plan, registered in March 2025 and co-signed by the works council, committed it to at least 35% women on L1 and L2 by the end of 2027; the unit stood at 24%. The EU pay-transparency directive transposed into Spanish law in June 2026, putting the unit's first pay-gap submission in September. Workforce data had fed the group's externally audited sustainability reporting since FY2025. A group return-to-office mandate from January 2026 required three office days a week for non-shift roles, applied unevenly from team to team.

## The Situation

Wednesday's panel had eighteen senior-engineer candidates, six promotion slots, four panellists and Ana facilitating. The slate was ten men and eight women; the pre-read that arrived Sunday night showed the panel's favoured top six as five men and one woman. On Saturday the panel chair, Eduardo Castelar-Riera, had messaged Ana: he did not want a conversation about quotas, and did not want to sign a five-to-one list and face the works council the week after.

Tuesday's decision was a Plant Operations Manager appointment for Aranda, an L2 role on the extended leadership team. Of the three candidates interviewed, Rodrigo Iñarritu-Salas had the longest operational record and the highest score from the technical panel; Isabel Cañizares-Domínguez had taken a smaller site through a turnaround; Paula Restrepo-Hidalgo had the thinnest plant record and was the only candidate every interviewer had rated as one they would actively fight to hire. Iván Soares-Lourenço, the Operations Director, had written that Rodrigo was the only one of the three who had run a plant of Aranda's size, that the site could not carry a learning curve this year, and that he would answer for the appointment himself. He meant to sign on Tuesday. The hire would take the L1+L2 figure from 24% to 22%. Ana had forty-five minutes with him at eleven, before he left for Setúbal.

Friday's files were three accommodation escalations that had backed up during two weeks without HRBP cover (see **Exhibit 2**). Marta López-Cendán had been refused a Mondays-and-Fridays remote arrangement by a manager who said it had to be the same for everybody. Diego Almazán-Pinilla was in the eighth week of an ulcerative-colitis flare under an informal arrangement nobody had recorded. Núria Bertran-Alquézar's quiet desk, protected midday focus block and no-interruptions norm had been agreed verbally two years earlier by a manager since retired; her successor was unwinding all three, no record of them existing.

If Ana did nothing beyond turning up, the week resolved itself without her. The panel would sign a five-to-one list built from aggregate pre-ratings, with no record of how the published criteria had been applied to any candidate. Marta's request would stay refused, Diego's arrangement would enter a ninth undocumented week, Núria's accommodation would finish being unwound — and all of it would reach the September pay-gap submission and the FY2026 sustainability disclosure with nothing written underneath it.

## What Ana Controlled

Wednesday's panel gave her four choices. The Sunday-night reply to Eduardo: a line saying a protocol existed, five lines setting out what it was, or a line putting the question to Monday. The opening of the meeting: procedure framed to make the distribution visible without making it the subject, procedure alone and nothing about the slate, or the principle and what the unit's commitments asked of the room. The re-rating mechanic: discussion of the aggregate pre-ratings, joint evaluation with names withheld on a first pass, or criterion-by-criterion scoring recorded before any aggregate. The rule for disagreement: a majority vote of the four panellists, a written note from each panellist who differed naming the criterion, or a consensus rating negotiated in the room.

Tuesday's conversation gave her four more. Her position on the hire: a structured re-evaluation put to Iván before anything was signed, a block on Rodrigo's appointment on Equality-Plan grounds, or approval of it and the file handed back. The method to propose: reopening the search with a fresh slate, re-scoring the existing three against a re-stated criterion set, or a documented panel discussion of all three with Iván deciding at the end. The leadership-balance metric: left out of the conversation, named as one factor among several, or led with and the plan behind it. The published studies linking executive-team diversity to financial performance: held back for the moment Iván rejected the method, used to open the conversation, or left out altogether.

Friday's memos gave her the last four. The documentation format: one template across all three cases, free text written to each case as it came, or a works-council-defensible template varied by legal route. The routes: three named separately — family-care, temporary sickness plus adjustment, and disability accommodation — two named with the third folded under one of them, or a single reasonable-adjustment heading across all three. What to send Lucía: all three escalated for her decision, the three signed with two paragraphs on the pattern underneath them, or the three signed and nothing sent. The works council: nothing before the memos were signed, a call to its president Jordi Vilanova-Esquerda and a formal note at the next quarterly meeting, or that call alone.

Four measures followed her through the week. **Defensibility** asked whether a decision would stand up to the audit and works-council inquiry that were coming: criteria named, alternatives considered, rejection reasons explicit. **Documentation** asked whether the record was in a form the auditor and the works council would recognise — dated, named decision-maker, legal route, review trigger. **Fairness-by-design** asked how much of the fairness sat in the procedure rather than in the argument in the room. **Restraint** asked whether she had held the seat as the role defined it: process integrity rather than moral authority, no encroachment on line-manager decisions. The four combined into a **composite score** out of 100, reported after each situation.

## Closing

At 8:33 Ana closed the pre-read, opened a blank message to Eduardo and looked at the calendar. The panel was Wednesday at ten. Iván left for Setúbal at two on Tuesday. The three memos had to carry Friday's date. Nothing on the list was hers to decide, and every one of them would be filed under her name.
