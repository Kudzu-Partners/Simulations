# Arquidiócesis de San Joaquín: The Corpus and the Covenant

**Case Code:** FIN-2026-1606 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1606 — *Endowment Crisis at the Arquidiócesis* · **v1.0 · September 2026**

---

On a Tuesday morning in July 2026, in the curial offices on Calle Segunda, Hna. Margarita Solís-Bautista put two things on Padre Andrés Quesada-Marenco's desk: a coffee that had gone cold on the walk over, and a sealed letter from the chief financial officer of the Universidad Católica San Joaquín. The letter attached a number to something the Vicar General had been carrying as an impression for a year. The Universidad's bonds faced a covenant test in the fourth quarter of 2026. Without a one-time recapitalisation of USD 22 million, it would fail that test, and the trustee would be entitled to accelerate.

The Finance Council voted on the Friday of the eighth week. Padre Quesada had until then to put a single financial architecture in front of it — one document covering the endowment, the Universidad and the operating budget together. The Archbishop had reserved forty-five minutes on the Sunday afternoon before the vote.

## The Protagonist

Padre Quesada, 54, had been Vicar General for Administrative Affairs since 2016. Ordained in 1995, he had taken a business degree after ordination with his Archbishop's permission, and had run the administrative side of the archdiocese since. He was, in function if not in title, its chief financial officer. The lay members of the Finance Council took his numbers seriously; Mons. Gabriel Aguirre-Cerdán took his judgement seriously. He had signed the quarterly endowment distribution at the same rate for six consecutive years, on a policy set before he arrived.

## The Decision

Padre Quesada had to recommend a financial architecture and carry it through three stages in eight weeks. First, a diagnosis: what the endowment could sustainably pay out, and how far the current distribution sat from that figure. Second, a design: what to sell, what to cut, and what to do about the Universidad. Third, a defence, conducted one conversation at a time with the three people whose support the package needed and who wanted three different things from it.

## The Company and its Setting

The Arquidiócesis de San Joaquín was a metropolitan see in Costaria, a mid-income Latin American country. It ran 142 parishes and sponsored the Universidad Católica San Joaquín, on whose board the archdiocese held the majority.

Its endowment stood at USD 380 million, invested 70% in equities and 30% in fixed income, and had returned 4.8% a year in real terms over the preceding decade. The spending policy, unchanged for years, distributed 5.0% of the trailing three-year average market value, paid quarterly.

Two features of the governance shaped what was possible. The Finance Council, chaired by the layman Sr. Fernando Esquivel-Belaúnde, had to consent to anything outside routine administration. And under canon law any single transaction above USD 5 million required the consent of the Holy See — a process that ran on Rome's calendar rather than the Council's.

## The Situation

Three pressures were converging on the same balance sheet (see **Exhibit 1**).

Sunday collections had been falling across the 142 parishes for several years, steadily and for demographic reasons that no campaign had reversed. The clergy pension had no separate plan assets and was paid out of operating cash. And the Universidad absorbed a recurring subsidy of USD 14 million a year, on top of the USD 22 million recapitalisation its bondholders' covenant now required.

The endowment had been absorbing the difference. That was the part Hna. Margarita wanted looked at first. The external advisor the secretariat had retained — a former endowment officer now working with foundations and dioceses across the region — put the arithmetic simply. A long-horizon portfolio could distribute roughly what it earned in real terms, less the cost of running the money and less a margin for being wrong. On his figures that meant a cost margin of 0.4 percentage points and a prudence haircut of 0.2 points against a long-run real return the advisor put nearer 5.0%, leaving a sustainable distribution of 4.4% to 4.8% real. The archdiocese was distributing 5.0%.

If nothing changed, two things followed. The distribution would keep running at or above the top of the advisor's range, so the corpus would lose purchasing power year after year rather than hold it, and the shortfall would fall on parish budgets in a decade rather than on this one. And in the fourth quarter of 2026 the Universidad would fail its covenant test with no recapitalisation behind it, the trustee's right to accelerate would become live, and the archdiocese, as the sponsoring institution, would answer for it.

## What Padre Quesada Controlled

Four instruments, and the range the secretariat had prepared papers for, were set out on one sheet (see **Exhibit 2**).

He could set the **distribution rate** anywhere between 3.0% and 6.0% of the trailing three-year average corpus. He could set the **asset allocation** anywhere between 30% and 90% in equities, with the balance in fixed income; the portfolio stood at 70/30, and a move toward 60/40 would reduce volatility and trim expected return. He could sell part of the **Centro Pastoral San Joaquín**, a 1970s complex on the edge of the cathedral district, for anything from nothing up to USD 80 million: at USD 80 million the sale carried the office tower, the retail wing, the chapel and the formation centre; at about USD 50 million it carried the office tower and the rear retail wing alone. He could take an **annual operating cut** of between USD 0 and USD 22 million out of curial overhead and the diocesan publications; parish-level pastoral programmes sat outside that perimeter.

The **Universidad subsidy** could be left as it stood at USD 14 million a year, recurring and terminable at will. It could be converted into a 25-year capital injection with a present value of USD 220 million, at a 12% control adjustment that preserved the archdiocese's reserved-matter vetoes. It could be converted into a 30-year injection with a present value of USD 280 million, diluting the archdiocese to 48%. Or the Universidad could be transferred outright, the episcopal majority surrendered and the recurring subsidy ended with it. A binding capital-injection contract, unlike a subsidy voted annually, would let the Universidad book the commitment as an asset and satisfy the fourth-quarter tests.

Then there were the three conversations. With Mons. Aguirre-Cerdán he could open with the pastoral account of what the plan protected and let the financial reasoning sit underneath it; give the two accounts equal weight side by side; or open with the ten-year arithmetic and let the pastoral consequences follow from the numbers. With Sr. Esquivel-Belaúnde he could present a USD 6 million cut as a floor, attached to a quarterly measurement covenant and a second-phase review in year two; move straight to USD 12 million and take the larger reduction in the first year; or present USD 6 million as the right size and defend it on its own terms. With Padre Tomás Iturrizaga-Mendoza, pastor of San Pedro and the voice of the other 141, he could confirm that no parish-level programme was inside the reduction and add a USD 1 million parish revitalisation pilot; give a general assurance that pastoral work was protected without itemising it; or decline the exemption and say that every line remained under review.

He would be judged on five things the secretariat tracked round by round: the endowment corpus in USD millions on a ten-year projection; whether the Universidad covenant was resolved or still at risk; the distance in percentage points between what the archdiocese distributed and what the advisor called sustainable; where each of the three men stood, expressed as a percentage of full support; and a cumulative mark for how the package measured against the standard the Council had set for it.

## Closing

At 7:40 on the Tuesday morning Padre Quesada cleared the desk, took the cold coffee to the window, and wrote four headings on a single sheet: the rate, the portfolio, the property, the Universidad. Under them he wrote three names. The Council sat on the Friday of the eighth week.
