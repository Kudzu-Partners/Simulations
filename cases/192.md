## Abstract

Aceros del Sur was, in the case universe, a LatAm integrated steel group operating a blast-furnace-and-basic-oxygen primary mill and three rolling facilities across Chile and Argentina, with USD 1.8B in 2024 revenue, a 2024 Scope-1/2/3 emissions baseline of 4.90 MtCO2e (Scope 1: 3.40 Mt; Scope 2: 0.70 Mt; Scope 3: 0.80 Mt), a 2024 year-end cash position of USD 252M, a 2024 Debt/EBITDA reading of 2.80×, a BB+ S&P Global Ratings credit designation, and a 2024 CBAM-exposed export book to European rebar-and-wire buyers totalling USD 420M or 23% of group revenue. The Q4 2024 board had approved a USD 320M eighteen-month climate-transition-finance programme — the ClimateCFO cycle — and had positioned it as the group's response to three overlapping pressures: the 2026 CBAM operational phase that had moved from 2023 reporting-only into carbon-priced import-adjustment charges on EU-bound steel imports, the 2024 ISSB S2 and Chilean CMF disclosure-alignment mandate that had moved TCFD from voluntary into supervisor-audited, and a Q4 2024 activist-investor letter pressing for a 2030 Scope-1-intensity target and a green-bond inaugural issuance. The case opens at 6:48 a.m. on Thursday, April 23, 2026, at the Aceros del Sur Santiago head office as **Renata Villagrán-Cifuentes**, a 49-year-old Chief Financial Officer, prepares the Round-1 emissions-baseline-and-materiality commitment. Sixty-six days separate her from the June 28 investor-day disclosure session.

5. Design the Round-5 board-review commitment that couples the ClimateCFO cycle to a BB+ → BBB− credit upgrade narrative and secures an activist-investor-satisfaction reading above 78, anchored on the principle that climate disclosure is a discount rate.

## The Protagonist

Villagrán-Cifuentes, 49, had assumed the Chief-Financial-Officer role at Aceros del Sur on July 1, 2022, after fifteen years across treasury, capital-markets and ESG-finance roles at two LatAm industrials and a London-based infrastructure fund. She had, in 2019–2021, co-structured a USD 200M inaugural green-bond issuance at a Chilean utility that had priced eighteen basis points inside the conventional curve and had anchored a subsequent BBB− to BBB upgrade. The Chief Executive Officer had recruited her in May 2022 specifically on two capabilities: her documented discipline of running full Scope-1/2/3 baselines rather than the Scope-1/2-only envelope the 2019–2021 industry default had accepted, and her willingness to structure transition-finance instruments against disclosure-audit defensibility rather than marketing-narrative defensibility. Note that this case differs from sim 132 (Alpha-Klima — ClimateRiskPortfolio), which addresses the portfolio-level climate-risk frame for an asset manager; sim 192 is the *CFO-side transition-finance-and-TCFD-disclosure* frame specifically. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 25% to the emissions-baseline floor, 25% to the investor-confidence floor and 20% to the credit-rating trajectory.

## The Decision

Villagrán-Cifuentes had to commit, by the May 14 investor-day pre-review, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–5.

1. **Round 1 — Emissions baseline and materiality.** Three archetypes: comprehensive-Scope-1/2/3 (full Scope-3 categories 1, 3, 4, 11 priced, Composite 76), Scope-1/2-only (Composite 28, ISSB-non-compliant), or phased (Scope-1/2 + selected Scope-3 — Composite 54). Working envelope: comprehensive.
2. **Round 2 — Decarbonization roadmap.** Three vectors: integrated (USD 180M DRI-EAF retrofit, a 15-year renewable PPA and a CCS pilot, 12% green-steel revenue by 2028, Composite 78), deferred (capex after 2030 — Composite 26), or phased (DRI-EAF only — Composite 52). Working envelope: integrated.
3. **Round 3 — Green-bond envelope.** Three archetypes: USD-150M-green (18 bps greenium, ICMA-GBP aligned, second-party opinion, Composite 78), USD-150M-conventional (Composite 34), or USD-150M-sustainability-linked (SLB with rebar-intensity KPI — Composite 62). Working envelope: USD-150M-green.
4. **Round 4 — TCFD scenario analysis.** Three vectors: three-pathway (1.5°C / 2°C / 3°C with EBITDA sensitivity and transition-risk heatmap, Composite 78), single-scenario (Composite 26, ISSB S2-non-compliant), or two-pathway (1.5°C and 3°C — Composite 56). Working envelope: three-pathway.
5. **Round 5 — Board-review archetype.** Three vectors: transition-finance commitment (green bond issued, TCFD published, roadmap board-approved, activist-satisfaction above 78, ratings-agency upgrade BB+ → BBB−, Composite 78), narrative-only (Composite 32), or deferred (Composite 44). Working envelope: transition-finance commitment.

On Villagrán-Cifuentes' working model, a Comprehensive / Integrated / USD-150M-Green / Three-Pathway / Transition-Commitment envelope delivered Scope-1 reduction 8%, Investor Confidence 78, Credit Rating BBB−, Composite 73.

## Company Background

Aceros del Sur had been founded in Concepción in 1962 and had, across sixty-four years, built a USD 1.8B-revenue integrated steel group with a blast-furnace-and-basic-oxygen-furnace primary mill at Concepción, a DRI-ready secondary route at Mendoza, three rolling-and-wire facilities, and a distribution footprint into construction, automotive and oil-and-gas customers across Chile, Argentina and the European CBAM-covered corridor. The 2018–2024 emissions cycle had left the group on a 4.90 MtCO2e baseline with Scope-1 intensity of 1.89 tCO2e/t-steel against an EU-27 benchmark of 1.55 tCO2e/t-steel and a best-in-class 1.10 tCO2e/t-steel — the 34-point gap the January 2025 board paper had flagged as the operative CBAM-cost exposure. The Q4 2024 board-approved USD 320M ClimateCFO envelope had been positioned explicitly as a five-round rebuild of emissions-baselining, decarbonization-capex, transition-finance instruments, TCFD scenario analysis and credit-rating trajectory.

Three 2024–2026 events were named in the March 2026 investor-day dossier. First, the January 2024 European CBAM transitional reporting had closed and the October 2026 operational phase had moved into priced import-adjustment charges, projected by the Treasury team at USD 42M per year at prevailing EU-ETS price levels. Second, the Chilean CMF July 2024 circular had moved ISSB S2 alignment into supervisor-audited disclosure for listed issuers, with the 2026 reporting year as the first audited cycle. Third, the Q4 2024 activist-investor letter — an 11% shareholder — had priced the 2030 Scope-1-intensity target at 1.55 tCO2e/t-steel and the green-bond inaugural issuance at USD 150M as preconditions for continued equity support.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2020–2025 LatAm transition-finance literature had, across 26 documented integrated-steel and cement issuances above USD 100M, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, inaugural green-bond issuances priced 12–22 basis points inside the conventional curve in 78% of cases when paired with a comprehensive Scope-1/2/3 baseline and an ICMA-GBP second-party opinion. Second, ratings-agency transition-finance methodologies had, across the 2022–2025 cycle, treated TCFD three-pathway scenario analysis as a prerequisite for positive transition-finance credit-opinion uplift — single-scenario disclosures had failed the threshold in 84% of cases. Third, the 2024 European CBAM operational phase had priced Scope-1 intensity directly into the EU-bound rebar and wire export price, with every 0.10 tCO2e/t-steel reduction translating into USD 2.1M of annual CBAM-cost avoidance at prevailing EU-ETS levels.

Empirically, the post-2022 investor-engagement literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, activist-investor satisfaction readings above 75 had preceded positive credit-rating transitions in 68% of documented cases, and had preceded negative transitions in under 7%. Second, integrated-roadmap envelopes anchored on DRI-EAF retrofits, renewable PPAs and CCS pilots had delivered 2030 Scope-1 intensity readings 18–26% below sector averages in 74% of cases.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the December 2024 session, approved the USD 320M envelope and the June 28 investor-day calendar. The Head of Treasury had, on March 5, delivered the green-bond-structure workbook. The Head of Sustainability had, on March 12, delivered the Scope-1/2/3 baseline. The Investor-Relations Lead had, on March 19, delivered the activist-engagement brief. The Head of Industrial Strategy had, on March 26, delivered the DRI-EAF-plus-CCS capex stack.

### What Villagrán-Cifuentes knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. A Comprehensive / Integrated / Green-Bond / Three-Pathway / Transition-Commitment envelope projected Composite 73. A Scope-1/2-only / Deferred / Conventional / Single-Scenario / Narrative envelope projected Composite 18. A Hybrid / Phased / SLB / Two-Pathway / Partial envelope projected Composite 46.

### What she did not know

Whether the October 2026 CBAM operational phase would exceed the USD 42M annual exposure estimate under higher EU-ETS prices; whether the activist-investor coalition would expand beyond the current 11% stake after the investor day; whether the Argentine macro environment would support the peso-tranche of the green-bond issuance; and whether the Concepción blast-furnace retrofit calendar would clear the 2028 green-steel revenue-share threshold.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Treasury**: *"Green bond. A hundred and fifty million. Eighteen basis points of greenium. The inaugural issuance is the pricing signal."*

**The Head of Sustainability**: *"Full Scope 1, 2, 3. ISSB is not asking for a PDF. It is asking for a baseline that survives an audit."*

**The Head of Industrial Strategy**: *"DRI-EAF, renewable PPA, and a CCS pilot. Twelve percent green steel by 2028. The CBAM exposure halves."*

**The Chief Executive Officer**: *"Climate disclosure is a discount rate. We earn the upgrade — or we pay the spread."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Villagrán-Cifuentes had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Comprehensive / Integrated / Green-Bond / Three-Pathway / Transition-Commitment:** the recommended envelope. Scope-1 reduction 8%. Investor Confidence 78. Credit Rating BBB−. Composite 73. Recommended.
- **Option B — Scope-1/2-only / Deferred / Conventional / Single-Scenario / Narrative:** the legacy envelope. Composite 18. Rejected — CBAM-exposure and ISSB-non-compliance risks above 82%.
- **Option C — Hybrid / Phased / SLB / Two-Pathway / Partial:** the partial envelope. Composite 46. Rejected — ratings-agency uplift probability below 28% without full three-pathway TCFD.

Villagrán-Cifuentes' private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 3 — the green-bond envelope — because the USD 150M inaugural issuance with 18 bps greenium was both the pricing signal to the conventional-bond market and the credibility signal to the ratings agency that the Round-1 baseline and the Round-2 roadmap were more than a narrative.
