## Abstract

Estudio Austral was, in the case universe, a LATAM media studio with Bogotá and Mexico City hubs, starting with USD 15M in cash, zero hits shipped, no distribution platform of its own, and no franchise IP in catalog, against a streaming-buyer concentration of StreamGlobal at 48% of LATAM licensing, Vendaral Video at 18%, Velario at 12%, and local players at 22% combined. The ad market was soft. On Monday, January 12, 2026, the Board approved a three-year strategic commitment inside which the Chief Executive would commit content-portfolio architecture in Year 1, distribution-platform strategy in Year 2, and IP monetization in Year 3. Lucía Ibarra, a 46-year-old former head of drama at a peer LATAM studio appointed Chief Executive on September 22, 2025, faced a Board mandate that the three-year trajectory had to ship at least six hits across fourteen projects, close Year 3 with an owned-platform subscriber base above 400K, lift IP value above USD 40M, carry franchise value above 40% of IP, and close Year 3 on a composite above 80. The case opens at 5:48 a.m. on Monday, January 19, 2026, at the Austral head-office-and-writers-room floor in Bogotá's creative-industries district, as Ibarra prepares the Year-1 content-portfolio commitment.

3. Diagnose the Year-3 IP-monetization commitments (spinoff to 24%, platform-exclusive fourteen-week window, USD 8M capex to eight countries, merchandise-plus-gaming-license ancillary, rev-share-retained licensing) and design the commitment that converts hit IP into franchise IP.
4. Design a three-year integrated sequence that pays for platform with the first-year hits' earned upside and pays for franchise with the platform's subscriber base and rev-share licensing.
5. Recommend a complete three-year sequence, identify the year most correlated with the Year-3 composite, and write the Board-facing paragraph the Year-1-to-Year-3 trajectory would anchor.

On the morning of **Monday, January 19, 2026**, **Lucía Ibarra** arrived at the Austral head-office-and-writers-room floor on Carrera 11 in Bogotá at 5:48 a.m., opened the Year-1 content-portfolio sheet her Head of Scripted and Head of Development had finalized at 3:12 a.m., and re-read the sentence she had pinned to the writers-room whiteboard in her December all-creative off-site: "We are not a studio that licenses to StreamGlobal. We are a studio that still owns the IP when StreamGlobal's contract is up." Twelve days separated her from the January 31 Board commitment.

---

## The Protagonist

Lucía Ibarra, 46, had spent the decade from 2015 to 2025 as head of drama development at one of LATAM's top-five peer studios, where she had, across 2018–2024, overseen the commissioning slate that produced four drama series that crossed to international distribution and two animation series that traveled beyond LATAM. A graduate of the Universidad Autónoma de Valle Celeste in film-and-media studies with a screenwriting MFA from New York's Wrenfield School of Film, she had, in her head-of-drama tenure, rebuilt her previous studio's development-to-greenlight framework on a drama-franchise-potential-at-greenlight protocol, and had, in 2025, accepted the Chief Executive role at Estudio Austral on a Board mandate to build a studio that could beat the streaming-buyer-concentration trap — the mandate that framed the three-year commitment.

---

## The Decision

Ibarra had to commit, by the January 31 Board commitment, the Year-1 sheet and the Year-2 and Year-3 intent:

1. **Year-1 Content Portfolio** — whether to commit 35% of budget to drama (one flagship series), 15% to reality, 20% to documentary-and-animation (animation as long-duration IP bet), 20% to film, and 10% to spinoff (from a peer-proven reality franchise), on a hybrid production mix (two studio-led, one co-production, two acquisition), a weekly release schedule, USD 2M of platform capex (Year 1 of a three-year hybrid platform plan), and StreamGlobal as the primary licensing partner for the drama flagship.
2. **Year-2 Distribution Platform** — whether to trim drama to 30% (one flagship, one limited series, one dev slate), lift reality to 20% (with a drama-reality spinoff), lift spinoff line to 17%, shift release to hybrid (platform-exclusive for ten weeks before licensing), commit USD 6M of platform capex for Austral+ soft launch in four countries, and sign an international franchise license for the flagship with three European broadcasters (format license, not finished product).
3. **Year-3 IP Monetization** — whether to hold drama at 28%, lift spinoff to 24% (flagship universe with a prequel, a podcast, and a video-game license), commit platform-exclusive for fourteen weeks before wide license, commit USD 8M of platform capex to scale Austral+ to eight countries, commit a merchandise-plus-gaming-license ancillary line, retain licensing on rev-share (not flat-fee), and commit 8% of budget to a creator-incubator program for short-form adjacent talent.

The three sub-decisions would compound. On Ibarra's working model, Year 1 delivered revenue at USD 22.4M on USD 15M invested, profit at USD 2.1M, 9 projects shipped, 2 hits (a drama traveling to 32 countries via StreamGlobal and a reality hitting #3 LATAM), IP value of USD 6.8M, and a hit rate of 22% (against a peer median of 12%). Year 2 delivered revenue at USD 38.6M on USD 12M invested, profit at USD 5.4M, 3 more hits (flagship Season 2 topping StreamGlobal LATAM, an animation crossing to US Latino, the reality spinoff in eight new markets), Austral+ subscribers at 140K at USD 7.99 per month across four countries, and IP value at USD 18.2M (more than doubled). Year 3 delivered revenue at USD 58.4M, profit at USD 11.8M, 14 projects across the three years with 7 hits (a 50% hit rate against industry's 12%), Austral+ subscribers at 480K, IP value at USD 42.6M (a 6x multiple on start), franchise value at 45% of IP, and a composite of 85.

## Company Background

Estudio Austral had been established in May 2025 as a Bogotá-Mexico-City-dual-hub studio by a consortium of LATAM media investors and a LATAM-diaspora family office, with a USD 15M-cash starting position, a 24-seat creative-and-development team, and no prior production slate. The 2025 Q4 infrastructure included two writers' rooms (one in each hub), two production-services offices, and a shared post-production partnership with a Miami-based animation house. The 2025 Q4 commercial commitments included a non-exclusive first-look arrangement with StreamGlobal on LATAM drama, a development-fund agreement with the Ibero-American co-production support fund, and three Q3 2025 script-option commitments across drama, animation, and reality genres.

The Board's December 2025 strategy dossier had identified three specific pressures the three-year envelope would inherit. First, the 2024–2025 LATAM streaming-buyer concentration audit had, across 84 commissioned series, placed StreamGlobal's share of licensing at 48%, signaling a buyer-concentration risk that framed the Year-2 platform commitment. Second, the 2025 LATAM animation commercial press had placed the animation-franchise Year-3-plus IP-value multiple at 4–6x series-cost at studios that had retained streaming-license rev-share rather than selling flat-fee — a pattern that framed the Year-1 animation-as-long-duration-IP-bet commitment. Third, the Q4 2025 European co-production landscape had opened three drama-format-license commitments conditional on a LATAM flagship with international travel — a pattern that framed the Year-2 format-license-not-finished-product commitment.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM media-studio landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2020–2024 drama-flagship-plus-hybrid-production evidence base had, across a commercial-summary sample of 18 LATAM studio launches that had committed drama above 30% of Year-1 budget with a two-studio-led-plus-one-co-pro mix, documented a first-year hit rate of 18–28% against a 6–12% range for studios that had committed drama below 20% — a pattern that monetized the Year-1 drama-flagship commitment. Second, the owned-platform-plus-platform-exclusive evidence base had, across a 2021–2024 sample of 11 LATAM studio-platform launches, produced a Year-2 subscriber range of 100–180K and a Year-3 IP-value multiple of 3–5x at studios that had committed platform capex above USD 5M in Year 2 — a pattern that anchored the Year-2 commitment. Third, the franchise-monetization evidence base had, across a 2020–2024 sample of 14 LATAM franchise expansions, produced a Year-3 franchise-share of 35–48% of total IP value at studios that had committed ancillary-plus-gaming lines and rev-share licensing — a trajectory that anchored the Year-3 commitment.

Empirically, the post-2020 streaming-era studio literature had two robust findings the envelope had to respect. First, studios that had sequenced content-flagship (Year 1) ahead of platform-build (Year 2) ahead of franchise-monetization (Year 3) had, across a 2020–2024 sample of 21 peer studios, closed the composite 2.1 times higher than studios that had sequenced platform-first. Second, studios that had retained rev-share licensing (rather than flat-fee) through the first three-year envelope had, across the same sample, preserved Year-3 IP-value multiples above 5x against a 2.4x median for flat-fee peers.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Board had, in the December 2025 off-site, approved a three-year composite target of 80, a Year-3 subscriber floor of 400K, an IP-value floor of USD 40M, and a franchise-share floor of 40%. The Head of Scripted had, on January 5, circulated a Year-1 pre-reading that proposed the drama-flagship-plus-hybrid envelope. The Head of Development had, on January 8, signed off on the animation-as-long-duration-IP-bet conditional on the post-production partnership holding. The Chief Commercial Officer had, on January 12, argued for the European format-license-not-finished-product commitment conditional on the Year-1 drama-flagship achieving international travel.

### What Ibarra knew

The team had modeled three three-year envelopes. A Content-Platform-Franchise Envelope (Year-1 drama-flagship-plus-hybrid; Year-2 Austral+-plus-format-license; Year-3 eight-country-plus-ancillary-plus-rev-share) projected a Year-3 composite of 85. A StreamGlobal-Only Envelope (Year-3 no platform, StreamGlobal-only licensing) projected a composite of 58 — IP-value floor not met because flat-fee licensing sold the upside. A Platform-First Envelope (Year-1 USD 10M platform capex before content) projected a composite of 47 — platform launched but no subscriber-pulling content.

### What she did not know

Whether StreamGlobal's first-look first-renewal window would hold the flagship's licensing economics or reprice down under the Year-2 format-license; whether the Austral+ soft launch would clear the four-country licensing-and-regulatory framework inside Year 2; whether the European format-license counterparties would sign on format-only terms or insist on finished-product terms; and whether the Year-3 video-game-license partner would commit to the modeled rev-share-cap or to a flat-fee structure.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Scripted**: *"Year-1 drama-flagship. One drama that travels changes everything downstream."*

**The Head of Development**: *"Animation as long-duration IP. The IP-value multiple on animation at Year 3 is 4–6x. Drama is 2–3x."*

**The Chief Commercial Officer**: *"Rev-share, not flat-fee. A flat fee sells the upside to StreamGlobal."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Ibarra had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Content-Platform-Franchise:** Year-1 drama-flagship-plus-hybrid; Year-2 Austral+ soft-launch-plus-format-license; Year-3 eight-country-plus-ancillary-plus-rev-share. Year-3 composite 85. Aligns with Head of Scripted, Head of Development, CCO. Recommended.

- **Option B — StreamGlobal-Only:** Year-3 no platform, StreamGlobal-only licensing. Year-3 composite 58. IP-value floor not met. Rejected.

- **Option C — Platform-First:** Year-1 USD 10M platform capex before content. Year-3 composite 47. Subscriber floor not met. Rejected.

Ibarra's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single year most correlated with the Year-3 composite was Year 1 — the drama-flagship commitment, which produced the one-series-that-traveled that enabled every platform and franchise commitment that followed.
