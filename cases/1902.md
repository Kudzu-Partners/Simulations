# Rheinwerk Systeme AG: The Adler Cut-Off

**Case Code:** FIN-2026-1902 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1902 — *Fair View* · **v1.0 · August 2026**

---

On the morning of 14 January 2027, Annika Reuter opened the auditor's query before she opened the draft accounts. Two pages, three requests: the Project Adler contract file, the analysis of which of that contract's promises Rheinwerk Systeme AG had delivered by 31 December, and management's assessment of a EUR 9 million receivable overdue since June. The engagement partner wanted all of it in seven days.

Reuter was the group financial controller. The audited 2026 accounts were due to the supervisory board on 18 February, and her name went on the memoranda that supported them. Before it sat, she had to settle how much of a EUR 24 million contract belonged in 2026, what to provide against the overdue receivable and what to say about it in the notes, what to write back to the auditor, and who else to tell if the numbers moved.

## The Protagonist

Annika Reuter, 44, had been group financial controller of Rheinwerk since 2023. She spent six years in audit at a mid-tier Frankfurt firm before joining group reporting in 2018, and had run the last four closes. Colleagues in the commercial organisation called her slow to sign and hard to move. Her variable pay sat inside the senior-management scheme — a pool worth EUR 3.8 million across the top team, vesting only if reported earnings cleared a threshold the board set the previous February.

## The Decision

Four things were open, and Reuter had two dates: 21 January for the auditor, 18 February for the board. The revenue figure for Adler. The provision and the note on the EUR 9 million receivable. The basis of the written reply. And what followed once her arithmetic left her desk.

## The Company and its Setting

Rheinwerk Systeme AG built industrial automation equipment for automotive and process-industry clients across German-speaking Europe. Headquartered in Mannheim and listed in Frankfurt's Prime Standard, it reported under International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS). Draft 2026 revenue was EUR 540 million; the group employed 2,100 people.

The business had changed shape faster than the accounting for it. Rheinwerk no longer sold machines but what the sales organisation called outcome contracts — hardware, a licensed software platform, commissioning on site and a thirty-six-month service subscription, priced as one negotiated number. Nothing in the contract said what each piece was worth.

Two instruments outside the accounts moved with reported earnings: a EUR 200 million syndicated loan capping net debt at three times earnings before interest and tax (EBIT) on the audited figure, and the bonus pool, vesting above an earnings threshold set on the same basis.

## The Situation

Project Adler was signed on 22 December 2026 with a large automotive client. The hardware left Rheinwerk's works on 29 December. Software access had been granted, but the custom integration that made the platform usable was unfinished; commissioning and the thirty-six-month service term were to begin in the first quarter of 2027. Commercial booked the full EUR 24 million into 2026 revenue the day it was countersigned. Rheinwerk's pricing file split that EUR 24 million into roughly EUR 14 million of hardware, EUR 6 million of software and integration, and EUR 4 million of service (see **Exhibit 2**).

With the whole contract in 2026, draft revenue was EUR 540 million, EBIT EUR 51.0 million, and the covenant ratio 2.94 times — inside the 3.00 limit. Recognising only the hardware delivered in December took about EUR 10 million of revenue and EUR 4.2 million of EBIT out of the year: EBIT of EUR 46.8 million, a covenant ratio of 3.21 times, above the cap (see **Exhibit 1**).

The receivable was a separate file. EUR 9 million had been outstanding from a process-industry client for more than 180 days; in November that client entered a formal restructuring. The commercial director's written position was one sentence: they have always paid. Nothing had been provided against the balance and no note appeared in the draft.

Group internal audit scored contested judgements out of 100 on four readings. On 14 January the two items scored 18 for how far recognition and measurement were supported by the standards, 16 for earnings quality — whether the figures described the year's work or its target — and 14 for what the board and the lender had been told. The fourth reading, the risk that the auditor would modify its opinion, stood at 62, where high was bad.

The engagement partner had signed Rheinwerk's accounts for nine years. He was experienced and quick, and knew the group's contracts and people as well as anyone outside it. Left as Reuter found it, the file would reach the board showing EUR 51.0 million of EBIT, a covenant at 2.94 times, a EUR 9 million asset at face value and no note on either.

## What Reuter Controlled

On the revenue figure, three positions were open: recognise only the hardware handed over in December, about EUR 14 million, carrying EUR 10 million into 2027 as the integration and the service were performed; recognise hardware and software licence together, about EUR 20 million, access having been granted before year-end; or recognise the whole EUR 24 million, the contract signed and the equipment delivered.

That figure followed from how she described the contract: as three separate promises — hardware handed over on one date, a software platform with its integration, thirty-six months of service — each earned on its own timetable; as two, the hardware and everything after it; or as one promise under one signature, earned on delivery. Either way the EUR 24 million had to be split across the pieces, on evidence she chose: a quick apportionment from the deal sheet, a partial reconstruction, or a full build from what Rheinwerk charged for each element sold separately. Rigour cost hours from a close team already behind.

On the receivable she could recognise the full loss the restructuring implied and take the charge in 2026; recognise part of it, acknowledging the exposure without absorbing the whole amount; or carry the EUR 9 million at face value on the commercial record. Each needed a percentage attached — the share of the EUR 9 million she expected not to collect. Disclosure was a separate choice: a note setting out the provision and the credit exposure behind it; the balance left intact with the restructuring mentioned as a contingency; or nothing.

The reply to the auditor could rest on the standards alone — which promises had been satisfied, what the restructuring implied; on the standards with the covenant consequence alongside where the technical argument was thin; or on the consequences, the covenant and the threshold arguing for a position the reply then presented as judgement. If earnings moved the covenant moved with them: she could take that to the audit committee and the syndicate at once and ask for a waiver; put her position to the auditor and let the ratio surface through the audit; or leave it to the February board and the filing. The partner's nine years were hers to handle too: a review of both judgements by a partner outside the engagement, requested and recorded; her own reasoning documented in full, the team unchanged; or the existing engagement relied on, its accumulated knowledge of the group standing as assurance.

At the February meeting she would describe the numbers to the audit-committee chair: as what the year's work amounted to; as a balanced account, the judgements defensible and the figures still meeting the board's targets; or as an executed contract and a sound receivable, the equipment delivered and the client's record of payment behind it. The chair would ask, routinely, where each estimate had come from and whether the set of them ran one way. Reuter could answer with the reasoning and its dates, showing what was concluded before anyone costed it; concede the appearance and defend each call on its merits; or hold that each estimate rested on its own evidence, the threshold no part of the reasoning. She chose too how hard to press her line.

## Closing

At 7:20 that evening Reuter laid the Adler contract summary, the delivery notes, the receivable ageing and the covenant calculation out in the auditor's order. The reply was due on 21 January, the board would sit on 18 February, and one signature went under all four judgements.
