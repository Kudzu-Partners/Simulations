## Abstract

Industrias Maipú S.A. was, in the case universe, a 65-year-old Buenos-Aires / Rosario-headquartered Argentine industrial manufacturer founded in 1961 by Don Héctor Rodríguez-Maipú, listed on BCBA under ticker MIPU since 2003, manufacturing pumps, valves and fluid-control systems for oil, gas and mining, 2025 consolidated revenue ARS 48B, 1,850 employees, 60% family control and 40% public float. The Executive Committee, five seats, met weekly. On **Monday, April 20, 2026**, at 7:30 a.m. Buenos Aires local, in the third-floor committee room at the Puerto Madero corporate headquarters, the committee opened the Q2 2026 strategy offsite with the GDFLO German-uptime-disruptor having captured 18% of the market in three years and YPF, Tecpetrol and Alumbrera all running pilots with it.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Apply the Westerman-Bonnet-McAfee 2014 digital-maturity matrix to Industrias Maipú's starting point, locate the company as a Conservative at 3% digital intensity against the 8% benchmark, and articulate why the Digirati +26% profit premium is the target the committee must defend to the Board.
2. Apply the Stolterman-Fors 2004 vocabulary discipline to Maipú's current initiatives portfolio, classify each initiative as digitization, digitalization or transformation, and argue why most companies fund the first two and call it transformation.
3. Apply the Iansiti-Lakhani 2020 build-buy-partner framework across the three capabilities required for the uptime-subscription model — IoT sensors (buy), data-analytics platform (partner), outcome-based pricing model (build) — and defend the mode assigned to each against the speed-integration trade-off.
4. Apply the O'Reilly-Tushman 2004 ambidexterity framework to the digital-unit architecture — separate CDO, embedded in BUs, or hybrid — and couple it to the Bridges 1991 Transitions framework to name the ending, the neutral zone and the beginning for the three-front legacy resistance (80 technical sellers, engineering team, distributor network).
5. Apply the Zuora subscription J-curve governance discipline to hold the Board and the Rodríguez-Maipú family through a two-to-three-year reported-revenue decline as the ARS 8.5M equipment-sale transitions to an ARS 1.2M/year subscription that breaks even in year 8, and articulate why *El Salto Digital* (sim 342) is an executive-digital-transformation frame distinct from ANDEX Digital Transformation (sim 005), Banco Central Quisqueda Governor (sim 053), Grupo Nativa GenAI Management (sim 058), Hotel Mirador Digital (sim 077), Capstone COMPETE CPG (sim 083) and Distribuidora Andina Gran Salto (sim 300).

On the morning of **Monday, April 20, 2026**, at 7:30 a.m., **Juliana Rodríguez-Maipú-Vignola** opened the third-floor committee room at the Industrias Maipú corporate headquarters on Alicia Moreau de Justo 1150, Puerto Madero, Buenos Aires. Around the table sat Fernández-Altagracia, Sandoval-Vasena, Lagos-Perrone and — by Zoom from the Rosario plant — Don Héctor. On the screen was the Boston Consulting Group benchmark report, the GDFLO 2023–2026 share-capture chart, and the three client pilot letters from YPF, Tecpetrol and Alumbrera. Maipú's 3% digital-intensity line sat next to the 8% industry benchmark.

## The Protagonist

Rodríguez-Maipú-Vignola, 44, had graduated in 2004 from UBA Ingeniería Industrial, had completed an IAE MBA in 2010, had worked 2004–2011 at Techint in operations planning, 2011–2019 at Siemens Argentina — rising to head of industrial-services after-sales — and had returned to Maipú as Executive Vice-President in 2019 and was promoted to CEO by her uncle and chairman Don Héctor Rodríguez-Maipú, 78, on March 15, 2024 under the mandate *"saltá, nena, que si no saltamos ahora nos comemos el garrón en cinco años."* The family holding sat at 60%. Her cousin Alejandro Rodríguez-Maipú-Rodas, 47, ran the BU that would lose most in the J-curve.

---

## The Decision

The Executive Committee had to commit, by the end of the three-day Q2 2026 offsite (April 20–22), a four-round digital-transformation package.

1. **Round 1 — Maturity and vocabulary.** Westerman-Bonnet-McAfee diagnosis. Stolterman-Fors audit.
2. **Round 2 — Build, buy, partner.** Three capabilities assigned.
3. **Round 3 — Organization.** Ambidexterity pick. Bridges transitions discipline.
4. **Round 4 — Business model.** Zuora J-curve governance with family and Board.

## Sectoral Background

The 2015–2025 Latin American industrial-equipment category had, in the case universe, seen the classical equipment-sale model lose 14 share points to uptime-subscription and outcome-based-pricing models pioneered by German, Finnish and U.S. operators. Across the set, the Digirati quadrant reached 26% more profit than Conservatives and 9% more than Fashionistas. Argentine operators faced a distinct twist — BCRA capital controls delayed IoT hardware import by an average 11 weeks, and ARS volatility forced quarterly price resets that made subscription economics harder to model than in USD-denominated markets. The 2014 Westerman-Bonnet-McAfee *Leading Digital* canon, the 2004 Stolterman-Fors vocabulary paper, the 2020 Iansiti-Lakhani *Competing in the Age of AI*, the 2004 O'Reilly-Tushman ambidexterity research, the 1991 Bridges *Managing Transitions* and the 2015 Zuora subscription-economy reference were the anchors the simulation had to respect.

## The Situation

### How the Committee got here

The 1961 Rodríguez-Maipú founding; the 1978 first valve-export order to YPF; the 1998 Rosario plant expansion; the 2003 BCBA IPO; the 2014 failed ERP-modernization project; the 2019 Rodríguez-Maipú-Vignola return as EVP; the 2022 GDFLO entry into Argentina; the 2023 first YPF uptime pilot with GDFLO; the 2024 Rodríguez-Maipú-Vignola CEO appointment; the Q1 2026 close showing 18% share loss to GDFLO; the April 20 offsite opening.

### What the Committee knew

The 3% digital-intensity line against the 8% benchmark; the +26% Digirati profit premium; the four-quadrant Westerman-Bonnet-McAfee archetypes; the Stolterman-Fors three-level vocabulary; the three capability requirements; the GDFLO pricing-model architecture; the three ambidexterity options; the 80-technical-seller commission structure and the engineering and distributor resistance lines; the Zuora J-curve shape; the BCRA capital-control window; the Board's June 24 review date.

### What they did not know

Whether YPF, Tecpetrol or Alumbrera would sign a three-year uptime pilot with Maipú before the GDFLO pilots converted; whether the BCRA's Q3 2026 hardware-import window would shorten or widen; whether Don Héctor would hold the J-curve for two-to-three years at the Board; whether Alejandro would accept the BU economic hit or force a Preservation architecture; and whether the ARS would stabilize enough for the uptime subscription to price coherently.

## Options Under Consideration

The Executive Committee had narrowed the digital-transformation commitment to three postures.

- **Option A — Conservative-to-Digirati path through FY2028 with a separate CDO-led digital unit based in Rosario, buy commodity IoT sensors, partner on the data-analytics platform with a Siemens-MindSphere-or-AWS-IoT-type counterparty, build the outcome-based pricing model internally, freeze the 80-technical-seller commission plan and layer a digital-incentive sleeve, commit to the Zuora J-curve with a two-to-three-year reported-revenue decline disclosed to the Board, and design a BCRA-coherent hardware-import calendar (Recommended).** Respects the Westerman / Iansiti-Lakhani / O'Reilly-Tushman / Bridges / Zuora canon; protects the core from antibody attack; defends a credible client response to YPF before the GDFLO pilot converts.
- **Option B — Embedded digital unit inside the existing BU, build the analytics platform internally, defer the J-curve commitment, retain the current commission plan.** Rejected — preserves short-term economics but guarantees antibody capture in 18 months and hands the uptime market to GDFLO.
- **Option C — Follow-the-developed-market progression through digitalization first (digitize the equipment-order workflow, modernize CRM) before committing to transformation.** Defensible under a BCRA-stress scenario but cedes the uptime market and contradicts the 2024 CEO-appointment mandate.

The Committee's majority view was that Option A was the right commitment, and that the single decision most correlated with the 36-month outcome was the structural-separate CDO — because the 2018–2025 Latin American industrial-services track record documented that embedded digital units inside legacy BUs survive approximately 18 months before legacy-engineering-and-sales antibodies capture them, and because the Siemens-MindSphere-partnership pattern replicated the one documented 2019–2023 German industrial incumbent pivot. The *El Salto Digital* frame sits at the executive-digital-transformation tier in the 2026 Express catalogue, distinct from ANDEX Digital Transformation (005), Banco Central Quisqueda Governor (053), Grupo Nativa GenAI Management (058), Hotel Mirador Digital (077), Capstone COMPETE CPG (083) and Distribuidora Andina Gran Salto (300).
