## Abstract

Quarrystone Credit Solutions LLC was, in the case universe, a New York-headquartered credit-derivatives and CLO manager with USD 3.2B in AUM across six warehouses and four listed CLOs, a 2025 desk revenue of USD 118M, 64 credit professionals, a single-name CDS trading book of USD 620M in notional and a 2023–2025 track record of managing twelve CLO transactions through the full life-cycle without a AAA downgrade. The 2024–2025 cycle had left the desk at a CLO-warehouse-full but correlation-under-stressed posture: the Quarrystone CLO 2026-1 warehouse had aggregated USD 420M of leveraged loans across 125 borrowers, with a weighted-average rating of B+, a weighted-average spread of 380 bps over benchmarks, and a base-case Vasicek default-correlation assumption of 0.22. The Q2 2026 context had compounded three pressures: a leveraged-loan spread tightening that had compressed new-issue spreads by 35 bps across the quarter; an energy-sector credit-deterioration signal that had read through the CLO's 14% energy exposure on a cluster-concentration basis; and a credit-rating-agency consultation on the AAA subordination floor that had pointed to stress-ρ scenarios at 0.30 and 0.35 as critical thresholds. The case opens at 6:44 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Quarrystone Credit Solutions New York trading floor as **Dmitri Velasco-Roehrig**, a 50-year-old Managing Director and Head of Credit, prepares the Phase-1 portfolio-characterisation commitment.

## The Protagonist

Velasco-Roehrig, 50, had assumed the Managing-Director and Head-of-Credit role at Quarrystone on June 1, 2021, after twenty-two years across single-name CDS, structured-credit and CLO-management leadership at two US investment banks and one European credit-hedge fund. He had, in 2018–2021, managed a USD 1.8B CLO and single-name CDS book at a predecessor manager that had preserved AAA principal through the 2020 energy drawdown via a disciplined dual-ρ stress framework and a USD 22M single-name CDS hedge that had paid USD 14M at the stress peak. He had, in 2019, publicly insisted at an industry conference that "correlation isn't a parameter — it's the game", becoming associated with the dual-ρ stress-disclosure reflex that three peer managers had subsequently adopted. The Quarrystone management committee had recruited him on three capabilities: his documented discipline of running dual-ρ stress envelopes at 0.22 base and 0.35 stress rather than single-ρ defaults; his willingness to purchase single-name CDS hedges on the weakest cluster-concentration exposures at an acknowledged premium; and his command of CLO-equity-tail-risk economics that had, at the predecessor manager, surfaced the equity-as-correlation-hedge-you-didn't-buy insight.

## The Decision

Velasco-Roehrig had to commit, by the 8:00 a.m. credit-committee session, the Phase-1 portfolio-characterisation envelope and the directional intent for Phases 2–5.

1. **Phase 1 — Portfolio characterisation.** Three archetypes. A Vasicek-one-factor envelope (ρ 0.22 base calibrated to 20-year realised correlations; 125 names; B+ average; 380 bps spread; 14% energy exposure). A historical-average shortcut (single-ρ default without cluster-concentration adjustment). A concentration-adjusted envelope (ρ 0.22 base with explicit energy-cluster sub-correlation at 0.28). Working envelope: Vasicek-one-factor with concentration overlay.
2. **Phase 2 — CDS single-name hedge.** Three envelopes. A five-name hedge envelope (USD 40M notional on five weakest borrowers at 280 bps average; USD 200k annual premium). A index-hedge envelope (buy CDX-HY protection at 320 bps; broader but less precise). A no-hedge envelope (rely on CLO subordination; leave equity as the correlation hedge). Working envelope: five-name hedge.
3. **Phase 3 — CLO tranching.** A single envelope: structure AAA 65% / AA 10% / BBB 10% / BB 6% / Equity 9% with Equity target IRR 15%. Working envelope: accept.
4. **Phase 4 — Correlation stress.** Three vectors. A dual-ρ envelope (run base at ρ 0.22 and stress at ρ 0.35). A triple-ρ envelope (base 0.22 / moderate 0.30 / severe 0.40). A single-ρ envelope (base only). Working envelope: dual-ρ.
5. **Phase 5 — Outcome dashboard.** Target envelope: AAA IRR 4.1%, AA 5.8%, BBB 7.2% base / 2.1% stressed, BB 10% base / -18% stressed, Equity 15% base / -75% stressed, CDS payout USD 6.2M, Phase Score 71.

On Velasco-Roehrig's working model, a Vasicek / Five-Name-Hedge / Accept / Dual-ρ / Score-71 envelope delivered the Phase-5 dashboard above and a Composite correlation-pays-or-buries rating. A counterfactual no-hedge / single-ρ envelope would have dropped Phase-5 Score to 52.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2016–2025 CLO-and-credit-derivatives-manager literature had, across 58 documented five-phase credit cycles at USD 300M–USD 800M CLO envelopes, produced three findings the credit desk had to respect. First, Vasicek-one-factor correlation calibration with an explicit concentration overlay on cluster-exposed sectors had, on B+-average pools, preserved AAA principal through 12-month stress windows at realised correlations up to 0.42 in 78% of documented cases. Second, single-name CDS hedges at 6–10% of CLO notional on the weakest 4–6 borrowers had, at cluster-stress windows, delivered payout multiples of 3–8× the cumulative premium paid across five-year CLO life-cycles. Third, dual-ρ stress disclosure frameworks (base at 0.20–0.25, stress at 0.32–0.38) had retained investor confidence through stressed-cycle windows at a 2.1:1 premium versus single-ρ disclosure alternatives.

Empirically, the 2020–2025 CLO-equity literature had two further findings. First, CLO equity had functioned as a de facto correlation-long position, losing 70–95% of principal at realised correlations above 0.38. Second, equity-IRR stress-scenarios at -75% had been survivable for equity holders with a diversified four-to-six-CLO book but catastrophic for single-CLO equity investors.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Q1 2026 leveraged-loan warehouse had aggregated USD 420M across 125 names with a weighted-average rating of B+ and a 14% energy-sector concentration. The Q4 2025 peer-issuance data had shown a -35-bp spread compression across comparable CLOs. The April 2026 energy-sector credit signals had flagged a cluster-level deterioration consistent with the 2020 energy drawdown. The CRA consultation had flagged ρ 0.30 and 0.35 as the critical subordination-stress thresholds.

### What Velasco-Roehrig knew

The Vasicek base-ρ calibration at 0.22 was consistent with 20-year realised correlations on comparable pools; the five weakest borrowers by 12-month default probability were all energy-related; the single-name CDS premia had tightened from 320 bps to 280 bps across Q1 2026; and the CLO equity was priced at a 15% target IRR consistent with peer issuance.

### What he did not know

Whether the energy-cluster deterioration would escalate to a ten-default sequence with realised correlation of 0.41 as in the simulator's severe scenario; whether the CDS protection on five names would cover the tail risk on additional energy credits that were not in the initial hedge basket; whether the AAA subordination would hold through a realised correlation of 0.45 or above; and whether a Q3 CRA downgrade on AA or BBB tranches would trigger investor redemption pressure.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Structured Credit**: *"Dual-ρ. Base twenty-two, stress thirty-five. Transparent is what sells the stack."*

**The Head of Single-Name CDS**: *"Forty million of notional on the five weakest. The cost of the coverage is one event — if it comes."*

**The Chief Risk Officer**: *"Correlation isn't a parameter. It's the game."*

**The Managing Director of Capital Markets**: *"Equity nine percent. Target fifteen IRR. The subordination pays the seniors."*

## Options Under Consideration

Velasco-Roehrig had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Vasicek / Five-Name-Hedge / Accept / Dual-ρ / Score-71:** the recommended envelope. Phase-5 AAA IRR 4.1%, Equity 15% base / -75% stressed, CDS payout USD 6.2M, Score 71. Recommended.
- **Option B — Historical-Average / No-Hedge / Accept / Single-ρ / Score-52:** the template envelope. Score 52. Rejected — correlation-hedge absent and CRA-stress friction.
- **Option C — Vasicek / Index-Hedge / Accept / Dual-ρ / Score-64:** the broad-hedge envelope. Score 64. Rejected — index basis-risk and higher premium for less precise coverage.

His private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the phase most correlated with the correlation-pays-or-buries posture was Phase 2 — the CDS single-name hedge — because it was the phase that decided whether Quarrystone owned a targeted tail-risk hedge on the five weakest energy-cluster names (Option A) or relied on CLO subordination alone (Option B) or over-paid index premium for imprecise coverage (Option C).
