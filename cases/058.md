## Abstract

Grupo Nativa was, in the case universe, a LATAM consumer-goods company headquartered in Bogotá with operations in Colombia, Mexico, Chile, and Peru, 2025 revenue of USD 2.8B, and a portfolio of seventy-six SKUs across four categories (Fresh, Dry, Personal Care, Home Care). The Fresh category — which included premium dairy, plant-based alternatives, and fresh-produce-under-the-own-brand — represented USD 620M of 2025 revenue and had, in 2024, been the subject of a twelve-month internal transformation in which Grupo Nativa had licensed and customized an enterprise GenAI platform (branded internally as Nativa AI) and rolled it out as a decision-assistant tool for the five category general managers. On Monday, March 2, 2026, the Chief Executive placed a single instruction in the weekly leadership meeting: "The AI is not a project. It is a tool. Your score this year is the decisions you made with it and the ones you caught it getting wrong." Valentina Espino, Fresh-Category General Manager, opened the case at 6:45 a.m. on Tuesday, March 3, 2026, as she prepared the Round-1 prompt for the competitive-threat analysis on the just-announced Grupo Natura acquisition of Vida Natural. Five rounds would unfold across a ninety-day window.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the three prompt templates (Narrow, Broad, Context-Rich) on output quality, decision relevance, and verification burden.
2. Analyze the four most common hallucination patterns (fabricated citations, geography mix-ups, sanctions blind spots, stale data) and design a verification protocol for each.
3. Diagnose the privacy-versus-utility trade-off in a team-diagnostic prompt and recommend a data-sharing rubric that preserves model utility without violating personnel privacy.
4. Design a five-round GenAI-use protocol that converts the AI from an answer-generator to a structured decision-assistant with an audit trail.
5. Recommend a complete Round-1 package and identify the single Round-5 Board-review hallucination most likely to require in-meeting correction rather than post-meeting remediation.

## The Protagonist

Valentina Espino, 41, had been General Manager of the Fresh category at Grupo Nativa since November 2023. A graduate of Universidad de los Andes with an MBA from IESE Business School in Barcelona, she had spent eight years at a European consumer-goods multinational, including a four-year stint in Switzerland running a premium-dairy category across seven European markets. She had, in the first six months of her Grupo Nativa tenure, launched the premium-dairy-alternatives brand that now held 18% share in the category — the share the Grupo Natura-Vida-Natural launch was about to attack directly. She had, in her December 2025 performance review, been graded at the "exceeds expectations" tier on managerial decision-making, the tier the Chief Executive had linked in the January 2026 leadership meeting to the competency of "managing the AI rather than being managed by it."

## The Decision

Espino had to commit, by 5:00 p.m. on Tuesday, March 3, the Round-1 prompt-and-decision package for the competitive-threat analysis on the Grupo Natura-Vida-Natural launch:

1. **Prompt Template** — Narrow ("summarize competitor"), Broad ("analyze this launch"), or Context-Rich (category size, Nativa SKU roadmap, Natura's Brazilian portfolio, and an explicit request for a channel-by-channel threat scoring).
2. **Verification Protocol** — level of human-auditor review applied to the AI output before the decision is committed: none, spot-check on citations, or full cross-check with two external reports.
3. **Decision Output** — single-SKU defensive response, multi-SKU portfolio response, or channel-and-portfolio response.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across five rounds. A Context-Rich prompt with a full cross-check and a channel-and-portfolio response produced, on the internal scoring model, a Round-1 prompt-quality score of +12 against a Round-1 baseline of 0 and a decision-quality score of +8 against a baseline of 0; a Narrow prompt with no verification and a single-SKU response produced a prompt score of +2 and a decision score of −6 at the modelled follow-on market-share contestation outcome.

## Company Background

Grupo Nativa had been founded in 1968 as a single-plant dairy operator in Cundinamarca, Colombia, and had expanded across fifty-eight years into the LATAM regional consumer-goods conglomerate the case studied. The Fresh category — the company's heritage category — represented 22% of 2025 revenue at a gross margin of 38% and an EBITDA margin of 14.6%. The category's SKU portfolio spanned twenty-eight dairy SKUs (including eight premium-dairy-alternatives), fourteen fresh-produce SKUs, and six private-label SKUs under contract with three regional retailers. The category held 18% share in the premium-dairy-alternatives subsegment in Colombia, against the subsegment leader (Alpina Natural Origin) at 26% and three smaller players at a combined 28%.

The Nativa AI platform had been built on a commercial enterprise-grade large-language-model provider (unspecified here) and fine-tuned on Grupo Nativa's internal decision-history archive of 22,000 documented decisions across twelve years. The platform's monthly usage by category general managers, measured across January and February 2026, stood at 14 prompts per manager per week, with an output-verification rate (prompts for which the user applied at least one verification step before committing to a decision) of 32%. The Chief Executive's January 2026 leadership-meeting instruction had been, in part, a response to this verification-rate gap.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM consumer-goods managerial-GenAI landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Round-1 decision sat inside. First, the 2024–2025 enterprise-GenAI adoption cohort — a publicly observed fifty-four-company sample — had produced a bimodal adoption outcome: thirty-one companies had reached a "AI-augmented decision" outcome (measurable decision-quality improvement, structured verification protocol, audit-trail practice) and twenty-three had reached an "AI-dependency" outcome (decision-quality degradation, hallucination-pass-through into board materials, erosion of managerial muscle); the single strongest predictor of the outcome was the percentage of prompts for which the user applied at least one verification step before committing. Second, the hallucination-pattern literature had converged on four common failure modes: fabricated citations (Nielsen/Kantar/AC Nielsen references to reports that did not exist), geography mix-ups (the model applied Brazilian price-elasticity to Colombian channel decisions), sanctions and regulatory blind spots (the model surfaced suppliers in sanctioned jurisdictions), and stale data (the model applied pre-acquisition information about a post-acquisition entity). Third, the prompt-engineering research had shown that Context-Rich prompts — prompts that included category size, internal roadmap, competitor portfolio, and an explicit decision frame — produced, in a 2024 enterprise-research sample, 41% higher decision-relevance-scores than Narrow prompts.

Empirically, the privacy-and-utility research had two robust findings. First, anonymized and aggregated team-diagnostic prompts produced 92% of the diagnostic power of fully-identified prompts while eliminating the compliance exposure of personal-data sharing with the large-language-model provider. Second, pricing-decision prompts that specified the geography, channel, and elasticity data-source produced 63% fewer geography-confusion errors than prompts that relied on the model's default-geography assumption.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Grupo Natura-Vida-Natural acquisition had been announced at 6:00 a.m. on Monday, March 2, 2026. The public materials — the press release, the investor-call transcript, and a three-page deal summary prepared by Grupo Nativa's corporate-development team — had been on Espino's desk by 10:30 a.m. the same morning. The ninety-day relaunch window disclosed in the acquisition press release would place the Vida-Natural premium-dairy-alternatives relaunch at approximately Monday, June 1, 2026. The Quarterly Business Review at which Espino would present the Fresh-category defense plan was scheduled for Tuesday, June 2, 2026.

### What Espino knew

The internal team had modeled three Round-1 packages. A Conservative Package (Narrow prompt, no verification, single-SKU response) projected Round-1 prompt-quality score +2, decision-quality score −6, a follow-on three-month share-loss range of 1.6–2.8 points. A Balanced Package (Context-Rich prompt, full cross-check, channel-and-portfolio response) projected Round-1 prompt-quality score +12, decision-quality score +8, share-loss range 0.3–0.8 points. An Aggressive Package (Context-Rich plus external-scenario prompt, full cross-check plus scenario-audit, channel-and-portfolio-with-promotion response) projected prompt-quality score +14, decision-quality score +9, share-loss range 0.2–0.7 points, at a 90-day execution bandwidth that the Fresh-category team flagged as at-capacity.

### What she did not know

Whether the Vida-Natural relaunch would lead with the premium-dairy-alternatives SKU (base case) or with a new fresh-produce SKU (downside case for Nativa) that would broaden the competitive fight beyond the premium-dairy-alternatives subsegment; whether the Nativa AI, when presented with the Round-1 Context-Rich prompt, would generate a hallucinated Kantar citation — the pattern the internal-audit log had flagged at approximately 11% of competitive-threat prompts in January and February — and whether Espino would catch it in the verification step; whether the Round-5 Quarterly Business Review prompt would generate a fabricated Nielsen-Q3-report citation that the Chief Financial Officer would question in-meeting; and whether the five-round total prompt-quality-score the Chief Executive had linked to the "exceeds-expectations" competency tier would land above the threshold of +50 across the five rounds.

### What others were saying

**Ernesto Jurado**, Chief Marketing Officer: *"Context-Rich with a channel-by-channel threat scoring. The Narrow prompt gives you a three-sentence summary of a press release. You already have a three-sentence summary. You need the channel-by-channel map — which of the sixty-two Vida-Natural pre-launch SKUs are going where, and when."*

**Paola Jiménez**, Head of Data and AI: *"Verification protocol is not optional. Every Round output goes through the spot-check on citations. Round-5 output goes through a full cross-check with two external reports. The internal-audit log tells you, eleven percent of competitive-threat prompts last quarter had a fabricated citation. Round-5 is Board-level."*

**The Chief Executive** (January 2026 leadership meeting): *"The score is the decisions. Not the prompts. Not the outputs. The decisions — and the ones you caught the model getting wrong."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Espino had narrowed the space to three Round-1 packages.

- **Option A — Conservative Narrow:** Narrow prompt, no verification, single-SKU response. Prompt score +2. Decision score −6. Simpler and faster; loses the channel-by-channel threat map.

- **Option B — Balanced Context-Rich:** Context-Rich prompt, full cross-check, channel-and-portfolio response. Prompt score +12. Decision score +8. Share-loss range 0.3–0.8 points. Aligns with the Chief Executive's decision-quality framing.

- **Option C — Aggressive Context-Rich with Scenario:** Context-Rich plus scenario prompt, scenario-audit verification, channel-portfolio-with-promotion response. Prompt score +14. Decision score +9. Share-loss range 0.2–0.7 points. Execution bandwidth at capacity.

Espino's private view, not yet shared with Ernesto Jurado, was that Option B was the right Round-1 package and that the single subsequent decision most correlated with the Round-5 Board-review outcome was the Round-5 Nielsen-Q3-citation verification step — specifically, the pre-meeting commissioning of a Nielsen pull on the one contested share number that would allow in-meeting correction without undermining the full AI-assisted analysis.
