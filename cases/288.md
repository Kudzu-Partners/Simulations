## Abstract

The Marketing Director at the case-universe Lima-headquartered Sabores del Valle S.A. — approximately S/. 680k in six-month post-launch revenue on the energy bar plus S/. 1.8m on the inherited granola-and-cookies portfolio, 18 employees, competing in the Lima functional-snack category against QualiBar (premium imported from the United States, S/. 9.50 shelf, 22% category share) and Fuerza Natural (Arequipa price-fighter, S/. 4.20 shelf, 31% category share), a remaining 47% category share distributed across Sabores del Valle (9%), two smaller domestic artisans, and the private-label segment — had been asked, in the April 2026 CEO directive, to run the 60-minute three-quarter market-selection sim and deliver a market-selection commitment. The launch had succeeded on coherence terms (sim 287) and failed to dent either rival's share in the six months since. The question the sim asked was different from the launch question. The launch question had been product-packaging-price-promotion coherence. The market question was STP — Segment, Target, Position — and whether Sabores could pick an axis that QualiBar and Fuerza Natural could not copy fast. The case opens at 2:20 p.m. on Thursday, April 23, 2026, at Sabores del Valle's San Borja office as **Marcela Quiroz-Valdivia**, now 28 and promoted to Marketing Director post-launch, prepares the three-quarter commitment.

1. 2. Place Sabores del Valle on the 2×2 positioning map (price tier × health sophistication) with QualiBar upper-right and Fuerza Natural lower-left, and articulate why differentiation requires placing Sabores somewhere the rivals are not, why mid-price-and-local-authenticity is a defensible axis for the Lima segment, and why Porter's stuck-in-the-middle warning applies to marketing placement as well as to corporate strategy.
3. Allocate the Round-1 S/. 45,000 quarterly budget across product development, price promotion, distribution expansion and advertising, and articulate why every soles in one bucket is a soles out of another, why Porter's generic-strategies discipline excludes the stuck-in-the-middle option, and why Sabores' differentiation-through-local-authenticity axis shapes the budget allocation more than category averages would.
4. Respond to the Round-2 competitor moves — QualiBar's three-pack bundle and Fuerza Natural's 8% price cut — by reading Court's Consumer Decision Journey rather than the weekly-revenue headline, and articulate why the teams that under-invested in the purchase-conversion and repeat-purchase stages lose share to both rivals simultaneously and why reallocation between phases is more valuable than lever-level optimisation.
5. Hold discipline through Round-3 under Fuerza Natural's flash promo — resist the bullwhip-style over-reaction, hold price, double down on trial with a loyalty programme, escalate the Deportista segment — and commit to the Aaker brand-equity panel (awareness, perceived quality, associations, loyalty) as the leading indicator of next-quarter revenue, articulating why choosing an axis the rival cannot copy fast is the marketing strategy and the rest is tactics.

## The Protagonist

Quiroz-Valdivia, 28, had been Marketing Director since January 2026 following the successful quinoa-energy-bar launch she had run as Marketing Coordinator in October–December 2025 (documented in sim 287). The promotion had been the CEO's response to the coherent-launch outcome and the flat share trajectory against the two category incumbents. In the four months since, she had absorbed the three-quarter market-selection brief, the Segment Explorer data from the 2025 Ipsos panel, and the two rivals' public price-and-promotion behaviour. The CEO had assigned her the April exercise on three considerations: her command of the launch-mix coherence discipline; her willingness to pick a primary segment and visibly renounce the three she did not pick; and her comfort holding price against Fuerza Natural's flash-promo pressure.

---

## The Decision

Quiroz-Valdivia had to commit, by the 4:45 p.m. CEO-and-Co-founder debrief, the three-quarter market-selection posture.

1. **Round 1 — STP and budget.** Primary segment: Estudiante Consciente. Secondary: Deportista Universitario. 2×2 placement: mid-price, local-authenticity (distinct from QualiBar's premium-sophisticated and Fuerza Natural's cheap-simple). S/. 45,000 Q1 budget: S/. 13,500 product development (resealable pack iteration, formula variants), S/. 9,000 price promotion (trial-building, loyalty seed), S/. 13,500 distribution expansion (three university-adjacent bodega clusters and two gym chains), S/. 9,000 advertising (micro-influencer continuation plus UGC activation).
2. **Round 2 — Competitor moves.** QualiBar three-pack bundle and Fuerza Natural 8% price cut. Response: hold Sabores price, reallocate S/. 6,000 from advertising to purchase-conversion (sampling at bodegas) and repeat-purchase (loyalty programme seed), maintain STP discipline.
3. **Round 3 — Fuerza Natural flash promo.** Resist the bullwhip over-reaction. Hold price. Double down on trial with loyalty-programme activation. Escalate the Deportista Universitario segment with gym-chain sampling. Aaker brand-equity panel tracked: awareness +, perceived quality +, associations +, loyalty ++.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1960–2025 strategic-marketing literature produced three findings the sim had to respect. First, explicit primary-and-secondary-segment choice (STP discipline) produced documented first-year market-share readings approximately 35–50% higher than undifferentiated-marketing strategies on comparable 2015–2024 food-startup cycles. Second, Court's Consumer Decision Journey — superseding the linear purchase funnel in the 2009 McKinsey update — correctly predicts that reallocation across the active-evaluation, purchase-conversion and post-purchase stages is more valuable than single-stage optimisation. Third, Aaker's four-pillar brand-equity panel (awareness, perceived quality, associations, loyalty) remains the documented strongest leading indicator of next-period revenue in emerging consumer brands, with loyalty the single most predictive pillar.

The post-2010 competitive-response literature added two findings. Bullwhip-style over-reactions to flash promos — matching the rival's price for the promo window — produced documented margin compression approximately 2.3× the revenue uplift, and hold-price-plus-loyalty responses produced documented share retention approximately 85–95% of pre-promo levels while preserving margin.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The October 2025 launch produced coherent six-month results (S/. 680k energy-bar revenue) but flat share against QualiBar and Fuerza Natural. The April 2026 CEO directive asked for the three-quarter market-selection sim. QualiBar launched the three-pack bundle on April 2. Fuerza Natural cut price 8% on April 18. Neither move was a surprise; both were telegraphed in Q1 promotional filings.

### What Quiroz-Valdivia knew

Estudiante Consciente is the highest-absolute-spend segment available to Sabores; the mid-price, local-authenticity axis is defensible; QualiBar will not cross to mid-price without dilution; Fuerza Natural will not cross to health sophistication without a capability investment; Aaker loyalty is the leading indicator; bullwhip over-reactions destroy margin faster than they preserve share.

### What she did not know

Whether the Deportista Universitario secondary segment would absorb the Round-1 investment or absorb it slowly; whether QualiBar's bundle would spill into the student segment or hold in the professional segment; whether Fuerza Natural's 8% cut was the full promotional envelope or the opening move; whether the gym-chain distribution expansion would produce Round-3 volume or Round-4 volume; and whether the CEO would hold the price-hold discipline under the Round-3 flash-promo pressure.

### What others were saying

**The CEO and Co-founder**: *"I want share growth, not just margin preservation. But not at the cost of the axis."*

**The Head of Sales**: *"The three-pack bundle from QualiBar will pull about four points of share out of the student segment. We have to answer in Round 2."*

**The Trade Marketing Partner**: *"Sampling at bodegas is the Round-2 move. Not the Round-3 move. Round 3 is about loyalty activation."*

**The Data Analyst**: *"Aaker loyalty is already reading a 42. Two quarters of discipline and it crosses 50. Three quarters of over-reaction and it falls to 35."*

**The Gym-Chain Account Manager**: *"Round-3 activation at Smart Fit and Bodytech. Ten-store test. Three-month exclusive."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Quiroz-Valdivia had narrowed the space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — Estudiante Consciente primary, Deportista Universitario secondary, mid-price local-authenticity positioning, hold-price discipline through Round 3 with loyalty double-down (Recommended):** STP discipline, axis QualiBar and Fuerza Natural cannot cross without dilution, Aaker loyalty trajectory preserved. Recommended.
- **Option B — Match Fuerza Natural's 8% cut in Round 2, match QualiBar's bundle with a promotional three-pack in Round 3:** rejected — bullwhip over-reaction, margin compression exceeds share gain, axis abandoned.
- **Option C — Reposition upward toward QualiBar's premium-sophisticated axis:** rejected — capability investment in packaging and formulation does not fit the three-quarter envelope, and the QualiBar axis is imported-brand-advantaged.

Her private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the round most correlated with three-quarter end-state share was Round 3 — the flash-promo resistance — because Round 3 was the round where discipline either held or degraded, and the Aaker loyalty trajectory across Round 3 predicted Q4 revenue.
