## Abstract

Harmonia Capital Fund II, a USD 200M North-American mid-market buyout fund vintage 2020, entered its sixth year with four portfolio companies (HealthSpan, an outpatient multi-specialty platform; OmniRetail, a regional home-goods chain; CoreTech, a B2B vertical-SaaS ARR of USD 38M; VeraFood, a specialty-ingredients manufacturer), USD 152M of equity deployed, USD 48M of dry powder, and a limited-partner expectation that 2026 would be the primary harvest year. Blended fund IRR stood at 14.2%, MOIC at 1.4x, DPI at 0.3x, TVPI at 1.5x; the fund's IRR target was 22%, MOIC 2.2x. The Managing Partner, Theodore Chen, had to table, by end of April, a four-quarter plan that sequenced value-creation initiatives, capital-structure moves, add-on acquisitions, and exits across the four companies. Two companies were underperforming plan; one was at-plan; one was meaningfully above. The case stages Chen's Q1 investment-committee meeting and sets up the first-round decision set in the companion simulation.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate trade-offs between value-creation levers (operational, capital-structure, add-on, exit) across a heterogeneous portfolio.
2. Analyze how fund-level economics (IRR, MOIC, DPI, TVPI) shape individual portfolio-company decisions.
3. Design an exit-sequencing strategy that balances DPI delivery against residual portfolio risk.
4. Diagnose the interaction between dry-powder conservation and opportunistic add-on acquisitions.
5. Recommend a Q1 plan and articulate the failure modes that the fund's LPs will, at exit, most heavily weight.

On the night of **April 20, 2026**, **Theodore Chen** sat in Harmonia Capital's conference room on the 34th floor of a Midtown Manhattan tower and looked, without speaking, at the four manila folders in front of him on the polished walnut table. Each folder carried a color-coded tab. HealthSpan — blue. OmniRetail — red. CoreTech — green. VeraFood — amber. Across the top of the table, an IC deck was open to page seven. The page read, in 72-point type: *Q1 2026: Decisions*.

---

## The Protagonist

Theodore Chen, 52, had been founding Managing Partner of Harmonia Capital since the firm's inception in 2012. A Stanford MBA and former principal at a large megacap fund, he had built Harmonia's thesis around mid-market operational value creation, with Fund I returning a 2.3x net MOIC and a 24.1% net IRR over its 2013–2020 life. Fund II had been raised in 2020 at USD 200M with an LP base of family offices, two university endowments, and a fund-of-funds. Chen was personally invested in Fund II at 2.5% of committed capital and carried a majority of the firm's carry allocation.

He had, eighteen months earlier, begun signalling to his LPs that Fund III fundraising would depend on Fund II's harvest. An early harvest with strong DPI was his preferred signal; a late harvest with strong MOIC was acceptable; a late harvest with weak MOIC was not.

---

## The Decision

By **April 30, 2026**, Chen had to table for investment-committee approval a Q1 plan spanning four decisions per company (sixteen decisions total):

1. **HealthSpan** — operational initiative (physician compensation restructure; multi-site EHR consolidation; payer-contract renegotiation); capital structure (refinance debt at today's rates; maintain); add-on (close on signed LOI with a cardiology platform for USD 22M; pass); exit (begin IPO prep; hold for Q3 2027; dual-track process).

2. **OmniRetail** — operational initiative (store-base rationalization; price re-architecture; e-commerce rebuild); capital structure (extend maturity; partial dividend recap; default on plan); add-on (tuck-in of three failing competitors at NAV; pass); exit (sell to strategic; sell to secondary PE; run-to-cash).

3. **CoreTech** — operational initiative (enterprise sales-motion rebuild; vertical expansion to adjacent sub-segment; pricing reset); capital structure (issue preferred equity; maintain); add-on (acquire a USD 9M ARR competitor for USD 54M; pass); exit (process Q4 2026; Q2 2027; hold).

4. **VeraFood** — operational initiative (supply-chain reconfiguration; product mix reweight to higher-margin specialty; plant consolidation); capital structure (maintain; recapitalize for a Q3 special dividend); add-on (pass — no quality targets in market); exit (sell to strategic food ingredient buyer; run-to-cash; hold).

The investment committee would vote each company plan up or down as a single package.

---

## Fund Background

Harmonia Capital had been founded in 2012 with a USD 90M Fund I and had grown to USD 200M Fund II. The firm had, across both funds, pursued a thesis of "operational value creation at the mid-market tier," targeting companies with USD 50–250M of enterprise value at entry, three-to-five-year holding periods, and a four-lever value-creation playbook: operations, capital structure, add-ons, exit.

Fund II's portfolio had been fully deployed by 2023 across four companies, each with a ticket between USD 32M and USD 46M of equity. The fund had reserved USD 48M for add-ons and follow-ons. As of Q1 2026 the fund's key metrics stood at: committed USD 200M, deployed USD 152M, dry powder USD 48M, distributions-to-paid-in (DPI) 0.3x, total-value-to-paid-in (TVPI) 1.5x, net IRR 14.2%, net MOIC 1.4x. The five-year fund target was 22% IRR / 2.2x MOIC.

Portfolio company status at entry into Q1:

- **HealthSpan**: entered 2022, equity USD 42M, current modeled enterprise value USD 124M, leverage 3.6x EBITDA, growth 11% YoY.
- **OmniRetail**: entered 2021, equity USD 38M, current modeled enterprise value USD 46M (below entry), leverage 5.1x EBITDA, same-store sales −4.2%.
- **CoreTech**: entered 2022, equity USD 36M, current modeled enterprise value USD 188M, ARR USD 38M growing 34% YoY, leverage 2.1x ARR.
- **VeraFood**: entered 2023, equity USD 36M, current modeled enterprise value USD 92M, EBITDA margin 14%, leverage 3.4x EBITDA.

## Industry and Market Context

The mid-market private-equity environment in 2026 carried three structural realities. First, the cost of debt — at SOFR+425 for mid-market leveraged credit — was roughly 300 basis points above the 2021 trough; refinancing economics had deteriorated. Second, exit multiples had normalized downward: median enterprise-value-to-EBITDA in the four portfolio sub-sectors had contracted by 1.4 turns from 2021 peaks, though CoreTech's sub-sector (vertical SaaS) had held roughly flat. Third, LPs had tightened the DPI clock: Preqin's 2026 investor-sentiment survey reported that 68% of LPs weighted DPI-by-year-6 more heavily than peak TVPI in Fund III commitment decisions.

Research on fund concentration suggested two propositions. First, a fund's harvest year outcomes were disproportionately driven by one or two companies; over-concentrating value-creation capital in the weakest names historically under-performed allocating to the strongest. Second, add-on economics were most accretive in the last eighteen months of a hold, when they could be integrated quickly and priced into the exit multiple.

## The Situation

### What Chen knew

The internal model, run twice by the firm's operating partners, placed HealthSpan as exit-ready under either an IPO (Q2 2027, EV USD 155M, MOIC 2.6x) or a dual-track (Q3 2027, EV USD 148M, MOIC 2.5x); OmniRetail as a problem (EV 46M, leverage 5.1x, deteriorating), with a run-to-cash option that returned 0.7x MOIC at best and a strategic sale that might clear 0.9x; CoreTech as the fund's likely best outcome (EV trajectory 188 → 310 by Q4 2026 under the enterprise sales-motion rebuild, 3.0x MOIC at exit); VeraFood as an above-plan performer with unclear exit window.

### What he did not know

Whether the HealthSpan cardiology add-on would clear regulatory review in time for it to be folded into an IPO prospectus. Whether the CoreTech customer-logo-concentration risk (the top ten customers represented 54% of ARR) would be dispositive in a Q4 2026 process. Whether the OmniRetail restructuring could be executed cleanly under existing covenants without a consent solicitation.

### What others were saying

**Priya Shankar**, Head of Operations, argued for *concentrate on winners*: accelerate CoreTech growth capital (the USD 54M add-on, financed by the full dry powder); ride HealthSpan to a 2027 IPO; restructure VeraFood for a 2027 dividend recap; run OmniRetail to cash.

**Marcus Hirschberg**, CFO, argued for *harvest early and cleanly*: process HealthSpan in Q4 2026; sell VeraFood to a strategic in Q3 2026; sell OmniRetail to secondary PE in Q2 2026; hold CoreTech through 2027. DPI ≈ 1.1x by end-2026; MOIC ≈ 1.8x at fund life.

**Jennifer Kwon**, Head of Strategy, argued for *bar-bell*: accelerate CoreTech (aggressive); accelerate HealthSpan (aggressive); sell OmniRetail quickly (defensive); VeraFood flexible. MOIC ≈ 2.1x at fund life; DPI 0.7x by end-2026.

---

## Options Under Consideration

- **Option A — Concentrate on Winners (Shankar plan):** Deploy full USD 48M into CoreTech add-on; operational overhaul across all four; delay all exits to 2027–2028. Target MOIC 2.3x; DPI 0.4x by end-2026.

- **Option B — Harvest Early and Cleanly (Hirschberg plan):** Process HealthSpan and VeraFood exits in 2026; sell OmniRetail to secondary; hold CoreTech; no add-ons. Target MOIC 1.8x; DPI 1.1x by end-2026.

- **Option C — Bar-Bell (Kwon plan):** Accelerate CoreTech (USD 36M add-on) and HealthSpan (IPO 2027); sell OmniRetail to secondary quickly; VeraFood flexible. Target MOIC 2.1x; DPI 0.7x by end-2026.

Chen's private bet, not yet articulated to the investment committee, was that Option C was most defensible to LPs provided the OmniRetail sale closed by the end of Q3 2026.
