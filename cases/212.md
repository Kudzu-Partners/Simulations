## Abstract

Condor Holdings S.A. was, in the case universe, a Santiago-headquartered LATAM diversified holdings group with USD 2.4B 2025 consolidated revenue, 7,850 employees, seven operating subsidiaries across Mexico, Brazil, Spain and Chile, four functional currencies (MXN, BRL, EUR, CLP) and a US-dollar presentation currency. The parent had, over 2024–2025, completed the IFRS 15 / IFRS 16 / IAS 36 transition under external-audit supervision and had, in Q4 2025, appointed a new Group Controller with a single mandate: compress the quarterly close from fourteen business days to nine, produce a single consolidated reporting pack that articulated seven subsidiary stories into one group narrative, and leave the audit-firm partner — scheduled to arrive on day ten — without a single unresolved finding. The Q3 2026 close opened at 6:00 a.m. on Friday, the subsidiary trial balances had landed overnight, four of them in local currency, three intercompany streams had run, one Brazilian CGU had triggered an impairment indicator, and a USD 18M lease-commitment schedule required IFRS 16 right-of-use capitalisation. The case opens at 7:05 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Condor Holdings Santiago corporate-reporting floor as **Lucía Benegas-Carrillo**, a 44-year-old Group Controller, prepares the Round-1 FX-translation commitment.

Eliminate the Round-2 intercompany stream: the USD 14M Mexico-to-Spain sale at 35% margin, USD 3M residual in Spain's inventory at quarter-end, USD 1.05M unrealised-margin elimination from both consolidated inventory and consolidated profit — and diagnose why failure to eliminate systematically overstates group revenue, group cost-of-sales and group earnings.

Post the Round-3 IFRS-adjustment stack: USD 4M IFRS 15 revenue deferral (control-transfer test failed), USD 18M IFRS 16 right-of-use and lease liability (operating-lease-off-balance-sheet era ended), and USD 6M IAS 36 impairment on the Brazil CGU (carrying USD 45M vs. recoverable USD 39M) — and diagnose the group-margin, group-asset and group-equity impact of each.

On the morning of **Wednesday, April 22, 2026**, **Lucía Benegas-Carrillo** arrived at the Condor Holdings Santiago corporate-reporting floor at 7:05 a.m., opened the four-round Q3 2026 close workbook her Consolidation Manager and her four Subsidiary Controllers had pre-staged the previous night at 11:22 p.m., and re-read the sentence the audit partner had left with her at the Q2 2026 close review: *"Consolidation is not a calculation. It is a translation of seven stories into one."*

---

## The Protagonist

Benegas-Carrillo, 44, had assumed the Group-Controller role at Condor Holdings on November 1, 2025, after sixteen years across audit, technical-accounting and group-reporting leadership at a Big-Four Chilean firm and a São Paulo-based food-and-beverage multinational. She had, in 2018–2024, led the IFRS 15 transition for a USD 4.2B São Paulo food-and-beverage consolidation across five functional currencies and had, in 2022–2024, led the IFRS 16 right-of-use adoption for a 680-store retail footprint. The Chief Financial Officer had recruited her in October 2025 specifically on three capabilities: her documented discipline of running FX translation at the line-item level (closing for balance sheet, average for P&L, historical for equity) rather than at single-rate shortcuts that had misprice OCI by USD 5M–USD 12M at peer holdings operators, her willingness to eliminate intercompany unrealised margin at quarter-end rather than at year-end (which had generated USD 3M–USD 8M of profit overstatement in three documented LATAM cases), and her command of the IFRS 15 / IFRS 16 / IAS 36 stack that the Q4 2025 audit firm had rated "ready-state" in the pre-audit walk-through.

---

## The Decision

Benegas-Carrillo had to commit, by the 8:30 a.m. audit-committee pre-call, the Round-1 FX envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–4.

1. **Round 1 — FX translation.** Four subsidiaries in four functional currencies. The architecture: balance-sheet line items at the closing rate (MXN 19.42, BRL 5.01, EUR 0.91, CLP 952), income-statement line items at the average rate (MXN 19.18, BRL 4.94, EUR 0.92, CLP 948), equity items at the historical rate, and the plug — the Cumulative Translation Adjustment — landing in OCI at USD 2.1M this quarter. The shortcut alternative, applying the closing rate uniformly across all line items, would have mispriced operating income by USD 1.6M and would have eliminated the OCI plug line entirely.
2. **Round 2 — Intercompany elimination.** Mexico had sold USD 14M of finished goods to Spain at a 35% intercompany margin. Spain had sold USD 11M of that lot to third-party customers by quarter-end. USD 3M remained in Spain's quarter-end inventory. Benegas-Carrillo had to eliminate USD 1.05M of unrealised margin (35% of USD 3M) from consolidated inventory and from consolidated profit; the USD 14M intercompany revenue and the USD 11M intercompany cost-of-sales had to be eliminated in full from the top and gross-margin lines.
3. **Round 3 — IFRS adjustments.** Three named adjustments. IFRS 15: a USD 4M service contract failed the control-transfer test at quarter-end — revenue deferral to deferred-revenue liability. IFRS 16: a USD 18M portfolio of multi-year real-estate and fleet leases required right-of-use asset and lease-liability capitalisation at the lessee. IAS 36: the Brazil CGU — a USD 45M carrying-value retail-and-logistics unit — had triggered an impairment indicator (three-quarter revenue run-rate down 11%); the recoverable amount on the Value-in-Use model was USD 39M, generating a USD 6M impairment charge.
4. **Round 4 — NCI allocation.** The Mexico subsidiary was 80%-owned by the parent. Q3 2026 Mexico Net Income was USD 8M; Q3 Mexico dividend to shareholders was USD 2M. NCI share of Net Income: USD 1.6M. NCI share of dividend: USD 0.4M. Consolidated equity had to reflect the 20% that was not the parent's.

On Benegas-Carrillo's working model, the Q3 2026 close delivered consolidation accuracy of 94%, three documented audit-issue catches (the intercompany-margin residual, the Brazil CGU impairment, and an IFRS 15 control-transfer mis-read in one Chile subsidiary), a nine-day close-to-sign-off and a single reporting pack that articulated the seven subsidiary stories into one group narrative.

## Company Background

Condor Holdings had been founded in 1974 in Santiago, had expanded into Mexico in 1988, Brazil in 1996 and Spain in 2004, and had, across fifty years, built a USD 2.4B-revenue LATAM diversified holdings group organised into three operating verticals: a food-and-beverage vertical (four subsidiaries, USD 1.3B revenue), a retail-and-logistics vertical (two subsidiaries, USD 0.7B revenue) and a financial-services vertical (one subsidiary, USD 0.4B revenue). The group reported in US dollars and produced IFRS-compliant consolidated financial statements quarterly. The 2024 IFRS 15 transition had clarified three multi-element contracts, and the 2024 IFRS 16 adoption had moved USD 680M of operating-lease commitments onto the balance sheet. The Q4 2025 audit review had rated the group as "ready-state" but had flagged a close-cycle compression target: from fourteen business days to nine by Q3 2026.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2019–2025 LATAM-holdings-group close-cycle literature had, across 54 documented multi-subsidiary close windows on USD 1B–USD 5B-revenue IFRS-reporting groups, produced three findings the close had to respect. First, line-item-level FX translation had reduced OCI-plug mispricing by USD 5M–USD 12M in 76% of documented cases against single-rate shortcuts. Second, intercompany-unrealised-margin eliminations at quarter-end rather than at year-end had avoided USD 3M–USD 8M of profit-overstatement reversals in 72% of cases. Third, IAS-36 impairment-indicator testing at the quarter-end trigger (rather than the annual cycle) had caught USD 4M–USD 14M of carrying-value overstatement in 64% of cases on LATAM diversified groups.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The close-cycle compression had, in Q1 2026, fallen from fourteen days to twelve. The Q2 2026 close had landed at ten. The Q3 2026 close had a target of nine, with the audit firm scheduled to arrive on day ten.

### What Benegas-Carrillo knew

The Consolidation Manager had delivered the four subsidiary trial balances on the night of day one. The Intercompany Manager had reconciled the Mexico-to-Spain stream on day two. The Technical Accounting Lead had flagged the Brazil CGU impairment indicator and the IFRS 15 control-transfer mis-read on day three. The lease-accounting team had closed the IFRS 16 right-of-use book on day four.

### What she did not know

Whether the audit partner would accept the Brazil CGU Value-in-Use model at 11.2% WACC and 2% perpetuity growth, or would demand a Fair-Value-Less-Costs-of-Disposal recomputation; whether the IFRS 15 control-transfer deferral on the USD 4M contract would survive the auditor's five-step test; whether the IFRS 16 incremental-borrowing-rate of 7.4% would be re-priced to 7.8% based on the Q2 2026 benchmark; and whether the Mexico NCI dividend would trigger a withholding-tax reconciliation at the parent level.

### What others were saying

**The Audit Partner** (in the pre-call): *"Show me the three catches. The Brazil impairment. The intercompany residual. The IFRS 15 deferral. If those are clean, the close is clean."*

**The Mexico Subsidiary Controller**: *"NCI 20%. Don't forget the dividend split."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"Nine days. One pack. One narrative."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Benegas-Carrillo had narrowed the close to three postures.

- **Option A — Line-Item FX + Intercompany-at-Quarter-End + Full IFRS Stack + NCI at Line-Item:** the recommended posture. Consolidation accuracy 94%. Three audit catches. Nine-day close. Recommended.
- **Option B — Single-Rate FX + Intercompany-at-Year-End + Annual-Only IFRS + NCI at Equity-Roll:** the shortcut. Consolidation accuracy 71%. Audit findings risk. Rejected — fails the Q3 close-cycle standard and the audit readiness.
- **Option C — Line-Item FX + Intercompany-at-Quarter-End + Partial IFRS Stack + NCI at Line-Item:** the compromise. Consolidation accuracy 86%. Risk on the Brazil CGU. Rejected — partial IFRS posture exposes the group to impairment-finding reversals.

Her private view was that Option A was the right posture and that the round most correlated with the audit-cycle defensibility was Round 3 — the IFRS-adjustment stack — because it was the round that caught the Brazil impairment, cleared the IFRS 15 deferral and closed the IFRS 16 right-of-use on the same day the trial balances translated.
