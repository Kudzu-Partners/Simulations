# Verder Reizen: The Placement

**Case Code:** SUS-2026-2131 · **Companion simulation:** Express 2131 — *The Placement* · **v1.0 · September 2026**

---

On Friday, September 11, 2026, Femke Hoekstra, managing director of Verder Reizen, laid out the two letters that had arrived that week. The first came from Hogeschool Zuidrand, whose accredited service-learning minor sent 380 students a year on Verder placements: 18% of volume and the largest single channel. From March 1, 2027, its ethics committee would withdraw accreditation from any placement that could not show a need defined by the host, a match between the volunteer's skills and the task, no displacement of paid local labour, and no unsupervised contact between volunteers and children in residential care. Verder had ninety days to reply.

The second came from Marisol Quiej Tzoc, director of the Ixchel Ruk'u'x cooperative in Guatemala's western highlands and Verder's host partner since 2013. The cooperative would not renew in 2027 on the current terms; she enclosed her own analysis of Verder's impact report. Before the 2027 season went on sale, Hoekstra had to decide the future of Verder's five programmes, the terms with the cooperative, the shape of the catalogue, and what the company told the people who bought its placements.

## The Protagonist

Femke Hoekstra, 52, had joined Verder in 2011 as an in-country coordinator in Ecuador and had been managing director since 2017. On her watch Verder had signed the Dutch responsible-travel code early, adopted a safeguarding policy, and put its coordinators on local permanent contracts.

## The Decision

The board wanted the answers in four steps, in that order. At the second, the cooperative's director could reject any proposal affecting her programme; failing agreement, the cooperative would leave with its 340 placements, its reputation and the photographs Verder used to sell them. The third had to satisfy the university and the covenant on Verder's bank facility at once; the fourth set words, prices and recruitment.

## The Company and its Setting

Verder Reizen B.V., founded in Utrecht in 2009, sent 2,140 volunteers abroad in 2025 on placements averaging 17 days, at an average fee of €1,980 excluding flights. Revenue was €4.24 million and the net result €288,000, a net margin of 6.8%. It employed 61 people in Utrecht and in the host countries. Overheads ran at €450,000 a year plus €121 for every volunteer sent; the net result was what the five programmes' gross margin left after them (see Exhibit 1).

Customers were mostly Dutch students with a summer or professionals with annual leave. Nearly every recruitment call asked whether the volunteer would get to work with the children.

Page 14 of Verder's annual impact report showed that €611 of the average fee reached the host country in some form and €214 reached the host organisation as unrestricted funds (see Exhibit 2). The website said volunteers "directly fund the communities they serve."

Joost Verbeek, head of marketing and recruitment, noted that €611 a volunteer meant about €1.3 million a year spent in host countries, that Verder's in-country coordinators held permanent jobs there, that without Casa Luz the other four programmes did not cover Verder's overheads, and that customers bought seventeen days because that was the time they had. Recruitment cost more, he added, when host partners set the dates.

Inge Mulder, head of safeguarding and impact, noted that the same page said €214; that Verder's own dossier found Casa Luz failed all four tests, against a research consensus on short-term volunteering in residential childcare it called unambiguous and negative; that local masons who needed the work built faster and better than volunteers; and that Aula Abierta's schools wanted continuity and met a new volunteer every seventeen days. Verder's insurer, she added, required a named responsible entity for every placement.

## The Situation

Casa Luz placed volunteers in support roles at residential children's homes in two countries. At the catalogue's highest fee it earned €412,000 of gross margin, more than two-fifths of the €997,000 all five programmes earned. Finance calculated that closing it, with nothing else changed, would turn the €288,000 net result into a loss of about €74,000. Zuidrand's students were spread across all five programmes.

Marisol Quiej Tzoc, an agronomist with an MBA from a Guatemalan university, had run the cooperative for nine years. Under her proposal the cooperative would set the placement calendar, define the work, employ the coordinators, and receive €740 per volunteer as unrestricted funds, €526 more than the €214 it received now. Verder would recruit, screen, and arrange travel and insurance, for a defined fee rather than whatever margin was left over. Her letter observed that the only thing Verder supplied which the cooperative could not replicate was European customer acquisition. Two coordinators had said they would leave rather than move to the cooperative's payroll.

If Verder changed nothing, the university would withdraw in March, the cooperative would leave with its 340 placements and €94,000 of gross margin, and the website's sentence would stay beside page 14.

## What Hoekstra Controlled

For each programme Hoekstra would choose one of four dispositions: keep it, redesign it, hand it over to the host organisation, or close it.

With the cooperative she would settle six terms: who set the placement calendar (Verder, around customers' holidays; the cooperative, around the agricultural season; or shared, the cooperative proposing and Verder recruiting to fit); who defined the volunteers' work (Verder's programme team, the cooperative, or jointly); who employed the coordinators (Verder or the cooperative); the unrestricted payment per volunteer (€214, €400, €600 or €740); how Verder was paid (the residual margin left from each fee, or a defined service fee for recruitment, screening, travel and insurance); and what else Verder offered (a seat on its advisory board, co-branding of the placement, a joint annual impact report, or nothing beyond the terms).

For the redesign, Construcción Comunitaria could keep volunteers on the building work, accept only volunteers with trade qualifications, have volunteers fund local masons to do the building, or close the programme and stop building; local masons cost €38 a day. Aula Abierta could keep rotating volunteer teachers, place conversation assistants under a local teacher, take certified teachers only on a 42-day minimum, or close. Placements could split into a short learning placement and a long skilled one; stay at 17 days, the length customers bought; rise to a 28-day minimum, lengthening every placement; or rise to a 42-day minimum, lengthening them further. Delivery could pass to Ixchel only, on the terms agreed with the cooperative; pass to every host organisation, leaving Verder recruitment and compliance; or stay with Verder's own teams in every programme. Page 14 could stay in the report where it was, be published on the website as a fee breakdown, or be published with a commitment to a minimum unrestricted share.

For the market, the website could keep its sentence or replace it with "Your fee funds the community's own priorities; your labour is the smaller part" or with "You will learn a great deal. The community's gain is your fee and the paid local work it buys, not your labour." The construction page could invite volunteers to help build a school or to fund a build by local masons and learn alongside it, or disappear with the programme. Asked about children, recruiters could say yes, in supervised settings; no, giving Verder's reasons; or that it depended on the placement. Prices could be raised on long skilled placements with the short placement priced low, held where they were to protect margin, or cut across the board to hold volume. Recruitment could turn to professionals and mid-career volunteers, stay with gap-year and summer students, or move to accredited university programmes only.

The board would read the outcome through five measures: projected net result against 2025's €288,000, volunteers a year, whether Ixchel Ruk'u'x remained a partner, whether Hogeschool Zuidrand still accredited the minor, and how many of the 61 jobs remained. It would also mark each step in points against a stated maximum, adding up to a score out of 100.

## Closing

That evening Hoekstra printed the five programmes on one sheet and drew four columns beside them: keep, redesign, hand over, close. The university's ninety days had started on Monday; the cooperative wanted an answer on the €740 before it set its 2027 harvest calendar.
