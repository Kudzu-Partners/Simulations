## Abstract

Fiduciaria Sabanor S.A. was, in the case universe, a forty-one-year-old Bogotá-headquartered Colombian fiduciary founded in 1985 and owned by a Colombian financial-group parent, operating in fixed-income, multi-asset and trust services, and managing COP 850B of fixed-income AUM at 2025 year-end for eleven institutional mandates — three Colombian private pension funds, four insurance companies, two corporate treasuries, one multilateral-agency peso-denominated reserve mandate, and one family-office fixed-income sleeve. The 2025 portfolio duration read 5.8 years against a 5.2-year benchmark, convexity 68.2 against 61.4 benchmark, yield 7.1% against 6.9% benchmark, credit allocation 60% COLTES / 30% high-grade corporates / 10% high-yield, and a 2025 track record of +74bp of active return before fees and net –12bp after fees. On the **morning of Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 7:14 a.m., **Dr. Santiago Montoya-Pineda**, 42, Head of Fixed Income Portfolio Management at Fiduciaria Sabanor, opened the Round-1 positioning file his Investment Risk Committee had refreshed at 11:08 p.m. the previous evening and sat down to commit the four-round rates-and-credit package for the 8:30 a.m. IRC session.

Apply Merton's 1974 structural credit model and the Jarrow-Turnbull 1995 reduced-form intensity alternative to the Colombian high-grade and high-yield corporate universe, decompose the corporate spread over TES into expected loss, risk premium and liquidity premium, and articulate why the gap between market-implied PD and fundamental-assessment PD is the portfolio-manager's edge.

On the morning of **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 7:14 a.m., **Dr. Santiago Montoya-Pineda** arrived at Fiduciaria Sabanor's Calle 72 offices in Chapinero, greeted the two Fixed-Income senior analysts already at their Bloomberg terminals, and walked into the portfolio-management war room past the Banrep-TES-curve screen his team had left running overnight. The 5.8-year-duration positioning file his IRC had refreshed at 11:08 p.m. was open on his workstation. The April 17 Banrep communiqué — in which the central bank had signalled a hiking cycle and a peso-defence reaction function — was on the desk. The March 14 Veridane communiqué — placing Colombia on a negative outlook — was pinned to the corkboard.

---

## The Protagonist

Montoya-Pineda, 42, had been Head of Fixed Income Portfolio Management at Fiduciaria Sabanor since 2022, had completed a Matemáticas at the Universidad Estatal de Teusacá in 2006, a CFA by 2012 and a Doctorado in Finance at the Universidad Altavista in 2019, had served between 2010 and 2018 at a Colombian commercial bank's rates-trading desk and between 2018 and 2022 as Senior Portfolio Manager at Fiduciaria Sabanor, and had lived the 2021 Veridane downgrade and the 2022–2023 Banrep hiking cycle at the desk. His fixed-income instincts had been shaped by three formative exposures: the 2012 CFA Level III curriculum on Nelson-Siegel parametric curve fitting; the May 2021 downgrade day when his desk had lost COP 38B in eight hours against a parametric-VaR projection of COP 12B; and the 2023 Banrep pivot where he had held portfolio duration at 5.1 and had delivered +188bp of active return as the curve steepened.

---

## The Decision

Montoya-Pineda had to commit, by the 8:30 a.m. IRC session, the four-round rates-and-credit package.

1. **Round 1 — Duration and convexity.** Reduce portfolio duration from 5.8 to 5.1 years ahead of the Banrep hiking cycle. Preserve convexity through a modest barbell tilt.
2. **Round 2 — Curve positioning.** Move to a barbell expressing a curvature view — the Round-1 cycle predicts short-end sell-off, belly outperformance and long-end anchor on the terminal-rate repricing — plus a selective TES-26 underweight.
3. **Round 3 — Credit and VaR.** Reduce the high-yield allocation from 10% to 6%. Hold the high-grade corporate allocation at 30%. Acknowledge that neither VaR model captures the downgrade tail — supplement with regime-switching stress scenarios.
4. **Round 4 — Transmission and attribution.** Recognise the Mundell-Fleming Banrep 200bp defence. Close the quarter with the attribution transparency the pension trustees will ask for at the May 12 quarterly review.

Commitments: the Round-1 duration cut; the Round-2 barbell with TES-26 underweight; the Round-3 credit tilt and VaR supplementation; the Round-4 attribution transparency; and a one-page IRC memo that frames the four-round commitment as a single Q2 2026 rates-and-credit cycle commitment.

## Sectoral Background

The 1985–2025 Colombian fixed-income category had, in the case universe, registered approximately COP 312 trillion of outstanding TES, COP 184 trillion of high-grade corporate and COP 42 trillion of high-yield credit at 2025 year-end, with institutional fixed-income AUM in Colombia totalling approximately COP 94 trillion across the 14 fiduciarias and asset managers active in the segment. The 1985–2025 Colombian rates panel had documented that fixed-income-portfolio-manager active-return readings averaged 72–108 basis points before fees across the top-decile managers with dispersion driven approximately 62% by duration decisions, approximately 24% by curve decisions and approximately 14% by credit decisions, confirming the industry heuristic that fixed income is duration, curve, credit — in that order of size, every quarter.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1938–2025 fixed-income literature produced three findings the simulation had to respect. First, Macaulay's 1938 duration construct combined with Fisher-Weil 1971 convexity and Redington 1952 immunisation had produced documented portfolio-manager-outcome readings across approximately 2,400 institutional-portfolio panel years averaging duration as the dominant driver of quarterly active return, confirming that duration-cut versus duration-hold was the single highest-information choice before a hiking cycle. Second, Nelson-Siegel's 1987 parametric curve fitting and Svensson's 1994 four-factor extension had produced documented curve-positioning-outcome readings averaging +32bp of active return when the barbell-versus-bullet choice was informed by a stated curvature view against a stated benchmark. Third, the 2015–2025 LATAM sovereign-rating panel had documented that regime-change events — the 2015 Brazil downgrade, the 2018 Argentina crisis, the 2021 Colombia downgrade — produced realised losses averaging 2.4–3.1× the 99% parametric-VaR projection, confirming that VaR never tells the whole story and that regime-change events are a different distribution rather than a tail.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The 1985 Fiduciaria Sabanor founding; the 2010 Montoya-Pineda junior-desk entry at a commercial bank; the 2018 Fiduciaria Sabanor Senior Portfolio Manager appointment; the May 2021 Colombia Veridane downgrade and the COP 38B eight-hour loss; the 2022 Head of Fixed Income promotion; the 2022–2023 Banrep hiking cycle and the +188bp active return; the March 14 2026 Veridane negative-outlook placement; the April 17 Banrep hiking-cycle signal; and the April 23 Q2 positioning session were the sequence.

### What Montoya-Pineda knew

The 5.8-year portfolio duration against the 5.2 benchmark; the 68.2 convexity; the 60/30/10 credit allocation; the barbell-versus-bullet-versus-ladder trade-offs at the current TES-curve read; the Merton-Jarrow-Turnbull decomposition on the ten-name Colombian corporate universe; the parametric-VaR at COP 46B and historical-VaR at COP 58B; the regime-change read on the downgrade event; the IPS VaR ceiling at COP 94B; and the May 12 pension-trustee quarterly review calendar.

### What he did not know

Whether Veridane would move to a downgrade on the May 7 calendar day or would hold the negative outlook through Q2; whether the Banrep hiking cycle would front-load 200bp or would phase 75bp, 75bp, 50bp across three communiqués; whether the ten-name Colombian corporate universe would widen uniformly or bifurcate between investment-grade and sub-investment-grade credits on a downgrade event; whether the pension trustees would accept the attribution transparency as risk-management evidence or would read it as performance apology; and whether the Round-3 credit cut from 10% to 6% HY would preserve the active-return ceiling if the cycle resolved with a no-downgrade reprieve.

### What others were saying

**The CIO (54, former Minhacienda Deputy Minister for Public Credit)**: *"Santiago, si recortás duration a 5,1 antes del ciclo, la data dice que ganás. Si mantenés en 5,8 y hay downgrade, la data dice que perdés el doble de lo que el VaR te dice. No hay un tercer camino."*

**The Head of Credit Research (38, former Brantwell Ratings LATAM analyst)**: *"El IG colombiano resiste el downgrade. El HY no. Si recortás, recortá HY."*

**The Head of Rates Trading (45, former Banco Republica traders desk)**: *"El barbell con TES-26 underweight es la expresión correcta de curvatura. La belly sobre-performa en el hiking. Lo vi en 2022."*

**The Investment Risk Committee Chair (61, independent board member)**: *"El VaR no me tranquiliza. Quiero el stress de cambio de régimen. Quiero ver qué pasa si el escenario de mayo 2021 se repite."*

**The pension-trustee representative (58, president of the largest mandate)**: *"Santiago, no quiero sorpresa en el comité del 12 de mayo. Quiero la decisión antes. Y quiero la atribución transparente."*

**The eleven institutional mandates (aggregate signal)**: *"Track-record. Disciplina. Cero sorpresas."*

## Options Under Consideration

Montoya-Pineda had narrowed the fixed-income decision space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — Duration 5.8 → 5.1 + barbell with TES-26 underweight + HY 10% → 6% + regime-switching VaR supplementation + attribution transparency at May 12 (Recommended).** Respects the Macaulay / Nelson-Siegel-Svensson / Merton-Jarrow-Turnbull / Mundell-Fleming canon; cuts duration ahead of the hiking cycle; expresses the curvature view the 2022 precedent confirmed; cuts HY before a potential downgrade; supplements VaR with regime-switching stress; provides attribution transparency the trustees have asked for. Projected Q2 2026 active return –42bp under downgrade / +38bp under no-downgrade against benchmark.
- **Option B — Duration hold at 5.8 + bullet at TES-30 + HY hold at 10% + parametric VaR only.** Rejected — replicates the May 2021 exposure pattern; surrenders the 2.8× tail-under-estimation finding; violates the trustee request for attribution transparency.
- **Option C — Duration 5.8 → 3.9 + ladder + HY 10% → 2% + full de-risking.** Defensible on capital-preservation grounds but surrenders the active-return mandate, crystallises 22bp of transaction costs the IPS would scrutinise, and mis-reads the probability of the downgrade scenario as near-certain rather than 35–45%.

Montoya-Pineda's private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the single decision most correlated with the Q2 2026 outcome was the Round-1 duration cut, because the 1985–2025 Colombian rates-panel decomposition had shown that duration dominated quarterly active return at 62% and because his own 2023 Banrep-cycle track record had been exactly the duration-discipline-at-the-top pattern. The *Mercado de Bonos* frame sits at the LATAM institutional fixed-income portfolio-management tier in the 2026 Express catalogue, distinct from FenmarrowCapital DerivEdge (sim 202, derivatives-trading frame) and TesseraFixedIncome DurationLab (sim 226, duration-laboratory instructional frame) — 324 emphasises the duration / curve / credit / VaR synthesis inside a COP-mandate portfolio-management commitment.
