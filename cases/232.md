## Abstract

Continuum Pricing Desk was, in the case universe, the derivatives-pricing group inside the markets division of a Zurich-headquartered universal bank, with a fifteen-person quant team, a USD 1.8B American-option book notional, 2025 desk revenue of USD 84M, and a pricing-methods mandate across the American-option life-cycle from model selection to production engine to validation overlay. The 2024–2025 cycle had left the desk at a method-pluralistic but under-reconciled posture: the single-stock American-put book had been priced on a binomial-tree engine calibrated at N=200 that the Q1 2026 model-validation walk-through had flagged as exhibiting the classical order-1/N convergence oscillation between even and odd N, with average absolute residuals of 1.2–1.8% against a higher-resolution reference across the at-the-money strike window. The Q2 2026 context had compounded three pressures: a model-validation committee review scheduled for May 19 that had demanded a three-engine convergence protocol on the flagship American-put book; a multi-asset early-exercise product in the new-business pipeline that required a Longstaff-Schwartz-grade high-dimensional fallback; and a senior-quant retention risk flagged by the Human-Resources business partner that had correlated with method-monoculture on the desk. The case opens at 7:14 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Continuum Pricing Desk Zurich quant-research floor as **Sofía Borges-Kovalenko**, a 41-year-old Head of Pricing Methods, prepares the Method-1 binomial-tree commitment.

## The Protagonist

Borges-Kovalenko, 41, had assumed the Head-of-Pricing-Methods role at Continuum on June 1, 2023, after seventeen years across quantitative-research, model-validation and pricing-methods leadership at a London bulge-bracket, a Chicago proprietary-trading firm and a Frankfurt specialist. She had, in 2019–2022, led the American-option pricing rebuild at a predecessor firm that had migrated the single-stock American-put book from a pure-binomial-tree engine to a PSOR-finite-difference grid with Longstaff-Schwartz fallback and had compressed the cross-engine residual spread from 2.4% to 0.6% across twelve months of production. She had, in 2021, delivered a model-validation-committee workshop titled *Reading the Early-Exercise Boundary* that the predecessor firm had subsequently adopted as required orientation for incoming quants. The Continuum management committee had recruited her on three capabilities: her documented discipline of refusing to trust a single-engine price on any early-exercise book; her willingness to treat the tree as a sanity check rather than a production engine; and her command of the Longstaff-Schwartz regression pipeline that multi-asset early-exercise products ultimately required.

## The Decision

Borges-Kovalenko had to commit, by the 9:00 a.m. model-validation pre-review session, the three-method numerical-methods envelope.

1. **Method 1 — Binomial Tree.** A single envelope: run N from 50 to 500 with Richardson-extrapolation overlay to smooth the even/odd-N oscillation; accept the tree as the sanity check, not the production engine. Working envelope: accept as sanity check.
2. **Method 2 — Finite-Difference Grid with PSOR.** A single envelope: S-axis 0 to 2K at 200 grid points, t-axis 0 to T at 252 daily steps, implicit time-stepping for stability, PSOR inner loop for the early-exercise constraint. Working envelope: accept as production engine.
3. **Method 3 — Longstaff-Schwartz Regression Monte Carlo.** A single envelope: 10,000 paths, polynomial regression of discounted cashflows on spot at every exercise date, continuation-value-vs-payoff max at each step. Working envelope: accept as high-dimensional fallback.

On Borges-Kovalenko's working model, a Tree-as-Sanity / Grid-as-Production / Longstaff-Schwartz-as-Fallback envelope delivered a reconciled American-put price at a cross-engine residual spread under 0.6% and a documented early-exercise-boundary curve on the grid that matched the tree's implicit boundary to within two grid points. A counterfactual tree-only envelope would leave the book exposed to the order-1/N oscillation; a counterfactual grid-only envelope would fail the dimensional-curse test on the multi-asset pipeline.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2005–2025 American-option pricing literature had, across 52 documented three-engine reconciliation cycles on USD 0.8B–USD 4B American-option books, produced three findings the desk had to respect. First, PSOR-grid production engines had, on single-asset American-put books, compressed cross-engine residual spreads from 1.8–2.6% to 0.5–0.9% in 76% of documented cases, against tree-only or explicit-finite-difference alternatives. Second, Longstaff-Schwartz regression Monte Carlo had become the practitioner default on three-plus-asset early-exercise products because the dimensional curse rendered tensor-product grids computationally infeasible above three state variables. Third, three-engine convergence protocols (tree as sanity, grid as production, Longstaff-Schwartz as high-dimensional fallback) had, on external-audit reviews, reduced audit-finding counts by 45% on average versus single-engine frameworks.

Empirically, the 2020–2025 early-exercise-boundary literature had two further findings. First, the boundary curve on at-the-money American puts had, across 240 calibrated product-grade grids, exhibited a stable concave-increasing shape from the money deep-out-of-the-money toward the strike, invariant to grid resolution above 150 S-axis points. Second, Richardson-extrapolation overlays on binomial trees had cut the order-1/N oscillation residual by a factor of three to five in 84% of documented applications, which was why the tree remained a viable sanity check despite its convergence properties.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Q1 2026 model-validation walk-through had flagged the tree-only production stance as exposed to the order-1/N oscillation. The Q2 2026 multi-asset early-exercise product pipeline had surfaced two candidate structures that required a Longstaff-Schwartz-grade fallback. The HR business partner had raised the method-monoculture concern in a Q1 pulse.

### What Borges-Kovalenko knew

The tree converges at order 1/N and oscillates between even and odd N; Richardson extrapolation fixes it; PSOR handles both stability and early exercise on the grid; Longstaff-Schwartz regresses continuation value on polynomial basis functions of spot; the dimensional curse makes grids infeasible above three state variables; three-engine reconciliation is the model-validation committee's default ask.

### What she did not know

Whether the PSOR grid would converge stably on the 2025 single-stock volatility regime or require an adaptive grid near the early-exercise boundary; whether the Longstaff-Schwartz basis-function choice would matter materially on the two-asset pipeline; whether the model-validation committee would accept a three-engine protocol over a four-engine ask; and whether the multi-asset pipeline would launch in Q3 or slip.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Tree Methods**: *"Richardson extrapolation. Average two adjacent N. The tree is our sanity check, not our cross."*

**The Head of Grid Methods**: *"PSOR is the production engine. 200 S-points. 252 t-steps. Implicit for stability. Early exercise snaps to the payoff."*

**The Head of Regression-MC Methods**: *"Longstaff-Schwartz on multi-asset. The grid dies at three state variables. Regression does not."*

**The Head of Model Validation**: *"Three engines. One price. Write down the spread."*

## Options Under Consideration

Borges-Kovalenko had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Tree-as-Sanity / Grid-as-Production / Longstaff-Schwartz-as-Fallback:** the recommended envelope. Cross-engine spread compresses from 1.2–1.8% to 0.5–0.7%. Multi-asset pipeline supportable. Method-monoculture risk resolved. Recommended.
- **Option B — Tree-only production:** the status-quo envelope. Rejected — order-1/N oscillation, multi-asset pipeline unsupportable.
- **Option C — Grid-only production:** the single-engine-simplification envelope. Rejected — dimensional curse on the multi-asset pipeline.

Her private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the method most correlated with the multi-asset expansion trajectory was Method 3 — the Longstaff-Schwartz regression — because it was the method that decided whether Continuum committed to a three-engine posture that scaled into multi-asset early-exercise territory (Option A), stayed trapped in a single-asset tree-only paradigm (Option B) or bet the book on a single-grid engine that would fail the dimensional test.
