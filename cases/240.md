## Abstract

Halstead & Co LLP was, in the case universe, a Chicago-headquartered mid-market audit and advisory firm with a fourteen-partner practice, USD 72M 2025 audit revenue, and a 2024 client acceptance of Precision Manufacturing Co., a mid-sized family-owned manufacturer with USD 340M 2025 revenue, a nine-month-tenure new Chief Financial Officer and a recent inorganic growth transaction that had added a 2024 acquired product line to the revenue base. The 2024–2025 cycle had left the engagement team at a diligent-but-exposed posture: the client-acceptance memorandum had noted three elevated-inherent-risk indicators (22% revenue growth against 38% receivables growth, new CFO tenure under twelve months, 2024 M&A integration without a documented post-close control walk-through) that had each independently passed the acceptance threshold but collectively had raised the engagement risk rating to "elevated" in the February 2026 engagement-risk refresh. The Q2 2026 context had compounded three pressures: a May 12 client-requested audit-opinion delivery deadline pegged to a refinancing discussion; a Halstead firm-risk-management review that had demanded a fully-documented four-phase audit-risk assessment on every elevated-risk first-year engagement; and a practice-wide tone-at-the-top survey protocol that had been formalised in Q1 2026 as a mandatory Phase-3 substantive procedure. The case opens at 7:02 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Halstead & Co Chicago audit-engagement floor as **Marcus Delaney-Okoro**, a 52-year-old Audit Engagement Partner, prepares the Phase-1 planning commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Phase-1 planning architecture (industry research, revenue-up-22% / receivables-up-38% growth divergence flag, nine-month-tenure CFO flag, 2024 M&A integration flag) and articulate why the revenue-receivables gap is the first signal that growth is not sitting in cash.
2. Analyse the Phase-2 controls testing result (broken segregation of duties on cash disbursements — same individual authorises and releases payment — rendering the control ineffective and expanding the substantive-testing scope) and articulate why a single broken segregation migrates the audit from control-reliance to substantive-coverage mode.
3. Diagnose the Phase-3 substantive procedures (three of twenty receivables confirmations with discrepancies, a five-percent physical-inventory shortfall versus the ledger, a tone-at-the-top survey failure with management-override citations) and articulate how the three independent evidence streams compound into control-risk-at-maximum.
4. Respond to the Phase-4 opinion-formation choice (Unqualified, Qualified with "except-for" paragraph, Adverse, or Disclaimer) and the Qualified commitment on receivables and inventory with a quality-panel score at 78/100 and five-of-eight documented red flags.
5. Design the four-phase audit-risk-assessment archetype that couples a planning-flag discipline, a controls-testing verdict, a substantive-procedures evidence base and an opinion-formation commitment — anchored on the principle that an audit is not a math check but a controlled opinion built on evidence.

On the morning of **Wednesday, April 22, 2026**, **Marcus Delaney-Okoro** arrived at the Halstead & Co Chicago audit-engagement floor at 7:02 a.m., opened the four-phase audit-risk-assessment workbook his Engagement Senior Manager, his Engagement Manager and his Firm-Risk Advisor had finalised at 11:02 p.m. the night before, and re-read the sentence the firm's Managing Partner had pinned to the engagement-room wall: *"An audit is not a math check. It is a controlled opinion, built on evidence."*

---

## The Protagonist

Delaney-Okoro, 52, had been an Audit Engagement Partner at Halstead for eleven years, had been promoted from Senior Manager in 2015 after a decade on the Halstead mid-market manufacturing bench, and had run thirty-eight first-year audit engagements across manufacturing, distribution and industrial-services clients. He had, in 2022, led the engagement that had produced the firm's most-cited qualified opinion of the decade on a USD 210M family-owned industrial distributor that had subsequently restated three prior years and that the firm had held up as the reference engagement for tone-at-the-top-survey integration. He had, in 2023, co-authored with the firm's Risk Management Partner an internal memorandum titled *Four Phases, One Opinion* that had subsequently been adopted as Halstead's required engagement-risk framework on all elevated-risk first-year audits. The Halstead Risk Management Committee had assigned him to the Precision Manufacturing Co. engagement on three considerations: his documented discipline of expanding substantive scope immediately on any broken-segregation control finding; his willingness to commit to a qualified opinion when evidence supported it, rather than defer to a third-period audit; and his command of the four-phase framework that the firm's quality bench expected on elevated-risk engagements.

---

## The Decision

Delaney-Okoro had to commit, by the 5:00 p.m. engagement-team close-out meeting, the four-phase audit-opinion envelope.

1. **Phase 1 — Planning.** A single envelope: industry research, 22% revenue / 38% receivables growth divergence flagged, nine-month-tenure CFO flagged, 2024 M&A integration flagged. Working envelope: accept as three planning flags, elevate engagement-risk rating.
2. **Phase 2 — Controls testing.** A single envelope: test segregation on cash disbursements; observe same individual authorising and releasing; declare control ineffective; expand substantive scope. Working envelope: accept.
3. **Phase 3 — Substantive procedures.** A single envelope: twenty receivables confirmations with three discrepancies; physical inventory with 5% shortfall; tone-at-the-top survey with management-override citations. Working envelope: accept as three substantive flags, control risk at maximum.
4. **Phase 4 — Opinion formation.** Four options. Unqualified (clean). Qualified ("except-for" paragraph). Adverse (materially misstated). Disclaimer (unable to conclude). Working envelope: Qualified, with receivables and inventory in the "except-for" paragraph.

On Delaney-Okoro's working model, a Three-Flag-Planning / Broken-Segregation / Three-Substantive-Flag / Qualified-Opinion envelope delivered an opinion the engagement-quality bench could sign off on, a client-communication pack Precision Manufacturing's audit committee could review, and a firm-risk-management paper-trail that documented the evidence-to-opinion path at a quality-panel score of 78/100 and five-of-eight red flags. A counterfactual Unqualified envelope would contradict the evidence base; a counterfactual Adverse envelope would exceed the evidence base; a counterfactual Disclaimer envelope would under-state the evidence-gathered-and-tested posture the engagement had actually achieved.

## Company Background

Halstead & Co LLP had been established in 1994 as a Chicago-headquartered mid-market audit and advisory firm, had grown the partner practice from six partners in 2005 to fourteen by 2025, had built the Audit Practice across manufacturing, distribution and industrial-services verticals, and had, across thirty-two years, delivered approximately 2,400 audit opinions with a firm-level qualified-opinion frequency of approximately 4% in line with the mid-market audit-firm average. The 2023–2025 cycle had produced three named milestones: the 2023 adoption of the four-phase engagement-risk framework; the 2024 formalisation of the tone-at-the-top survey protocol; and the Q4 2025 audit-revenue print of USD 72M. The Q2 2026 context had compounded three pressures: the May 12 opinion-delivery deadline, the firm-risk-management review on elevated-risk engagements, and the Q1 2026 tone-at-the-top survey protocol formalisation.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2005–2025 audit-risk-assessment literature had, across 120 documented elevated-risk first-year engagements at mid-market manufacturing clients, produced three findings the engagement had to respect. First, revenue-receivables growth divergences above fifteen percentage points had, in 78% of documented cases, correlated with at least one material substantive finding in the same audit cycle. Second, broken segregation on cash disbursements had, on external-peer-review samples, been the single most-cited control finding between 2020 and 2025, with a firm-wide frequency of approximately 14% on first-year mid-market engagements. Third, tone-at-the-top survey failures had, in 68% of documented engagements, co-occurred with at least one control-override substantive finding.

Empirically, the 2020–2025 opinion-frequency literature had two further findings. First, qualified opinions had, on mid-market manufacturing first-year engagements, arrived in 9–14% of documented cases against the firm-level qualified-opinion frequency of approximately 4%. Second, the receivables-and-inventory "except-for" paragraph combination had, in 74% of documented qualified opinions at first-year engagements, been the canonical scope of the qualification.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The client-acceptance memorandum had documented three elevated-inherent-risk indicators. The February 2026 engagement-risk refresh had elevated the engagement-risk rating. The Q1 2026 tone-at-the-top survey protocol had been formalised. The May 12 opinion-delivery deadline had been pegged to a refinancing discussion.

### What Delaney-Okoro knew

Revenue-up-22% / receivables-up-38% is not cash-converted growth; nine-month CFO tenure is a control-environment risk signal; broken segregation on cash disbursements is a control-ineffective finding; three-of-twenty confirmation discrepancies at 15% failure rate requires expanded scope; five-percent physical-inventory shortfall is a material discrepancy; tone-at-the-top management-override citations migrate control risk to maximum; five material findings at control risk at maximum supports a qualified opinion with an "except-for" paragraph on receivables and inventory.

### What he did not know

Whether the Precision Manufacturing audit committee would dispute the qualified opinion and request a disclaimer-instead commitment; whether the refinancing bank would accept a qualified opinion or require an unqualified one; whether the tone-at-the-top survey signals would migrate in the post-close communication window into regulatory-attention territory; and whether the 2024 M&A-integrated product line would require a separate scope-limitation paragraph.

### What others were saying

**The Engagement Senior Manager**: *"Twenty-two on revenue. Thirty-eight on receivables. That is the first flag. The rest follows."*

**The Engagement Manager**: *"Same person authorises and releases cash. Control ineffective. Substantive scope doubles. The math is automatic."*

**The Firm-Risk Advisor**: *"Tone-at-the-top survey failure plus broken segregation plus three-in-twenty confirmations plus five-percent inventory short is not a close call. Qualified, with receivables and inventory named in the except-for paragraph."*

**The Halstead Managing Partner**: *"An audit is not a math check. It is a controlled opinion, built on evidence."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Delaney-Okoro had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Qualified opinion with "except-for" paragraph covering receivables and inventory:** the recommended envelope. Evidence-to-opinion path documentable. Firm-risk-management paper-trail complete. Recommended.
- **Option B — Unqualified opinion with emphasis-of-matter paragraph:** the client-management-preferred envelope. Rejected — evidence base supports a scope-of-qualification, not a paragraph-of-emphasis.
- **Option C — Disclaimer of opinion:** the evidence-insufficient envelope. Rejected — evidence was gathered and tested; the engagement has a basis to conclude.

His private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the phase most correlated with the eventual opinion trajectory was Phase 3 — the substantive-procedures evidence base — because it was the phase that decided whether Halstead committed to a qualified opinion grounded in three independent evidence streams (Option A), retreated into an emphasis-of-matter posture that would under-state the substantive findings (Option B) or escaped into a disclaimer posture that would under-state the engagement's actual evidence-gathering (Option C).
