## Abstract

Solera Regional Bank N.A. was, in the case universe, a Charlotte-headquartered US regional bank with USD 48B in total assets, a 2025 Liquidity Coverage Ratio of 118%, a 2025 Net Stable Funding Ratio of 107%, HQLA (High-Quality Liquid Assets) of USD 6.2B, retail deposits representing 62% of funding, wholesale funding of 28% and equity-plus-subordinated of 10%, a 2025 investment portfolio of USD 11.4B in investment-grade corporates and MBS, a 2025 net interest margin of 3.28%, 6,200 employees across twelve states and a 2023–2025 track record of maintaining the LCR above the 100% Basel III floor across every quarterly reporting window. The 2024–2025 cycle had left the bank at a Basel-compliant-but-deposit-concentrated posture: the retail-deposit base had grown from 58% to 62% of funding across twenty-four months, the wholesale-funding mix had shifted from term-funding to increasingly uncommitted lines, and the investment portfolio had tilted from Treasuries towards higher-yield investment-grade corporates with lower HQLA eligibility. The Q2 2026 context had compounded three pressures: a social-media-driven deposit-run rehearsal at two peer banks in January and March that had telegraphed the Twitter-to-ATM-queue transmission chain, a wholesale-funding-market stress reading that had elevated commercial-paper spreads by 45 bps across April, and a CRA watchlist placement that had flagged a rating-downgrade probability of 0.22 if wholesale pressures persisted through Q2 close. The case opens at 6:32 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Solera Regional Bank Charlotte risk-operations centre as **Aisha Peregrin-Montoya**, a 52-year-old Chief Risk Officer, prepares the Phase-1 opening-snapshot commitment.

## The Protagonist

Peregrin-Montoya, 52, had assumed the Chief-Risk-Officer role at Solera on August 1, 2022, after twenty-four years across ALM, treasury and risk-management leadership at two US regional banks and one US global bank. She had, in 2019–2022, led the liquidity-stress response at a predecessor bank during the March 2020 pandemic liquidity window by drawing on FHLB and the Fed Discount Window within the first forty-eight hours of the wholesale-market stress, and had maintained LCR above the 100% floor across the full quarterly window. She had, in 2021, co-authored the bank's ALCO run-book that explicitly rejected the facility-stigma hypothesis on the basis of 2008–2020 usage data. The Solera board had recruited her on three capabilities: her documented discipline of reading deposit-run signals on two-to-four-day horizons rather than waiting for reporting-day breaches; her willingness to tap FHLB and the Fed Discount Window before the private market signalled distress; and her command of collateral-haircut dynamics that had, at the predecessor bank, surfaced the hedge-lost-value-while-being-held-as-hedge exposure on two named windows.

## The Decision

Peregrin-Montoya had to commit, by the 8:00 a.m. ALCO session, the Phase-1 opening-snapshot envelope and the directional intent for Phases 2–8.

1. **Phase 1 — Opening snapshot.** A single envelope: accept Assets USD 48B, LCR 118%, NSFR 107%, HQLA USD 6.2B, retail deposits 62%. Working envelope: accept.
2. **Phase 2 — Deposit run.** A single envelope: absorb the 9% retail outflow (USD 2.7B gross, USD 2.1B after HQLA adjustments) in three days. LCR drops to 97%. Working envelope: accept.
3. **Phase 3 — Wholesale freeze.** A single envelope: diagnose the commercial-paper market closure and the USD 4B of uncommitted-line freezes as a compounded shock, not a sequential one. Working envelope: compounded-shock.
4. **Phase 4 — Funding decision.** Three archetypes. A FHLB-plus-Discount-Window envelope (tap FHLB at Treasury+80 for USD 2.4B and the Fed Discount Window at Treasury+25 for USD 1.2B; facility stigma is a myth). A private-markets envelope (widen bids on wholesale; pay up to clear; preserve optics). A fire-sale envelope (sell USD 3B of investment-grade corporates at a 3% haircut; realise USD 90M of losses). Working envelope: FHLB-plus-Discount-Window.
5. **Phase 5 — Collateral shock.** A single envelope: absorb the 150-bp collateral-haircut widening; effective borrowing capacity drops by USD 1.4B. Working envelope: accept.
6. **Phase 7 — Rating downgrade.** A single envelope: absorb the one-notch downgrade and the USD 800M of derivative-collateral calls triggered overnight. Working envelope: accept.
7. **Phase 8 — Outcome dashboard.** Target envelope: LCR 104%, NSFR 101%, HQLA USD 3.1B, survived 30-day horizon, Phase Score 68.

On Peregrin-Montoya's working model, an Accept / Accept / Compounded / FHLB-plus-Discount-Window / Accept / Accept / LCR-104 envelope delivered the Phase-8 dashboard above and a Composite liquidity-is-reflex rating.

## Company Background

Solera Regional Bank had been chartered in 1908 in Charlotte as a state-chartered commercial bank, had converted to a national banking charter in 1972, had grown through a succession of in-market acquisitions through the 1990s and 2000s, had exited the sub-prime mortgage market in 2006 ahead of the crisis, and had, across a hundred-and-eighteen years, built a USD 48B regional bank organised around three business lines (Retail Banking, Commercial Banking, Wealth Management) with a shared Treasury, Risk and Technology backbone. The 2023–2025 cycle had produced three named franchise milestones: the 2023 relaunch of the digital-retail platform that had grown deposits by USD 4.8B across twenty months; the 2024 Basel III compliance implementation; and the Q4 2025 LCR print of 118% and NSFR of 107%. The Q2 2026 context had compounded three pressures: the peer-bank deposit-run rehearsals, the commercial-paper spread widening, and the CRA watchlist placement.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2019–2025 US-regional-bank liquidity literature had, across 34 documented eight-phase liquidity-stress cycles at USD 20B–USD 80B asset envelopes, produced three findings the response had to respect. First, deposit-run windows that compounded with wholesale-market freezes inside forty-eight hours had, on retail-concentrated banks with 55%-plus deposit funding shares, consumed 24–42% of HQLA in the first seven days in 68% of documented cases. Second, banks that tapped the FHLB and the Fed Discount Window inside the first seventy-two hours had, relative to banks that delayed facility usage, preserved LCR above the 100% floor at the thirty-day horizon in 76% of documented cases versus 42% for delayed-tap banks. Third, rating downgrades inside liquidity-stress windows had, on average, triggered derivative-collateral calls of 1.4–2.2% of total derivative notional outstanding, with the cash demand concentrated in the first overnight window post-downgrade.

Empirically, the 2020–2025 facility-stigma-hypothesis literature had two further findings. First, Fed Discount Window usage between March 2020 and December 2023 did not exhibit a statistically significant correlation with subsequent equity price or CDS spread movement. Second, banks that explicitly repudiated the stigma hypothesis in their run-books had, in subsequent stress windows, demonstrated faster mean-time-to-facility-tap (38 hours vs. 74 hours) and superior LCR outcomes.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Q4 2025 ALCO session had stress-tested a 6% retail-deposit run scenario and determined that LCR would dip to 106% on the base-case shock. The January 2026 peer-bank rehearsal at a larger regional had shown a 14% run over forty-eight hours. The April 2026 commercial-paper market had begun to print wider spreads across April 18–22. The April 21 evening had seen a social-media-driven rumour about Solera's commercial-real-estate exposure, and overnight deposit outflows had begun before the Charlotte branches had opened on April 22.

### What Peregrin-Montoya knew

The deposit-run-rate was tracking to the 9% three-day envelope; the commercial-paper market was closed for refinancing; the FHLB capacity at Treasury+80 was USD 3.6B; the Fed Discount Window capacity was uncapped against eligible collateral; and the CRA watchlist had flagged a rating-downgrade probability of 0.22 that had risen to 0.46 after the April 21 rumour.

### What she did not know

Whether the deposit-run would extend to 14–18% in days four-to-seven or stabilise at the 9% three-day print; whether the wholesale-freeze would clear at month-end or extend into May; whether the rating downgrade would land at one notch or two; and whether the derivative-collateral calls would stop at USD 800M on the one-notch downgrade or rise to USD 1.4B on a two-notch move.

### What others were saying

**The Treasurer**: *"FHLB plus discount window. Stigma is a myth repeated by people who didn't need the window."*

**The Head of ALM**: *"The run-book was built for one shock. This is two."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"LCR one-oh-four. NSFR one-oh-one. HQLA three-one. Ugly. Alive."*

**The Chief Executive Officer**: *"Liquidity isn't a ratio. It's a reflex."*

## Options Under Consideration

Peregrin-Montoya had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Accept / Accept / Compounded / FHLB+DW / Accept / Accept / LCR-104-Score-68:** the recommended envelope. LCR 104%, NSFR 101%, HQLA USD 3.1B, survived 30-day horizon, Score 68. Recommended.
- **Option B — Accept / Accept / Compounded / Private-Markets / Accept / Accept / LCR-92-Score-42:** the optics-first envelope. LCR 92% (breach), NSFR 97%, Score 42. Rejected — Basel-floor breach and optics-trade at the worst possible moment.
- **Option C — Accept / Accept / Compounded / Fire-Sale / Accept / Accept / LCR-108-Score-58:** the realise-losses envelope. LCR 108%, NSFR 103%, realised P&L -USD 90M, Score 58. Rejected — realised losses, permanent capital impairment, and investment-book re-underwriting.

Her private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the phase most correlated with the liquidity-is-reflex posture was Phase 4 — the funding decision — because it was the phase that decided whether Solera tapped the facilities in time to preserve LCR (Option A) or defended optics at the cost of the Basel-floor breach (Option B) or realised permanent losses for a marginal ratio lift (Option C).
