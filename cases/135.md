## Abstract

NEXABANK was, in the case universe, a Madrid-headquartered mid-tier European digital bank with EUR 1.8B in 2025 revenue, a 2.4-million-customer retail franchise and a 2024-launched small-and-medium-enterprise (SME) business line that had, across Q1–Q3 2025, committed an EUR 18M build envelope to a B2B Expense Management Platform covering corporate-card issuance, receipt-capture, expense-approval workflows and general-ledger integration. The platform had, at three successive go-live commitments across Q2–Q4 2025, slipped to a Q1 2026 release anchor after two failed sprint-review sign-offs. The Q4 2025 Engineering Leadership Committee had approved a four-sprint close-out cycle against a Scrum team with a trailing four-sprint average velocity of 28 story points, an accumulated tech-debt register of 112 story points, a Product Backlog of 196 points across features / defects / tech debt / discovery, and a release-readiness target against a Delivered Scope above 75%, a Defect Count below 8, a sustained Velocity above 25, and a composite Delivery Score above 70. The case opens at 6:28 a.m. on Tuesday, March 24, 2026, at the NexaBank Madrid engineering floor as **Anika Velasco**, a 37-year-old Engineering Manager, prepares the Sprint-1 commitment. Fifty-six days separate her from the May 19 release-readiness review.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Sprint-1 commitment discipline (committing 28 points against a 28-velocity team versus committing 34 points under stakeholder pressure) and diagnose when commitment discipline dominates scope over-commitment on the Defect-Count / Sustained-Velocity trade-off across four sprints.
2. Analyze the Sprint-2 tech-debt-versus-features decision (allocating 20% of capacity to tech debt against shipping more features) and recommend the 20%-tech-debt-paydown envelope against the features-only envelope on the Sprint-3 Defect-Count trajectory.
3. Respond to the URGENT Sprint-3 defect-spike event (defect count triples from 3 to 11 and velocity drops to 22) and recommend the defect-triage-and-stabilisation envelope against the push-through-scope envelope on the Sprint-4 release-readiness trajectory.
4. Calibrate the stakeholder-engineering commitment (Product Owner pressure, stakeholder scope-creep, team burn-out management) and recommend the transparency-and-velocity-signal protocol against the heroics-and-overtime protocol.
5. Design a four-sprint Scrum-delivery sequence that closes Delivered Scope above 75%, Defect Count below 8, Velocity above 25, and Composite above 70 under a Sprint-3 defect-spike event and sustained Product-Owner scope pressure.

## The Protagonist

Anika Velasco, 37, had joined NexaBank in November 2022 as Engineering Manager on the B2B platform from a European payments unicorn where she had, across six years, run four product-delivery cycles on card-issuance, KYC-onboarding and ledger-integration workstreams, and had led the 2021–2022 platform re-architecture that had closed a tech-debt register of 280 points inside nine months. Before that, she had spent five years as a senior software engineer at a Barcelona neobank. The Chief Product and Technology Officer had recruited her specifically on the strength of two capabilities: her experience running Scrum teams that survived mid-cycle defect spikes without over-committing the downstream sprints, and her reputation for refusing to sign a release-readiness review that had not cleared a documented defect-count floor. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 35% to the release-readiness outcome and 15% to the sustained-velocity floor.

## The Decision

Velasco had to commit, by the March 31 Engineering-Leadership pre-review, the Sprint-1 commitment and the directional intent for Sprints 2–4.

1. **Sprint 1 — Commitment discipline.** Three archetypes: commitment-discipline (commit 28 points against a 28-velocity team, Delivered-Scope 100% of committed, Defect-Count 2), scope-over-commitment (commit 34 points under Product-Owner pressure, Delivered-Scope 82%, carry-over 6 points, Defect-Count 5, team-burnout signal), or conservative-commit (commit 22 points, Delivered-Scope 100% but stakeholder-satisfaction red). Working envelope: commitment-discipline at 28 points.
2. **Sprint 2 — Tech-debt-versus-features.** Three vectors: 20%-tech-debt-paydown (commit 22 feature points + 6 tech-debt points, Sprint-3 defect-count forecast 4, sustained Velocity 26), features-only (commit 28 feature points, Sprint-3 defect-count forecast 11, Velocity falls to 22), or 40%-tech-debt-paydown (commit 17 feature points + 11 tech-debt points, Delivered-Scope concern). Working envelope: 20%-tech-debt-paydown.
3. **Sprint 3 — URGENT defect-spike event.** Event: production defects climb from 3 to 11 mid-sprint under a Sprint-2 features-only path; Velocity drops from 28 to 22 under defect-swarm context-switching. Three vectors: defect-triage-and-stabilisation (pause new feature work, run a three-day swarm, Velocity recovers to 25 for Sprint 4, Delivered-Scope 68%), push-through-scope (continue feature work at degraded velocity, defect-count climbs to 18 by Sprint-4 start), or partial-swarm-plus-reduced-feature-work (Velocity 24, Defect-Count 9, hybrid outcome). Working envelope: defect-triage-and-stabilisation — triggered only if the Sprint-2 features-only path is chosen.
4. **Sprint 4 — Release-readiness.** Three vectors: release-readiness-floor-plus-feature-freeze (Delivered-Scope 78%, Defect-Count 5, release-ready), feature-rush (Delivered-Scope 88%, Defect-Count 14, release-readiness red), or phased-release (Delivered-Scope 72% in Sprint 4 plus Sprint-5 tail, release-readiness amber). Working envelope: release-readiness-floor-plus-feature-freeze.

On Velasco's working model, a Commitment-Discipline / 20%-Debt-Paydown / No-Spike / Feature-Freeze envelope delivered Delivered Scope 78%, Defect Count 4, Sustained Velocity 27, Composite 82 — release-ready trajectory. A Scope-Over-Commit / Features-Only / Spike-Absorbed / Feature-Rush envelope delivered Delivered Scope 65%, Defect Count 18, Velocity 22, Composite 42. A Commitment-Discipline / Features-Only / Spike-Triage / Feature-Freeze envelope delivered Delivered Scope 72%, Defect Count 9, Velocity 24, Composite 64 — trajectory that recovered but forfeited the sustained-velocity floor.

## Bank Background

NexaBank had been founded in Madrid in 2018 as a mobile-first digital bank and had, across eight years, grown into a mid-tier European retail-and-SME bank with EUR 1.8B in 2025 revenue, a 2.4-million-customer retail book and a 2024-launched SME line. The B2B Expense Management Platform had been approved by the November 2023 board with a Q3 2025 go-live anchor. Across 2024–2025, three sprint-review-driven delivery slippages had reset the go-live to Q1 2026: the first slippage had been driven by a Q2 2024 stakeholder-scope-creep that had added general-ledger-integration scope after Sprint 6; the second by a Q4 2024 tech-debt-plus-defect collapse inside Sprints 11–12; the third by a Q3 2025 Product-Owner turnover that had reset the backlog.

The Q4 2025 Engineering-Leadership dossier had identified three specific pressures the four-sprint envelope would inherit. First, the accumulated tech-debt register of 112 story points had placed any features-only sprint at elevated Sprint-3-defect-spike probability (78% on the 2023–2024 internal pattern). Second, the Q1 2026 SME-line go-to-market had committed the platform to a May 19 beta-customer cohort of fourteen SME corporates. Third, the Q3 2025 Product-Owner turnover had placed a first-time Product Owner on the platform, with a predictable scope-pressure pattern framing the Sprint-1 commitment-discipline decision.

## Industry and Market Context

The European digital-bank product-delivery landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2019–2024 Scrum-delivery evidence base had, across 128 mid-tier-bank platform builds, documented a Sprint-3 defect-spike rate differential of 42 percentage points between commitment-discipline baselines and scope-over-commitment baselines — a pattern that anchored the Sprint-1 commitment. Second, the 2020–2024 tech-debt literature had, across 74 European digital-bank platform builds, priced 20%-tech-debt-paydown at a six-month defect-count differential of 5.6–7.8 defects against features-only paths. Third, the 2018–2024 release-readiness evidence had, across 38 SME-platform go-lives, placed release-readiness-floor-plus-feature-freeze commitments at a customer-satisfaction-at-ninety-day differential of 18–22 points against feature-rush commitments.

Empirically, the post-2022 agile-delivery literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, Scrum teams that had committed at or below trailing-four-sprint-average velocity had, across a 2020–2024 sample of 58 European-digital-bank platform builds, shipped release-ready software inside the committed timeline in 83% of cases against 31% for over-committed teams. Second, teams that had pre-committed 20%-tech-debt-paydown across the final four sprints of a release cycle had, across the same sample, held the defect-count floor below 8 in 81% of cases against 26% for features-only teams.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Chief Product and Technology Officer had, in the December 2025 Engineering-Leadership committee, approved the four-sprint close-out cycle. The Product Owner had, on February 9, published the 196-point backlog re-prioritisation. The Tech Lead had, on February 16, delivered the 112-point tech-debt register. The QA Lead had, on February 23, published the Sprint-3-defect-spike forecast probability on the features-only path. The Chief Commercial Officer had, on March 2, confirmed the fourteen-SME beta-customer cohort. The Engineering-Leadership Committee had, on March 9, pre-committed the release-readiness-floor-plus-feature-freeze protocol on Sprint 4.

### What Velasco knew

The team had modelled three four-sprint envelopes. A Commitment-Discipline / 20%-Debt-Paydown / No-Spike / Feature-Freeze Envelope projected Composite 82. A Scope-Over-Commit / Features-Only / Spike-Absorbed / Feature-Rush Envelope projected Composite 42. A Commitment-Discipline / Features-Only / Spike-Triage / Feature-Freeze Envelope projected Composite 64.

### What she did not know

Whether the first-time Product Owner would hold the Sprint-1 28-point commitment against downstream stakeholder pressure; whether the general-ledger-integration tech-debt subset would surface a second-order defect in Sprint 3 outside the forecast register; whether the fourteen-SME beta-customer cohort would accept a phased-release under a feature-freeze posture; and whether the Chief Commercial Officer would tolerate a two-sprint delay on the three highest-demand SME-requested features.

### What others were saying

**The Product Owner**: *"I can hold 28 points. Stakeholders need to hear the sustained-velocity story."*

**The Tech Lead**: *"Twenty-percent tech-debt paydown. Without it, Sprint 3 will spike."*

**The QA Lead**: *"On a features-only path, Sprint-3 defect-spike probability is seventy-eight percent."*

**The Chief Commercial Officer**: *"Fourteen SMEs. May 19. Non-negotiable."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Velasco had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Commitment-Discipline / 20%-Tech-Debt-Paydown / No-Spike-Path / Feature-Freeze:** the recommended envelope. Delivered Scope 78%. Defect Count 4. Velocity 27. Composite 82. Recommended.
- **Option B — Scope-Over-Commit / Features-Only / Spike-Absorbed / Feature-Rush:** the pressure-response envelope. Composite 42. Rejected — trajectory ends with release-readiness red.
- **Option C — Commitment-Discipline / Features-Only / Spike-Triage / Feature-Freeze:** the debt-denial envelope. Composite 64. Rejected — recovers from Sprint-3 spike but forfeits sustained-velocity floor.

Velasco's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single sprint most correlated with the final Composite was Sprint 2 — the 20%-tech-debt-paydown commitment that, on the 2020–2024 evidence base, prevented the Sprint-3 defect-spike event that converted Sprint-4 into a feature-freeze-versus-feature-rush dilemma under commercial pressure.
