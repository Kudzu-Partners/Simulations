# Filo Studio: Scale or Stall

**Case Code:** ENT-2026-1929 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1929 — *Creative Pivot* · **v1.0 · August 2026**

---

On the morning of Monday, 9 March 2026, Giulia Ferrante read the week's figures twice before she accepted them. In seven days Filo Studio's web traffic had risen to nine times its normal level and a EUR 180,000 drop had sold out in forty-eight hours. Roughly EUR 320,000 of orders had gone unfilled because the machines could not knit any faster. Beside the figures lay a photograph from a high street eight blocks away: a global fast-fashion chain had put a near-copy of the signature knit in its window at one-sixth of Ferrante's price, three weeks after the celebrity photograph that started all of it.

In six weeks Ferrante would sit in front of the fund that had closed Filo Studio's EUR 1.4 million seed round and account for two things: a business model that did not depend on two founders personally designing every piece, and a credible plan for selling outside Italy. Without both, the second tranche of EUR 800,000 would be withheld — and eleven months of cash would become five.

## The Protagonist

Giulia Ferrante, 36, had co-founded Filo Studio in 2022 with Marco Sella, a fellow designer, and Nadia Bonetti, a textile engineer. Ferrante had run a knitwear line inside an established Milanese house for six years before leaving to build one that could prove where its wool came from. She took the chief executive's title and still drew: every collection had passed through her hands and Sella's. Hers was the largest single shareholding, and the tranche was priced against a valuation she had negotiated herself.

## The Decision

Ferrante had to arrive at the review on 20 April with one integrated plan. It had to say which of Filo Studio's assets was genuinely defensible against a copyist, what the business model would become, how the company would sell outside Italy for the first time, and where the ring-fenced EUR 600,000 would go. Rosa Vitale, the partner at the fund leading the round, had put her condition in writing. She had backed a creative company, not a factory. The EUR 600,000 was for knowledge and capability and could not buy inventory. She wanted to be told what would be measurably different about Filo Studio in eighteen months.

## The Company and its Setting

Filo Studio S.r.l. made limited-run, traceable knitwear from recycled Italian wool and sold it through its own website and a pop-up in the Brera design district. In its third year it turned over EUR 2.8 million: 70% direct to consumers, 20% wholesale through three concept stores, and 10% from paid capsule designs for larger brands (see **Exhibit 1**). Nineteen people worked there. Its audience of 140,000 followers had been built on the founders' own voices, and every euro of revenue had been earned in Italy. Production ran on contracted mill capacity booked a season ahead — an arrangement that kept the balance sheet light and had been invisible until the first week of March.

## The Situation

Vitale's letter had arrived on the Friday. Of the EUR 1.4 million closed, EUR 600,000 was ring-fenced as a knowledge-and-capability budget and EUR 800,000 held back against the reshaped model and the international plan.

Inside the company the argument had already formed. Sella wanted to license the signature to manufacturers and take royalties: fast money, and an end to the two of them being the bottleneck. Bonetti wanted two more machines and a larger yarn commitment: the studio had proved demand and had failed only to supply it. Since the photograph, Ferrante had taken three approaches: a Paris showroom, a Copenhagen concept-store buyer, and a Berlin studio offering to build the brand in Germany. She had never sold a garment outside Italy and knew none of the three well enough to price the risk. A route that did not work, she reckoned, would absorb six to nine months and a third of the round.

Vitale's review template carried four measures and a budget counter, and no revenue line (see **Exhibit 2**). Defensible Scalability stood at 18 out of 100. It read how much of the company's value came from assets that did not run through Ferrante and Sella, and that a larger rival could not copy quickly. Brand Equity, at 62, tracked growth and engagement in the community of 140,000. International Readiness, at 10, asked whether the firm held, or had paid for, the market knowledge and relationships its route abroad required. Cash Runway read eleven months. The counter beneath it, for the EUR 600,000 committed so far, read zero. Vitale would keep the sheet after each of the four sessions, adding a line for what had been decided and what the measures then read.

If nothing structural changed, the arithmetic was not in dispute. The copy would stay in the chain's window at one-sixth of the price. The next spike would meet the same booked capacity. Revenue would remain wholly Italian and wholly dependent on two people drawing, the tranche would stay withheld, and eleven months would become five.

## What Ferrante Controlled

Four working sessions stood between the Monday and the review.

In the first she would settle the diagnosis. She would name the defensible asset: the knit itself, proved by a two-day sell-out; the design signature, the community, the low-waste know-how and traceability; the price position, matched down to the chain's; or the celebrity relationship and the further endorsements it could be worked into. She would name what the pivot had to break: a stock shortage, which the next run would clear; the booked capacity, which a larger mill commitment would lift; the brand's reach, which more advertising would lift; or capacity-bound drops, founder dependence and wholly domestic revenue together. She would fix, as a share of the six weeks, how much went to reshaping the model rather than to getting the next drop out.

In the second she would set the model: produce far more of the signature knit and outsell the copy; license the signature to manufacturers for royalty income; combine selective licensing, a paid membership, a designer platform and made-to-order; or move entirely to designing capsules for larger brands and retire the label. With it went a target for the share of revenue coming from recurring, non-drop streams, against the present 70/20/10. And a decision on the signature: every piece continuing to pass through her and Sella; written briefs and a loose style guide with the two of them still approving each piece; or a codified design language, briefs and processes the team could run.

In the third she would choose the route abroad: opening Filo Studio's own stores in three countries, on its own price and margin; selling direct across borders from the website, on the audience she already had; wholesale through foreign multi-brand retailers, on their buyers and their shelf space; or a joint venture with a local creative partner, on the partner's market and contacts. She would set how concentrated the entry was — three or more markets at once, one priority market, or the markets where freight cost least — and how much of the plan went to buying the market understanding and relationships the route demanded.

In the fourth she would divide the EUR 600,000 across five lines totalling exactly that: senior commercial and design-operations hires; a design system and protection for the signature; market knowledge and localisation for the route chosen; partnership and network development, meaning buyers, agents and local partners; and paid customer acquisition with an inventory top-up. She would choose the eighteen-month scorecard she put in front of Vitale: revenue growth and the return on investment; revenue mix, market-entry return, brand equity and capability use; or follower count and mentions in the press. And she would decide how hard to press, in the room, the argument that compounding capability was worth more than one strong quarter of sales.

## Closing

On the Monday evening Ferrante pinned the photograph of the chain's window above her desk and wrote the four questions on a single sheet. The review was on 20 April. Nineteen people, EUR 800,000 and the six months between eleven and five turned on what she put under them.
