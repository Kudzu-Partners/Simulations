# Assicura Verde: The Regulated Pivot

**Case Code:** STR-2026-1928 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1928 — *Transform Under Rules* · **v1.0 · August 2026**

---

On Monday, February 2, 2026, Chiara Beltrami read three documents that had reached Assicura Verde S.p.A. in the same week. The first was a teardown of Lumo, a digital-native motor insurer that had taken 9% of new motor policies in the company's northern markets in eighteen months and quoted a driver in under three minutes. Assicura Verde's own online quote took fourteen. The second was a sector letter from IVASS, the Italian insurance supervisor, warning that any move to artificial-intelligence underwriting had to meet the European Union AI Act's high-risk obligations — documentation, human oversight, bias testing — before deployment, not after. The third was a launch paper for a usage-based motor product that the data-science team wanted in market for the spring renewal season.

Beltrami had arrived as Chief Strategy Officer with EUR 90 million of board-approved transformation budget, three years to spend it, and one instruction from the chair: modernize without tripping the regulator and without breaking the agency network. The spring renewal window closed in eight weeks. Inside it she had to name what the company was transforming, choose a path and divide the EUR 90 million across it, rule on the launch, and set the order the programme would run in across twelve quarters.

## The Protagonist

Chiara Beltrami, 44, had joined Assicura Verde in November 2025 from a European reinsurer, where she had run corporate development for six years and had twice sat on the company's side of a supervisory review. Before that, nine years in strategy consulting, mostly on insurers' distribution economics. She had never held a line role and had never launched a product. The transformation mandate was why the board had hired her. The EUR 90 million and the three-year horizon were written into her appointment, and her variable pay moved with the programme scorecard.

## The Decision

Beltrami had to settle four things in sequence, each narrowing the next. What the threat behind the share loss actually was, and how much room the rulebook left her. Which path the EUR 90 million would buy, and on what terms the money would be released. Whether the usage-based product went to market this season, and under what controls. And in what order the programme's parts would run. The chair wanted the answer as one argument rather than four. An IVASS supervisor would sit in on the presentation.

## The Company and its Setting

Assicura Verde S.p.A., founded in Milan in 1961 and demutualized in 2004, wrote motor, home, health and small-commercial cover for 2.4 million policyholders. Gross written premium the previous year was EUR 3.1 billion. Its combined ratio stood at 96%, meaning claims and expenses consumed 96 cents of every euro of premium earned. Its Solvency II ratio — capital held against the regulatory minimum — stood at 178%.

The distinguishing fact was distribution. A network of 1,850 tied agencies carried 78% of premium, roughly EUR 2.4 billion, under contracts and a culture that made any digital move a negotiation rather than a memo. The direct-online channel was thin, and it was the one that took fourteen minutes.

Four European frameworks bounded the option set: Solvency II on capital, the General Data Protection Regulation on personal data, the Insurance Distribution Directive on how cover was sold, and the incoming EU AI Act, which placed insurance pricing in its high-risk category. IVASS supervised all four.

## The Situation

The board tracked the programme on four measures, all of them still at their opening marks (see **Exhibit 1**). Transformation progress — how far modernization had moved against Lumo, and in an order that held together — stood at 10 out of 100. Compliance standing, the firm's position against the EU AI Act, IVASS and Solvency II, stood at 60 out of 100. Decision quality, which the chair had defined as whether the firm's big calls used both its underwriters' judgement and its analytics while keeping the agencies alongside, stood at 25 out of 100. The fourth was an ROI outlook: net value created over three years against carrying on unchanged, at EUR 0.0 million. None of the EUR 90 million had been committed.

The launch paper had split the executive floor. Davide Orsini, head of data science, put the usage-based product at four points of combined-ratio improvement and said the spring season was the only window that mattered this year. Franco Tessaro and a second motor underwriter, each with twenty-five years of loss experience, held that the model had been fitted to a benign claims period and would mis-price young drivers. The general counsel had put a number on the other side: a launch IVASS later judged to have gone live without the high-risk obligations met would cost an estimated EUR 12 million to EUR 18 million in remediation, plus a Solvency II add-on.

Ilaria Poletti, who chaired the tied agents' association, had asked for a meeting the week Beltrami's appointment was announced. She had not yet had one.

If nothing changed, the arithmetic was undramatic. Lumo would keep taking new motor business at the rate that had produced 9% of new policies in eighteen months. The quote gap would stay at fourteen minutes against three, the combined ratio near 96%, and the scorecard at 10 out of 100 with the EUR 90 million uncommitted.

## What Beltrami Controlled

Her first move was a reading of the problem. The share loss could be read as pricing speed, with the quote engine as the fix. It could be read as channel economics, with the tied-agent model as the cost problem. It could be read as data capability, the platform first and the rest following. Or it could be read as all three working as one interlocking system. The rulebook needed the same kind of call: an obstacle to be handled at deployment, a formality that rarely moved fast enough to bind a first mover, or a design parameter mapped before anything was designed. She also had to fix how sharply to commit to one testable thesis.

Her second move was the path and the money. Four paths were on the table: rebuild the data and quote infrastructure first; digitize the agency network first; build a direct brand against Lumo; or run the platform and the agencies together with AI underwriting behind both. The EUR 90 million had to be divided, to the euro, across the four initiatives competing for it (see **Exhibit 2**). She also had to set the release rule — the whole envelope committed in Year 1, one third each year on the calendar, or each tranche released only when the initiative before it cleared its milestone.

Her third move was the launch. She could take the product to market as modelled, shelve it, hold it back for full bias testing and miss the season, or put it out with caps, a human in the loop and limited segments while the bias testing ran. She had to design the oversight protocol alongside it: none, a written policy with no underwriter in the loop, or the underwriters as a live control interrogating the model's assumptions and reviewing young-driver pricing before go-live. And she had to set what share of the motor book, from none of it to all of it, the new pricing would touch this season.

Her fourth move was the order. She could put AI underwriting in the second quarter with the platform arriving in the sixth; move customers to the direct channel first and retrain the agencies afterwards; or run data platform, agent buy-in, AI underwriting and channel shift each after the thing it depended on. Every high-risk step would clear its compliance gate after go-live, in parallel with the build, or before it. The 1,850 agencies would get no change plan, a memo and a hotline, or a full programme of incentives, retraining and a stated gain ahead of any channel shift. And she had to settle how hard to defend the sequence, to the board and the supervisor, as gated rather than front-loaded.

## Closing

That evening Beltrami wrote the four initiatives on one sheet and left the euro figures blank. The renewal window closed in eight weeks. The chair wanted one argument, not four, and the supervisor would be in the room. She had EUR 90 million and no order to spend it in.
