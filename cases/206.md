## Abstract

Grupo Andino was, in the case universe, a Lima-headquartered LatAm diversified industrial group with USD 1.4B in 2025 revenue, 4,820 employees across Peru, Chile, Colombia and Ecuador, three operating divisions (mining-services equipment, specialty-industrial-components and industrial-digital-services), a 2025 aggregate commercial-deal pipeline of EUR 48M, and a Q4 2025 strategic plan that had priced cross-division IP licensing as the 2026–2028 growth engine. The Q1 2026 commercial-deal docket had opened a named five-year EUR 3.4M technology-licensing transaction: a cross-region IP-and-technical-know-how licence to be negotiated across twenty named clauses and three named counterparties (two European industrial-service licensees and one LatAm industrial-services alternative buyer). The Q4 2025 board had approved a four-round ClauseWork negotiation charter — a four-month Head-of-Commercial-Negotiations commitment — and had positioned it as the group's response to three overlapping pressures: a 2024–2025 commercial-deal-cycle diagnostic that had priced Grupo Andino below the LatAm-industrial-peer deal-value median by 32% on a comparable-licence basis, a 2025 counterparty-relationship audit that had documented five named commercial relationships as below-trust-threshold, and a Q4 2025 CFO commitment to the board that the 2026 commercial-deal pipeline would deliver EUR 14M in new run-rate licensing revenue by year-end. The case opens at 6:18 a.m. on Tuesday, April 21, 2026, at the Grupo Andino Lima head office as **Ignacio Arredondo-Miranda**, a 45-year-old Head of Commercial Negotiations, prepares the Round-1 stance-and-focus commitment.

## The Protagonist

Arredondo-Miranda, 45, had assumed the Head-of-Commercial-Negotiations role at Grupo Andino on February 1, 2025, after sixteen years across commercial-deal-making, IP-licensing negotiation and multi-counterparty-deal leadership at a Santiago-based industrial-services group and a Madrid-based commercial-advisory firm. He had, in 2020–2024, led a EUR 18M cumulative-deal-portfolio cycle across eight multi-counterparty negotiations that had closed nineteen-of-twenty clauses on average with Counterparty Trust above 68% at close in 82% of cases. The Chief Commercial Officer had recruited him in December 2024 specifically on two capabilities: his documented discipline of defaulting to stance-and-focus calibration rather than single-variable stance postures that had priced two peer deals into named-trust-collapse, and his willingness to default to cooperative-pivot envelopes inside the third round rather than aggressive-throughout postures that had exhausted Bargaining Power reserves in adjacent deals. His 2026 variable compensation tied 35% to the aggregate annual Deal Value, 25% to the Counterparty-Trust-at-close floor and 20% to the Clauses-Resolved composite.

## The Decision

Arredondo-Miranda had to commit, by the 8:30 a.m. commercial-committee opening session, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–4.

1. **Round 1 — Stance-and-focus calibration.** Three archetypes: Aggressive-Commercial-Anchor with Concession 20 (Composite 58, Deal Value +EUR 1.8M anchored, Trust –16, Power stable), Balanced-Opening with Concession 40 (Composite 66, Deal Value +EUR 1.2M, Trust stable), or Cooperative-IP-Opening with Concession 50 (Composite 48, Deal Value +EUR 0.9M, Trust +8). Working envelope: Aggressive-Commercial-Anchor.
2. **Round 2 — BATNA reframing after Counterparty-B alternative-licensor walk-in.** Three vectors: Cooperative-IP-pivot with Concession 55 (Composite 76, Power restored to 58%, Trust +18), re-anchor-at-Aggressive (Composite 24, Power collapse below 30%), or pause-the-round (Composite 42, narrative drift). Working envelope: Cooperative-IP-pivot.
3. **Round 3 — Concession-pivot.** Three vectors: Concession 55 on IP-and-liability (Composite 76, 12 clauses resolved, Trust +18), Concession 30 hold (Composite 46, 7 clauses resolved, Trust +4), or Concession 70 accommodate (Composite 52, 14 clauses resolved but Deal Value giveback EUR 0.6M). Working envelope: Concession 55.
4. **Round 4 — Closing commitment.** Two vectors: 19-of-20-clauses close at EUR 3.4M Deal Value (Composite 76, governance clause deferred to post-close addendum) or full-20-push (Composite 42, 30% no-deal probability on the twentieth clause). Working envelope: 19-of-20-close.

On Arredondo-Miranda's working model, an Aggressive-Anchor / Cooperative-Pivot / Concession-55 / 19-of-20-Close envelope delivered Deal Value EUR 3.4M, Clauses 19/20, Trust 72%, Power 58%, Composite 74.

## Company Background

Grupo Andino had been founded in Lima in 1992 and had, across thirty-four years, built a USD 1.4B-revenue LatAm diversified industrial group organised into three operating divisions: a mining-services-equipment division (USD 680M revenue, 22 named rig-and-service customers), a specialty-industrial-components division (USD 420M revenue, 54 named industrial customers) and an industrial-digital-services division (USD 300M revenue, 18 named data-and-digital-platform customers). The 2023–2025 cycle had left the group at a defended-cost-leadership posture in mining services, a specification-based posture in components, and a named three-customer concentration in industrial-digital-services. The Q4 2025 strategic plan had priced cross-division IP licensing as the 2026–2028 growth engine, and had identified the EUR 3.4M five-year technology-licensing deal (the ClauseWork programme) as the 2026 pacing deal.

Three Q1 2026 counterparties were named in the March 2026 deal-committee dossier. First, Counterparty A: a German industrial-service licensee looking for a five-year exclusive European-territory licence on the industrial-digital-services platform. Second, Counterparty B: a Spanish industrial-service licensee with a documented alternative-licensor relationship (a LatAm competitor's IP-licensing desk). Third, Counterparty C: a Colombian industrial-services alternative buyer looking for a non-exclusive Andean-territory licence. The March 2026 deal-team diagnostic had priced Counterparty B as the BATNA-pressure counterparty on the 20-clause negotiation matrix.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2019–2025 industrial-IP-licensing literature had, across 42 documented multi-counterparty commercial-deal negotiations above EUR 2M contract value, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, Aggressive-Commercial-Anchor openings with Concession below 25 had priced Deal Value 18–28 points above Balanced openings but had consumed 12–18 points of Counterparty Trust in 68% of documented deals. Second, BATNA-reframing events mid-negotiation (alternative-licensor walk-ins, alternative-buyer entries) had reduced Bargaining Power by 14–22 points in 72% of documented cases, and had been restored in 64% of cases through Cooperative-IP-pivot envelopes inside a two-round window. Third, closing commitments at 18–19-of-20 clauses-resolved with a deferred-addendum mechanism had closed 76% of documented deals above EUR 2M, against 44% for full-clause-push closings.

Empirically, the post-2022 commercial-negotiation literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, Counterparty-Trust readings above 70% at close had priced 24-month renewal rates at 78%, against 34% for closes below the 55% Trust threshold. Second, concession-sequencing discipline (Aggressive-anchor → Cooperative-pivot → Concession-55-hold) had sustained deal-value-above-pre-anchor in 58% of documented multi-counterparty deals.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The commercial committee had, in the December 2025 session, approved the EUR 3.4M deal scope and the twenty-clause negotiation charter. The Head of Legal had, on March 7, delivered the clause-by-clause risk diagnostic. The Head of IP Licensing had, on March 14, delivered the BATNA read on Counterparty B's alternative-licensor relationship. The Chief Commercial Officer had, on March 21, delivered the deal-value target range (EUR 3.0M–EUR 3.8M). The Head of Counterparty Relations had, on March 28, delivered the Counterparty-A-and-C trust baseline (both at 70% pre-round).

### What Arredondo-Miranda knew

The team had modelled three four-round envelopes. An Aggressive-Anchor / Cooperative-Pivot / Concession-55 / 19-of-20-Close envelope projected Composite 74. An Aggressive-Anchor / Re-Anchor-Aggressive / Concession-30 / Full-20-Push envelope projected Composite 14 with no-deal probability above 60%. A Balanced-Opening / Cooperative-Opening / Concession-55 / 19-of-20-Close envelope projected Composite 52.

### What he did not know

Whether Counterparty B's alternative-licensor relationship was a real BATNA or a negotiation-posture signal; whether the governance-clause (the twentieth) would hold as a deferred-addendum mechanism at close; whether Counterparty A's German territory exclusivity requirement would clash with Counterparty C's Andean-territory non-exclusive requirement inside the joint-closing window; and whether the Q2 2026 commercial-pipeline would hold Counterparty-Trust reserves through the close.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Legal**: *"Anchor on commercial. Twenty percent concession. Let them respond. The first move sets the frame for the next three rounds."*

**The Head of IP Licensing**: *"Pivot in round two. IP and liability. Fifty-five percent concession. Trust recovery is arithmetic — eighteen points back."*

**The Chief Commercial Officer**: *"Close at nineteen. Not twenty. The last clause is always the one that costs more than it resolves."*

**The Head of Counterparty Relations**: *"BATNA is oxygen. Trust is a discount you pay in concessions."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Arredondo-Miranda had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Aggressive-Anchor / Cooperative-Pivot / Concession-55 / 19-of-20-Close:** the recommended envelope. Deal Value EUR 3.4M. Clauses 19/20. Trust 72%. Power 58%. Composite 74. Recommended.
- **Option B — Aggressive-Anchor / Re-Anchor-Aggressive / Concession-30 / Full-20-Push:** the hard-line envelope. Composite 14. Rejected — no-deal probability above 60%.
- **Option C — Balanced-Opening / Cooperative-Opening / Concession-55 / 19-of-20-Close:** the soft envelope. Composite 52. Rejected — Deal Value below the EUR 3.0M floor.

Arredondo-Miranda's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 2 — the Cooperative-IP-pivot after the Counterparty-B walk-in — because it was the round that decided whether the Bargaining Power line continued downward below the 40% floor or whether the pivot restored it inside the two-round window the negotiation sequence required.
