## Abstract

Avanta Retail Group, a USD 320M specialty retailer with 48 stores across five Latin American cities, entered 2026 with an EBITDA margin of 6.8%, an e-commerce share of 4%, a customer NPS of 34, and a cost per online order of USD 18.40 — the last of which was unsustainable against a two-marketplace competitive set whose equivalent figure sat near USD 11. A new CEO, Lucía Rivas, had been appointed in December 2025 with a five-round board mandate to deliver omnichannel transformation: Round 1 foundation (commerce platform, inventory, organization); Round 2 fulfillment (BOPIS, ship-from-store, last-mile); Round 3 merchandising (pricing engine, assortment, parity); Round 4 customer (CDP, loyalty, personalization); Round 5 competitive response (store footprint, marketplaces, live commerce). Every Round-1 decision bound a later round. The case opens on the morning of the first foundation vote and sets up the Round 1 decision in the simulation.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Distinguish the three foundation decisions of an omnichannel transformation — platform, inventory, organization — and explain why none of them is recoverable cheaply.
2. Evaluate the trade-off between a best-of-breed stack and a unified commerce suite under a mid-sized-retailer budget.
3. Analyze the interaction between organizational design (dedicated e-commerce vs. channel-agnostic) and the fulfillment choices that follow.
4. Diagnose the cost-per-order economics of fulfillment design and identify the single decision with the largest effect on the number.
5. Recommend a Round-1 foundation package and specify the Round-2 options it leaves open.

On the morning of **January 20, 2026**, **Lucía Rivas** sat in the boardroom on the fourth floor of Avanta Retail Group's Mexico City headquarters and read, for the fourth time, the six-slide deck her Chief Digital Officer had put together on a weekend. The deck carried three headings in bold on the cover: *Platform. Inventory. Organization.* Below the headings, handwritten by Rivas herself in blue ink, a single line: *"Whichever three we pick today, we spend two years living with."* The Round 1 Executive Committee vote was scheduled for 11:00 a.m.

---

## The Protagonist

Lucía Rivas, 48, had joined Avanta on December 3, 2025. A civil engineer with an MBA from a Brazilian school, she had spent eighteen years in retail: eight years at a Chilean department-store group running merchandising and supply chain, six years as Chief Commercial Officer of a Colombian pharmacy chain (where she had led a three-year omnichannel transformation from 2% to 22% online share), and four years as an operating partner at a regional PE firm advising retail portfolio companies. She had been recruited by the Avanta board on the strength of the pharmacy-chain transformation and against an internal candidate well-liked by the store operations team. Her contract carried a five-round vesting gate tied to revenue, EBITDA, e-commerce share, and cost per order.

---

## The Decision

Rivas had to ratify, by 11:00 a.m. on January 20, three Round-1 foundation decisions the simulation would treat as her opening input:

1. **Commerce Platform** — Best-of-Breed (bolt storefront, catalog, OMS, payments from separate vendors), Unified Suite (single-vendor commerce cloud), or Extended Current (stretch the 2019-vintage stack).
2. **Inventory Management System** — Channel-Siloed (store and online stock separate), Network-View (single network-wide inventory with allocation rules), or Full Unification (real-time store-to-online stock visibility for BOPIS and ship-from-store).
3. **Organizational Structure** — Dedicated E-Commerce (separate P&L, separate general manager), Channel-Agnostic (merchants own both channels with a digital functional group), or Hybrid Transitional.

Each Round-1 decision bound the Round-2 fulfillment space. A Channel-Siloed inventory system ruled out BOPIS at national scale. A Dedicated E-Commerce organization made ship-from-store politically difficult because store P&Ls paid for online fulfillment. A Best-of-Breed platform bought speed-of-launch at the cost of multi-year integration burden.

---

## Company Background

Avanta Retail Group, founded in 1989 in Monterrey, had grown over three decades into a 48-store specialty retailer in the adjacent categories of home, kids, and personal care, with operations in five LATAM countries. 2025 revenue was USD 320M with an EBITDA margin of 6.8% (below the 9.2% segment median). Customer count was 2.4M active shoppers on the loyalty program; average basket was USD 38; store traffic had declined 2.1% per year since 2023. E-commerce contributed 4% of revenue, served primarily from a single dedicated fulfillment center in Mexico City that could not ship to three of the five countries without cross-border customs friction.

Avanta was 71% owned by the founding Saldaña family, through a holding company, with the remaining 29% held by a regional family-of-funds consortium that had entered the cap table in 2019 via a minority recap. The consortium's investment memorandum had identified omnichannel as the value-creation lever; the family's mandate had emphasized brand preservation and store-employee continuity. The two shareholder groups had, in the negotiation of Rivas's contract, agreed on one constraint — no net store closures in Year 1 — that limited the Round-5 competitive-response space.

## Industry and Market Context

The Latin American specialty-retail segment had bifurcated in 2022–2025. A cluster of mid-sized retailers had committed to unified commerce stacks with network-view inventory systems and had reached 20–30% online share by 2025 while preserving store traffic. A second cluster had extended legacy stacks with channel-siloed inventory and had stalled at 3–8% online share with declining store traffic. The two marketplaces that dominated regional discovery — two pan-LATAM platforms — had, since 2023, begun offering retailer-seller programs that delivered 18–32% of online volume for marketplace-participating retailers but demanded 12–16% take rates on Gross Merchandise Value.

Three findings from the post-2020 omnichannel literature were consequential for Rivas's decision. First, unified-suite platforms delivered faster time-to-launch (median 9 months vs. 14 for best-of-breed) but higher total cost of ownership over five years (median +24%). Second, network-view inventory systems delivered 60% of BOPIS-potential benefit even without full real-time unification, making them the highest-ROI intermediate path. Third, channel-agnostic organizations recovered 1.8 percentage points of EBITDA over dedicated-e-commerce organizations in the 18–36 month window by avoiding a second P&L, but produced lower e-commerce share growth in the first 12 months because the transition taxed merchant attention.

## The Situation

### How we got here

Avanta's 2023 strategic review had identified omnichannel as the core lever and had recommended a three-year program. The 2023–2025 execution had been partial: a new storefront launched on the 2019 platform, a regional payments upgrade, and a failed attempt to unify the inventory system that had been suspended in Q2 2024 when the ERP vendor changed pricing. The store operations team — led by Javier Pacheco, a 22-year Avanta veteran — had, during the inventory project, publicly opposed real-time visibility on the grounds that it would expose in-store stock adjustments and hurt store-bonus calculations. Pacheco's opposition had killed the project. Rivas had spent her first six weeks mapping Pacheco's coalition.

### What Rivas knew

The internal planning team had modeled three Round-1 foundation packages across the five-round horizon. A *Conservative Foundation* (Extended Current platform, Channel-Siloed inventory, Hybrid Transitional org) cost USD 14M in Year 1 and projected Year-5 e-commerce share of 12%, EBITDA 8.4%, NPS 42. A *Unified Foundation* (Unified Suite, Network-View inventory, Channel-Agnostic org) cost USD 26M in Year 1 and projected Year-5 e-commerce share of 28%, EBITDA 11.2%, NPS 56. A *Best-of-Breed Foundation* (Best-of-Breed, Full Unification, Dedicated E-Commerce org) cost USD 34M in Year 1 and projected Year-5 e-commerce share of 31%, EBITDA 10.4%, NPS 54.

### What she did not know

Whether Pacheco would resign under either the Network-View or the Full Unification path (he had told three members of the executive committee he would, and his resignation would cost Avanta roughly USD 2M of store-level know-how transfer). Whether the Saldaña family would ratify a Year-1 capex plan at USD 26M or would hold at the USD 18M expected figure. Whether the consortium would accept a two-year online-share trajectory that looked flat in Year 1 under a Channel-Agnostic organization.

### What others were saying

**Javier Pacheco**, Executive Vice President of Store Operations, argued for the **Conservative Foundation**: *"Extend the current platform. Leave store inventory alone. Put the digital team on a dotted line to stores. We are not a technology company. Be patient."*

**Diego Cárdenas**, Chief Digital Officer, argued for the **Best-of-Breed Foundation**: *"Hire the best vendor in each of platform, OMS, CDP, and personalization. Dedicated org. Separate P&L. Move fast. Pay the integration tax with speed-to-launch."*

**Esteban Saldaña**, family representative on the board, argued for the **Unified Foundation**: *"One vendor. One system. One organization. The budget has to be twenty-six. The consortium needs a trajectory. The stores need an architecture that will not be rewritten in three years."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Rivas had narrowed the space to three options.

- **Option A — Conservative Foundation (Pacheco plan):** Extended Current platform; Channel-Siloed inventory; Hybrid Transitional org. Year-1 cost USD 14M; Year-5 e-commerce share 12%; Year-5 EBITDA 8.4%; Year-5 NPS 42. Preserves store politics; forecloses national-scale BOPIS in Round 2.

- **Option B — Unified Foundation (Saldaña plan):** Unified Suite; Network-View inventory; Channel-Agnostic org. Year-1 cost USD 26M; Year-5 e-commerce share 28%; Year-5 EBITDA 11.2%; Year-5 NPS 56. Pacheco resignation risk high; Round 2 BOPIS-on-48-stores feasible.

- **Option C — Best-of-Breed Foundation (Cárdenas plan):** Best-of-Breed; Full Unification; Dedicated E-Commerce org. Year-1 cost USD 34M; Year-5 e-commerce share 31%; Year-5 EBITDA 10.4%; Year-5 NPS 54. Highest integration burden; highest speed-to-launch. Requires Year-1 capex above family's comfort level.

Rivas suspected Option B dominated on five-round composite and on shareholder alignment; Option C dominated on speed and lost on integration cost; Option A was the option whose failure had created the vacancy she now filled. At 10:18 a.m. she picked up the deck and walked to the boardroom.
