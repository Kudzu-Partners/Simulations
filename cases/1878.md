# Atlântico Distribuição: The Power of Yes

**Case Code:** STR-2026-1878 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1878 — *The Power of Yes* · **v1.0 · August 2026**

---

On Tuesday, 2 June 2026, Marta Coutinho sat down for her first board meeting as Chief Executive of Atlântico Distribuição S.A. and heard the Chair table a single figure: €220 million, committed over three years to a digital-and-format transformation. He wanted a direction — not a study — inside her first hundred days. "This company has perfected the art of the maybe," he told the room. "We hired you for the power of yes."

Coutinho left with ninety-eight days to decide what the transformation would be: which problem it solved, how much of the €220 million went where, in what order, and how she would hold a divided board behind it. The envelope would not cover everything on the table — only roughly two of the four ideas circulated for a year. Which two, and how completely, was hers to decide.

## The Protagonist

Marta Coutinho, 51, had spent nine years at Atlântico before the board made her CEO, most running the commercial and store-format division, where she had argued, without quite winning the argument, for real investment in online grocery rather than a side project's budget. She had joined in 2017 from a supply-chain role in manufacturing, after a decade at a consumer-goods multinational. Colleagues knew her as the director who kept raising digital investment in meetings that preferred store openings — why the Chair had put her name forward: the person who had argued for boldness would now be accountable for it. Her new contract tied a meaningful share of her pay to how the direction she set played out.

## The Decision

Coutinho's decision unfolded in four parts: settle what question the €220 million was meant to answer, since the four candidate bets looked different depending on the diagnosis; decide how much capital went to which bets and by what mechanism each was committed; sequence the moves across a 36-month roadmap, deciding what happened first and what would trigger or halt what followed; and, once a direction existed, carry it into a boardroom split between two directors pushing for boldness, two for caution, and a founding family holding 35% of the company, watching her first hundred days as a test of whether she could lead.

## The Company and its Setting

Atlântico Distribuição S.A. had sold groceries and general merchandise in Portugal since 1981, and by 2026 ran 240 supermarkets and 18 hypermarkets across the mainland and islands, employing 19,000 people (see **Exhibit 1**). Revenue in 2025 had reached €3.1 billion at an operating margin of 3.4% — about €105 million — respectable by grocery standards but sliding. Atlântico held 14% of the national market, third place behind two larger rivals, in a company that had built its identity over four decades on caution: incremental openings, consensus, waiting to see how a trend developed before spending on it. That had suited a market that held still. It had not adapted to one that no longer did.

## The Situation

Online grocery was 3% of Atlântico's revenue, against 9–11% at the two market leaders, and the online channel lost about €18 million a year with no clear route to profit. In the twelve months before her appointment, Atlântico had lost 1.1 percentage points of share to discounters and a well-funded quick-commerce entrant, squeezed from the value end and the convenience end at once. On that trajectory, projections put it slipping from third to fourth place within roughly 30 months, a threshold below which supplier terms and buying scale were expected to deteriorate further. The board had fixed one more constraint: net debt could not exceed 2.5 times EBITDA, a covenant a large misstep could put at risk. If Coutinho committed to nothing beyond what was already under way, the losses, the share slide and the clock on fourth place all continued.

Four ideas had reached the board table over the previous year, priced from roughly €40 million to €130 million, and €220 million would not stretch to all four at a scale that mattered (see **Exhibit 2**): an omnichannel rebuild, the boldest option; a data and loyalty platform built on AI personalisation; a hard-discount banner defending the value end; and quick-commerce, as a partnership or a costlier in-house build. Funding all four at once, board members had noted, meant roughly €55 million apiece — enough to say yes to everything, not obviously enough to make any one work.

## What Coutinho Controlled

Four measures updated as she went: Strategic Clarity, how coherent and committed her direction looked, starting at 12 of 100; Competitive Position, how well the plan defended the third-place share, starting at 20; Board Mandate, alignment across the split board and family, starting at 15; and Capital Committed, how much of the €220 million was at work, against a covenant-risk indicator.

Before committing a euro, Coutinho had to name the question the money answered: a digital-relevance problem, Atlântico becoming irrelevant to how people shopped; a value-defence problem, stopping discounters on price; a scale-survival problem, protecting supplier terms over changing the model; or a question left open for more information. She then had to judge where €220 million could build an advantage rivals could not copy — a focused pair reinforcing each other, matching what the two larger rivals were doing, spending wherever cheapest to preserve cash and margin, or not yet committing because the picture was unclear — and set, 0 to 100, how firmly she would commit inside the hundred days rather than hedge.

With or without a settled diagnosis, she had to put numbers against the four bets — any split not exceeding the envelope — and decide how her lead bet was committed: staged, releasing more money once a trigger was hit; committed fully and irreversibly from day one with no exit; handed to a partner who carried the capability and the risk instead; or funded as a small pilot revisited in twelve months. Whatever she did not fund, she had to name it and redirect its budget into the bets she kept, leave a smaller amount in each so none sat at zero, or under-fund it without addressing the choice.

Direction chosen, she had to sequence it — the faster, cheaper bet first, to bank an early result and de-risk the larger move; the biggest, slowest build first; or both at once from month one — and decide how the next tranche released: against a scale trigger paired with stopping criteria, a trigger alone with no stopping point, or no fixed gate, revisited at each board meeting. She also had to decide when the market and the family holding were told — now, with progress reported against milestones; only after the first proof point; or via a broader review — and how much headroom, 0 to 100, to keep under the covenant before the next tranche.

Only then did she face the board. Two prudent directors and the family holding would ask why not wait a year; her answer had to rest on gated milestones, stopping criteria and a capped downside letting them vote in favour without narrowing the plan — or on overruling them with the two bold votes and the Chair's mandate, or on revising the plan into a version every faction could accept. Accountability metrics staked her position on leading signals plus the three-year outcome, the lagging share number reported once a year, or no hard metric at all. Asked what happened if she was wrong about the omnichannel bet, her answer rested on the stopping criteria and downside cap built in, on her own conviction, or on having kept enough spread that no bet could hurt much. A last number, 0 to 100, set how firmly she would defend one committed direction over a comfortable spread of smaller ones.

## Closing

It was still 2 June 2026, and Coutinho had ninety-eight days left before the deadline the Chair had named in front of the room. Four bets sat on the table, priced and ready, and splitting €220 million four ways was simple arithmetic. Everything else was open — which question the money answered, how much went where, in what order, and how she would keep a divided board behind whatever she chose. Somewhere between the art of the maybe and the power of yes, Coutinho had to find her answer.
