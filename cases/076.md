## Abstract

Hotel Cielo F&B was, in the case universe, the food-and-beverage operation of Hotel Cielo, a 180-room luxury boutique property located in the colonial-era quarter of a Latin American capital. On Friday, January 2, 2026, the F&B operation served 280 covers per day across a 42-item menu spread over three restaurants (signature, all-day, rooftop bar), carried a food cost of 34.2% of F&B revenue, a labor cost of 31.1% of F&B revenue, a banquet backlog of USD 128K, and an average check of USD 58. 2025 hotel occupancy averaged 71%. Mariana Quesada, appointed Director of Food & Beverage on December 1, 2025 after four years at the property as Executive Sous-Chef and two years as Assistant F&B Manager, faced six monthly decision rounds, a March seafood supply shock the supply-chain team had flagged at an 18% wholesale-price uplift, an April wedding-season bid window with three RFPs on the table, and a June summer-programming launch. The case opens at 5:47 a.m. on Monday, January 5, 2026, as Quesada prepares the Month-1 operating package for the January 14 General Manager review.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Month-1 menu-engineering decision across a forty-two-item menu using the star-puzzle-plowhorse-dog matrix, and recommend an SKU rationalization that preserves covers while compressing food cost.
2. Analyze the Month-3 seafood-supply-shock scenario and recommend an ingredient-substitution and transparent-repricing response that holds signature-menu integrity.
3. Diagnose the Month-4 wedding-season bidding decision across three RFPs, and design a selective-bid posture that preserves margin without hollowing the banquet-revenue pipeline.
4. Design a Month-6 summer-programming launch that converts a Year-1 labor-cross-training investment into a beach-club and rooftop-extended-hours operating model.
5. Recommend a complete Month-1 operating package and identify the mid-cycle move most correlated with the Month-6 composite outcome.

On the morning of **Monday, January 5, 2026**, **Mariana Quesada** arrived at the service entrance of Hotel Cielo at 5:47 a.m., changed into chef whites, and opened the Month-1 operating workbook her Executive Chef had finalized at 10:34 p.m. Sunday. Nine days separated her from the General Manager's Month-1 review. The F&B dashboard on the back-office monitor read 280 covers per day forecast, 34.2% food cost, 31.1% labor, USD 128K banquet backlog, and forty-two active menu items.

---

## The Protagonist

Mariana Quesada, 38, had joined Hotel Cielo in 2020 as Executive Sous-Chef on the signature-restaurant line and had moved to the Assistant F&B Manager role in 2023. A graduate of Le Cordon Bleu Paris with an executive program in hospitality P&L management at EHL, she had, in her 2023–2025 Assistant F&B Manager role, led the property's 2024 banquet-sales restructure that had moved the banquet backlog from USD 64K to USD 128K. Her December appointment had followed the previous F&B Director's move to a larger group property. The General Manager's December written mandate had been: "Make the F&B P&L subsidize rooms by June. The covers are the same; the decisions have to be different."

---

## The Decision

Quesada had to commit, by 9:00 a.m. on Wednesday, January 14, the Month-1 operating package the General Manager would treat as the six-month operating envelope:

1. **Menu Engineering** — whether to trim eight dogs from the forty-two-item menu, whether to reprice four puzzle-category items plus 6–8%, and whether to dedicate in-room-tablet marketing to three star-category items.
2. **Labor Schedule and Procurement** — whether to cross-train two line-cooks onto banquets to compress overtime, whether to renew two produce contracts with a volume-guarantee-lower-unit-cost clause, and whether to delay the sous-chef hire to Q2.
3. **Banquet Pipeline Posture** — whether to run proactive outreach to the corporate-account portfolio, whether to introduce a pre-paid Mother's Day bundle at plus-12%, and whether to bid selectively on the three wedding-season RFPs arriving in April.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across six monthly rounds. A Month-1 package that trimmed eight dogs, repriced four puzzles, marketed three stars, cross-trained labor, renewed produce contracts with volume-guarantee clauses, ran proactive banquet outreach, introduced the Mother's Day bundle, and bid selectively on wedding RFPs produced, on the internal model, a Month-2 food cost of 32.8%, a Month-5 banquet revenue of plus-38% year-over-year, a Month-6 food cost of 29.4%, a Month-6 labor cost of 28.9%, and a six-month composite of 84 out of 100.

## Company Background

Hotel Cielo was, in the case universe, a 180-room luxury boutique property whose F&B operation carried three distinct outlets: the signature restaurant (70 seats), the all-day restaurant and breakfast service (120 seats), and the rooftop bar (95 seats plus event capacity). 2025 F&B revenue was USD 14.8M, of which signature 34%, all-day 38%, rooftop 11%, in-room dining 9%, banquets 8%. The food-cost structure of 34.2% reflected a 2024 produce-contract lapse that had moved produce cost 180 basis points above the 2023 baseline; the labor cost of 31.1% reflected a Q4 2025 overtime spike during the holiday banquet window. Hotel-loyalty F&B attach was 51% among repeat guests, a figure the General Manager had flagged as under-indexed relative to the boutique peer group.

The competitive set for Hotel Cielo's F&B outlets carried two principal actors beyond the in-hotel set. The Palazzo Dorato, a five-star competitor two blocks north, had opened a chef-led tasting menu in August 2025 that had absorbed a measurable share of the signature-restaurant tourist cover mix. La Terraza, an independent rooftop bar three blocks east, had, in Q3 2025, extended evening hours and pulled weekend rooftop covers by approximately 8%.

## Industry and Market Context

The luxury-boutique hospitality F&B landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Month-1 decision sat inside. First, the post-2023 produce-contract environment had moved from spot-purchase to volume-guarantee-based structures, with top-quartile operators running 70–85% of produce volume through guarantee contracts with embedded price-protection clauses against commodity swings above 12%. Second, wedding-season pricing had, in the 2025 LATAM boutique sample, varied by 18–32% depending on menu customization and venue configuration, with the RFP win rate at full margin ranging from 28% to 44% depending on market positioning and the sales-team's bid discipline. Third, the summer-programming cycle had, in urban-luxury-boutique properties across Europe and LATAM, shifted toward outdoor beach-club-equivalent and extended-hour rooftop models, with top-quartile operators generating a Year-1-to-Year-2 F&B contribution-margin lift of 3–5 percentage points.

Empirically, the post-2020 F&B-operations research had two robust findings the Month-1 package had to respect. First, menu-engineering-based dog-removal — specifically, removing eight to twelve low-contribution low-popularity items from a forty- to fifty-item menu — had, across a 2020–2024 sample of sixty-eight hotel F&B operations, compressed food cost 120–180 basis points at a measured cover-count impact below 2%. Second, labor cross-training between line-cook and banquet roles had, across a 2022–2024 sample of forty-two full-service hotels, reduced banquet-peak overtime by 34% on average while producing a six-month labor-cost compression of 140–220 basis points.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The General Manager had, in the November 2025 P&L review, committed to the hotel ownership a six-month F&B contribution-margin target of plus-6 percentage points versus the January baseline. The Supply-Chain office had, on December 22, circulated a procurement-market outlook flagging the March seafood supply shock at an 18% wholesale-price uplift with a 68% probability. The Sales office had, on January 2, signalled three wedding-season RFPs with decision deadlines in the April 10 – April 28 window. The Executive Chef had, on January 4, presented three Month-1 operating packages.

### What Quesada knew

The team had modeled three Month-1 packages. A Volume-First Package (maintain the forty-two-item menu, no repricing, no cross-training, passive banquet outreach) projected Month-6 food cost 33.8%, Month-6 labor 30.4%, six-month composite 64. A Discipline Package (trim eight dogs, reprice four puzzles plus 6-8%, cross-train two line-cooks, renew produce contracts with volume-guarantee clauses, proactive banquet outreach, Mother's Day pre-paid bundle, selective wedding-RFP bidding) projected Month-6 food cost 29.4%, Month-6 labor 28.9%, six-month composite 84. A Maximum-Cut Package (trim fourteen items, reprice nine items plus 10%, cross-train four line-cooks, aggressive banquet outreach, full wedding-RFP bidding) projected Month-6 food cost 28.6%, Month-6 labor 27.8%, six-month composite 76 — margin-aggressive but guest-experience-exposed.

### What she did not know

Whether the March seafood supply shock would land at the Supply-Chain-modeled 18% or at the alternate 28% scenario that the regional purchasing-cooperative had referenced; whether the April wedding-season RFPs would include venue configurations that required tent rentals (the internal-Sales-team estimate was that one of the three would); whether the Mother's Day pre-paid bundle would sell out inside the April window (the internal-Marketing-team estimate was 78% probability); and whether the June beach-club and rooftop-extended-hour operating model would clear municipal noise-ordinance review without a rooftop-hour curtailment.

### What others were saying

**The Executive Chef**: *"Trim the dogs. Eight items move zero covers because they are invisible in a forty-two-item menu. The produce contracts are the lever; the volume guarantee is the contract clause."*

**The Banquet Sales Manager**: *"Selective bid on the April RFPs. Two are winnable at full margin with custom menu builds. The third has a venue configuration that destroys margin on a tent rental. Do not chase."*

**The Food & Beverage Supply-Chain Lead**: *"Renew produce contracts now. A March seafood shock is coming at 18% base case. Substitute to domestic lamb and root vegetables on the spring menu — the rehearsed sub is the protection."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Quesada had narrowed the space to three Month-1 operating packages.

- **Option A — Volume-First:** Maintain full forty-two-item menu, no repricing, no cross-training, passive banquet outreach. Month-6 composite 64. Covers-preserving, margin-eroding.

- **Option B — Discipline:** Trim eight dogs, reprice four puzzles, cross-train labor, renew produce contracts with volume-guarantee clauses, proactive banquet outreach, Mother's Day bundle, selective wedding-RFP bidding. Month-6 composite 84. Aligns with Executive Chef, Banquet Sales Manager, and Supply-Chain Lead.

- **Option C — Maximum-Cut:** Trim fourteen items, reprice nine items plus 10%, cross-train four line-cooks, full wedding-RFP bidding. Month-6 composite 76. Margin-aggressive. Guest-experience-exposed.

Quesada's private view, not yet shared with the General Manager, was that Option B was the right Month-1 package and that the single subsequent decision most correlated with the Month-6 composite was the Month-3 spring-menu launch — specifically, a spring menu that leaned into domestic lamb and root vegetables (rehearsed pre-shock), a labor cross-training completion that covered the April wedding-season spike without overtime, and a transparent seafood-item reprice plus-4% flagged directly on the menu.
