## Abstract

CLEARVIEW ASSURANCE was, in the case universe, a London-and-Dublin-headquartered sustainability-assurance firm with roughly 340 auditors across seven European offices and a 2025 fee book of EUR 68M, with a 2025 engagement portfolio comprising 112 CSRD-in-scope statutory sustainability-report assurance engagements. The TerraGlow Manufacturing engagement was, in 2026, a EUR 420K limited-assurance engagement on TerraGlow's 2025 sustainability-report filed under the EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive — an engagement that represented 40% of the London-based sustainability-assurance practice's fee book and was, on a 2024 rollover, expected to convert to a EUR 680K reasonable-assurance engagement for the 2026 filing cycle. TerraGlow's 2025 sustainability report contained three claims that the ClearView engagement-planning memorandum had flagged as material greenwashing risk: a headline Scope-3 emissions reduction of 42% against a 2020 baseline, a "net-zero by 2035" commitment anchored on carbon-removal credits of unverified tonnage, and a social-metric disclosure on living-wage coverage that relied on a methodology that excluded 38% of Tier-2 suppliers. The engagement partner had approved a five-round Lead-ESG-Auditor-led forensic testing cycle against a Detection-Integrity floor above 75, an Evidence-Strength floor above 72, a Client-Relationship ceiling above 58 (preserved-to-a-defensible-minimum), a CSRD-Compliance Score above 68, and a composite Assurance-Integrity Score above 72. The case opens at 6:14 a.m. on Monday, April 13, 2026, at ClearView's Canary Wharf office as **Siobhán O'Connor**, a 41-year-old Lead ESG Auditor, prepares the Round-1 Scope-3 recalculation commitment. Sixty-eight days separate her from the June 20 engagement-committee sign-off.

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

2. Analyze the Round-2 net-zero-claim-testing commitment (removal-credit-tonnage verification with chain-of-custody documentation vs. issuer-attestation reliance) and recommend the verification envelope against the attestation envelope on the CSRD-Compliance-Score basis.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 social-metric-verification commitment (full-Tier-2-supplier expansion on living-wage methodology vs. Tier-1-only) and recommend the expanded envelope against the narrow envelope on the Detection-Integrity basis.
4. Respond to the Round-4 governance-disclosure cross-check commitment (board-level climate-competence attestation against documented expertise vs. self-attestation) and recommend the documented envelope against the self-attestation envelope on the Evidence-Strength basis.
5. Design the Round-5 audit-opinion-framing commitment (modified opinion with explicit qualifications vs. clean opinion with management letter) and diagnose when audit integrity becomes a durable practice-building asset rather than a fee-preservation tax.

## The Protagonist

Siobhán O'Connor, 41, had assumed the Lead-ESG-Auditor role on the TerraGlow engagement on February 2, 2026, from a four-year rotation on the ClearView Financial Services sustainability-assurance practice where she had, across eight years, led two forensic-assurance engagements and had closed a 2023 greenwashing-detection investigation on an FTSE 250 consumer-goods issuer that had resulted in a modified opinion and a regulator referral to the Financial Reporting Council. Before ClearView she had spent five years as a Senior Manager at a Dublin-based boutique ESG-assurance firm. The Engagement Partner had staffed her specifically on two capabilities: her documented record of refusing to sign clean opinions on unverified Scope-3 data, and her technical fluency in the GHG-Protocol Category-15 methodology. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 25% to the Detection-Integrity outcome and 20% to the CSRD-Compliance Score — with an explicit independence-and-integrity gate that disqualified any compensation tied to fee retention.

## The Decision

O'Connor had to commit, by the May 5 engagement-committee pre-review, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–5.

1. **Round 1 — Scope-3 emissions recalculation.** Three archetypes: full-forensic (recalculate GHG-Protocol Category-15 emissions across the top-42 Tier-1-and-Tier-2 suppliers using primary supplier-reported data, test the 42% reduction claim against a reconstructed baseline, Detection-Integrity +28 points, Composite 76), spend-based-proxy (rely on spend-based Scope-3 proxy methodology as disclosed by TerraGlow — Composite 38, detection gap), or sampling (20% sample of Tier-1 suppliers without primary data expansion — Composite 56). Working envelope: full-forensic.
2. **Round 2 — Net-zero-claim testing.** Three archetypes: removal-credit-verification (chain-of-custody documentation on all removal credits referenced in the 2035 claim, tonnage-cross-check against the issuance register, removal-permanence assessment, Detection-Integrity +22, Composite 74), issuer-attestation (rely on issuer self-attestation — Composite 34, detection gap and CSRD-compliance risk), or aggregate-review (aggregate tonnage review without chain-of-custody — Composite 52). Working envelope: removal-credit-verification.
3. **Round 3 — Social-metric verification.** Three vectors: full-Tier-2-expansion (expand living-wage methodology to the 38% of Tier-2 suppliers excluded from TerraGlow's disclosure, re-test coverage against ILO methodology, Detection-Integrity +18, Composite 72), Tier-1-only (accept the TerraGlow Tier-1-only methodology — Composite 38), or sampling-expansion (30% sample of Tier-2 without full coverage — Composite 54). Working envelope: full-Tier-2-expansion.
4. **Round 4 — Governance-disclosure cross-check.** Three vectors: documented-competence (test board-level climate-competence attestation against documented training records, external directorships and climate-credential CVs, Evidence-Strength +14, Composite 74), self-attestation (accept TerraGlow self-attestation — Composite 38), or interview-only (director-interview evidence without documentary cross-check — Composite 56). Working envelope: documented-competence.
5. **Round 5 — Audit-opinion framing.** Three vectors: modified-opinion (modified limited-assurance opinion with explicit qualifications on Scope-3 methodology, net-zero-claim evidence, and Tier-2-exclusion pattern, Detection-Integrity sustained, Composite 80), clean-opinion-with-management-letter (clean opinion plus private management letter — Composite 32, integrity erosion and fee-retention-risk label), or disclaimed-opinion (disclaim opinion — Composite 54, engagement-termination risk and detection-integrity reputational gain). Working envelope: modified-opinion.

On O'Connor's working model, a Full-Forensic / Removal-Verification / Tier-2-Expansion / Documented-Competence / Modified-Opinion envelope delivered Detection-Integrity 82, Evidence-Strength 76, Client-Relationship 58, CSRD-Compliance 74, Composite 76. A Spend-Proxy / Issuer-Attestation / Tier-1-Only / Self-Attestation / Clean-Opinion envelope delivered Detection-Integrity 32, Evidence-Strength 36, Client-Relationship 76, CSRD-Compliance 38, Composite 30. A Sampling / Aggregate-Review / Sampling-Expansion / Interview-Only / Disclaimed-Opinion envelope delivered Composite 54.

## Company Background

ClearView Assurance had been founded in 2012 as a specialist European sustainability-assurance practice and had, across fourteen years, grown into a 340-auditor firm with offices in London, Dublin, Amsterdam, Frankfurt, Paris, Madrid and Milan. The 2024 expansion of the EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive had delivered a 2024–2026 engagement-book growth of 84% and had, on the London sustainability-assurance practice, created a fee-concentration pattern where five clients (including TerraGlow) accounted for 62% of practice fees — a pattern the ClearView engagement-quality committee had flagged in Q4 2025.

The Q1 2026 engagement dossier had identified three specific pressures the five-round envelope would inherit. First, the TerraGlow 2025 sustainability-report claimed a 42% Scope-3 reduction against a 2020 baseline that had been restated twice during the 2023–2025 horizon, with the restatements each time moving the baseline upward in a pattern consistent with baseline-inflation-for-reduction-percentage artefacts. Second, the net-zero-by-2035 claim referenced an aggregate removal-credit tonnage of 1.8MtCO2e across a portfolio where the ClearView pre-engagement review had been unable to locate chain-of-custody documentation for 0.7MtCO2e. Third, the living-wage methodology disclosed in the report excluded 38% of Tier-2 suppliers — the 38% that represented the lowest-income supplier cluster concentrated in four Southeast-Asian garment-and-textile sourcing regions.

## Industry and Market Context

The European ESG-assurance landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2023–2025 CSRD-limited-assurance evidence base had, across 86 CSRD engagements reviewed by regulators, documented a modified-opinion rate of 14% on Scope-3 claims above 30% reduction where forensic recalculation had been applied, against 2% where spend-based-proxy methodology had been accepted. Second, the 2022–2024 removal-credit chain-of-custody literature had, across 42 net-zero-claim investigations, documented a tonnage-gap of 22–38% between issuer-attested tonnage and chain-of-custody-verified tonnage. Third, the 2020–2024 living-wage methodology literature had, across 38 apparel and manufacturing sourcing-audit reviews, documented a Tier-2-exclusion pattern on 28–44% of supplier spend, concentrated in low-income sourcing regions.

Empirically, the post-2022 assurance literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, engagements that had recalculated Scope-3 against primary supplier data had, across a 2020–2024 sample of 48 engagements, preserved Detection-Integrity above 72 in 86% of cases against 18% for engagements that had relied on spend-based-proxy methodology. Second, engagements that had issued modified opinions with explicit qualifications had, across the same sample, sustained CSRD-Compliance Score above 68 in 82% of cases and had produced twelve-month practice-building outcomes (new clients + fee growth) comparable to engagements that had issued clean opinions — a finding that contradicted the fee-retention argument for clean opinions.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Engagement Partner had, in the November 2025 engagement-planning review, approved the scope and the June 20 sign-off calendar. The Assurance Manager had, on February 12, delivered the Scope-3 restatement-pattern analysis. The Technical Director had, on February 26, delivered the removal-credit chain-of-custody pre-engagement review. The Social-Assurance Manager had, on March 9, delivered the Tier-2-exclusion diagnostic. The Engagement-Quality Committee had, on March 20, flagged the 40% fee-concentration risk. The Independence Partner had, on March 27, confirmed the engagement could proceed under the fee-concentration-with-documented-safeguards protocol.

### What O'Connor knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. A Full-Forensic / Removal-Verification / Tier-2-Expansion / Documented-Competence / Modified-Opinion Envelope projected Composite 76. A Spend-Proxy / Issuer-Attestation / Tier-1-Only / Self-Attestation / Clean-Opinion Envelope projected Composite 30. A Sampling / Aggregate-Review / Sampling-Expansion / Interview-Only / Disclaimed-Opinion Envelope projected Composite 54.

### What she did not know

Whether the primary-supplier-data cooperation from the top-42 Tier-1-and-Tier-2 suppliers would be delivered inside the forensic window; whether the chain-of-custody documentation on the 0.7MtCO2e removal-credit gap would be recoverable in the 68-day window; whether the Engagement Partner would defend the modified-opinion framing against a TerraGlow request for a clean opinion with a management letter; and whether the TerraGlow CEO's public response to a modified opinion would trigger a practice-building backlash in the fee-concentration cohort.

### What others were saying

**The Technical Director**: *"Full-forensic on Scope-3. The spend-proxy methodology hides the baseline-inflation pattern."*

**The Social-Assurance Manager**: *"Tier-2 expansion. The thirty-eight percent excluded is the cluster the CSRD-compliance test is built to surface."*

**The Assurance Manager**: *"Modified-opinion with explicit qualifications. A clean opinion with a management letter is the fee-retention envelope and the practice cannot survive a second Fame-and-Shame."*

**The Engagement Partner**: *"Audits don't protect the auditor — integrity does. Ship the modified opinion."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

O'Connor had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Full-Forensic / Removal-Verification / Tier-2-Expansion / Documented-Competence / Modified-Opinion:** the recommended envelope. Detection-Integrity 82. Evidence-Strength 76. Client-Relationship 58. CSRD-Compliance 74. Composite 76. Recommended.
- **Option B — Spend-Proxy / Issuer-Attestation / Tier-1-Only / Self-Attestation / Clean-Opinion:** the fee-retention envelope. Composite 30. Rejected — Detection-Integrity and CSRD-Compliance collapse; regulator-referral risk.
- **Option C — Sampling / Aggregate-Review / Sampling-Expansion / Interview-Only / Disclaimed-Opinion:** the partial envelope. Composite 54. Rejected — forfeits the modified-opinion architecture without preserving client continuity.

O'Connor's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 1 — the full-forensic Scope-3 recalculation — because it was the round that surfaced the baseline-inflation pattern that every downstream round relied upon as evidence for the modified opinion.
