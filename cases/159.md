## Abstract

CONTINENTAL INSURANCE GROUP was, in the case universe, a Brussels-headquartered European multiline insurance group operating across 11 markets (Benelux, France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Portugal, Austria, Switzerland, Poland and the Nordics), with 14,200 employees, EUR 18.4B in 2025 gross written premium, a 2025 combined-operating-ratio of 94.8%, a 2025 AI-Adoption Index of 25 against a pan-European insurance peer-benchmark band of 48–62 (% of in-scope roles using approved AI tools weekly), an Engagement Index of 65 against a peer-benchmark of 68, a 2025 Middle-Manager Resistance Index of 68 against a peer-benchmark of 42 (higher is worse), a Psychological-Safety reading of 52 against a peer-benchmark of 66, and a 2025 productivity-uplift benchmark of 2.8% against a peer-benchmark of 6–9% on comparable deployed AI-adoption envelopes. The 2023–2025 transformation-operating model had been anchored on a top-down mandate discipline that had delivered communication cascades but had failed to unlock adoption inside the 1,240-middle-manager cohort that controlled claims, underwriting, customer-service and broker-network workflows across the 11 markets. The Q1 2026 group-executive committee had approved a EUR 38M transformation-rebuild envelope and a Chief-Transformation-Officer-led five-round commitment cycle against an AI-Adoption floor above 55, an Engagement floor above 70, a Middle-Manager Resistance ceiling below 38, a Psychological-Safety floor above 66, and a composite Transformation Score above 72. The case opens at 6:02 a.m. on Tuesday, April 14, 2026, at Continental's Brussels Avenue Louise office as **Clara Huygens**, a 45-year-old Chief Transformation Officer, prepares the Round-1 narrative-framing commitment. Seventy-six days separate her from the June 29 group-executive review.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Round-1 narrative-framing commitment (augmentation-first narrative with documented role-continuity commitment vs. productivity-cost-cut narrative) and diagnose when an augmentation envelope dominates a cost-cut envelope on the Middle-Manager Resistance and Engagement trade-off.
2. Analyze the Round-2 middle-manager enablement commitment (90-day cohort-based capability build with 1,240 middle-managers, peer-learning pods, protected practice time and dedicated coaches vs. top-down mandate with two-week go-live) and recommend the cohort envelope against the mandate envelope on the AI-Adoption and Resistance basis.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 pilot-to-scale sequencing commitment (six claim-adjudication-desks pilot with 14-week learning horizon and instrumentation vs. enterprise big-bang rollout) and recommend the pilot-to-scale envelope against the big-bang envelope on the twelve-month adoption basis.
4. Respond to the Round-4 psychological-safety-architecture commitment (no-reprisal questioning protocol, anonymous feedback channel reviewed monthly by the group-exec, manager-level psychological-safety metric published quarterly vs. engagement-survey-only) and recommend the architecture envelope against the survey envelope on the Psychological-Safety basis.
5. Design the Round-5 productivity-dashboard-credibility commitment (transparent productivity dashboard with outcome-based metrics, middle-manager co-authorship and audit visibility vs. opaque efficiency dashboard) and diagnose when AI rollout becomes a durable capability rather than a one-time programme.

On the morning of **Tuesday, April 14, 2026**, **Clara Huygens** arrived at the Continental Insurance Group Brussels Avenue Louise office at 6:02 a.m., opened the five-round AIChangeOps workbook her Head of Organisational Change and her Head of AI Capability had finalised at 10:54 p.m. the night before, and re-read the sentence she had pinned to her monitor since her January 2026 transformation offsite: *"AI rollouts fail in the middle of the org chart, not the top."* Seventy-six days separated her from the June 29 group-executive review.

---

## The Protagonist

Clara Huygens, 45, had assumed the Chief-Transformation-Officer role at Continental Insurance Group on October 6, 2025, from a Tier-1 European banking group where she had, across eleven years, led two enterprise AI-adoption programmes and had closed a 2023 AI-capability rebuild across a 19,800-employee retail-banking operation that had delivered an AI-Adoption uplift from 28 to 62 and a Middle-Manager Resistance reduction from 64 to 34 inside sixteen months. Before that she had spent six years as a Head of Change Management at a Paris-based multiline insurer. The Group-Executive Committee had recruited her specifically on two capabilities: her documented discipline of framing AI deployment as role-augmentation rather than role-replacement, and her willingness to protect middle-manager enablement timelines against board-level ship-date pressure. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the AI-Adoption outcome and 25% to the Middle-Manager Resistance ceiling.

---

## The Decision

Huygens had to commit, by the May 7 group-executive pre-review, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–5.

1. **Round 1 — Narrative framing.** Three archetypes: augmentation-first (AI-deployment framed as role-augmentation with documented role-continuity commitment to each of the 1,240 middle-managers, Middle-Manager Resistance −22 points, Composite 74), productivity-cost-cut (efficiency narrative citing headcount upside — Composite 26, resistance entrenchment), or mixed (augmentation copy with an efficiency target — Composite 44, credibility dilution). Working envelope: augmentation-first.
2. **Round 2 — Middle-manager enablement.** Three archetypes: cohort-based-90-day (1,240-middle-manager capability build across 62 cohorts of 20, peer-learning pods, protected practice time of 4 hours per week, dedicated coaches, AI-Adoption +26, Composite 76), top-down-mandate (two-week go-live with no retraining budget — Composite 22, adoption collapse), or self-serve (e-learning library with optional cohort — Composite 48). Working envelope: cohort-based-90-day.
3. **Round 3 — Pilot-to-scale sequencing.** Three vectors: six-desks-pilot (14-week pilot across six claim-adjudication desks with instrumentation, iterative workflow redesign, published learning report before Wave-2 scale — Composite 74), enterprise-big-bang (simultaneous 11-market rollout — Composite 22, adoption and learning-loop failure), or two-country-pilot (Benelux + France pilot for 9 months — Composite 58). Working envelope: six-desks-pilot.
4. **Round 4 — Psychological-safety architecture.** Three vectors: full-architecture (no-reprisal questioning protocol, anonymous feedback channel reviewed monthly by group-exec, manager-level psychological-safety metric published quarterly, Psychological-Safety +14, Composite 76), engagement-survey-only (annual engagement survey — Composite 38, durability gap), or partial (anonymous channel without manager-level metric — Composite 56). Working envelope: full-architecture.
5. **Round 5 — Productivity-dashboard credibility.** Three vectors: transparent-co-authored (outcome-based metrics, middle-manager co-authorship of dashboard definitions, audit visibility to the group-exec, Composite 78), opaque-efficiency (top-down efficiency dashboard without middle-manager co-authorship — Composite 38, credibility gap), or published-aggregate (aggregate metrics published without manager-level co-authorship — Composite 54). Working envelope: transparent-co-authored.

On Huygens's working model, an Augmentation / Cohort / Six-Desks-Pilot / Full-Safety / Transparent-Dashboard envelope delivered AI Adoption 62, Engagement 74, Middle-Manager Resistance 34, Psychological Safety 72, Composite 76. A Productivity-Cost-Cut / Top-Down / Big-Bang / Survey-Only / Opaque envelope delivered AI Adoption 18, Engagement 48, Middle-Manager Resistance 78, Psychological Safety 32, Composite 22. A Mixed / Self-Serve / Two-Country / Partial / Published-Aggregate envelope delivered Composite 50.

## Company Background

Continental Insurance Group had been founded in Brussels in 1958 through the merger of four Benelux mutual insurers and had, across sixty-eight years, grown into an 11-market multiline European insurance group with 14,200 employees, EUR 18.4B in gross written premium, and a product portfolio spanning P&C, life, health and commercial lines. The 2023–2025 transformation-operating model had delivered a baseline digitalisation programme at a cumulative EUR 126M spend but had produced an AI-Adoption reading of 25% against the 48–62% peer-benchmark band, a gap the Q3 2025 internal review had attributed to a middle-manager bottleneck rather than to tool availability or senior-executive commitment.

The Q1 2026 group-executive dossier had identified three specific pressures the five-round envelope would inherit. First, the Q4 2025 middle-manager survey across the 1,240-middle-manager cohort had surfaced that 68% of respondents flagged either 'no-time-to-learn' or 'fear-of-role-displacement' as dominant barriers to AI adoption — an answer distribution concentrated in the claims, underwriting and broker-relationship workflows that AI deployment was designed to re-shape. Second, the Q1 2026 AI-deployment audit had documented that 22 of 34 deployed tools had adoption-rates below 18% at the six-month mark, with middle-manager enablement cited in 19 of the 22 root-cause analyses. Third, the Q1 2026 benchmarking had documented that three pan-European multiline peers had delivered AI-Adoption uplift from below 30 to above 55 inside 18 months, in every case anchored on cohort-based middle-manager enablement and pilot-to-scale sequencing.

## Industry and Market Context

The European enterprise AI-adoption landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2022–2024 narrative-framing evidence base had, across 42 enterprise AI-adoption programmes, documented a Middle-Manager Resistance differential of 18–26 points between augmentation-first envelopes and productivity-cost-cut envelopes. Second, the 2020–2024 cohort-based enablement literature had, across 38 AI-adoption programmes, placed 90-day cohort envelopes at an AI-Adoption uplift of 20–30 points against top-down mandate envelopes. Third, the 2022–2024 pilot-to-scale sequencing literature had, across 32 enterprise AI deployments, placed six-to-nine-desk pilot envelopes at a twelve-month adoption preservation of 74% against 32% for enterprise big-bang envelopes.

Empirically, the post-2022 transformation literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, programmes that had installed no-reprisal questioning protocols plus anonymous feedback channels reviewed monthly by the group-exec had, across a 2019–2024 sample of 32 programmes, preserved Psychological-Safety above 66 in 84% of cases against 22% for programmes on engagement-survey-only architectures. Second, programmes that had co-authored productivity dashboards with middle-managers had sustained AI-Adoption trajectory above 55 at the eighteen-month mark in 78% of cases against 22% for programmes on opaque efficiency dashboards.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Group CEO had, in the November 2025 transformation review, approved the EUR 38M envelope and the June 29 review calendar. The Head of AI Capability had, on February 2, delivered the 34-tool AI-adoption audit. The Head of Organisational Change had, on February 16, delivered the middle-manager survey analysis. The Chief People Officer had, on March 2, delivered the Psychological-Safety baseline. The Chief Financial Officer had, on March 13, confirmed the EUR 38M envelope. The Head of Communications had, on March 20, delivered the narrative-framing draft.

### What Huygens knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. An Augmentation / Cohort / Six-Desks-Pilot / Full-Safety / Transparent-Dashboard Envelope projected Composite 76. A Productivity-Cost-Cut / Top-Down / Big-Bang / Survey-Only / Opaque Envelope projected Composite 22. A Mixed / Self-Serve / Two-Country / Partial / Published-Aggregate Envelope projected Composite 50.

### What she did not know

Whether the augmentation-first narrative would survive a H2 2026 combined-ratio compression that pressured the group-exec to reframe AI as a cost-cut story; whether the 62-cohort 90-day enablement schedule would clear against competing transformation priorities inside the 11-market calendar; whether the six-desks pilot learning report would surface workflow-redesign patterns that could be scaled to the 11-market 1,240-middle-manager cohort; and whether the psychological-safety architecture would be sustained by market-CEOs under Market-level quarterly pressure.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Organisational Change**: *"Augmentation-first. The moment the narrative flips to cost-cut, the 1,240 middle managers will go passive-aggressive and the adoption curve will freeze."*

**The Head of AI Capability**: *"Cohort-based enablement. Top-down mandate is what got us to twenty-five percent adoption with thirty-four tools deployed."*

**The Chief People Officer**: *"Full psychological-safety architecture. Survey-only is a reporting artefact, not an architecture."*

**The Group CEO**: *"AI rollouts fail in the middle of the org chart, not the top. Ship the cohort, ship the architecture."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Huygens had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Augmentation / Cohort / Six-Desks-Pilot / Full-Safety / Transparent-Dashboard:** the recommended envelope. AI Adoption 62. Engagement 74. Middle-Manager Resistance 34. Psychological Safety 72. Composite 76. Recommended.
- **Option B — Productivity-Cost-Cut / Top-Down / Big-Bang / Survey-Only / Opaque:** the status-quo envelope. Composite 22. Rejected — adoption and resistance collapse.
- **Option C — Mixed / Self-Serve / Two-Country / Partial / Published-Aggregate:** the partial envelope. Composite 50. Rejected — forfeits middle-manager enablement durability.

Huygens's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 2 — the 90-day cohort middle-manager enablement — because it was the round that converted the 1,240-middle-manager cohort from a bottleneck into the primary vector of AI-adoption propagation across the 11 markets.
