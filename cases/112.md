## Abstract

ARCTURUS GROUP was, in the case universe, a diversified industrial holding with USD 4.8B in 2025 revenue, two operating business units (BU North, a mature commodity-logistics operator; BU South, a mid-growth specialty-chemicals operator), seven power nodes at the executive committee level (CEO, CFO, CHRO, COO, CIO, GM of BU North, GM of BU South), and a ninety-day Project Meridian calendar that, on January 19, 2026, had framed three binding votes the group's newly promoted VP of Strategy would sit at the centre of. Helena Ardiles, 39, a former senior consultant who had joined Arcturus in 2022 as Director of Strategy and had been promoted to Vice President on December 15, 2025, had inherited from her predecessor an unresolved capital-allocation debate, an impending compensation-redesign proposal from the CHRO, and an open Chief Data Officer appointment. The sim scored the ninety-day window on two scoreboards — a Personal Score (the career trajectory Ardiles could still offer her next employer) and an Organization Score (the three-year operating trajectory Arcturus would inherit from the three votes) — because the 2018–2024 senior-executive literature had, across a peer-reviewed sample of seventy-three senior promotions, documented that leaders who optimised one scoreboard at the expense of the other had, in 64% of cases, been out of the company within eighteen months. The case opens at 5:56 a.m. on Tuesday, January 27, 2026, at the Arcturus HQ as Ardiles prepares the Round-1 capital-allocation vote.

On the morning of **Tuesday, January 27, 2026**, **Helena Ardiles** arrived at the Arcturus HQ on Calle Serrano at 5:56 a.m., opened the Round-1 sheet her Chief of Staff had finalised at 11:48 p.m. the night before, and re-read the sentence she had written in her December 2025 promotion-transition notebook: *"Influence compounds. So does resentment."* Twenty-eight days separated her from the February 24 board-review date for the Project Meridian commitments.

---

## The Protagonist

Helena Ardiles, 39, had joined Arcturus in March 2022 as Director of Strategy from a tier-one strategy consulting firm where she had, across eight years, led capital-allocation and M&A engagements for industrial holdings across Europe and LATAM. Before consulting, she had run a post-MBA industrial-finance rotation at a European development bank. The Chief Executive Officer had promoted her to Vice President on December 15, 2025, specifically on the strength of two capabilities: her experience navigating coalitional decisions across operating-company boundaries, and her reputation for refusing to win votes that cost her the next three votes. Her long-term incentive plan, signed at promotion, tied 40% of equity-vesting to a composite three-year Organization Score and 25% to a qualitative leadership-review metric — a compensation design that the CEO had explicitly anchored on the dual-scoreboard architecture.

---

## The Decision

Ardiles had to commit, by the February 24 board review, the Round-1 capital-allocation vote and the directional intent for Rounds 2 and 3.

1. **Round 1 — Capital Allocation** — whether to cast her swing vote for a BU North growth tilt (protecting the mature cash-flow engine and the GM North coalition), a BU South restructuring tilt (unlocking a specialty-chemicals pivot the CFO had partially endorsed), or a balanced envelope (splitting the decision down the middle and preserving optionality but forfeiting narrative clarity). The CFO was undecided. The GM North had privately threatened to escalate to the CEO if the tilt went South.
2. **Round 2 — Compensation Redesign** — how to respond to the CHRO's variable-pay proposal after it leaked to the trade press on the morning of February 9. Three vectors: distance herself from the proposal (Personal plus four, Organization minus seven), defend it publicly (Personal minus three, Organization plus three), or broker a revised design with the CEO and CFO (Personal minus six, Organization plus twelve) that preserved the variable-pay principle inside a governance wrapper the board could approve.
3. **Round 3 — Chief Data Officer Appointment** — whether to recommend the internal loyalist who owed her a favour from the 2024 portfolio-review (Personal plus eight, Organization plus two, data-maturity risk), the external star with the right sector track record (Personal minus four, Organization plus nine, onboarding risk), or the dark-horse internal candidate the CIO had quietly sponsored (Personal plus two, Organization plus five, defensibility risk).

The three rounds would compound into a single ninety-day trajectory. On Ardiles's working model, a BU-South-Tilt-plus-Broker-plus-External-Star envelope delivered Personal 72, Organization 84 and a "Statesperson" badge. A BU-North-Tilt-plus-Distance-plus-Internal-Loyalist envelope delivered Personal 81 but Organization 58 — the scoreboard the CEO's compensation design had been written to prevent. A Balanced-plus-Defend-plus-Dark-Horse envelope delivered Personal 64, Organization 68 and a "Survivor" badge — a trajectory the board would tolerate but not reward.

## Group Background

Arcturus Group had been built over four decades by the founding family from a single logistics operator into a diversified industrial holding with USD 4.8B in 2025 revenue, 14,200 employees, and two operating business units. BU North was a commodity-logistics operator with USD 3.1B in 2025 revenue, a Q4 2025 EBITDA margin of 9.2%, and a capital-intensity ratio of 0.34 that had held steady across 2022–2025. BU South was a specialty-chemicals operator with USD 1.7B in 2025 revenue, a Q4 2025 EBITDA margin of 13.8%, and a pending 2026 pivot into performance-additives that the outgoing VP of Strategy had scoped at a USD 220M three-year envelope.

The December 2025 board dossier had identified three specific pressures the ninety-day envelope would inherit. First, Project Meridian had been framed as the signature initiative of the 2026 operating plan; the three binding votes inside the ninety-day window would anchor the 2026–2028 operating trajectory. Second, the Q3 2025 executive-committee engagement survey had, across the seven-node ExCo, flagged CFO-CHRO trust as the weakest dyad in the power map — a signal that framed the Round-2 compensation-redesign broker commitment. Third, the Q1 2026 board-governance calendar had, on the February 24 board review, placed the three Project Meridian votes as the single item the non-executive directors had asked to be walked through — a calendar that placed the Round-3 CDO appointment squarely inside the board's field of view.

## Industry and Market Context

The senior-executive-political-coalition literature in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2018–2024 senior-promotion longitudinal evidence base had, across seventy-three VP-and-above promotions at diversified industrial holdings, documented that leaders who had optimised Personal Score at the expense of Organization Score had been out of the company within eighteen months in 64% of cases — a pattern that anchored the dual-scoreboard architecture. Second, the 2016–2024 coalition-building evidence base had, across fifty-one binding-vote sequences at executive-committee level, placed data-led one-on-one preparation at a trust-meter uplift of 1.8x against direct-ask preparation — a pattern that framed the Round-1 CFO-meeting architecture. Third, the 2015–2024 crisis-communication literature had, across thirty-six leak events, placed brokered-redesign responses at a two-year Organization Score uplift of 11–14 points against distance responses — a pattern that framed the Round-2 commitment.

Empirically, the post-2018 leadership literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, VPs who had protected organization outcomes on a first binding vote had, across a 2018–2023 sample, been 2.3x more likely to be granted the swing vote on a second binding issue than VPs who had won a first vote at an organizational cost. Second, right-hire commitments on senior-sponsor appointments had, across the same sample, closed eighteen-month retention ratios of 84% against 47% for loyalty-commitment appointments — a pattern that anchored the Round-3 external-star recommendation.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Chief Executive Officer had, at the December 2025 executive off-site, asked Ardiles to own the three Project Meridian votes end-to-end. The Chief Financial Officer had, on January 14, opened the capital-allocation debate with a written memorandum that tilted roughly 55–45 toward BU South on EVA projections but deferred the final call pending the Round-1 vote. The Chief Human Resources Officer had, on January 20, circulated the variable-pay proposal that would, by the Round-2 window, leak to the trade press. The Chief Information Officer had, on January 22, quietly sponsored the dark-horse CDO candidate. The General Manager of BU North had, on January 25, privately threatened to escalate to the CEO.

### What Ardiles knew

The team had modelled three envelopes. A BU-South-Tilt-plus-Broker-plus-External-Star Envelope projected Personal 72, Organization 84. A BU-North-Tilt-plus-Distance-plus-Internal-Loyalist Envelope projected Personal 81, Organization 58. A Balanced-plus-Defend-plus-Dark-Horse Envelope projected Personal 64, Organization 68.

### What she did not know

Whether the trade-press leak would re-surface during the Round-3 CDO appointment window and re-open the Round-2 trust-meter damage; whether the GM of BU North would escalate to the CEO inside the Round-1 window or hold fire to the Round-3 vote; whether the external CDO candidate would clear the governance-committee reference pass inside the February 24 window; and whether the CFO-CHRO dyad trust-meter would recover enough by Round 2 to hold the broker commitment.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Executive Officer**: *"I want both scoreboards. That is why I promoted you."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"The EVA math points South. I want to see the Round-1 commitment before I put my name on it."*

**The Chief Human Resources Officer**: *"I need a broker on the variable-pay proposal. Distance and I lose the compensation story for two years."*

**The General Manager of BU North**: *"If the tilt goes South without a BU-North-protection wrapper, I go to the CEO."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Ardiles had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — BU-South-Tilt (with BU-North-protection wrapper) / Broker / External-Star:** Round 1 votes South with a wrapper that protects GM North's capital envelope for two quarters. Round 2 brokers the CHRO's variable-pay proposal inside a governance frame. Round 3 recommends the external star with a ninety-day onboarding architecture. Personal 72. Organization 84. Statesperson. Recommended.

- **Option B — BU-North-Tilt / Distance / Internal-Loyalist:** Round 1 protects GM North. Round 2 distances from CHRO. Round 3 seats the loyalist. Personal 81. Organization 58. Rejected.

- **Option C — Balanced / Defend / Dark-Horse:** Round 1 balanced. Round 2 public defence. Round 3 CIO-sponsored dark horse. Personal 64. Organization 68. Survivor. Rejected.

Ardiles's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Organization Score was Round 1 — the BU-South tilt that, with a BU-North-protection wrapper, would unlock the CFO's public endorsement and fund the Round-2 broker commitment.
