## Abstract

Manufacturera ANDEX, a Colombian industrial-products company with three plants and USD 380 million in 2025 revenue, committed in late 2025 to a USD 22 million, three-round digital transformation. The new Chief Digital Officer, Laura Restrepo, inherited a sixteen-initiative catalogue across four families — AI, Logistics-IoT, Agile-Cloud, and Security — with explicit dependencies forming a directed acyclic graph, a Build/Buy/Partner sourcing choice on every initiative, and a separate six-action change-management panel. The Round 1 budget stood at USD 9M. A single operational fact governed the financials: actual ROI on any initiative equaled max ROI multiplied by adoption factor multiplied by sourcing multiplier. Adoption had to be earned. The case ends with Restrepo at her desk the day before the Round 1 submission, weighing three Round 1 packages against a known resistance event — a plant-manager-led revolt against IoT induction at Barranquilla.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate initiative sequencing in a portfolio with hard technical dependencies.
2. Analyze the interaction of sourcing-mode choice and change-management spend on realized ROI.
3. Design a change-management package targeted to identified resistance nodes.
4. Diagnose the adoption-gap problem — the gulf between "shipped" and "used" — in a manufacturing context.
5. Recommend a Round 1 initiative mix and defend the rationale under operational and financial challenge.

On the night of **February 23, 2026**, **Laura Restrepo** sat at the large oak table of the Manufacturera ANDEX executive conference room in Bogotá with sixteen printed initiative cards fanned across the surface. The cards were color-coded — blue for AI, green for Logistics-IoT, orange for Agile-Cloud, grey for Security. Seven carried a small red arrow indicating a prerequisite. One card — *L3: IoT Induction, Barranquilla* — carried, in Restrepo's own handwriting, the word *Ricardo*. The CEO would open the Round 1 package file the next morning at 7:30 a.m.

---

## The Protagonist

Laura Restrepo, 41, had joined ANDEX in October 2025 from a large Colombian food processor, where she had led a USD 40M digital program across eight plants. Before that she had spent six years in consulting and four years as Head of Operations Excellence at an Andean logistics company. The ANDEX board had recruited her over two internal candidates — the CIO and the COO — after fifteen months of visible stagnation in the previous digital program, which had been run out of the CIO's office. Her mandate, written into a one-page letter from the Chair, was short: "Deliver the transformation. Do not antagonize the plants." Her compensation carried a three-year vesting tranche tied to a Digital Transformation Index (DTI) and a separate gate on Employee Engagement Index that prevented full vesting if engagement fell below a defined floor.

---

## The Decision

Restrepo had to submit, by 7:30 a.m. on February 24, a single Round 1 package covering April–December 2026. The package had three dimensions:

1. **Initiative selection:** which of the sixteen initiatives to fund in Round 1, subject to dependency constraints (e.g., A4 requires A1; S3 requires S1).
2. **Sourcing mode per initiative:** Build internally (cheaper in dollars, slower, higher ownership), Buy (one-to-one), or Partner (faster adoption, lower ownership).
3. **Change-management allocation:** distribution of up to USD 1.8M across six actions — Executive Sponsor Events, Manager Training, Champion Network, Communications, Quick-Win Showcase, External Consultant.

The round budget ceiling was USD 9M. Any Round-1 initiative not funded would consume its dependency slot in Round 2. Round-2 budget was USD 7M; Round 3, USD 6M.

---

## Company Background

ANDEX was a Colombian producer of industrial valves, pumps, and flow-control equipment for the oil & gas and water-infrastructure sectors. Founded in 1978 by the Andrade family, it operated three plants — Bogotá (headquarters and precision machining), Cali (assembly and testing), and Barranquilla (casting and rough machining) — and distributed through a direct-sales force and three distribution partners across the Andean region and Central America. 2025 revenue was USD 380M with an EBITDA margin of 11.8%. The Andrade family held 58% of the equity; a regional PE fund held 24% (since 2020); float and employees held the remainder.

The three plants had distinct cultures. Bogotá was engineering-led, Cali was efficiency-led under a reformist plant manager, and Barranquilla — the oldest plant, dating from 1981 — was dominated by a long-tenured plant manager, Ricardo Vargas, who had, in eighteen years, successfully resisted every central initiative he had judged "imposed by headquarters."

Technology infrastructure was heterogeneous. Bogotá had a 2022 ERP upgrade. Cali ran an older version of the same ERP and a custom MES. Barranquilla ran a hybrid of paper and a spreadsheet-based production tracker. Previous transformation attempts — a 2019 predictive-maintenance pilot and a 2022 plant-floor IoT rollout — had succeeded in Cali, partially succeeded in Bogotá, and failed in Barranquilla.

## Industry and Market Context

The Latin American industrial-products sector had entered 2026 under three pressures. First, customers — especially oil majors and water utilities — had begun to condition tenders on supplier-side digital-maturity certifications (ISA/IEC 62443, ISO 55001) that presumed connected-asset infrastructure. Second, peer competition from Brazilian and Mexican firms had intensified as those countries' digital investment had begun to translate into lower cycle times and lower defect rates. Third, cyber-security standards had tightened: a February 2025 regulatory directive in Colombia required critical-infrastructure suppliers to demonstrate ISA/IEC 62443-2-1 compliance within 24 months.

Research on digital transformations in Latin American manufacturing converged on two findings. First, realized ROI averaged approximately 55–70% of business-case ROI, with adoption failure the largest driver. Second, plants with strong local leadership on change management outperformed plants with equivalent technology spend but weaker change management by approximately 2.5x on realized outcomes.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The previous ANDEX digital program, run by the CIO between 2022 and 2025, had delivered a modest ERP upgrade in Bogotá and then stalled. The board's October 2025 decision to retask the program under a new CDO was accompanied by a doubled budget (USD 22M total, up from USD 9M) and a three-round structure that prevented a single-year "everything-at-once" release.

### What Restrepo knew

The sixteen-initiative catalogue had been developed by Restrepo's own team in January–February 2026 after plant visits and functional workshops. Dependencies were mapped and validated. The initiatives with the highest business case were A4 (Demand-Forecast AI, requires A1 Data Lake), L4 (Predictive Maintenance, requires L2 Plant-Floor IoT at all three sites), and AG3 (Cloud-Native Quality System, requires AG1 Cloud Foundation). The change-management survey run in January had flagged Barranquilla as high-risk on the IoT initiatives.

### What Restrepo did not know

Whether Ricardo Vargas, the Barranquilla plant manager, could be won over, worked around, or removed — and which choice the CEO would back. Whether the ISA/IEC 62443 tender window, expected to open in Q3 2026 from a large oil-major customer, would in fact materialize at that time. Whether the CFO, Pablo Herrán, would hold Round 1 to strict budget or allow a 10–15% Round-1 overage against Round 2 reserves.

### What others were saying

**Pablo Herrán**, CFO, pressed for a low-risk Round 1. "Funded foundation — Data Lake, Cloud Foundation, Security baseline. Pilot in Cali only. Buy where possible. No Partner above USD 1.5M. Change management at minimum until Round 2."

**Carolina Beltrán**, COO, pressed for plant-pull initiatives. "IoT at Cali. Predictive Maintenance pilot. If we skip the plant wins we lose the operators. Ricardo Vargas must be handled — by me, not by a consultant."

**Marco Zapata**, CIO (Restrepo's internal rival), pressed for AI-first concentration. "A1 Data Lake, A2 ML Ops, A4 Demand Forecast. Build internally. The rest waits. Our engineers can do it."

---

## Options Under Consideration

- **Option A — Foundation-First (the Herrán plan):** A1 Data Lake (Buy, USD 2.2M), AG1 Cloud Foundation (Partner, USD 1.8M), S1 Security Baseline (Buy, USD 1.4M), AG2 DevOps Enablement (Build, USD 1.2M), L1 MES Upgrade Cali only (Buy, USD 1.8M). Change-management spend USD 0.6M (Executive Sponsor + Communications). Round 1 spend USD 9.0M. No initiative at Barranquilla.

- **Option B — Balanced-with-Change (the Beltrán plan):** A1 Data Lake (Buy, USD 2.2M), AG1 Cloud Foundation (Partner, USD 1.8M), L2 Plant-Floor IoT Cali (Buy, USD 1.0M), L2 Plant-Floor IoT Bogotá (Buy, USD 1.0M), S1 Security Baseline (Buy, USD 1.4M). Change-management spend USD 1.6M (Executive Sponsor, Manager Training at all plants, Champion Network at Barranquilla, Quick-Win Showcase). Round 1 spend USD 9.0M.

- **Option C — AI-First (the Zapata plan):** A1 Data Lake (Build, USD 2.8M), A2 ML Ops Foundation (Build, USD 2.2M), A4 Demand Forecast (Build, USD 2.6M, requires A1), AG1 Cloud Foundation (Partner, USD 1.0M). Change-management spend USD 0.4M. Round 1 spend USD 9.0M. Highest stated business case; lowest adoption readiness; Barranquilla untouched in Round 1.

(See **Exhibit 3** for modeled DTI and adoption trajectories under each option and **Exhibit 4** for the change-management action-to-adoption-point mapping.)

---

Restrepo stacked the sixteen cards and slid them into a folder. The CEO would open the file at 7:30. She had the night to choose among Option A, Option B, and Option C — and to decide whether to call Ricardo Vargas before the Round 1 package hit his plant. The formula on the board did not change: realized ROI equaled max ROI times adoption times sourcing multiplier. The code was the cheapest part.
