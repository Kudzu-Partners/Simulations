## Abstract

CARDIVEX, a mid-cap U.S. pharmaceutical company, was ninety days from the commercial launch of *empavatin* — a novel Type-2 Diabetes agent with differentiated cardiovascular outcomes data — and three years from the expiry of its composition-of-matter patent. The Commercial Launch Director, Anika Rao, held an eighteen-million-dollar launch budget, six levers — market access, medical affairs, salesforce, brand, patient support, and lifecycle — and a pre-launch round in which one of those levers carried a hard floor: a market-access outlay below USD 1.5M in Round 1 would permanently exclude *Payer Beta*, the second-largest commercial payer in the U.S. A single competitor, *GLORIAX*, had launched nine months earlier, had captured 14% market share, and was already calling CARDIVEX's top-200 prescriber list. The *Launch Excellence Index* (LEI) — a composite of reach, formulary share, persistence, awareness, and lifecycle defense — would be the final grade at Round 5. The case opens on April 20, 2026, as Rao prepares the Round-1 budget request for the April 27 Commercial Council.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Apply the launch-excellence framework to a pre-launch budget allocation across six commercial levers.
2. Evaluate the strategic value of a market-access outlay with a discontinuous threshold effect (permanent payer exclusion) under budget constraint.
3. Analyze the interaction between Round-2 competitor-response decisions and Round-1 pre-launch sequencing.
4. Diagnose the value of early lifecycle-management investment (Round 2) as an option against the Month-36 patent cliff.
5. Recommend a Round-1 allocation and articulate the Round-2 condition that would force a re-tune.

On the morning of **Monday, April 20, 2026**, **Anika Rao** stood in her office at CARDIVEX's commercial headquarters in Lawrenceville, New Jersey, and read, for the seventh time, the one-page launch-readiness brief her Launch Program Manager had emailed at 6:41 a.m. The brief carried six numbers: *Budget USD 18.0M. Revenue 0. Market share 0%. LEI 0. Formulary 0%. KOL engagement 0.* Below those numbers, in bold red: *"Payer Beta Round-1 floor: USD 1.5M. Below → permanent exclusion."*

## The Protagonist

Anika Rao, 42, had been Commercial Launch Director at CARDIVEX since January 2025. A graduate of Johns Hopkins with a PharmD and of the Wharton School with an MBA, she had spent fifteen years in pharmaceutical commercialization: six years at a global company across brand-management and market-access roles, four years as Senior Director for a specialty-diabetes franchise at a top-five manufacturer, and five years in a succession of launch-director roles at three mid-caps. She had led two prior launches: one diabetes agent (a Me-Too entering a crowded class) that had reached a Launch Excellence Index of 62, and one first-in-class cardiorenal agent that had reached LEI 81. The empavatin launch was her largest budget, her most competitive class, and her first drug with a discrete thirty-six-month patent horizon from the launch date.

---

## The Decision

Rao had to recommend, by 9:00 a.m. on April 27, the Round-1 (Pre-Launch) allocation of USD 18.0M across six levers, in units of USD 250,000.

1. **Lever A — Market Access & HEOR.** Payer-facing activity: ICER modeling, budget-impact models, risk-sharing agreement design, formulary-committee engagement. Hard floor: USD 1.5M in Round 1 to preserve *Payer Beta* eligibility; a standard practitioner ceiling at USD 3.0M for Round 1.
2. **Lever B — Medical Affairs & KOL.** Scientific-affairs activity: KOL identification, advisory boards, investigator-initiated trials, medical education. Empirical Round-1 median for a cardiometabolic agent at this scale is USD 1.0–1.5M.
3. **Lever C — Salesforce.** Field-force build, training, and deployment. In Round 1 this was primarily pre-launch training and territory design; the first live detailing call would be Round 2 (Launch). Round-1 practitioner median USD 0.5–1.0M.
4. **Lever D — Brand & Promotion.** Brand identity, HCP promotion, DTC campaign design (DTC does not go live until Round 3). Round-1 practitioner median USD 0.5–1.0M.
5. **Lever E — Patient Support.** Adherence program design, co-pay program setup, nurse-educator network, digital engagement platform. Round-1 practitioner median USD 0.25–0.75M.
6. **Lever F — Lifecycle Management.** Investment in the Round-5 lifecycle defense — a line extension (empavatin XR, a once-daily modified-release formulation), an authorized-generic partnership, and separate composition-of-matter claim prosecution. Round-1 practitioner median USD 0.25–0.75M, Round-2 median USD 1.0–2.0M.

The six levers would be re-allocated at each of the four subsequent rounds along a pre-committed event calendar: Round 2 — Launch Day (competitor GLORIAX's top-200 attack, adverse-event signal management). Round 3 — Uptake Conversion (formulary status locked; convert access into prescriptions). Round 4 — Month-12 Diagnostic (reach, formulary, persistence, awareness data review; one underperformance identified and addressed). Round 5 — Patent Cliff Defense (three generic filings; lifecycle plan executed).

## Company Background

CARDIVEX was a mid-cap specialty pharmaceutical company headquartered in New Jersey with 2025 revenue of USD 820M and 1,800 employees, including a 260-person commercial-field organization and an established medical-affairs capability in cardiometabolic disease. Its portfolio comprised four marketed agents, three of which were in cardiorenal and metabolic therapy areas; empavatin would be the fourth. The company had been spun out of a larger parent in 2018 and had traded publicly since 2020.

Empavatin had been approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration on March 14, 2026, with a broad Type-2 Diabetes indication and a cardiovascular-outcomes label addition that the FDA had granted on the strength of a 14,200-patient randomized trial completed in 2024. The commercial launch date was set for July 1, 2026. The wholesale acquisition cost (WAC) had been set at USD 622/month, positioned at a 6% discount to GLORIAX's list price. The commercial payer mix the Finance team had modeled estimated 38% of covered lives at *Payer Alpha* (the largest U.S. commercial payer), 24% at *Payer Beta*, 18% at Medicare Part D, 11% at Medicaid, and 9% at smaller PBMs and regional plans. Payer Alpha accepted ICERs up to USD 25,000 per QALY; Payer Beta required budget-neutrality plus a risk-share arrangement.

The competitor GLORIAX had launched in the same class on October 1, 2025, without a cardiovascular-outcomes label. By Q1 2026 GLORIAX had reached 14% market share and had been added to Payer Alpha's preferred formulary at tier 2.

## Industry and Market Context

The U.S. cardiometabolic drug market entered 2026 under five simultaneous pressures. First, commercial-payer decision timelines had compressed: a Round-1 market-access outlay below the discontinuous threshold had produced permanent payer exclusion in three of the twelve launches studied by the CARDIVEX Market Access function in 2023–2025. Second, GLP-1-class drugs had redefined the competitive landscape, with both class-internal competition and cross-class substitution intensifying across metabolic therapy areas. Third, persistence — the share of patients continuing therapy at 12 months — had become the most-watched commercial KPI in diabetes; a launch that over-indexed on reach and under-indexed on adherence had, in four of seven recent launches, underperformed revenue projections by 18–24%. Fourth, direct-to-consumer promotion had, despite regulatory scrutiny, returned as a meaningful driver in diabetes, with a typical DTC-adjusted persistence lift of 4–6 percentage points. Fifth, patent-cliff defense had moved from the two-year horizon to the Round-1 conversation: launches that had pre-committed a line-extension plan at pre-launch had, in the 2019–2025 cohort, preserved 28–36% more post-expiry revenue than launches that deferred lifecycle investment to Year 2 of the brand plan.

Empirically, cardiometabolic launches that had preserved both payers' formulary eligibility through Round 1 and had reached a Round-4 persistence above 55% had achieved LEI scores in the 70–80 band; launches that had lost a major payer in Round 1 had been capped at LEI 60 regardless of subsequent performance.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The CARDIVEX Commercial Council had, in its January 2026 off-site, approved the USD 18.0M Round-1 envelope and a commitment to the LEI as the composite launch metric. Payer Alpha's ICER acceptance had been confirmed in a private meeting on March 4. Payer Beta's Round-1 floor had been established through a pre-read meeting on March 28. GLORIAX had, on April 11, announced a patient-loyalty card program targeting the same physician-prescriber base empavatin would attack.

### What Rao knew

The Market Access team had modeled four Round-1 packages against a forward five-round scenario. A conservative package (A USD 1.5M, B USD 1.0M, C USD 1.0M, D USD 0.75M, E USD 0.5M, F USD 0.25M, residual USD 13.0M carried forward) preserved Payer Beta, starved KOL engagement, and delivered a projected LEI of 64. A balanced package (A USD 2.0M, B USD 1.25M, C USD 0.75M, D USD 0.5M, E USD 0.25M, F USD 0.25M, residual USD 13.0M) preserved Payer Beta, KOL-tested well, and delivered LEI 72. An aggressive-access package (A USD 2.5M, B USD 1.75M, C USD 0.5M, D USD 0.5M, E USD 0.25M, F USD 0.25M, residual USD 12.25M) maximized the Round-1 payer position at the cost of Round-2 salesforce readiness, LEI 69. A lifecycle-forward package (A USD 1.5M, B USD 1.0M, C USD 0.75M, D USD 0.5M, E USD 0.25M, F USD 0.75M, residual USD 13.25M) preserved Payer Beta, under-funded KOL, and over-funded Round-1 lifecycle — LEI 70 with a 2-point Round-5 defense lift.

### What she did not know

Whether Payer Alpha's ICER-acceptance decision would be re-reviewed in Round 2 under the GLORIAX-loyalty-card pressure; whether the expected GI-adverse-event cluster in the first 500 patients would, if it materialized in Round 2, force additional medical-affairs outlay; and whether the Round-4 persistence outcome would behave like the cohort median or like the underperforming quartile.

### What others were saying

**Ethan Park**, Vice President of Market Access, argued for the **aggressive-access package**: "Buy Payer Beta permanently. Spend USD 2.5M on access Round 1. We can rebuild KOL in Round 2."

**Maria Santoro**, Vice President of Medical Affairs, argued for the **balanced package**: "KOL is the persistence engine. Under-fund us now and the Round-4 persistence number will punish us."

**Jared Lo**, Vice President of Salesforce, argued for the **balanced package with a Round-2 salesforce front-loading commitment**: "Round 1 is training. Round 2 is the door. Commit the salesforce ramp for Round 2 now in the Round-1 framing."

**Vikram Menon**, Vice President of Lifecycle Management, argued for the **lifecycle-forward package**: "The patent cliff is thirty-six months. Do not treat Round 5 as a Round-5 decision."

---

## Options Under Consideration

Rao had narrowed the space to three concrete Round-1 packages.

- **Option A — Balanced Pre-Launch (the Santoro-Lo midpoint):** A USD 2.0M, B USD 1.25M, C USD 0.75M, D USD 0.5M, E USD 0.25M, F USD 0.25M, residual USD 13.0M for Rounds 2–5. Preserves Payer Beta. Projected LEI 72.

- **Option B — Aggressive Access (the Park plan):** A USD 2.5M, B USD 1.75M, C USD 0.5M, D USD 0.5M, E USD 0.25M, F USD 0.25M, residual USD 12.25M. Maximum payer position Round 1, salesforce under-readiness Round 2. Projected LEI 69.

- **Option C — Lifecycle-Forward (the Menon plan):** A USD 1.5M, B USD 1.0M, C USD 0.75M, D USD 0.5M, E USD 0.25M, F USD 0.75M, residual USD 13.25M. Preserves Payer Beta at the floor; invests early in lifecycle. Projected LEI 70 with a 2-point Round-5 boost.

Rao's own instinct, not yet shared with the Commercial Council, was that Option A dominated on the central case and that Option C dominated on the fat tail where the patent-cliff defense outperformed — and that the real question was whether to pre-commit a Round-2 salesforce ramp that would make Option A survive the GLORIAX-loyalty response without sacrificing the Round-5 lifecycle spend. She had drafted, in her notebook, a single sentence: *"A pharma launch is not six decisions. It is six bets, placed in the right sequence."*

She closed the brief. The Commercial Council convened at 9:00 a.m. April 27. The launch was seventy-two days away. Payer Beta's Round-1 floor was seven days away.
