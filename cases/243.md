## Abstract

Cascade Industries Inc. was, in the case universe, an NYSE-listed Chicago-headquartered industrial diversified operator with USD 3.2B 2025 revenue, 11,800 employees across fourteen manufacturing plants and three shared-service centres in the United States, Mexico and Poland, a 2018 IPO that had brought the entity into the Section 404 population, and a 2026 SOX program in its eighth attestation cycle. The 2024–2025 cycle had left the entity at a mature-but-stretched ICFR posture: the SOX control library contained 1,240 controls (468 classified as key, 772 as non-key) across ten in-scope processes, the management-assessment had produced zero material weaknesses for three consecutive cycles, and the external auditor had relied on management's testing for approximately 62% of its Section 404 attestation work. The Q1 2026 context had compounded three pressures: a 22% year-on-year increase in the manual-journal-entry population from the 2025 ERP consolidation; an emergent pattern of PP&E-disclosure findings surfaced during the Q4 2025 walkthrough refresh; and a revised PCAOB inspection focus on entity-level controls and journal-entry review that had, in the 2025 inspection cycle, driven a spike in significant-deficiency findings industry-wide. The case opens at 7:22 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Cascade Industries Chicago finance floor as **Marissa Okonkwo-Halvorsen**, a 46-year-old SOX Program Director, prepares the ten-process SOXmap commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the ten-process SOX 404 scope against the five financial-statement assertions (Existence / Occurrence, Completeness, Valuation / Accuracy, Rights & Obligations, Presentation & Disclosure) and articulate why each control should be tagged to one primary assertion rather than all five.
2. Analyse the key-versus-non-key classification heuristic (a key control is one whose failure would cause the financial statements to be materially misstated) and apply the classification to the three-way match, the quarterly JE review, the physical-inventory count and the disclosure-committee checklist.
3. Diagnose the PCAOB AS 2201 sample-size convention (key quarterly = 8, key monthly = 15–25, key weekly = 25–40, key daily = 40–60, non-key quarterly = 2, non-key monthly = 5) and apply it to the Cascade ten-process scope.
4. Respond to the three significant deficiencies (SD1 on manual-journal-entry review, SD2 on PP&E disclosure completeness, SD3 on access management in the consolidated ERP environment) and articulate the remediation-plus-re-test envelope that keeps the aggregation below the material-weakness threshold.
5. Position the SOXmap frame inside the 2026 Express risk catalogue: SOXmap is the preparer-side public-company ICFR attestation, complementary to and distinct from sim 240 AuditTrail (the external-audit seat at Halstead & Co on a private-company engagement), sim 241 ControlMatrix (the internal-audit planner at NovaMart) and sim 242 DigiAudit (the IT-audit-of-record at Meridian Trust).

## The Protagonist

Okonkwo-Halvorsen, 46, had assumed the SOX Program Director role at Cascade Industries on June 1, 2022, after eleven years across the external-audit practice of a Big Four firm and seven years in controllership at two NYSE-listed industrials. She had, in 2019–2021, led the post-IPO SOX readiness work at a Houston-headquartered energy services operator that had cleared three consecutive unqualified ICFR attestations, and had authored the internal practitioner guide *Ten Processes, Five Assertions, One Attestation* that had subsequently been adopted as the Cascade SOX program's reference rubric. The Chief Accounting Officer had recruited her in April 2022 specifically on two capabilities: her documented discipline of tagging controls to a single primary assertion and forcing the difficult conversations about classification at the scoping stage; and her willingness to carry a significant-deficiency finding into the attestation cycle with an evidenced remediation plan rather than to negotiate it into a non-deficiency category. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 35% to the external-auditor-reliance percentage, 20% to the documentation-quality reading and 20% to the zero-material-weakness record.

## The Decision

Okonkwo-Halvorsen had to commit, by the 4:30 p.m. SOX Steering Committee pre-brief, the ten-process SOXmap envelope and the directional intent for the February 2027 10-K attestation.

1. **Process 1 — Order-to-Cash.** Three key controls (credit-limit check, revenue cut-off, A/R aging review); assertion tags Existence, Cut-off, Valuation; AS-2201 samples 8 + 25 + 8.
2. **Process 2 — Procure-to-Pay.** Three-way match (key; Existence + Valuation), goods-receipt check (key; Completeness), PO approval (non-key; Existence). Samples 40 + 25 + 5.
3. **Process 3 — Financial Close.** Manual-journal-entry review (key, quarterly, sample 8); management-estimate review (key, quarterly, sample 8); consolidation-check (key, monthly, sample 15).
4. **Process 4 — Inventory.** Physical-count reconciliation (key, quarterly, sample 8); cycle-count sampling (non-key, weekly, sample 25).
5. **Process 5 — Capex.** Capex-authorisation review (key, project-based, sample 40 projects); capitalisation-versus-expense check (key, monthly, sample 15).
6. **Process 6 — Tax.** Tax-provision review (key, quarterly, sample 8); deferred-tax computation review (key, quarterly, sample 8).
7. **Process 7 — Access Management.** User-provisioning review (key, monthly, sample 15); privileged-access review (key, quarterly, sample 8).
8. **Process 8 — Financial Reporting.** Account reconciliations (key, monthly, sample 25); sub-ledger-to-GL reconciliation (key, monthly, sample 15).
9. **Process 9 — FX Translation.** Translation-rate review (key, monthly, sample 15); intercompany elimination (key, monthly, sample 15).
10. **Process 10 — PP&E and Disclosures.** PP&E roll-forward review (key, quarterly, sample 8); disclosure-committee checklist (key, quarterly, sample 8).

On Okonkwo-Halvorsen's working model, the ten-process SOXmap envelope produced three significant deficiencies (SD1 manual-journal-entry review, SD2 PP&E disclosure completeness, SD3 ERP access management) and zero material weaknesses. Aggregation analysis under AS 2201 paragraph 69 placed the combined severity below the material-weakness threshold. The documentation-quality reading landed at 86%. The external-auditor-reliance percentage trended to 64%, one point above the 2025 cycle.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2010–2025 SOX 404 literature on NYSE-listed industrials produced three findings the program had to respect. First, post-ERP-consolidation cycles produced at least one significant deficiency on manual-journal-entry review in the first post-consolidation cycle in 64% of documented cases, and cleared it with evidenced remediation in the subsequent cycle in 42%. Second, PP&E-disclosure completeness was the fastest-growing significant-deficiency category between 2022 and 2025, ~11% of all new SDs reported on industrial issuers in 2025. Third, aggregation analysis under AS 2201 paragraph 69 was the single most-contested item between preparers and external auditors on cycles producing two or more SDs.

The post-2022 PCAOB inspection literature added two findings. Inspections increasingly focused on entity-level controls and journal-entry review, driving a 34% increase in SD findings on those domains between 2022 and 2025. And external-auditor-reliance percentages drifted from a 2020 peer average of 68% to a 2025 peer average of 58% — placing Cascade's 64% reliance one standard deviation above peer.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The SOX Steering Committee approved the eighth-cycle ICFR program in October 2025. The ten-process scoping refresh, the access-management remediation plan for the consolidated ERP, the manual-journal-entry population analysis and the external-auditor Q2 walkthrough schedule had all landed by late March.

### What Okonkwo-Halvorsen knew

Ten processes, 468 key controls and 772 non-key controls in the library; AS 2201 sample sizes for the key controls summed to roughly 1,920 tested items across the year; three significant deficiencies were credible findings that survived aggregation without crossing into material-weakness territory; and the 86% documentation-quality reading was the inner number that the external-auditor-reliance percentage would be anchored to. The 2025 PCAOB inspection pattern meant journal-entry review would be scrutinised.

### What she did not know

Whether the external audit partner would accept the aggregation analysis as keeping the three SDs below the material-weakness threshold or push one of them into the MW category; whether the Q3 2026 walkthrough refresh would produce a fourth SD on the disclosure-committee process; whether the 22% increase in the manual-journal-entry population would be fully absorbed by the remediated JE-review control before year-end; and whether the PP&E disclosure completeness finding would recur in the 2026 cycle once the remediation was in place.

### What others were saying

**The SOX Program Manager**: *"Tag each control to one primary assertion. Three-way match is Existence and Valuation. Goods receipt is Completeness. That discipline is what keeps the library testable."*

**The IT-SOX Lead**: *"SD3 is access management. The consolidated ERP environment introduced new privileged-access roles. Remediation is in flight. We re-test in Q3."*

**The Chief Accounting Officer**: *"Three SDs is fine. Four is not. Keep the aggregation below the material-weakness threshold and the 10-K attestation is clean."*

**The External Audit Partner**: *"I'll rely on management's testing for the processes where documentation quality is above eighty. Below eighty I'll re-perform."*

## Options Under Consideration

Okonkwo-Halvorsen had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Ten-process mapping / three SDs / zero MWs / 86% documentation / 64% external-auditor reliance:** the recommended envelope. Aggregation below MW threshold, evidence-grounded, cycle-over-cycle improvement visible. Recommended.
- **Option B — Aggressive-scope envelope (1,360 controls including four additional non-key):** the risk-averse variant. Rejected — over-scopes the program, erodes the external-auditor-reliance percentage as documentation quality drops.
- **Option C — Narrow-scope envelope (1,140 controls, several non-key pruned):** the efficiency-first variant. Rejected — pushes one of the three SDs toward material-weakness territory under aggregation analysis.

Her private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the process most correlated with the eventual attestation posture was Process 3 — Financial Close — because the manual-journal-entry review was both the largest remediation-lift and the control the 2025 PCAOB inspection pattern had most scrutinised. The SOXmap frame is explicitly positioned as the *public-company preparer-side ICFR attestation* inside the 2026 Express risk catalogue: it is complementary to and distinct from sim 240 AuditTrail (external audit at Halstead & Co on a private-company engagement with different standards), sim 241 ControlMatrix (internal-audit planner at NovaMart, broader scope, not ICFR-specific) and sim 242 DigiAudit (IT-audit-of-record at Meridian Trust, 200-hour engagement framework). ControlMatrix surfaces internal controls across the enterprise; SOXmap proves them to the SEC.
