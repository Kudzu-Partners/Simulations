## Abstract

Andina Industrial S.A. was, in the case universe, a LATAM diversified industrial group headquartered in Bogotá with specialty-chemicals and industrial-equipment operations across Colombia, Argentina, Brazil, and Mexico and USD-denominated bond debt listed in New York, 2025 group revenue of USD 2.68B, EBITDA of USD 368M, net debt of USD 1.06B, and a Net-Debt/EBITDA covenant ceiling of 3.50x against a Q4 2025 pro-forma ratio of 2.88x. On Thursday, January 22, 2026, the Argentine central bank executed a thirty-five-percent step devaluation of the official rate; Andina's Argentine subsidiary carried USD 42M of annual ARS-denominated revenue, ARS 800M of cash on balance-sheet, and no FX hedge. Juliana Palacios, CFO since October 2024, faced six quarterly decision rounds across 2026 and the first half of 2027 inside a macro-volatility envelope the Board had repeatedly framed as "earnings stability." The case opens at 5:48 a.m. on Monday, January 26, 2026, as Palacios prepares the Q1 Treasury package for the February 6 Audit Committee.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Q1 response to an Argentine step devaluation — repricing-USD-linked clauses, operational-hedge conversion of ARS cash, Board disclosure — and design a forward-looking Argentine treasury posture.
2. Analyze the Q2 Brazilian SELIC-and-BRL shock, diagnose when to hold versus monetize an in-the-money interest-rate swap, and design a term-loan-refinancing posture.
3. Diagnose the Q3 Mexican peso-appreciation dynamic and design a layered MXN hedging program that separates translation from operating performance in Board guidance.
4. Design the Q4 Colombian downgrade-and-windfall-tax response — RCF renegotiation, tranche pre-payment, natural-hedge restructuring, technical-memorandum engagement.
5. Recommend the Q5 covenant-breach response — proactive waiver versus quiet absorption — and identify the Q6 hedging-policy ratification that locks in the playbook.

On the morning of **Monday, January 26, 2026**, **Juliana Palacios** sat at the CFO's desk on the twenty-seventh floor of the Andina Industrial tower in Bogotá and opened the Treasury dashboard the Climate-Analytics-and-Treasury lead had emailed at 4:14 a.m. Eleven days separated her from the Audit Committee meeting. The dashboard carried seven numbers: *Net Debt/EBITDA 2.88x. Covenant 3.50x. ARS revenue USD 42M/yr. ARS cash 800M. FX hedge coverage 0%. Q1 pro-forma hit USD 5.2M.* Below the numbers, the lead had written one line: *"The step devaluation is the announcement. The hedging policy is the answer."*

---

## The Protagonist

Juliana Palacios, 50, had joined Andina Industrial on October 3, 2024 after nine years as Group Treasurer of a Brazilian steel and mining company, where she had, across three macro cycles, led the treasury architecture through two Brazilian rate-hiking cycles and one sovereign downgrade event. A graduate of Universidad Externado de Colombia with an MBA from Kellogg and the INSEAD International Treasury program, she had published, in 2021, a Treasury-Series note on covenant-management that the Andina Industrial CEO had circulated in the July 2024 search dossier. The CEO's October 2024 written mandate had been: "Build the treasury the company needs for the next macro cycle, not the last one."

---

## The Decision

Palacios had to commit, by 9:00 a.m. on Friday, February 6, the Q1 Treasury package the Audit Committee would treat as the six-quarter operating envelope:

1. **Argentine Response** — repricing USD-linked clauses on a defined subset of the ARS revenue book, converting ARS cash into local hard assets or inventory, and the Board-disclosure-template for the Q1 translation hit.
2. **Hedging Architecture** — whether to initiate a rolling-forwards program on ARS and MXN in the first quarter or to wait for the Q3 MXN rally signal the Latin-America-macro team had flagged.
3. **Covenant-Headroom Management** — whether to pre-position a proactive-waiver framework with the bondholder agent syndicate or to manage the covenant path quarter-by-quarter inside the existing disclosure calendar.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across six quarters. A Q1 package that executed immediate repricing and ARS-cash-conversion, initiated a rolling-forwards program on ARS and a Q3-triggered MXN program, and pre-positioned the proactive-waiver framework produced, on the internal treasury model, a Q5 covenant-breach response within an already-warmed bondholder relationship and a Q6 post-recovery Net-Debt/EBITDA at 3.18x. A Q1 package that deferred the hedging architecture to Q3 and kept the bondholder relationship at arm's length produced, on the same model, a Q5 breach inside a cold relationship and a Q6 Net-Debt/EBITDA at 3.34x with a step-up of twenty-five basis points.

## Company Background

Andina Industrial had been founded in 1987 through the consolidation of three Colombian specialty-chemicals firms and had, across thirty-nine years, expanded into the four-country industrial group the case studied. 2025 group revenue was USD 2.68B distributed as 38% Colombia, 21% Brazil, 16% Mexico, 14% Argentina, 11% United States export. EBITDA was USD 368M, net income USD 142M, and the group carried USD 1.06B of net debt: a USD 420M senior-unsecured bond due 2031, a BRL term loan (equivalent USD 280M) with a pay-twelve-point-five-receive-CDI interest-rate swap, a USD 210M Colombian RCF, and USD 150M of working-capital and export facilities.

The Argentine operation, acquired in 2013, produced specialty-plastic additives for the local automotive and consumer-goods sectors and had, across 2025, generated USD 42M of revenue at a 16% EBITDA margin. The ARS 800M cash balance had accumulated through the 2024–2025 period as cross-border-dividend restrictions tightened. The unhedged position reflected a 2022 Board decision to deprioritize Argentine FX hedging on the grounds that forwards were unavailable at commercial rates and that the political economy produced structural step-changes rather than continuous drift.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM macro-volatility landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Q1 decision sat inside. First, the Argentine step devaluation of January 22, 2026 — thirty-five percent on the official rate — followed the December 2025 post-election policy reset and placed the ARS/USD at 1,420 against a pre-event 1,051; across LATAM corporates with Argentine exposure, the Q1 2026 translation impact averaged USD 4.2M per USD 100M of annual Argentine revenue, with a post-event recovery path running through repricing and intercompany-loan restructuring rather than hedging. Second, the Brazilian central bank had, in late 2025, signaled a return to a hiking cycle; the SELIC +300-basis-point move the case modelled for Q2 sat inside a broader cycle the market was pricing at 150–350 basis points of cumulative rate increase through the first three quarters of 2026. Third, the US-policy-driven nearshoring capital flows had, in late 2025, pushed the MXN from 18.5 toward the 16-handle, with the consensus forward curve implying a 15.8-to-16.4 band for Q2–Q3 2026.

Empirically, the post-2000 CFO-playbook research had two robust findings the Q1 package had to respect. First, corporates that had pre-positioned a proactive-bondholder-waiver framework before a covenant-breach event had, across a 2012–2024 sample of sixty-one observed events, received waivers at a median step-up of 12 basis points against 45 basis points for corporates that had managed the breach event reactively. Second, layered hedging programs — rolling forwards on cost base, selective options on export book — had, across the same period, produced composite-FX-drag reductions averaging 280 basis points on EBITDA-to-revenue translation against unhedged positions and averaged 110 basis points of drag against full-coverage programs.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Board had, in the November 2025 strategy off-site, ratified the "earnings stability" framing as the single non-negotiable operating principle. The Argentine step devaluation on January 22 landed inside the first quarter of the new fiscal year and inside the ninety-day window of the new operating plan. The CEO had, on January 23, requested that Palacios deliver the Q1 Treasury package in twelve business days. The Audit Committee Chair had, on the same day, requested a pre-committee briefing on the covenant-path implications.

### What Palacios knew

The internal treasury team had modeled three Q1 packages. A Minimum-Response Package (disclose the translation hit, defer hedging to Q3, manage covenant quarter-by-quarter) projected Q5 breach inside a cold bondholder relationship, waiver step-up 25–40 bps, Q6 Net Debt/EBITDA 3.34x, composite 68/100. A Structural Package (repricing, ARS-cash conversion, rolling-forwards program on ARS with a Q3 MXN layered program, pre-positioned waiver framework) projected Q5 breach inside a warmed bondholder relationship, waiver step-up 10 bps, Q6 Net Debt/EBITDA 3.18x, composite 82/100. A Full-Coverage Package (repricing, full ARS and MXN hedging, covenant-amendment renegotiation before breach, debt-reduction tranche) projected Q6 Net Debt/EBITDA 3.08x, composite 80/100 — a two-point drag against the Structural Package at an incremental USD 14M of hedging cost.

### What she did not know

Whether the SELIC move in Q2 would land at +200 or +300 basis points (base case on the Andina Latin-America-macro team's model); whether the MXN would rally through 16 to 15.8 (base case) or through 16 to 17 on a policy-reversal shock; whether the Colombian windfall-tax proposal would be legislated in its drafted form or carved out for industrials with long-dated capex commitments; and whether the Q5 commodity spike the internal commodity team had placed at thirty-one-percent probability would materialize in the Q5 window and push Net-Debt/EBITDA through the covenant.

### What others were saying

**The Group Treasurer**: *"Structural Package. Pre-position the waiver framework now. The bondholder relationship is the single highest-leverage asset we have going into the commodity cycle."*

**The Chief Strategy Officer**: *"Argentine repricing first, ARS-cash conversion second, layered MXN program at the first Q3 signal. Translation losses are a disclosure problem. Operating-margin losses are a business-model problem."*

**The Audit Committee Chair**: *"Do not breach the covenant without the waiver pre-positioned. If we breach into a cold room, the market writes the story."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Palacios had narrowed the space to three Q1 packages.

- **Option A — Minimum-Response:** Disclose the translation hit, defer hedging, manage covenant reactively. Composite 68/100. Cheapest quarter. Costliest breach.

- **Option B — Structural Package:** Repricing, ARS-cash conversion, rolling-forwards program (Q1 ARS, Q3 MXN), pre-positioned waiver framework. Composite 82/100. Aligns with the Group Treasurer and Audit Committee Chair positions.

- **Option C — Full-Coverage:** Repricing, full ARS and MXN hedging, covenant amendment, debt-reduction tranche. Composite 80/100. Two-point composite drag against Option B at USD 14M incremental hedging cost.

Palacios's private view, not yet shared with the CEO, was that Option B was the right Q1 package and that the single subsequent decision most correlated with the Q6 outcome was the Q5 covenant-breach response — specifically, the proactive call to the syndicate agent with a pre-funded waiver request and revised commodity-hedge plan, and the commitment to refinance the USD bond twelve months ahead of maturity at the first Q6 recovery signal.
