## Abstract

URBANGRID was, in the case universe, an Amsterdam-headquartered smart-city platform operating across 85 European municipalities (Netherlands, Germany, France, Spain, Portugal and the Nordics), with 340 active complementors building mobility, energy, waste-management, air-quality and citizen-services applications on the UrbanGrid platform, USD 450M in 2025 revenue (USD 280M from municipality SaaS licences and USD 170M from complementor revenue-share), a 2025 Ecosystem-Health Index of 55 against a European-platform peer-benchmark of 72, a Regulatory Score of 50 against the post-DMA European-platform band of 62–76, and a Stakeholder-Trust Index of 50 against the 65–75 band. The 2023–2025 operating model had been anchored on a platform-openness-with-first-party-verticals discipline that had delivered revenue growth at a 22% five-year CAGR but had produced three governance contradictions the 2026 operating environment would not tolerate: a Q4 2025 European Commission preliminary letter flagging UrbanGrid as a candidate for DMA gatekeeper designation on the mobility-app layer, a January 2026 complementor-survey documenting a Trust-Index drop to 38 inside the mobility complementor cohort, and a February 2026 municipality-governance-council motion calling for an independent municipality-controlled oversight board. The Q1 2026 board had approved a USD 60M governance-rebuild envelope and a CEO-led five-round commitment cycle against an Ecosystem-Health floor above 70, a Regulatory Score above 68, a Stakeholder-Trust floor above 68, a Revenue-Growth floor above 14%, and a composite Platform-Governance Score above 72. The case opens at 5:58 a.m. on Monday, April 13, 2026, at UrbanGrid's Amsterdam Zuidas office as **Ilse Meijer**, a 47-year-old Chief Executive Officer, prepares the Round-1 platform-openness commitment. Seventy-four days separate her from the June 26 board platform-governance review.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Round-1 platform-openness commitment (open-API charter with documented non-discrimination clauses vs. gated-access) and diagnose when an open envelope dominates a gated envelope on the Ecosystem-Health and Regulatory-Score trade-off.
2. Analyze the Round-2 coopetition commitment on UrbanGrid Mobility (launch first-party vertical competing with complementors vs. ring-fence to complementor partnership) and recommend the ring-fence envelope against the first-party envelope on the Stakeholder-Trust and Regulatory-Risk basis.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 DMA-compliance commitment (gatekeeper-designation response with self-preferencing prohibition, interoperability commitment and data-portability architecture vs. minimal-compliance envelope) and recommend the proactive envelope against the minimal envelope on the Regulatory-Score basis.
4. Respond to the Round-4 complementor revenue-share redesign (tiered revenue-share with transparent terms vs. legacy discretionary take-rate) and recommend the tiered envelope against the legacy envelope on the Ecosystem-Health basis.
5. Design the Round-5 municipality-governance-board installation (independent municipality-controlled oversight with veto rights on gate-keeping decisions vs. advisory-only board) and diagnose when platform governance becomes a durable ecosystem asset rather than a compliance artefact.

## The Protagonist

Ilse Meijer, 47, had assumed the Chief-Executive-Officer role at UrbanGrid on September 15, 2025, from a Tier-1 European cloud-infrastructure company where she had, across eight years, led two platform-governance rebuilds and had closed a 2023 DMA-compliance response programme across a twelve-market B2B-developer platform that had preserved Ecosystem-Health above 70 while delivering a gatekeeper-designation response under the commitments procedure. Before that she had spent six years as a Director of Platform Strategy at a Munich-based industrial-software group. The board had recruited her specifically on two capabilities: her documented refusal to launch first-party verticals that cannibalised complementor revenue, and her willingness to codify platform non-discrimination commitments ahead of regulatory pressure. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the Ecosystem-Health outcome and 25% to the Regulatory Score.

## The Decision

Meijer had to commit, by the May 6 board pre-review, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–5.

1. **Round 1 — Platform openness.** Three archetypes: open-API-charter (documented non-discrimination and equal-access clauses, public-SLA commitments, third-party audit on gate-keeping decisions, Ecosystem-Health +14 points, Composite 74), gated-access (API access by commercial negotiation — Composite 38), or conditional-openness (open-API on core layer, gated on premium layer — Composite 58). Working envelope: open-API-charter.
2. **Round 2 — Coopetition on UrbanGrid Mobility.** Three archetypes: ring-fence (abandon first-party UrbanGrid Mobility vertical, convert to preferred-partnership envelope with the three incumbent complementors, Stakeholder-Trust +18, Composite 76), first-party-launch (launch UrbanGrid Mobility as a first-party vertical competing with complementors — Composite 28, DMA-designation trigger and complementor-exodus risk), or coopetition-with-firewall (first-party vertical but operated inside an internal information-firewall — Composite 52, residual regulatory and complementor-trust risk). Working envelope: ring-fence.
3. **Round 3 — DMA compliance.** Three vectors: proactive-commitments (self-preferencing prohibition, interoperability commitment, data-portability architecture committed under the commitments procedure — Regulatory Score +22, Composite 76), minimal-compliance (compliance on the explicit gatekeeper obligations only — Composite 48, residual enforcement risk), or contested-posture (challenge the gatekeeper designation in court — Composite 32, reputational and commercial risk). Working envelope: proactive-commitments.
4. **Round 4 — Complementor revenue-share redesign.** Three vectors: tiered-transparent (published take-rate tiers at 15%, 12% and 8% by complementor scale, with transparent qualification criteria, Complementor-Trust +22, Composite 78), legacy-discretionary (status-quo 20% blended take-rate with discretionary ad-hoc reductions — Composite 38), or uniform-cut (uniform 10% take-rate — Composite 56, revenue floor forfeited). Working envelope: tiered-transparent.
5. **Round 5 — Municipality-governance-board installation.** Three vectors: independent-with-veto (independent municipality-controlled oversight board with veto rights on gate-keeping decisions, Stakeholder-Trust +14, Composite 80), advisory-only (municipality advisory board without veto rights — Composite 54, governance-credibility gap), or existing-council-only (retain the existing municipality-council structure — Composite 42). Working envelope: independent-with-veto.

On Meijer's working model, an Open-API / Ring-Fence / Proactive-DMA / Tiered-Transparent / Independent-Board envelope delivered Ecosystem-Health 74, Regulatory 72, Stakeholder-Trust 72, Revenue-Growth 16%, Composite 76. A Gated / First-Party / Minimal-DMA / Legacy-Discretionary / Existing-Council envelope delivered Ecosystem-Health 32, Regulatory 34, Stakeholder-Trust 30, Revenue-Growth 22% (short-term), Composite 34 (DMA-enforcement trigger). A Conditional / Firewall / Contested / Uniform / Advisory envelope delivered Composite 52.

## Company Background

UrbanGrid had been founded in Amsterdam in 2015 as a citizen-services SaaS platform for four Dutch municipalities and had, across eleven years, grown into a pan-European smart-city platform with 85 municipalities and 340 active complementors across mobility, energy, waste-management, air-quality and citizen-services verticals. The 2023–2025 operating model had delivered a 22% five-year revenue CAGR but had produced a governance contradiction at the core of the 2026 strategic horizon.

The Q1 2026 board dossier had identified three specific pressures the five-round envelope would inherit. First, the December 2025 European Commission preliminary letter had flagged UrbanGrid as a candidate for DMA gatekeeper designation on the mobility-app layer, with a formal investigation window opening in Q3 2026. Second, the January 2026 complementor-survey had registered a Trust-Index drop to 38 inside the mobility complementor cohort, anchored on a concern that the announced UrbanGrid Mobility first-party vertical would replicate their product. Third, the February 2026 municipality-governance-council motion had placed 22 of 85 municipalities behind a call for an independent municipality-controlled oversight board.

## Industry and Market Context

The European platform-governance landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2020–2024 DMA gatekeeper-designation evidence base had, across 14 designated platforms, documented a Regulatory-Score differential of 18–24 points between proactive-commitments envelopes and minimal-compliance envelopes. Second, the 2018–2024 coopetition literature had, across 32 digital platforms that had launched first-party verticals inside complementor cohorts, documented a complementor-exit-rate of 28–42% inside 24 months, against 6–12% for platforms that had ring-fenced first-party expansion. Third, the 2020–2024 tiered-transparent-take-rate literature had, across 28 developer-platform economies, placed tiered envelopes at an Ecosystem-Health uplift of 18–24 points against legacy-discretionary envelopes.

Empirically, the post-2022 platform-governance literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, platforms that had installed independent-governance boards with veto rights on gate-keeping decisions had, across a 2019–2024 sample of 22 platforms, preserved Stakeholder-Trust above 68 in 86% of cases against 22% for platforms with advisory-only boards. Second, platforms that had codified non-discrimination clauses inside an open-API charter had, across the same sample, avoided enforcement action in 84% of cases against 42% for platforms under gated-access operating models.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the October 2025 platform-governance review, approved the USD 60M envelope and the June 26 review calendar. The Chief Legal Officer had, on February 3, delivered the DMA preliminary-letter analysis. The Head of Platform Governance had, on February 17, delivered the complementor-trust diagnostic. The Chief Commercial Officer had, on March 2, delivered the Mobility first-party-vertical commercial case. The Head of Municipality Relations had, on March 14, delivered the governance-council motion analysis. The Chief Financial Officer had, on March 25, confirmed the USD 60M envelope.

### What Meijer knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. An Open-API / Ring-Fence / Proactive-DMA / Tiered-Transparent / Independent-Board Envelope projected Composite 76. A Gated / First-Party / Minimal-DMA / Legacy-Discretionary / Existing-Council Envelope projected Composite 34. A Conditional / Firewall / Contested / Uniform / Advisory Envelope projected Composite 52.

### What she did not know

Whether the DMA preliminary-letter would escalate to a formal gatekeeper-designation decision inside the Q3 2026 window; whether abandoning the UrbanGrid Mobility first-party vertical would compress the 2027 revenue trajectory below the 14% growth floor; whether the independent-board-with-veto design would survive a political handover inside the 22-municipality coalition; and whether the tiered-transparent take-rate would trigger an offset from the three largest complementors whose prior ad-hoc discounts had exceeded the 8% tier floor.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Legal Officer**: *"Proactive-commitments. Contested posture burns the commercial franchise. Minimal compliance invites escalation."*

**The Head of Platform Governance**: *"Ring-fence Mobility. Competing with the three incumbents is a short-term revenue bump and a long-term trust collapse."*

**The Head of Municipality Relations**: *"Independent-board with veto rights. Advisory-only does not move the twenty-two-municipality coalition."*

**The Chief Executive Officer herself, in the January offsite**: *"A platform that eats its complementors ends up eating itself."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Meijer had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Open-API / Ring-Fence / Proactive-DMA / Tiered-Transparent / Independent-Board:** the recommended envelope. Ecosystem-Health 74. Regulatory 72. Stakeholder-Trust 72. Revenue-Growth 16%. Composite 76. Recommended.
- **Option B — Gated / First-Party / Minimal-DMA / Legacy-Discretionary / Existing-Council:** the status-quo envelope. Composite 34. Rejected — DMA designation trigger and complementor exodus.
- **Option C — Conditional / Firewall / Contested / Uniform / Advisory:** the partial envelope. Composite 52. Rejected — residual regulatory and trust risk.

Meijer's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 2 — the ring-fence on UrbanGrid Mobility — because it was the round that resolved the coopetition contradiction at the centre of the governance architecture and made every downstream commitment (DMA compliance, revenue-share, independent board) credible to complementors and municipalities alike.
