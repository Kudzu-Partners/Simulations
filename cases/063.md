## Abstract

Apex Tecnología was, in the case universe, a Bogotá-headquartered LATAM software-and-services company with 1,200 employees across Colombia, Peru, and Mexico, 2025 revenue of USD 184M, and a Product Engineering organization of 280 people inside which Diego Vargas ran a team of forty. On Monday, January 12, 2026, the executive committee announced a return-to-office mandate requiring three in-office days per week beginning February 2. On Thursday, January 15, an adjacent Data Analytics department laid off twelve people. On Friday, January 16, Vargas's most reliable senior engineer — Laura Chen, four years at Apex — missed the optional Friday team ritual for the second week in a row and submitted a pull-request code-review two days past the team standard. The case opens at 7:02 a.m. on Monday, January 19, 2026, as Vargas drafts the Week-Zero agenda for the team meeting he has scheduled for Tuesday at 10:00 a.m. and the one-on-one he has not yet scheduled with Laura. Five weeks and four conversations would define the team's Round-5 composite score — psychological safety, team performance, manager wellbeing, attrition risk, and legal compliance — and the launch of the product the team had already delayed twice.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the return-to-office rollout as a Week-Zero conversation — team meeting versus serial one-on-ones versus staged hybrid — and diagnose the conditions under which the team-meeting approach preserves psychological safety without surrendering on the mandate.
2. Analyze the early-warning pattern in a high-performing engineer's behavior (missed rituals, slower code reviews, first-time scope pushback) and design a one-on-one that surfaces the issue without diagnosing it.
3. Diagnose the appropriate Round-3 manager response when an employee discloses a formal mental-health diagnosis and explicitly requests that HR not be involved.
4. Design a Round-4 parallel-track response to an open interpersonal conflict when a private, confidential piece of information about one party is known to the manager but has not been disclosed by that party.
5. Recommend a complete Round-1 package and identify the single Round-5 manager-wellbeing move that holds the composite score above the 70 threshold.

On the morning of **Monday, January 19, 2026**, **Diego Vargas** sat at the breakfast table of his apartment in Chapinero in Bogotá with his laptop open to the Week-Zero agenda draft. The RTO announcement had dropped on page one of the internal newsletter a week earlier. The Data Analytics layoff had broken the signal-to-noise ratio of every team Slack channel since Thursday. The pull-request delay on Laura's code-review had landed in his direct-messages on Friday at 4:47 p.m. The team meeting was on Tuesday at 10:00 a.m. The one-on-one was not yet scheduled.

---

## The Protagonist

Diego Vargas, 38, had been at Apex Tecnología for six years, most recently as Senior Manager of Product Engineering since April 2024, where he had, across two annual cycles, shipped four product releases on or ahead of schedule and two product releases two to four weeks late — the current release sitting in the second category with an eight-week delay to the February 15 launch target. A computer-science graduate of Universidad Nacional de Colombia with an executive program in management at CESA, he had, in his prior role, led the team that had shipped Apex's first mobile-product launch in 2022. He had taken, in 2024, a two-day company workshop on psychological-safety framing and a one-day workshop on mental-health-first-aid; neither had, in his own self-assessment in the November 2025 performance review, produced the behavioral change he had hoped for.

---

## The Decision

Vargas had to commit, by end of day on Monday, January 19, the Round-1 Week-Zero package:

1. **RTO Rollout Approach** — team meeting with listening session and collaborative transition plan, serial one-on-ones with each of the forty team members, or a staged hybrid beginning with the four team-leads and broadening.
2. **Laura Chen Conversation** — private one-on-one that acknowledges the observed pattern without assuming a cause, a group-level scope re-planning conversation that defers the individual conversation, or a performance-feedback conversation that addresses the code-review delay directly.
3. **Follow-Up Commitments** — the scope-protection and ritual-attendance expectations Vargas is prepared to commit to in the Week-Zero window.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across five weeks. A Round-1 package that paired a team meeting with a listening-first transition plan and a private one-on-one that named the pattern without diagnosing it produced, on the internal culture model, a Week-Three Round-2 position with Tomás Reyes's open negativity surfaced earlier rather than later and Laura's delivery pattern beginning to re-stabilize. A package that compressed the RTO rollout into a single announcement-and-execute conversation produced, on the model, an eight-point Week-Three hit on psychological safety and a five-point hit on team performance at the precise point the product-launch deadline was already slipping.

## Company Background

Apex Tecnología had been founded in 2009 as a Bogotá-headquartered software-services company and had, across seventeen years, expanded into the cross-LATAM product-and-services organization the case studied. 2025 revenue was USD 184M, gross margin 62%, and EBITDA margin 16.8%. The Product Engineering organization was 280 people across five teams, of which Vargas's team of forty was the single team responsible for the core transaction-processing module that the February 15 launch would release. The forty-person composition was fourteen senior engineers, sixteen mid-level, six product and design roles, three engineering managers (reporting to Vargas), and one program manager.

The January 12 RTO announcement had been, in the announcement's own framing, a "cultural reset" in response to a perceived collaboration drag in the hybrid-first period. Three of Vargas's team members had two-hour round-trip commutes from Soacha and La Calera. Two were primary caregivers to young children. Seven had, in the November 2025 internal pulse survey, flagged that they had taken the 2024 fully-remote offer as a condition of accepting the Apex role. The adjacent Data Analytics layoff on January 15, announced internally as a "targeted capacity alignment," had affected twelve of the eighty-person department and had been, in the team-channel conversation, read as a signal rather than an isolated event.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM technology-sector workforce-mental-health landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Round-1 decision sat inside. First, the return-to-office cycle across LATAM technology companies had, between September 2024 and December 2025, compressed fully-remote options by thirty-eight percentage points; companies that had executed the transition with advance listening sessions and collaborative transition plans had, in a Q4 2025 industry-survey cohort, retained 84% of high-performing employees against 61% for companies that had executed with announcement-and-execute models. Second, the Colombian labor-protection framework treated a documented mental-health diagnosis as a protected category under the 2021 reasonable-accommodation framework, with a formal accommodation pathway running through HR and, in the case of disputed accommodations, through the Ministerio de Trabajo. Third, the post-2023 mental-health-at-work literature had converged on a finding: managers who offered a listening-only one-on-one at the first behavioral-change signal — without diagnosing, without jumping to solutions — produced, across an observed 2022–2024 sample of 140 mid-level managers, 34% higher six-month retention on the employees concerned.

Empirically, the front-line-manager research had two robust findings the Round-1 package had to respect. First, the single most consistent predictor of a team's six-month psychological-safety score was the manager's consistency in naming observed patterns without assigning cause; teams whose managers scored high on this dimension had, across a 2021–2024 sample, produced composite wellbeing-delivery scores averaging 76/100 against 58 for teams whose managers either avoided naming patterns or jumped to cause-assignment. Second, manager-wellbeing scores — the "container-holding tax" the simulation framed — had produced, in the same sample, a four-to-six-point drop in the rounds immediately following an escalation event, with recovery contingent on the manager's own support-system activation rather than the event resolution.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The February 15 launch had been announced internally in October and externally to the two beta-customer accounts in November. The eight-week delay had been disclosed internally on December 15; externally, a four-week slip had been disclosed with the commitment to "update in January." The RTO announcement had landed inside the launch-communication window. The Data Analytics layoff had landed three days after the RTO announcement. The Laura Chen pattern — missed ritual, slower code review — had become visible in the second week of January.

### What Vargas knew

The internal culture team had modeled three Round-1 packages. A Minimum-Escalation Package (announcement-and-execute RTO, group-level scope re-planning with Laura deferred) projected Round-5 composite 58/100, psychological safety 47, attrition risk 42. A Listening-First Package (team meeting with listening session plus collaborative transition plan, private one-on-one with Laura, scope protection for two team-members under visible commute burden) projected Round-5 composite 74/100, psychological safety 78, attrition risk 18. A Maximum-Accommodation Package (staged RTO with opt-out pathway, forty serial one-on-ones in the Week-Zero window, full scope protection) projected Round-5 composite 68/100, psychological safety 76, attrition risk 16, manager-wellbeing 48 — the manager-wellbeing line the simulation tracked as the hidden cost.

### What he did not know

Whether Laura Chen's behavioral pattern reflected an emerging burnout signal (base-case internal working hypothesis), a life-event he was not privy to (possible given her silence), or a deliberate scope-protection behavior as she considered an external offer (the scenario the internal team rated at seventeen-percent probability); whether Tomás Reyes, the UX Lead, would escalate from the "just collecting a paycheck" off-the-record signal to a formal grievance or to an exit in the Round-2 window; whether Sofía Ortega, the product analyst whose Round-3 disclosure the simulation signalled, would request the accommodation through a formal pathway or through a private conversation; and whether Andrés Muñoz's Round-4 verbal confrontation with Marco would precede or follow the team's on-time ship of the launch.

### What others were saying

**The Director of Engineering** (Vargas's manager): *"Ship the launch on the current slip. The RTO rollout is a leadership-alignment ask — I need Product Engineering to model the policy. The container-holding work is yours to do. I back you on the accommodations you negotiate. Don't negotiate them in public."*

**The Head of People Operations**: *"Run the team meeting with the listening session. Ship a written transition plan within five working days. Any individual accommodation — including Sofía's, when it lands — runs through me. Do not commit to anything that bypasses the formal pathway."*

**A trusted senior engineer on Vargas's team** (in a one-on-one on January 16): *"Laura is okay. Tomás is not okay. Andrés is going through a separation and has not told anyone. Sofía is afraid to ask for anything because the Data Analytics people were the ones asking for things."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Vargas had narrowed the space to three Round-1 packages.

- **Option A — Minimum-Escalation:** Announcement-and-execute RTO, group-level scope re-planning, defer the Laura conversation. Round-5 composite 58/100. Protects Vargas's own bandwidth. Forfeits psychological-safety recovery.

- **Option B — Listening-First:** Team meeting with listening session and collaborative transition plan, private one-on-one with Laura naming the pattern without diagnosing, scope protection for two commute-burdened team members. Round-5 composite 74/100. Aligns with the Director of Engineering and Head of People Operations positions.

- **Option C — Maximum-Accommodation:** Staged RTO with opt-out pathway, forty serial one-on-ones, full scope protection. Round-5 composite 68/100, manager-wellbeing 48. Protects the team at the cost of the manager's own capacity.

Vargas's private view, not yet shared with the Director of Engineering, was that Option B was the right Round-1 package and that the single subsequent decision most correlated with the Round-5 manager-wellbeing outcome was the Round-3 Sofía Ortega conversation — specifically, the middle-path response that thanked her for the trust, explained the formal-accommodation pathway, and promised to bring her into that conversation on her terms.

(See **Exhibit 4** for the five-round projected outcomes under each option and **Exhibit 5** for the "parallel-track" framework for the Round-4 Andrés-Marco conflict and the manager-wellbeing recovery protocol.)
