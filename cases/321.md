## Abstract

DataCampo S.A. was, in the case universe, a six-year-old Asunción-headquartered precision-agriculture SaaS founded in 2020 by three Universidad Nacional de Asunción agronomy-and-engineering alumni and now serving 47 paying Paraguayan farms with a satellite-plus-soil-probe irrigation-and-yield platform — ARR PYG 846M at 2025 year-end, 15-month cash runway, Series-A closed at PYG 4.8B in 2024, 22 employees of which 8 are in commercial and 9 in product-and-engineering, installed-base NPS 71, and 100% of the paying base in Moore's innovator and early-adopter bands with not a single customer from the pragmatist early-majority segment the 2030 plan required. The addressable universe — approximately 11,000 Paraguayan soy farms above 500 hectares — remained unpenetrated. On **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 7:42 a.m., **Lic. Joaquín Ríos-Benítez**, 34, co-founder and CEO, opened the Round-1 go-to-market planning file his Chief Commercial Officer had circulated at 11:18 p.m. the previous evening and sat down to commit the four-round technology-marketing package for the 9:30 a.m. founder-and-board session where an AgriGlobal competitive-entry announcement — a multinational's USD 8,000/farm/year Paraguay launch scheduled for July — had moved onto the agenda overnight.

On the morning of **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 7:42 a.m., **Lic. Joaquín Ríos-Benítez** arrived at DataCampo's Asunción office on Avenida Mariscal López on his one-hundred-and-thirteenth day as sole CEO after his co-founder and CTO had rotated into a Chief Product Officer role in January, greeted the two field-team data scientists already at their desks, and walked into the small conference room past the whiteboard where his CCO had drawn the six candidate beachhead segments the previous evening. The Round-1 go-to-market planning file she had circulated at 11:18 p.m. was open on his laptop. The AgriGlobal Paraguay-entry announcement — a USD 8,000/farm/year premium launch scheduled for July — was highlighted on the second page. The 47-customer chasm chart was on the fourth page, in red.

---

## The Protagonist

Ríos-Benítez, 34, had co-founded DataCampo in 2020 after completing a Licenciatura en Ingeniería Agronómica at the Universidad Nacional de Asunción in 2013 and an MBA at the INCAE Business School in Costa Rica in 2018, had spent three years between 2018 and 2020 at a Paulista agritech scale-up running LATAM business development before returning to Asunción to build DataCampo with two university classmates, and had led the 2024 Series-A round at PYG 4.8B. His go-to-market instincts had been shaped by three formative exposures: the 2017 INCAE technology-marketing elective taught on Moore's *Crossing the Chasm* and *Inside the Tornado*; the 2019 São Paulo agritech case where he had watched a Brazilian precision-ag scale-up exhaust its cash in the chasm by selling to everyone at once; and the 2023 first attempt at the Paraguayan cattle-ranching segment that had produced only four customers in seven months and had taught him that whole-product incompleteness kills the pragmatist sale.

---

## The Decision

Ríos-Benítez had to commit, by the 9:30 a.m. founder-and-board session, the four-round technology-marketing package.

1. **Round 1 — Chasm diagnosis and beachhead selection.** Accept the Moore diagnosis. Score the six candidate Paraguayan segments on the four-variable grid. Commit to soy 500–2,000 ha at 3,100 prospects. No resource splitting.
2. **Round 2 — Whole product and positioning.** Allocate PYG 180M across ecosystem partners, product localization, reference customers and agronomic content targeting WPCS 74. Commit the Moore positioning statement naming paper agronomist visits as the enemy. Allocate PYG 220M across the four channels with the agri-distributor anchor.
3. **Round 3 — Competitive defence.** Against AgriGlobal's USD 8,000/year entry, build switching costs through ERP API integrations and multi-season yield-history lock-in. Hold the DataCampo USD 2,480/year price.
4. **Round 4 — Bowling-alley adjacency.** Open the first adjacency pin into cattle 1,000–5,000 ha with one additional partner. Target 41% beachhead share and ARR PYG 2.4B by end-2027.

Commitments: the Round-1 beachhead selection; the Round-2 whole-product-and-positioning package; the Round-3 no-price-cut competitive defence; the Round-4 bowling-alley pin; and a one-page founder-and-board memo that frames the four-round commitment as a single 18-month chasm-crossing arc.

---

## Sectoral Background

The 2020–2025 Paraguayan agricultural-technology category had, in the case universe, registered approximately PYG 340bn in aggregate annual technology spend across the 11,000 soy-above-500-ha operators, concentrated in four buyer tiers (multinational-integrated large estates above 5,000 ha; commercial mid-size soy farms 500–2,000 ha; cattle operations 1,000–5,000 ha; smallholder cooperatives below 500 ha). The 2019–2025 LATAM precision-ag adoption panel had documented that scale-up chasm-crossing attempts in the category had resolved with documented success at approximately 28% of commercial ventures, with the failure mode distributed at approximately 62% on beachhead-fragmentation patterns, approximately 54% on whole-product-incompleteness readings at the pragmatist entry, and approximately 48% on premium-competitor-entry responses that had dropped price instead of building switching costs.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1991–2025 technology-marketing literature produced three findings the simulation had to respect. First, Moore's 1991/2014 chasm construct combined with Rogers's 1962 diffusion-of-innovations curve had produced documented scale-up-outcome readings across approximately 820 LATAM SaaS cases averaging +22–34 percentage-point improvement in Series-B probability when the beachhead was committed rather than split, and averaging –48% cash-runway extension when the whole-product gap was closed before the pragmatist push. Second, Levitt's 1980 whole-product framework, extended by Moore, had produced documented pragmatist-acceptance readings averaging a 65-point threshold on the WPCS index with marginal-return discontinuity above 75. Third, the 2015–2025 LATAM SaaS competitive-entry panel had documented that incumbent defence through switching-cost construction outperformed price-cutting on 12-month revenue retention by +28 percentage points on average, confirming Moore's insight that in the tornado and main-street phases the price itself is the segmentation.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The 2020 DataCampo founding; the 2021 first twelve farm customers in the Paraguayan Chaco; the 2022 seed round at PYG 1.2B; the 2023 first failed cattle-ranching push that had produced four customers in seven months; the 2024 Series-A at PYG 4.8B with the chasm-crossing plan committed to investors; the 2025 year-end installed base at 47 customers and the recognition that all 47 were innovators or early adopters; the April 2026 AgriGlobal Paraguay-entry announcement; and the 74-day diagnostic phase were the sequence.

### What Ríos-Benítez knew

The 47-customer chasm chart; the 11,000-farm addressable universe; the six-segment candidate grid; the soy-500–2,000-ha scoring read on pain, reachability, whole-product feasibility and strategic leverage; the PYG 180M whole-product and PYG 220M channel allocations his CCO had modelled; the AgriGlobal entry-price and partnership-signing calendar; the 15-month runway at current burn; and the Series-B soft-circle at PYG 12B contingent on crossing-the-chasm evidence by end-Q3-2026.

### What he did not know

Whether the three Paraguayan ecosystem partners (FOCEM-subsidy consulting firm, rural-internet operator, agronomic-consulting network) would sign the whole-product completion agreements on the 90-day timeline the plan assumed; whether the Asociación Rural soy-producer association would extend the reference-programme endorsement his CCO had negotiated verbally in March; whether AgriGlobal's USD 8,000 entry would trigger price-sensitivity in the soy 500–2,000 ha segment or would validate the premium category; whether the cattle 1,000–5,000 ha bowling-alley pin would open with the same partner leverage as the soy beachhead; and whether the one-page founder-and-board memo would hold through the board member who had funded the 2023 cattle-push and still argued for a reversal.

### What others were saying

**The CCO (37, former Syngenta Paraguay commercial director)**: *"Joaquín, si partimos el presupuesto en dos segmentos otra vez, el año que viene vendemos lo mismo que este. Decidamos uno. Y lo ganamos."*

**The CTO and co-founder (32)**: *"El producto está listo para el pragmático. No le falta software. Le falta ecosistema. El MVP del producto completo es el socio, no la feature."*

**The lead investor (senior partner, São Paulo-based LATAM fund)**: *"A Série B se decide en el 30 de septiembre con la data del beachhead. Si me mostrás 200 clientes dispersos en cuatro segmentos, no la firmo. Mostráme 150 en uno."*

**The board member who funded the 2023 cattle push (65, Paraguayan soy-and-cattle family office)**: *"Yo conozco la ganadería. La conozco hace cuarenta años. El cliente está ahí. No esperes dos años para volver."*

**The head of a 1,400-ha reference-customer soy estate in Alto Paraná (52)**: *"Yo les di la data y el caso de uso. Yo soy visionario. Mis vecinos no. Mis vecinos necesitan que otros vecinos les digan que funciona. Si ustedes no traen a los otros vecinos, yo solo no alcanzo."*

**The 47 installed-base customers (aggregate signal via the 2025 NPS panel at 71)**: *"El producto es bueno. La categoría no existe todavía."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Ríos-Benítez had narrowed the technology-marketing space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — Soy-500–2,000-ha beachhead + PYG 180M whole-product + agri-distributor channel + switching-cost competitive defence + cattle bowling-alley in Round 4 (Recommended).** Respects the Moore / Levitt / Ries-Trout canon; commits the beachhead; closes the whole-product gap to WPCS 74; holds the USD 2,480/year price against AgriGlobal; builds ERP-and-data switching costs; opens cattle 1,000–5,000 ha only after the soy pin has hit 41% share. Projected 18-month ARR PYG 2.4B and Series-B-readiness by Q3 2026.
- **Option B — Portfolio across three segments + price cut to USD 1,800 + direct-sales channel + no bowling-alley sequencing.** Rejected — reproduces the 2023 fragmentation failure; surrenders the price-as-segmentation finding; worsens the CAC math at 1.8× ACV direct; undermines the pragmatist reference case that the premium price supports.
- **Option C — Sell the company to AgriGlobal at the announced USD 14M offer.** Defensible on optionality grounds but surrenders the Series-B upside, strands the installed base in a multinational product-roadmap queue, and abandons the Paraguayan precision-ag category-creation thesis the 2024 Series-A deck committed to.

Ríos-Benítez's private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the single decision most correlated with the 18-month chasm-crossing outcome was the Round-1 beachhead commitment, because the 820-case LATAM-SaaS panel had shown that beachhead-commitment scale-ups crossed the chasm at 3.8× the rate of portfolio-across-segments scale-ups, and because his 2023 cattle-push failure had been exactly the portfolio-across-segments mistake. The *Cruzando el Abismo* frame sits at the LATAM precision-ag chasm-crossing tier in the 2026 Express catalogue, distinct from NexaCore Sales Force (sim 023, enterprise-sales frame) and MercadoAbierto EmbeddedFinance (sim 193, embedded-finance frame) — 321 emphasises the TALC / beachhead / whole product / positioning / bowling-alley synthesis inside a Paraguayan precision-ag SaaS.

At 9:21 a.m., Ríos-Benítez closed the Round-1 planning file, printed the one-page founder-and-board memo in Spanish and English, and walked toward the conference room where the 9:30 a.m. session was being assembled. His CCO was already at the whiteboard. His CTO was on video from a grower visit in Ciudad del Este. The lead investor was dialled in from São Paulo. Ríos-Benítez sat down and began: *"Un segmento. Un producto completo. Un enemigo. El abismo se cruza eligiendo. No se cruza vendiendo a todos."*
