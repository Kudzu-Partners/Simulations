# Helio Verde: The Costaria Bid

**Case Code:** STR-2026-1722 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1722 — Costaria RFP — Public-Sector Bid Discipline · v1.0 · September 2026

---

On a Tuesday morning, Emilio Vásquez-Ruiz's phone buzzed before seven. His CEO, Lucía Serrano-Beltrán, was calling from a departure gate: a public tender for a solar-and-storage project on Costaria's national grid had just gone live, weeks earlier than anyone at Helio Verde had expected. She wanted a bid or no-bid recommendation on her desk by Friday at end of day, and she wanted to know, before she signed off on anything, exactly what single fact would have to change for that recommendation to flip.

## The Protagonist

Vásquez-Ruiz, 47, was Director of Public-Sector Sales at Helio Verde, a Costaria-headquartered renewables developer. A chemical engineer with an MBA from the Montecielo School of Business, he had spent eleven years in public-sector roles at Halvard Electric and Wendler Energy in Mexico and Chile before Serrano-Beltrán recruited him in 2022 to build the firm's first dedicated public-sector practice. In three years he had led seven public-sector bids: three won, three lost, one withdrawn before submission.

## The Decision

Over the six weeks the tender allowed, Vásquez-Ruiz would have to make four calls in sequence: whether Helio Verde bid at all, and under which partner architecture; how to price the bid and which single theme to build the written proposal around; what to do about an unresolved integrity question attached to the partner the strongest architecture depended on; and how the internal approval process would be structured before anything left the building.

## The Company and its Setting

Helio Verde built grid-scale solar for two regional utilities and, since a 2024 acquisition, also ran supply-chain-optimization services for food manufacturers. Its public-sector practice, three years old, sat inside a firm reporting roughly USD 1.1 billion in annual revenue. Three named competitors had seen the same tender land: Brisamar Costariense, the local incumbent, priced aggressively and knew the grid; Lianshan Renewable Power Group, a Chinese state-backed manufacturer with its own panel and inverter lines, could underprice almost anyone; and Aranvel Renewables, the European utility, carried a longer record of financing solar bids with the Inter-American Development Bank (IDB) than either of the other two.

## The Situation

The project itself was financed jointly by the IDB and Costaria's Ministry of Energy and Public Works (MEPW). Against Helio Verde's own internal go/no-bid criteria, the team's working numbers scored the opportunity at 4.20 out of 5.00, with an expected value of USD 12.5 million once the odds of winning, the likely margin, and the cost of preparing a bid were netted against each other (Exhibit 1). Those odds stood at 22 percent. Alongside the financial numbers sat two others that did not come from a spreadsheet: an integrity score of 70 out of 100, reflecting Helio Verde's compliance record and the open question hanging over its preferred local partner, and a discipline score of 50 out of 100, reflecting how rigorously the bid decision itself was being argued rather than assumed. A fourth figure, executive air cover, stood at zero — the degree to which Serrano-Beltrán and the rest of the leadership team stood publicly behind the commitments the bid would require — because nothing had yet been brought to them for sign-off. A fifth figure, a cumulative score starting at zero, would add up, decision by decision, how each of the next six weeks' choices held against Helio Verde's own standard for bid discipline. If Vásquez-Ruiz did nothing, the tender's clock would run out in six weeks with Helio Verde simply absent from the list of bidders.

The complication sat with Constructora del Pacífico Sur (CPS), the local construction firm behind the strongest technical architecture and one Helio Verde had worked with before. Felipe, the firm's head of legal and compliance, had just closed out a due-diligence file on CPS: a 2019 IDB integrity finding on an unrelated project, settled with corrective action, with no findings currently open.

## What Vásquez-Ruiz Controlled

The first decision, due Friday, was architectural: which of four bid patterns to recommend — bidding as a subcontractor under Aranvel, contributing local content; bidding as prime with CPS as the local partner; not bidding at all, and holding the team for other work; or bidding as prime with an alternative local partner, Constructora Andina — and naming the single condition, whether a competitor's pricing move, a change to MEPW's evaluation rubric, or a new integrity finding on CPS, that would flip the recommendation if it changed inside the next two weeks (Exhibit 2). The second, due Wednesday of the following week, paired a price position for the tender's long-term tariff — aggressive, mid, or premium — with one theme, chosen from five on the table, to anchor the written proposal. The third, due Thursday morning, sent Vásquez-Ruiz back to the same four architectures under the integrity question specifically, alongside a separate choice of monitor — an internal compliance check, no dedicated monitor, or an independent third-party monitor — and a separate disclosure stance: a partial disclosure in a footnote, no disclosure of any kind, or a full disclosure in the submission itself. The fourth, to be locked in during two hours with Serrano-Beltrán on Thursday afternoon, set how many internal approval gates the bid would pass through before submission — two, one, or three — and where a material finding, if one surfaced later, would be escalated: to the chief executive alone, to the project team alone, or to the audit committee.

## Closing

At 09:15, Vásquez-Ruiz set two sheets of paper on the conference table — one for Friday's memo, one for everything after it — and waited for Adriana, Felipe, Mauricio and Lorena to take their seats. Serrano-Beltrán's flight would land in six hours. The tender's own clock had six weeks left on it, and four decisions still to make inside that time.
