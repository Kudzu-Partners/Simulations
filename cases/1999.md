# Fjordkart AS: Eleven Engineers and a Fixed Date

**Case Code:** ORG-2026-1999 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1999 — *The Trust Dial* · **v1.0 · August 2026**

---

On the morning of Wednesday, 4 March 2026, in her new office in Oslo, Marte Solheim laid the Nordfrakt contract beside the pulse survey run the previous Friday. The number she kept returning to was on the survey: the Atlas Core squad's intrinsic-motivation index had fallen from 82 to 71 in the eight days since the contract was announced, and nothing had been done to them yet. No monitoring software installed. No bonus offered. The engineers had reacted to a rumour.

Solheim had one week, until sprint zero opened, to set the operating model for the largest contract in Fjordkart's history: a NOK 24 million real-time fleet-routing build for the cold-chain logistics operator Nordfrakt, contractually due on 1 December 2026. The board had instructed her to get visibility and control over delivery. The eleven engineers she had just been handed had spent their careers at a company that had never asked anyone to fill in a timesheet.

## The Protagonist

Marte Solheim, 41, had been appointed Engineering Manager of Atlas Core on 2 March 2026, two days earlier. She had joined Fjordkart from a Trondheim mapping firm, spent three years as a staff engineer on the routing team and four running the public-sector squad, shipping to municipal deadlines that moved for no one without ever running a time report. Her appointment had come from the board's delivery committee rather than from the squad.

## The Decision

Solheim had to decide how eleven engineers would be managed for nine months, and to decide it before sprint zero: how closely the work was watched, how much of the planning the squad owned, what if anything it was paid, and what she would say to the board and to Nordfrakt when the plan met trouble.

## The Company and its Setting

Fjordkart AS built geospatial mapping and route-optimisation software from Oslo, licensed on annual subscriptions to logistics, energy and public-sector customers in Norway, Sweden and Denmark. Annual recurring revenue reached NOK 210 million in 2025. Fjordkart had never installed a time-tracking system: squads chose their own tools and set their own cadence, and the arrangement showed up in two numbers the company quoted often — an internal engagement survey placing it in the 88th percentile for Nordic technology firms, and voluntary attrition averaging 6% a year (see **Exhibit 1**).

Nordfrakt was the first customer to buy a fixed date backed by money. The contract carried a late penalty of NOK 400,000 per week, capped at NOK 4.8 million, and let Nordfrakt walk away after six weeks of slippage. Within days of signature a member of the delivery committee had drafted a three-part response: developer activity monitoring built on commit and keystroke dashboards, mandatory daily time-logging, and a delivery bonus of NOK 60,000 per engineer payable only if 1 December was hit — NOK 660,000 if all eleven were paid.

## The Situation

The build had been estimated internally at about 7,200 engineering-hours. The two squads assigned to it supplied roughly 6,400 productive hours across the window: a shortfall of about 800 hours, close to 12% of that capacity (see **Exhibit 1**).

Solheim inherited four measures on Atlas Core and a fifth derived from them (see **Exhibit 2**). The intrinsic-motivation index stood at 71 against 82 before the contract; Fjordkart's own history held that below 60 a squad's output fell under its own baseline. Effective velocity was an index in which 100 stood for the 6,400-hour baseline. Key-engineer flight risk stood at 28%, measured on two people rather than eleven — Eirik Lund and Sunniva Bakke, who between them held the routing algorithm. Above 40% the committee's model expected one to leave; replacing either cost about NOK 1.1 million and four to five months of lost pace. Team trust stood at 6.4 out of 10, delivery probability at 52%. Lund and Bakke had both updated their public profiles since the announcement; neither had said anything to Solheim.

Nothing in the position corrected itself. The 800 hours did not close on their own, the penalty clock started at NOK 400,000 a week on 2 December with Nordfrakt's exit right six weeks behind it, and the index had fallen eleven points before a single control was installed. The committee wanted the controls in place before sprint zero; the chief technology officer held that any dashboard would cost more in output than it returned. Neither had attached a number to the claim.

## What Solheim Controlled

Solheim's week produced three positions for the committee. The first was her reading of the survey: that none of the three proposals explained the fall, all three being ordinary management instruments and the eleven points nervousness about a hard date; that the daily logging explained it, being the most visible of the three; or that the monitoring and the deadline-contingent bonus together explained it, those two being the ones that reached into the working day and into pay. The second was her reading of the 800 hours: that the estimate was padded and the gap was not real; that it was real but recoverable through effort once the team was pushed and paid; or that it was structural and would close only through focus, a scope cut or added people. The third was her posture in the committee: install the controls quickly so the board could see the work; decline monitoring on principle and offer no reporting; or offer a delivery signal built on outcomes rather than activity.

She had to fix the money. Four schemes sat on the table: the committee's NOK 60,000 per engineer payable only on 1 December, NOK 660,000 in full; a completion bonus of NOK 25,000 a head paid to the squad collectively rather than to individuals; no cash at all, and instead a conference budget, a learning sprint after delivery and public credit; or no special pay, with the NOK 660,000 spent on a third squad. Whatever she chose, she had to say what it meant when she announced it — that the money was tied to the date and forfeit if the date slipped, that it was a share of what the contract earned, or nothing beyond the figure in the letter. She also had to decide how many of the 800 hours to act on that month, from none to all, through scope cuts or added capacity.

The operating model came down to three settings and a question about two people. She had to fix how closely the work was watched, on a scale running from outcome-only check-ins to real-time activity dashboards with daily logging; how much the squad decided for itself, from a manager assigning every task and its sequence to a squad that self-organised entirely; and how the date was carried, as a target handed down, as a commitment the squad co-owned with full sight of the constraint, or anywhere between. The question about the two people was whether she did something aimed specifically at Lund and Bakke, or something team-wide that included them, or nothing beyond managing the squad as a squad.

Nine-month builds met trouble, and the model had to say in advance how she would answer three things if this one did. Her first move could be more control and mandatory overtime; absorbing extra work inside the squad without raising it with the customer; or starting with the people and re-planning capacity in the open. Her position with Nordfrakt could be to hold 1 December on everything promised; to let the date slip and budget for the NOK 400,000 a week; or to reopen scope and state what hitting the full date would cost. Her account to the committee could lead with the activity figures; with the team's shortcomings; or with the position as she read it. Underneath all three sat what she could least evidence: how firmly she held that a squad left to organise itself would deliver a fixed date faster than a squad that was watched.

## Closing

Three words stood on the whiteboard in Solheim's office — *watched*, *decided*, *carried* — the space beside each one still empty. Sprint zero opened in seven days. The survey said 71, the committee's model said 52%, and nothing had been installed yet.
