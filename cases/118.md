## Abstract

MERIDIAN INDUSTRIAL GROUP was, in the case universe, a diversified industrial holding with USD 90B in 2025 revenue across six operating divisions, a twenty-four-member executive committee, a 2024-2026 CEO-adopted three-horizon growth framework that had explicitly committed to an H2/H3 innovation engine outside the operating divisions, and a 2025 board commitment of USD 48M across the first three years of the engine. The InnoLab Builder commitment at stake inside the 2026 operating cycle was the three-round design-launch-defend trajectory the newly appointed Founding Director had to navigate to produce a pipeline the core could use without swallowing it, an adoption path that could survive the Q3 2026 CFO-budget-review, and a defense architecture that could hold against the twin crises (Stage-Gate imposition, trade-press piece) the Q4 2025 risk-register had already priced. On Monday, February 16, 2026, Rafael Antunes, a 46-year-old former business-unit general manager who had joined Meridian Industrial Group in June 2024 as Head of Strategy for the Performance Materials division and had been appointed Founding Director of the Meridian Innovation Lab on January 5, 2026, had opened the Round-1 lab-design commitment the CEO had asked him to land by the March 4 executive-committee commitment. The case opens at 6:16 a.m. on Tuesday, February 24, 2026, at the Meridian Industrial HQ as Antunes prepares the Round-1 six-lever design commitment.

On the morning of **Tuesday, February 24, 2026**, **Rafael Antunes** arrived at the Meridian Industrial HQ in the Lisbon business district at 6:16 a.m., opened the Round-1 six-lever design sheet his Chief of Staff and Head of Pipeline had finalised at 11:08 p.m. the night before, and re-read the sentence he had written at the top of his January 2026 appointment-transition notebook: *"Innovation labs survive on design, not on stories."* Nine days separated him from the March 4 executive-committee commitment.

## The Protagonist

Rafael Antunes, 46, had joined Meridian Industrial Group in June 2024 as Head of Strategy for the Performance Materials division, where he had, across eighteen months, run a USD 4.2B operating business line through a 2024-2025 pricing reset and a 2025 emerging-market expansion. Before joining the group, he had been a business-unit general manager at a European specialty-chemicals operator for seven years and had, across that tour, led a 2018-2020 corporate-incubator pilot that had produced two businesses that had been absorbed into the parent's core operating portfolio and one business that had been spun out. The Chief Executive Officer had appointed him Founding Director on January 5, 2026, specifically on the strength of two capabilities: his experience running H2/H3 pipelines through corporate-antibody windows, and his reputation for refusing to take lab mandates that required him to run H1 rescue work. His appointment letter had tied 35% of his long-term incentive plan to a composite three-year Innovation Score and 15% to a qualitative CEO-review metric — a compensation design the CEO had anchored on the three-year pipeline-quality trajectory.

## The Decision

Antunes had to commit, by the March 4 executive-committee commitment, the Round-1 six-lever design and the directional intent for Rounds 2 and 3.

1. **Round 1 — Lab Design** — how to set six levers: mandate (H1/H2/H3 blend), operating model (corporate-embedded vs. arms-length with embedded sponsors), staffing (builder-heavy vs. operator-heavy vs. scout-heavy), funding (annual envelope vs. stage-gate), governance (CEO-reporting vs. CTO-reporting), and location (HQ vs. studio district). The working envelope had seated H2/H3 mandate, arms-length with embedded sponsors, builder-heavy staffing, annual envelope plus 20% external grants, CEO-reporting, and studio-district location — a "slightly defiant design" the sim engine would score on risk-profile as well as outcome.
2. **Round 2 — Pipeline Launch** — how to respond to two crises landing inside the launch window: a Q3 CFO-rescue request that asked the lab to solve a supply-chain KPI (pure H1 work, Cost Efficiency short-term plus four, Pipeline Quality minus nine if accepted), and a star-builder poach that offered the lab's top builder a PMD role inside the core Performance Materials division (Retention risk, Pipeline Quality minus twelve if lost).
3. **Round 3 — Defense** — how to respond to twin crises landing inside the same week: a CFO-driven Stage-Gate imposition across the full pipeline (Cost Efficiency plus twelve, Pipeline Quality minus fifteen if accepted in full) and a trade-press "corporate innovation theater" piece (Internal NPS minus eight if ignored, Internal NPS plus four if the journalist is invited to visit and the piece runs as a balanced portrait).

On Antunes's working model, an H2/H3-plus-Arms-Length-plus-Builder-Heavy-plus-Envelope-plus-CEO-Reporting-plus-Studio-District-plus-Decline-plus-Counter-plus-Lean-Gate-plus-Journalist-Invitation envelope delivered Pipeline Quality 78, Adoption Rate 64, Cost Efficiency 61, Internal NPS 42 and a Final Score of 76 — an outcome that cleared the Q3 2026 budget-review and extended the lab into a second operating cycle. A Safer H1/H2-plus-Corporate-Embedded-plus-Stage-Gate-plus-Accept-CFO-Rescue-plus-Lose-Star-plus-Full-Stage-Gate envelope delivered Pipeline Quality 48, Cost Efficiency 72, Internal NPS 28 and a Final Score of 54 — an outcome that the CEO would not re-commit to.

## Group Background

Meridian Industrial Group had been assembled across 1992–2018 through seventeen industrial-operating-company acquisitions into a diversified holding with six operating divisions (Performance Materials, Industrial Automation, Energy Infrastructure, Advanced Manufacturing, Supply Chain Solutions and Environmental Services), 72,000 employees across twenty-three countries, and a 2025 revenue of USD 90B. The group's 2024-2026 three-horizon growth framework had, under the CEO's December 2024 board commitment, allocated USD 48M to an H2/H3 innovation engine over three years and had explicitly committed the engine to be "outside the core, inside the strategy."

The Q4 2025 innovation-dossier had identified three specific pressures the three-round envelope would inherit. First, the Q4 2025 CFO engagement note had flagged the Q3 2026 budget-review as the single window in which the H2/H3 envelope would be defended against an H1 rescue demand — a calendar that framed the Round-2 CFO-rescue response. Second, the Q4 2025 talent-market scan had flagged the core-PMD-ladder poach risk on builder-heavy teams as a high-probability event inside the first twelve months of the lab — a signal that framed the Round-2 counter-offer response. Third, the Q4 2025 risk-register had priced the "innovation theater" trade-press piece as a foreseeable Round-3 event on a 42% expected-landing basis — a pricing that framed the Round-3 journalist-invitation response.

## Industry and Market Context

The corporate-innovation-infrastructure landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2018–2024 corporate-innovation-lab evidence base had, across 84 H2/H3 lab designs, documented a three-year Pipeline-Quality differential of 22–34 points between arms-length-plus-CEO-reporting designs and corporate-embedded-plus-CTO-reporting designs — a pattern that anchored the Round-1 commitment. Second, the 2020–2024 lab-defense literature had, across 36 Stage-Gate-imposition events, placed lean-gate-for-H2/H3-only responses at a twelve-month Pipeline-Quality preservation rate of 79% against 28% for full-Stage-Gate acceptance — a pattern that framed the Round-3 commitment. Third, the 2019–2024 lab-survival evidence base had, across 48 corporate labs, placed labs that had declined at least one H1 rescue request inside the first operating cycle at a second-cycle survival rate of 82% against 34% for labs that had accepted every rescue request — a pattern that framed the Round-2 decline commitment.

Empirically, the post-2019 corporate-innovation literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, labs that had sequenced arms-length-plus-CEO-reporting-plus-studio-district designs had, across a 2020–2024 sample of 34 European and North American industrial-group labs, cleared Q3 budget-reviews in 76% of cases against 31% for labs that had sequenced corporate-embedded designs. Second, labs that had declined H1 rescue work in the first operating cycle had, across the same sample, preserved builder-team retention rates above 80% in 74% of cases against 38% for labs that had accepted rescue work — a pattern that anchored the Round-2 commitment.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Chief Executive Officer had, in the January 2026 executive-committee appointment meeting, approved a three-round envelope, a three-year Pipeline-Quality target of 72, an Adoption Rate floor of 55, a Cost Efficiency floor of 55, and an Internal NPS floor of 35. The Chief of Staff had, on January 24, circulated the Round-1 six-lever design pre-read. The Head of Pipeline had, on February 4, circulated the Q1 2026 portfolio scan that identified twelve candidate projects across H1/H2/H3. The Chief Financial Officer had, on February 10, flagged the Q3 2026 supply-chain KPI that she would, absent a lab-decline, escalate to the lab. The Chief Human Resources Officer had, on February 14, confirmed the core-PMD-ladder poach risk on Antunes's top builder. The corporate-communications director had, on February 18, flagged the trade-press pitch.

### What Antunes knew

The team had modelled three three-round envelopes. An H2/H3-plus-Arms-Length-plus-Builder-Heavy-plus-Envelope-plus-CEO-Reporting-plus-Studio-District-plus-Decline-plus-Counter-plus-Lean-Gate-plus-Journalist-Invitation Envelope projected Pipeline Quality 78, Adoption Rate 64, Cost Efficiency 61, Internal NPS 42, Final 76. A Safer H1/H2-plus-Corporate-Embedded-plus-Stage-Gate-plus-Accept-plus-Lose-plus-Full-Gate Envelope projected Pipeline Quality 48, Cost Efficiency 72, Internal NPS 28, Final 54. A Hybrid Envelope (H2/H3 mandate, arms-length, stage-gate funding, CTO-reporting, accept one rescue request, counter-offer on poach, lean gate, journalist invitation) projected Final 68.

### What he did not know

Whether the Q3 2026 CFO-rescue request would land at the modelled supply-chain KPI scope or expand to a multi-division KPI portfolio; whether the core-PMD-ladder counter-offer would clear the Chief HR Officer's title-band governance; whether the trade-press piece would run as a balanced portrait or pivot to an adversarial frame inside the post-publication cycle; and whether the CFO's Stage-Gate imposition would stop at the lean-gate compromise or push for full-Stage-Gate at the Q3 budget-review.

### What others were saying

**The Chief of Staff**: *"H2/H3 mandate. Arms-length with embedded sponsors. CEO-reporting. The design decides the three years — everything else is consequence."*

**The Head of Pipeline**: *"Decline the CFO rescue. We lose a week of political cover and we keep four projects in H2/H3."*

**The Chief Human Resources Officer**: *"The core-PMD counter-offer needs title-band governance. I can clear it by Round 2."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"I want Stage-Gate on the full pipeline. You will not get it — but I want you to know I am going to ask for it."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Antunes had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — H2/H3 / Arms-Length / Builder-Heavy / Envelope / CEO / Studio / Decline / Counter / Lean-Gate / Journalist-Invitation:** The slightly-defiant design described above. Pipeline Quality 78. Adoption 64. Cost Efficiency 61. Internal NPS 42. Final 76. Recommended.

- **Option B — Safer H1/H2 / Corporate-Embedded / Stage-Gate / Accept / Lose / Full-Gate:** The low-risk design. Pipeline Quality 48. Cost Efficiency 72. Internal NPS 28. Final 54. Rejected.

- **Option C — Hybrid:** H2/H3 mandate, arms-length, stage-gate funding, CTO-reporting, accept one rescue request, counter-offer on poach, lean gate, journalist invitation. Final 68. Rejected as it trades the design optionality the CEO asked for.

Antunes's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final score was Round 1 — the six-lever design commitment that, on its own, anchored the H2/H3 mandate and the CEO-reporting governance that would survive the Q3 2026 budget-review.
