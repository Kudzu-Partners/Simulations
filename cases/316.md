## Abstract

Andean Capital Partners Fund III was, in the case universe, a 2023-vintage mid-market Chilean-headquartered private-equity sponsor with USD 380m of committed capital across 28 LP relationships, a 4-to-6-deal portfolio-construction target, a 3.5–4.5× net-MOIC strategy and a specialised healthcare-services-and-consumer-services deal thesis across the Andean and Southern-Cone LATAM corridor. CliniBird S.A., the third Fund-III portfolio investment, was a 48-clinic veterinary-services platform operating across Santiago, Buenos Aires, Lima, Bogotá and Mexico City serving approximately 420,000 pet-owner customers — acquired April 4, 2025 at an 8.0× trailing-EBITDA entry multiple on USD 15m EBITDA, for a USD 120m enterprise value, with a USD 48m Fund-III equity contribution and USD 72m of debt split into a USD 54m seven-year amortising senior-term-loan-A at SOFR+475 and a USD 18m mezzanine with a 12% PIK-toggle feature. On **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 8:52 a.m. — exactly 12 months and 19 days after close — **Tomás Errázuriz-Vial**, a 44-year-old Deal Partner on CliniBird, opened the post-Q1 2026 covenant-stress workbook his associate had finalised at 11:58 p.m. the previous evening and began drafting the four-round package for the 11:00 a.m. Investment-Committee session.

On the morning of **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 8:52 a.m., **Tomás Errázuriz-Vial** arrived at the Andean Capital Partners office on Cerro El Plomo in Las Condes on month 12 of the 48-month CliniBird hold, greeted the two associates finishing the post-Q1 covenant-stress model, and walked into the partner office past the wall of five framed deal tombstones (the 2019 Andean-Industrial-Foods acquisition by Fund I; the 2020 Andean-Chemicals carve-out by Fund II; the 2022 Andean-Logistics-Platform roll-up by Fund II; the 2023 Andean-Consumer-Health Fund-III close; the 2025 CliniBird Fund-III signing). On the desk sat the post-Q1 2026 covenant-stress workbook refreshed at 11:58 p.m. The interest-coverage reading was highlighted at 2.10×. The covenant minimum of 2.00× was one bad quarter away.

---

## The Protagonist

Errázuriz-Vial, 44, had been Deal Partner at Andean Capital Partners since 2022 — four years in role at the case opening — had completed an Ingeniería Comercial at the Universidad de Chile in 2004 and an MBA at the Stanford Graduate School of Business in 2010, had spent five years at Goldman Sachs LATAM M&A in New York and Santiago between 2010 and 2015, had served as Vice President and Principal at a LATAM regional PE firm between 2015 and 2022, and had been promoted to Deal Partner at Andean Capital Partners in 2022 on the strength of his Andean-Industrial-Foods exit at 3.8× MOIC. His PE instincts had been shaped by three formative exposures: the 2007–2010 Stanford PE-coursework exposure to the Kaplan-Strömberg canon; the 2017 LATAM-regional-PE workout on an Argentine-exposed portfolio company where an equity-cure-plus-amendment pattern had averted a senior-agent-remedies event; and the 2021 Andean-Chemicals carve-out exit at 4.1× MOIC that had taught him the strategic-sale-at-premium-to-comps path.

---

## The Decision

Errázuriz-Vial had to commit, by the 11:00 a.m. Investment-Committee session, the four-round package.

1. **Round 1 — LBO structure and Kaplan-Strömberg bridge.** Reaffirm the entry structure. Commit the value-bridge sequencing with EBITDA-growth as the primary driver and multiple-expansion and debt-paydown as amplifiers.
2. **Round 2 — PIK-toggle decision.** Commit the toggle-the-mezz posture to save USD 2.16m of cash interest for the subsequent two quarters, preserve the 2.0× covenant minimum, and avoid the senior-agent-remedies trigger.
3. **Round 3 — Altman-Z-Merton and amendment-cure.** Commit the amendment-and-waiver with the 40-bp step-up and the USD 3m equity-cure injection. Do not enter restructuring. Enter managed distress.
4. **Round 4 — Exit path.** Commit the strategic-sale path to the global vet-services consolidator at 11× subject to two additional quarters of stabilisation and EBITDA reset to USD 22m. Keep the secondary-PE option as a back-stop at 10×.

Commitments: the Round 1 structure-and-value-bridge commitment; the Round 2 PIK-toggle commitment; the Round 3 amendment-and-equity-cure commitment; the Round 4 strategic-sale-with-secondary-backstop commitment; and a one-page IC memo that frames the four-round commitment as a single 48-month hold with the managed-distress interlude as the Month-12-to-Month-18 window.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The April 4, 2025 CliniBird closing at 8.0× on USD 15m EBITDA; the 100-day-plan execution through Q3 2025; the centralised-procurement USD 1.8m run-rate saving by Q4 2025; the brand-unification launch in January 2026; the revenue-cycle-management-uplift by Q1 2026; the March 2026 Argentine-currency-shock hitting four of the 48 clinics; the Q1 2026 consolidated EBITDA −12% reading; the Q1 2026 interest-coverage at 2.10×; and the April 23 IC session were the sequence.

### What Errázuriz-Vial knew

The post-Q1 2026 covenant-stress readings; the PIK-toggle economics on the mezzanine; the Altman-Z-score and Merton-model distress readings; the senior-agent relationship and the amendment-and-waiver terms available; the Fund-III equity-cure capacity and the LP-communication-posture; the four add-on clinics in the pipeline at 5.5× with three Letter-of-Intent-ready; the strategic-sale approach from the global vet-services consolidator at an 11× indicative; and the IC's appetite for a managed-distress decision package.

### What he did not know

Whether the senior agent would accept the amendment-and-waiver terms at 40-bp step-up or would press for 60-bp step-up and a 10-bp commitment-fee adjustment; whether the Argentine-currency shock would re-compound in Q2 2026 and push the interest coverage below 2.0× despite the PIK toggle; whether the global vet-services consolidator would hold the 11× at a Month-18 transaction or would reset to 10×; whether the four add-on clinics would close at 5.5× or would reprice to 6.0–6.2× on the competitive signal from the CliniBird distress read; whether the secondary-PE back-stop would triangulate at 10× or would reset to 9.5× on the managed-distress signal; and whether Fund-III's LPs would accept the Month-12-to-Month-18 managed-distress interlude without eroding the 3.5–4.5× MOIC positioning.

### What others were saying

**The Managing Partner (56)**: *"Tomás, doce meses en la operación. La historia no la escribís al mes doce. La escribís al mes cuarenta y ocho. Pero el mes doce determina si te queda historia."*

**The CliniBird CEO (appointed by Andean at close, 49)**: *"Los cuatro add-ons están listos. Las cuatro clínicas argentinas están en revisión. Si toggleamos, gano seis meses. Si no, pierdo cuatro clínicas."*

**The senior-agent credit officer (at Banco BICE, 47)**: *"La enmienda es posible. Cuarenta puntos básicos de step-up, tres millones de equity cure, dos trimestres de covenant más ancho. Más que eso, no pasa."*

**The mezzanine lender (Andean Mezzanine Fund II, 51)**: *"El toggle está en el doc. Si lo usás, lo usás. No hay sorpresa."*

**The global vet-services consolidator M&A lead (Barcelona-based, 53)**: *"Once veces sobre veintidós millones de EBITDA estabilizados. No antes."*

**The 28 Fund-III LPs (aggregate signal via the Q1 LP letter)**: *"Cuarenta y tres por ciento de TIR proyectada. No nos muevan la proyección sin explicación escrita."*

## Options Under Consideration

Errázuriz-Vial had narrowed the advanced-CorpFin space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — PIK-toggle + amendment-and-equity-cure + managed-distress Month-12-to-Month-18 + strategic-sale at 11× at Month-18 (Recommended).** Respects the Kaplan-Strömberg value-bridge; saves USD 2.16m of cash interest over two quarters; preserves the covenant; prevents senior-agent remedies; stabilises EBITDA to USD 22m; triggers the strategic-sale at 11× for a projected 4.1× MOIC and 39% IRR. Projected MOIC lands inside the 3.5–4.5× target band.
- **Option B — No-toggle + aggressive restructuring + distressed-sale at 7.5×.** Rejected — does not utilise the mezzanine toggle feature; triggers senior-agent remedies at Q2 breach; forces a distressed-sale at a 20–25% discount to the stabilised exit path; produces a projected 1.2× MOIC at approximately 6% IRR.
- **Option C — No-toggle + LP-capital-call for full equity-cure + IPO-path at Month-36.** Defensible on LP-signal grounds but exceeds the Fund-III equity-cure governance limit; the IPO path is contingent on two more quarters of stabilisation the covenants may not permit; and the signal of a capital-call over a structural-feature utilisation may damage the LP relationship more than the PIK toggle would.

Errázuriz-Vial's private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the single decision most correlated with the 48-month MOIC outcome was the Round 2 PIK-toggle commitment, because the 2010–2025 LATAM panel had shown that PIK-toggle utilisation in Month-12-to-Month-18 managed-distress periods correlated at 0.71 with MOIC preservation readings and at 0.62 with strategic-sale-exit readings at 10×+, and because his 2017 LATAM-regional-PE workout had held a target-MOIC outcome under the same amendment-and-equity-cure pattern. The *Operación* frame sits at the full-cycle LBO mechanics with distressed-covenant-workout-and-exit-triangulation synthesis tier in the 2026 Express catalogue, distinct from PE Portfolio (sim 017, portfolio-construction frame), LATAM PE (sim 057, regional-PE thesis frame) and CFO Command (sim 210, portfolio-CFO frame) — 316 emphasises the sponsor-partner deal-execution synthesis across entry, operation, workout and exit.
