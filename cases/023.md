## Abstract

NexaCore Solutions, a $45M ARR B2B SaaS company selling workflow-automation software to mid-market customers in North America, entered Q1 2026 with 52% quota attainment, 34% sales-rep attrition, pipeline coverage of 2.1x, morale at 41 on a 100-point survey, and an internal Sales Health Score of 38. Its incoming VP of Sales, Priya Desai, had inherited a 42-rep organization designed around four dials — Territory Rebalance, Variable-Pay share of OTE, Above-Quota Accelerator, and Pipeline Hygiene threshold — that her predecessor had tuned conservatively and infrequently. Desai had ten quarters before her compensation cliff and forty-eight hours before her first all-hands. The case opens on her third day in the role, as she prepares the Q1 dial settings that the simulation will treat as her Round 1 decision.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Diagnose the interaction between territory design, compensation structure, and pipeline hygiene in a sales organization under-performing on two dimensions at once.
2. Evaluate the trade-offs between short-term quota attainment and long-term sales-rep retention under different variable-pay-to-base ratios.
3. Design an above-quota accelerator that pays the right reps without mispricing the book.
4. Analyze the second-order effects of a pipeline-hygiene threshold on forecast credibility and coaching capacity.
5. Recommend Quarter 1 dial settings and a forty-eight-hour narrative that line up with each other.

On the morning of **January 6, 2026**, **Priya Desai** sat on the edge of the desk in the VP of Sales office at NexaCore Solutions' Austin headquarters and read, for the third time, the one-page dashboard her sales operations lead had slid under the door at 6:58 a.m. The dashboard carried five numbers: *ARR $45M. Attainment 52%. Attrition 34%. Pipeline Coverage 2.1x. Morale 41.* Below those numbers, in her sales-ops lead's handwriting, a single annotation: *"Floor wants to hear from you by Friday."* Her first all-hands was scheduled for Thursday afternoon.

---

## The Protagonist

Priya Desai, 41, had joined NexaCore seven days earlier. A graduate of the University of Michigan and of Stanford GSB, she had built and scaled two prior sales organizations: a $15M–$60M ARR turnaround at a vertical SaaS company in 2017–2020, and a $60M–$140M ARR scale-up at a horizontal SaaS company in 2020–2024. Between roles she had spent fifteen months as a sales operator-in-residence at a growth-equity firm, where she had written the firm's internal diligence playbook on sales-compensation design. She had been recruited to NexaCore by its CEO against the CFO's preference for an internal promotion. Her contract carried a ten-quarter vesting gate tied to ARR growth, attrition reduction, and a composite Sales Health Score the board had not yet formally defined.

---

## The Decision

Desai had to approve, by Thursday January 8 at 9:00 a.m., the Quarter 1 settings on the four sales dials the simulation would treat as her Round 1 inputs:

1. **Territory Rebalance Effort (0–100)** — aggressive rebalance disrupts existing rep-account relationships but reduces the territory variance that lets underperformers hide.
2. **Variable Pay as % of OTE (30–70%)** — higher variable share attracts hunters and retains top performers but raises total comp volatility.
3. **Above-Quota Accelerator (1.0–3.0x)** — higher accelerator pays the top reps disproportionately and pulls pipeline forward, but mispricing it rewards one-deal wonders.
4. **Pipeline Hygiene Threshold (0–120 days)** — stale-deal removal improves forecast credibility at the cost of apparent pipeline coverage.

Her Q1 settings would compound across ten quarters against a fixed events schedule — a competitor launch in Q3, a rep-union drive in Q5, a macro demand shock in Q7 — and would be legible to the board through four lagging metrics (ARR, attainment, attrition, morale) and one leading composite (the Health Score).

---

## Company Background

NexaCore Solutions had been founded in 2017 in Austin as a workflow-automation product for mid-market operations teams. By 2025 it served 640 paying customers across manufacturing, logistics, and healthcare-adjacent verticals in the United States and Canada. ARR had grown from $4M (2020) to $45M (2025) at a compound rate of 62%, but the most recent two years had shown decompression: 38% growth in 2024 and 19% in 2025. The product had remained best-in-class by two of three industry analyst rankings. The commercial motion had not kept up.

The sales organization comprised 42 quota-carrying Account Executives across two segments (mid-market, $10K–$50K ACV; lower-enterprise, $50K–$200K ACV), supported by 14 Sales Development Representatives, 6 Solutions Engineers, and 3 Customer Success Managers. The team reported into three regional directors who reported to the VP of Sales. Variable pay had sat at 40% of OTE since 2021. The accelerator had been flat at 1.25x since 2022. Territories had not been rebalanced since 2023 — the last rebalance had preceded two top-rep resignations and had become, inside the team, a cautionary tale. Pipeline hygiene was informal; the CRM held 3,200 open opportunities, of which the sales-ops team estimated roughly 40% had been inactive for more than 90 days.

## Industry and Market Context

The mid-market B2B SaaS segment in which NexaCore competed had entered 2026 under three pressures. First, attainment distributions had flattened: top-quartile reps were carrying a larger share of team bookings than in 2020, and the median-rep contribution had fallen. Second, variable-pay structures across the segment had moved up: peer benchmarks placed the median variable share at 48% of OTE and the accelerator at 1.75x over-quota. Third, pipeline-hygiene discipline had bifurcated: one cluster of operators had moved aggressively to 60-day stale-deal removal and had recovered forecast credibility; a second cluster had held at informal hygiene and had lost it.

Three findings from the post-2020 commercial-operations literature were consequential for Desai's decision. First, aggressive territory rebalances recovered variance 40% faster than incremental rebalances but cost 4–8 points of morale in the quarter of execution. Second, variable-pay increases above the segment median correlated with attrition reductions of 6–10 percentage points at top reps but raised turnover among the bottom third. Third, above-quota accelerators above 2.0x correlated with pipeline pull-forward (good in the quarter, bad in the subsequent one) unless paired with a hygiene threshold below 75 days.

---

## The Situation

### How we got here

NexaCore's growth decompression had been diagnosed in a November 2025 board-commissioned operating review as a sales-motion problem rather than a product or market problem. Three of the top eight reps had resigned in 2025, two citing compensation ("base too high, cap too low"), one citing territory. Seven of the bottom fifteen reps had not hit quota in four consecutive quarters. The board had approved a search for a new VP of Sales in October. Desai had been offered the role on December 14 and had accepted on December 17. Her predecessor, well-liked internally, had been retained as a senior advisor through Q2.

### What Desai knew

The internal analytics team had modeled the four dials across the ten-quarter horizon. Aggressive territory rebalance (effort 60–80) recovered territory variance from 38 to roughly 22 over two quarters; a moderate rebalance (20–40) held at 32. A variable-pay lift from 40% to 55% reduced projected attrition from 34% to roughly 22% by Q4 but raised projected base-plus-variable cost per rep by 7%. An accelerator of 2.0x, paired with a 60-day hygiene threshold, projected Q2 attainment at 64% and Q4 attainment at 72%, against a base case of 54% and 58% respectively.

### What she did not know

Whether the two flight-risk reps she had been told about on Monday — both in the top five — would stay past Q2 under a rebalance, or would leave together. Whether the rep-union drive rumored for Q5 was real or was sales-floor theater. Whether the Q3 competitor launch, the single biggest pipeline event of the year, would hit the segment the board thought it would or the segment it actually would.

### What others were saying

**Mateo Pérez**, Director of Sales Operations, argued for **Aggressive Hygiene First**: *"Clean the pipeline before you touch comp. Three thousand two hundred opps and forty percent of them are garbage. You cannot redesign compensation on a lie."*

**Angela Chen**, Regional Director (East), argued for **Compensation First**: *"The top reps are already interviewing. Move variable pay to fifty-five and the accelerator to two. Hygiene is a six-month fix. Comp is a Friday fix."*

**Devon Booker**, interim VP of Sales (Desai's predecessor), argued for **Territory First**: *"Rebalance now — before reps form attachments to the new comp. You get one rebalance a year and you get it cleanly or not at all."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Desai had narrowed the space to three options.

- **Option A — Aggressive, All Dials (Pérez + Chen, combined):** Rebalance 60, Variable Pay 55%, Accelerator 2.0x, Hygiene 60 days. Projected Q4 attainment 72%, attrition 18%, morale 58 (from 41), Health Score 62 (from 38). Morale dip in Q1 to 36 before recovery. High variance.

- **Option B — Phased (Desai's draft):** Q1 — Rebalance 35, Variable Pay 50%, Accelerator 1.75x, Hygiene 75 days. Q2 escalation conditional on Q1 data. Projected Q4 attainment 66%, attrition 23%, morale 52, Health Score 54.

- **Option C — Conservative (the board's implicit default):** Rebalance 20, Variable Pay 45%, Accelerator 1.5x, Hygiene 90 days. Projected Q4 attainment 58%, attrition 29%, morale 46, Health Score 44. Preserves optionality; does not move the problem.

Desai suspected Option A was right on the ten-quarter composite and wrong on the Q1 morale math; Option B preserved flexibility at the cost of committing to a visible plan the floor could grade; Option C was the option her predecessor had run, twice, and was the reason she had the job. At 8:47 a.m. she would walk down the hall to finalize the dashboard she would present to the CEO at noon.
