## Abstract

Sofía Morales was, in the case universe, an 18-year-old Ecuadorian high-school graduate who had completed her Bachillerato General Unificado at a public school in the Valle de los Chillos district of Quito in July 2025 and had started her first job on March 2, 2026 as a sales associate at TiendaHogar, a medium-sized Ecuadorian department-store chain specialising in home goods and furnishings, at a gross monthly salary of USD 450 (USD 407 net after the 9.45% IESS social-security contribution). She rented a shared bedroom at USD 120 a month in her aunt's apartment on Avenida General Rumiñahui and was enrolled in a once-a-week evening accounting programme at a Quito tecnológico institute costing USD 28 a month. On **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 7:52 a.m., at the kitchen table of her aunt's apartment, **Sofía Morales** opened her second-month-salary envelope and sat down with a notebook, a pencil and a list of expenses she had written on a folded sheet of paper the evening before to make three decisions — how to budget the USD 407, whether to accept a store-credit offer her manager had mentioned during her lunch break on Tuesday, and how to start saving toward a laptop she needed for her accounting coursework.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Apply Elizabeth Warren's 2005 fifty-thirty-twenty budgeting rule to Sofía's USD 407 IESS-net monthly take-home, allocate the budget across needs (shared room, bus pass, groceries, phone, utilities), wants (entertainment, clothing, occasional dining out) and 20% to savings-and-debt, and articulate why the rule is a starting-point discipline rather than a rigid formula.
2. Apply the Rule-of-72 compound-interest approximation to Sofía's USD 45/month savings plan at 5% annual cooperativa interest, compute the USD 110,000 age-65 balance starting at 18 versus USD 65,000 starting at 28, and articulate why ten years of delay costs USD 45,000 in real money and why starting early is the single highest-return decision a young worker makes.
3. Evaluate the TiendaHogar 24% TEA store-credit offer on a USD 200 purchase over 12 months, compute the USD 27 interest cost, compare to the USD 10 opportunity-cost of saving the same USD 200 at a 5% cooperativa rate, and articulate why the net cost-of-credit of USD 37 represents 10% of a month's take-home pay in interest alone.
4. Commit a SMART financial goal (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) of a USD 450 laptop in 10 months at USD 45/month, and articulate why the SMART structure converts "I want to save" into a mechanism that the 50/30/20 budget enforces.
5. Triage Round-3 random events (USD 35 birthday dinner, USD 60 shoe sale, USD 80 medical expense) through the needs-versus-wants matrix, protect the 20% savings bucket and the SMART goal, and articulate why the *Primer Sueldo* frame sim 327 occupies is distinct from Elena Marquez PersonalTax (sim 246) and Tienda Azul Accounting Journey (sim 080) — 327 is a secondary-education personal-finance primer aimed at first-salary workers and Bachillerato-level students, not at professional tax-planning or SME bookkeeping.

On the morning of **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 7:52 a.m., **Sofía Morales** sat at her aunt's kitchen table in the Valle de los Chillos apartment with her second-month-salary envelope, a notebook with the Warren 50/30/20 rule her accounting-programme instructor had written on the board the previous Tuesday, a pencil, and a list of the expenses she had written the night before — the USD 120 shared-room rent she paid her aunt in cash on the first of the month, the USD 24 monthly Quito bus pass, approximately USD 85 in groceries and weekday lunches, USD 18 for her prepaid phone plan, USD 28 for the tecnológico accounting programme, and the small recurring expenses her aunt had helped her list on a blank sheet folded into the notebook. She had USD 407 to allocate. The TiendaHogar store-credit offer from her manager was in her uniform pocket. The laptop for her accounting coursework had been on her mind for a month.

---

## The Protagonist

Morales, 18, had completed her Bachillerato General Unificado in July 2025 with an 8.4-average on the Ecuadorian 10-point scale, had worked summer-holiday shifts at a Valle de los Chillos panadería between 2023 and 2025 earning approximately USD 12 per six-hour shift on weekends, had started her first full-time job at TiendaHogar on March 2, 2026 after a three-week recruitment process that included a written arithmetic test she had passed at the 92nd percentile, had enrolled in the Quito-tecnológico once-a-week evening accounting programme at the beginning of April, and had lived with her aunt and two cousins since the 2022 death of her mother. Her personal-finance instincts had been shaped by three formative exposures: her aunt's habit of keeping the household-budget notebook on the kitchen shelf where Morales had seen it since childhood; the April 2026 accounting-programme first-module lecture on the 50/30/20 rule and the Rule of 72 her instructor had taught with a hand-drawn chart on the board; and the example of a cousin two years older who had taken a TiendaVeloz store-credit offer at 28% TEA in 2024 and had paid USD 96 in interest on a USD 280 purchase.

---

## The Decision

Morales had to make, by the 8:45 a.m. bus to her 9:00 a.m. shift at TiendaHogar, the three-round personal-finance package.

1. **Round 1 — Budget and compound interest.** Allocate the USD 407 across 50/30/20. Open a cooperativa savings account. Start the USD 45/month transfer the first Friday of each month.
2. **Round 2 — Credit and SMART goal.** Decline the TiendaHogar 24% TEA store-credit offer on the USD 200 purchase. Commit the USD 45/month saving to the laptop SMART goal over 10 months.
3. **Round 3 — Random-event triage.** Accept the USD 80 medical expense as a need and pay from the emergency-fund seed. Trade off the USD 60 shoe sale against the USD 35 birthday dinner — choose one, not both.

Commitments: the Round-1 budget allocation with the specific peso-and-cents line items; the Round-2 no-store-credit decision and the SMART laptop goal; the Round-3 triage rule; and a one-paragraph note to herself at the back of the notebook that frames the three-round commitment as her first-salary financial-habit installation.

---

## Sectoral Background

The 2015–2025 Ecuadorian first-salary-and-young-worker category had, in the case universe, registered approximately 118,000 first-job entrants per year aged 18–22 across the formal-sector private-employer base, concentrated in three tiers (minimum-wage service-sector employees like Sofía on USD 450 per month; technical-skill employees at USD 650–900 per month; professional-degree entrants at USD 950+ per month). The 2020–2025 Superintendencia de Bancos y Seguros panel on Ecuadorian-young-worker personal-finance outcomes had documented that formal-credit-at-first-salary readings correlated with approximately 58% higher five-year debt-to-income ratios and approximately 32% lower emergency-fund coverage at the five-year mark, confirming the financial-literacy-programme finding that the first-salary period installs habits that persist for decades.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2005–2025 personal-finance literature accessible at a secondary-education level produced three findings the simulation had to respect. First, Warren's 2005 fifty-thirty-twenty rule had produced documented household-financial-health readings across approximately 2,400 household-panel cases averaging +18% higher emergency-fund coverage and −22% lower consumer-debt-to-income ratios among households that respected the rule as a starting-point discipline against households that did not. Second, the compound-interest Rule of 72 had produced the well-replicated finding that every decade of saving-delay approximately halved the terminal-age balance for a given monthly contribution, confirming that starting at 18 was the single highest-return decision available to a young Ecuadorian worker. Third, the 2015–2025 Ecuadorian-young-worker consumer-credit panel had documented that store-credit offers at 18–26% TEA produced documented five-year debt-trap readings of approximately 28% of first-acceptance consumers, with the trap activated through repeat-offer cycles rather than the initial purchase itself.

## The Situation

### How Sofía got here

The 2022 death of Sofía's mother; the move to her aunt's apartment; the 2023–2025 summer-holiday panadería shifts at USD 12 per six-hour shift; the July 2025 Bachillerato graduation with the 8.4 average; the January 2026 TiendaHogar application; the March 2 TiendaHogar start; the April enrollment in the tecnológico accounting programme; the Tuesday April 21 manager-mentioned store-credit offer during her lunch break; and the April 23 second-month-salary-envelope morning were the sequence.

### What Sofía knew

The USD 450 gross / USD 407 net take-home after 9.45% IESS; the USD 120 shared-room rent she paid her aunt in cash on the first; the USD 24 bus pass; the USD 85 approximate groceries and lunches; the USD 18 prepaid phone; the USD 28 tecnológico programme; the April 2026 accounting-instructor lecture on 50/30/20 and Rule-of-72; the TiendaHogar store-credit 24% TEA offer; the USD 450 laptop price her cousin had researched at a Quito electronics store the previous weekend; and the Cooperativa San Francisco savings-account-opening form her aunt had brought home from work.

### What she did not know

Whether the Cooperativa San Francisco savings account would accept the USD 45/month standing transfer at no maintenance fee (her aunt's account had one at another cooperativa); whether her TiendaHogar manager would repeat the store-credit offer each month (the cousin-example said yes); whether the 10-month laptop target would absorb a second or third random event before reaching 10 months; and whether the tecnológico accounting programme would assign homework that required the laptop within the first 10 months or could be completed on the Quito-library public computers until she had it.

### What others were saying

**The aunt (51, works at a Cooperativa San Francisco branch)**: *"Sofi, te abro la cuenta. Las transferencias automáticas se hacen el día del sueldo. Si la plata te llega al bolsillo, no se ahorra."*

**The two cousins (ages 20 and 23)**: *"El crédito de tienda no lo firmes. A los dos meses te ofrecen otro. A los seis tenés tres y no sabés cuánto debes."*

**The TiendaHogar manager (34)**: *"Sofía, la tarjeta de la tienda te da acceso. Y los empleados nuevos que la toman tienen un descuento de bienvenida."*

**The tecnológico accounting instructor (42)**: *"Cincuenta-treinta-veinte. Regla del setenta y dos. Los dos conceptos más importantes que van a aprender este cuatrimestre. Memorícenlos."*

**The cousin two years older (20, works at a medical-supplies distributor)**: *"Yo firmé la TiendaVeloz hace dos años. Pagué noventa y seis en intereses por una compra de doscientos ochenta. No firmés."*

## Options Under Consideration

Morales had narrowed the personal-finance decision space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — 50/30/20 budget + Cooperativa San Francisco account + USD 45/month laptop SMART goal + decline the TiendaHogar credit + emergency-fund seed from first-month surplus (Recommended).** Respects the Warren / Rule-of-72 / SMART / needs-versus-wants canon; installs the savings habit on day one; declines the debt trap before it activates; builds the laptop at month ten and the emergency-fund at the same time. Projected personal-financial-health score 72 and emergency-fund coverage at month three.
- **Option B — Accept the TiendaHogar credit for the laptop + carry USD 450 at 24% TEA over 12 months + skip the cooperativa account this year.** Rejected — costs USD 61 in interest against USD 0 if saved; installs the debt-trap habit; skips the 20% savings bucket and forgoes the Rule-of-72 compounding; replicates the cousin's 2024 pattern.
- **Option C — Allocate USD 180 a month to savings at the expense of the wants bucket.** Defensible on savings-intensity grounds but unsustainable on realistic Quito-lifestyle grounds for an 18-year-old, risks Round-3 triage overreach, and violates the 50/30/20 starting-point discipline.

Morales's private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the single decision most correlated with the 10-month outcome was the Round-1 cooperativa standing-transfer commitment, because her aunt's kitchen-shelf budget notebook had shown 18 years of habit-installed savings discipline, because her cousin's 2024 TiendaVeloz experience had shown the debt-trap consequence, and because her accounting-programme instructor had confirmed the Rule-of-72 with a hand-drawn chart on the Tuesday class. The *Primer Sueldo* frame sits at the secondary-education first-salary-personal-finance-primer tier in the 2026 Express catalogue, distinct from Elena Marquez PersonalTax (sim 246, professional personal-tax-planning frame) and Tienda Azul Accounting Journey (sim 080, SME bookkeeping frame) — 327 is a Bachillerato-level primer aimed at first-job workers aged 16–20.

At 8:24 a.m., Morales closed her notebook with the 50/30/20 allocation written on the first page, the USD 45/month SMART laptop goal on the second, the TiendaHogar-credit declined-reason on the third, and a short note to herself at the back that read: *"Me pago a mí primero. Rechazo el mal crédito. Empiezo a los 18. Cada sueldo instala el hábito. La laptop llega en diez meses."* She put the notebook in her bag, walked out to the bus stop on Avenida General Rumiñahui, and boarded the 8:30 Quito-bound bus with her second-month salary envelope now converted into a plan.
