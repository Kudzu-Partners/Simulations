## Abstract

Brasa Dorada was a premium Peruvian rotisserie-chicken concept founded in Lima in 2019 by Carolina Espinoza, a former hospitality consultant. By March 2026 the brand operated three corporate units in three Lima districts — Miraflores, San Isidro, and Surco — with a 2025 system-wide revenue of USD 4.2M, a unit-level EBITDA margin of 17.6%, a System Quality score of 82/100 in the firm's internal field-audit framework, and a Brand Equity score of 54/100 in an independent local consumer panel. The board — comprising Espinoza (52% equity), a Lima-based family office (28%), and a Peruvian gastronomy investor (20%) — had approved in February 2026 a six-period franchise expansion plan with a USD 789,000 corporate cash starting position and a six-period horizon to twelve operating units across at least two countries, USD 340,000 in recurring annual royalty income, and a System Quality score not below 80. The case opens at 7:30 a.m. on Tuesday, March 31, 2026, as Espinoza prepares the Period-1 franchise-system design she will defend to the board on April 9.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the franchise-system design parameters (fee, royalty, exclusivity, training, manual depth) and their interactions with franchisee economics.
2. Diagnose the franchisee-selection trade-off between liquid-capital availability and cultural-fit and operations-experience signals, and recommend a selection rubric.
3. Analyze the System Quality versus Franchisee Satisfaction tension when a franchisee violates a system protocol, and recommend an enforcement framework.
4. Design an international-expansion sequencing plan that uses cultural and regulatory proximity as a first filter and unit economics as a second.
5. Recommend a complete Period-1 system-design package and articulate the multi-unit-operator strategy that the system must support by Period 4.

Nine days remained until the April 9 board meeting at which Espinoza would defend the Period-1 franchise-system design. Twelve units were the six-period target. Three were corporate. Nine had to be franchised. None had been signed.

---

## The Protagonist

Carolina Espinoza, 39, had founded Brasa Dorada in 2019 after seven years in hospitality consulting at a regional advisory firm and three years as a multi-unit project lead at a Peruvian premium-bakery chain that had attempted franchise expansion in 2014–2017. The 2014–2017 expansion had reached eleven units before a System Quality collapse — concentrated in three franchisees — had forced the closure of seven units in eighteen months and the partial unwinding of the franchise model. Espinoza had been, in 2017, the named author of the post-mortem document. The Brasa Dorada concept had been built, from the first unit, around a single design principle: the operating manual and the field-audit cadence would be designed before the first franchise was sold. The 2019–2025 corporate-only growth had given her six years to refine the system before the test.

---

## The Decision

Espinoza had to commit, by 9:00 a.m. on April 9, the Period-1 franchise-system design package the board would treat as the system's launch terms:

1. **Franchise Fee** — USD 15,000, USD 30,000, or USD 45,000 per unit at signing.
2. **Royalty Rate** — 4%, 6%, or 8% of franchisee gross revenue, monthly.
3. **Territory Exclusivity** — yes (with a defined radius — 2 km, 3 km, or 5 km — within which no other franchise may open) or no.
4. **Training Duration** — 2 weeks, 4 weeks, or 8 weeks at the corporate training kitchen plus on-site shadowing.
5. **Operations Manual Depth** — Standard (high-level processes), Detailed (process plus station-level sequences), or Comprehensive (process, station, employee handbook, supplier protocols, marketing toolkit).

The five sub-decisions would interact and would compound across six periods. A higher royalty rate financed deeper field-audit and brand-marketing services; territory exclusivity protected franchisee unit economics but slowed system-wide scale; training depth and manual depth determined the System Quality risk in the first three audit cycles; the franchise fee, while small as a recurring revenue line, signalled the brand's positioning to the franchisee pipeline.

## Company Background

Brasa Dorada had opened the first Lima unit in May 2019. The 2025 unit-level economics were strong: average annual revenue per unit USD 1.4M, food cost 28% of revenue, labor cost 22%, occupancy and other 16%, EBITDA margin 17.6%. The unit-level investment was USD 320,000 (kitchen build-out, equipment, working capital), with a payback of approximately 2.6 years at corporate-operated economics. The brand carried a single menu — fourteen items built around four chicken preparations and ten Peruvian sides and sauces — and a single supplier list with five primary suppliers and three approved alternates per category.

The 2025 corporate-team headcount was 18: Espinoza, a Director of Operations, a Director of Marketing, a Director of Finance, a Head Trainer, a Head of Field Quality, three regional supervisors, and ten cross-trained corporate-unit team leads. The board comprised Espinoza, the family-office representative (also Chair), and the gastronomy investor, with quarterly board meetings and an annual strategy off-site.

## Industry and Market Context

The premium casual-dining franchise segment in Latin America in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Period-1 design sat inside. First, the LATAM franchising regulation was uneven: Peru and Colombia had relatively permissive disclosure regimes; Mexico had a comprehensive Federal Law on Industrial Property (LFPI) franchise disclosure framework requiring an FDD-equivalent (Documento de Información Previa) at least 30 days before signing; Chile sat in between. Second, the multi-unit operator (MUO) model had become, between 2018 and 2025, the dominant franchise growth path in LATAM premium dining: a 2024 industry survey had found that 54% of premium-segment franchise units were operated by operators of three or more units, up from 31% in 2017. Third, the System Quality literature had converged on a clear finding: franchise concepts that operated comprehensive operations manuals and conducted field audits at six-week or shorter cadences sustained System Quality scores above 75 in 78% of cases over a five-year window; concepts that did not, in 31% of cases.

Empirically, the franchise-fee and royalty-rate research had two robust findings. First, franchise fees in the USD 25,000–USD 35,000 range for premium casual dining produced the highest applicant quality at the qualification stage; fees below that range attracted under-capitalized applicants; fees above that range deterred otherwise-strong restaurant operators in markets where the Brasa Dorada brand was new. Second, royalty rates of 6% with territory exclusivity had been associated with 14% higher five-year unit-survival rates than 4%-without-exclusivity at premium positioning.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had approved the six-period plan in February with three explicit constraints: (a) System Quality score not below 80 at any point; (b) a corporate cash floor of USD 250,000 at all times during expansion; (c) at least one international country added by Period 4. Espinoza had, in March, opened a parallel pre-qualification process for prospective franchisees — twelve candidates had submitted applications by March 28 — and had, separately, initiated an exploratory conversation with a Bogotá-based restaurant-group operator regarding a Colombia master-franchise arrangement.

### What Espinoza knew

The internal team had modeled three Period-1 design packages. A Conservative Package (USD 15,000 fee, 4% royalty, no exclusivity, 2-week training, Standard manual) projected six-period royalty income USD 220K, System Quality 76, twelve units. A Balanced Package (USD 30,000 fee, 6% royalty, 3 km exclusivity, 4-week training, Comprehensive manual) projected royalty income USD 340K, System Quality 82, twelve units. A Premium Package (USD 45,000 fee, 8% royalty, 5 km exclusivity, 8-week training, Comprehensive manual) projected royalty income USD 410K, System Quality 86, eight units (under-target — the higher fee compressed the qualified-applicant pool).

### What she did not know

Whether the highest-liquid-capital applicant in the twelve-applicant pool — who had scored, in the cultural-fit assessment, in the bottom quartile — would, if rejected, signal to other applicants that the qualification process was selective; whether the Bogotá master-franchise operator would accept a 4-units-in-18-months commitment under a USD 300,000 master-franchise fee; whether the Period-3 Field Quality audit cycle would identify a first-audit failure in the franchised network and how that handling would be observed by the other franchisees; and whether the gastronomy investor, who had, in informal conversations, expressed a preference for the Conservative Package, would press the position formally at the April 9 board meeting.

### What others were saying

**Mariana Ortiz**, Head Trainer: *"Four weeks is the minimum at which I can certify a franchisee operator. Two weeks is not training. It is theatre."*

**Diego Linares**, Head of Field Quality: *"Comprehensive manual or no manual. The Standard manual is what was in place at my prior employer when the system collapsed. I will not run audits against a manual that does not exist at the station level."*

**Catalina Vargas**, Director of Finance: *"Six percent and exclusivity. Four percent without exclusivity is the package that pays for next year's audits and not for the year-three field-quality team. The royalty is not a price. It is the operating budget for the system the franchisees pay for."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Espinoza had narrowed the space to three Period-1 packages.

- **Option A — Conservative Volume Play:** USD 15,000 fee, 4% royalty, no exclusivity, 2-week training, Standard manual. Six-period royalty income USD 220K, System Quality 76 (below floor), twelve units, MUO compatibility low.

- **Option B — Balanced Standard Play:** USD 30,000 fee, 6% royalty, 3 km exclusivity, 4-week training, Comprehensive manual. Six-period royalty income USD 340K, System Quality 82, twelve units, MUO compatibility high.

- **Option C — Premium Selective Play:** USD 45,000 fee, 8% royalty, 5 km exclusivity, 8-week training, Comprehensive manual. Six-period royalty income USD 410K, System Quality 86, eight units (target miss), MUO compatibility high but applicant pool thin.

Espinoza's private view, not yet shared with the gastronomy investor, was that Option B was the right Period-1 design and that the Period-4 international decision (Colombia master franchise) and the Period-6 multi-unit operator unlock were the compounds that the System Quality score had to defend through Periods 2 and 3.
