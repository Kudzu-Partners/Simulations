## Abstract

BancoAndino was, in the case universe, a LatAm universal bank operating across Peru, Bolivia and Ecuador, with USD 3.2B in 2025 total assets, a 4.0M-customer retail and SME book, a 2025 IT-operations baseline of USD 18M in annual OpEx across two aging on-premises data centres in Lima and La Paz, a 2025 availability reading of 99.5% against a four-nines board target, a 2025 proprietary-stack share of 40% against a peer benchmark of 22%, and a 2025 year-end cash position of USD 142M. The Q4 2025 board had approved a seven-year USD 72M cloud-programme envelope — the CloudArch cycle — and had positioned it as the bank's response to three overlapping pressures: a 2025 peer-cost benchmark that had priced the bank's 40% proprietary-stack share at USD 4.4M in annual forgone OpEx savings, a 2025 SBS (Superintendencia de Banca, Seguros y AFP) supervisory directive that had priced all Peru-origin customer data at a Peru-residency standard, and a Q1 2026 internal audit that had surfaced a Peru-data caching posture at the CloudFront edge network inconsistent with the SBS directive. The case opens at 6:44 a.m. on Thursday, April 23, 2026, at the BancoAndino Lima head office as **Ignacio Leguizamón-Vera**, a 51-year-old Chief Technology Officer, prepares the Phase-1 migration-strategy commitment. Sixty-six days separate him from the June 28 board technology-review session.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Phase-1 migration-strategy commitment (hybrid re-platform envelope that combines lift-and-shift on commodity workloads with re-platform on core-banking vs. pure lift-and-shift vs. full re-architect) and diagnose when the hybrid envelope dominates on the OpEx-and-innovation basis.
2. Analyze the Phase-2 AWS-São-Paulo contract envelope (Reserved-Instance-anchored with Peru KMS, SOC 2 and ISO 27001 certifications, USD 2M professional services vs. on-demand envelope) and recommend the Reserved-Instance envelope on the 35% unit-cost-savings basis.
3. Diagnose the Phase-3 SBS data-residency remediation (CloudFront-edge purge, Peru-only caching configuration, remediation inside the six-month window vs. contest envelope) and recommend the remediation envelope on the fine-exposure basis.
4. Respond to the Phase-4 S3 misconfiguration disclosure (proactive regulator-notification envelope on the 500 GB / 18-hour exposure window vs. selective vs. minimize) on an evidence-first disclosure protocol.
5. Design the Phase-5 FinOps archetype that couples an aggressive chargeback-plus-cloud-native envelope to the Phase-1 hybrid migration, delivers an OpEx reduction above 20% and positions the bank as cloud-native-bank rather than bank-on-cloud.

## The Protagonist

Leguizamón-Vera, 51, had assumed the Chief-Technology-Officer role at BancoAndino on August 1, 2024, after seventeen years across infrastructure, cloud and financial-services IT at two Peruvian banks, a Santiago-based retail-banking group and a hyperscale cloud provider's LatAm financial-services practice. He had, in 2020–2023, led the seven-year cloud migration of the Santiago bank that had cleared the OpEx-reduction target at 22% and had absorbed two supervisory-directive cycles on data residency without triggering a fine event. The Chief Executive Officer had recruited him in June 2024 specifically on two capabilities: his documented discipline of anchoring FinOps chargeback on business-unit P&Ls rather than on cost-centre allocations, and his willingness to default to proactive regulator-notification on misconfiguration events against the litigation-risk posture that had precipitated two LatAm supervisor-escalations in the 2022–2024 cycle. His 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the OpEx reduction, 25% to the uptime floor and 20% to the data-sovereignty reading.

## The Decision

Leguizamón-Vera had to commit, by the May 14 board pre-review, the Phase-1 envelope and the directional intent for Phases 2–5.

1. **Phase 1 — Migration strategy.** Four archetypes: hybrid re-platform (lift-and-shift on commodity workloads, re-platform on core-banking and open-banking APIs, re-architect on the mobile stack over Years 4–7, Composite 78), lift-and-shift (Composite 42), full re-architect (Composite 54, six-year calendar), or hybrid lift-plus-innovate (Composite 62). Working envelope: hybrid re-platform.
2. **Phase 2 — AWS contract.** Three vectors: Reserved-Instance-anchored-with-Peru-KMS (São-Paulo region, SOC 2 and ISO 27001, USD 2M professional services, 35% unit-cost savings, Composite 78), on-demand (Composite 44), or three-year-RI (Composite 66, forecast-brittleness). Working envelope: Reserved-Instance-anchored.
3. **Phase 3 — SBS data-residency remediation.** Three vectors: remediation (CloudFront-edge purge, Peru-only caching, six-month window, Composite 78), contest (Composite 18, fine-risk), or phased (Composite 52). Working envelope: remediation.
4. **Phase 4 — S3 misconfiguration disclosure.** Three vectors: proactive (regulator-first disclosure inside 72 hours, Composite 78), selective (Composite 38), or minimize (Composite 14). Working envelope: proactive.
5. **Phase 5 — FinOps archetype.** Three vectors: aggressive-chargeback-plus-cloud-native (containers, serverless, open-banking-API build, USD 3M/year OpEx cut, Composite 78), soft (Composite 48), or deferred (Composite 34). Working envelope: aggressive.

On Leguizamón-Vera's working model, a Hybrid / Reserved-Instance / Remediation / Proactive / Aggressive envelope delivered OpEx reduction 22%, Uptime 99.95%, Data Sovereignty 98, Composite 73.

## Company Background

BancoAndino had been founded in Lima in 1981 and had, across forty-five years, built a USD 3.2B-asset universal bank with a 4.0M-customer retail and SME book, a commercial-banking franchise in Bolivia and an Ecuador subsidiary with an early digital-bank posture. The 2015–2024 IT cycle had left the group on a two-data-centre on-premises infrastructure with a 40% proprietary-stack share — the 2024 peer benchmark had priced the gap at USD 4.4M in annual forgone OpEx. The Q4 2025 board-approved USD 72M CloudArch envelope had been positioned explicitly as a five-phase rebuild of the migration strategy, vendor contract, data-residency posture, incident-disclosure protocol and FinOps architecture.

Three Q1 2026 events were named in the March 2026 technology-review dossier. First, the December 2025 SBS circular had priced the Peru-origin customer-data residency standard as a board-level supervisory directive with a six-month remediation window closing in June 2026. Second, the February 2026 internal audit had surfaced a Peru-data caching posture at the CloudFront edge network that had moved the bank into a remediation-urgency posture. Third, the March 2026 S3 misconfiguration event — a 500 GB exposure window of 18 hours without documented unauthorized-access evidence — had forced a 72-hour disclosure-protocol window with the SBS as lead regulator.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2019–2025 LatAm financial-services cloud literature had, across 42 documented bank migrations above USD 50M, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, hybrid re-platform envelopes had delivered OpEx-reduction readings 12–20 points above lift-and-shift and 6–12 points above full re-architect in the 5–7-year horizon in 74% of cases. Second, Reserved-Instance-anchored contracts with accurate workload-forecasting had sustained 32–38% unit-cost savings across the contract term, while on-demand envelopes had delivered 4–9% savings. Third, proactive disclosure on misconfiguration events had preserved supervisor-trust readings 14–22 points above selective or minimize postures in 76% of cases and had precluded fine-escalation in 84% of cases.

Empirically, the post-2022 LatAm data-sovereignty literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, residency-remediation envelopes closed inside the supervisory window had avoided fine events in 92% of cases, against 38% for contest envelopes. Second, aggressive FinOps postures combined with cloud-native builds had sustained double-digit annual-OpEx-reduction rates for 5–7 years in 68% of cases.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the December 2025 session, approved the USD 72M envelope and the June 28 technology-review calendar. The Head of Infrastructure had, on March 5, delivered the migration-strategy workbook. The Head of Information Security had, on March 12, delivered the SBS-remediation and disclosure-protocol brief. The AWS Account Executive had, on March 19, delivered the Reserved-Instance contract. The Chief Financial Officer had, on March 26, delivered the FinOps-chargeback pack.

### What Leguizamón-Vera knew

The team had modelled three five-phase envelopes. A Hybrid / Reserved-Instance / Remediation / Proactive / Aggressive envelope projected Composite 73. A Lift-and-Shift / On-Demand / Contest / Minimize / Deferred envelope projected Composite 14. A Full-Re-Architect / Three-Year-RI / Phased / Selective / Soft envelope projected Composite 52.

### What he did not know

Whether the SBS would escalate the CloudFront-edge-cache finding into a named fine-event if the June 2026 remediation window closed; whether the Bolivian and Ecuadorian supervisors would mirror the Peruvian posture; whether the March 2026 S3 misconfiguration would expand its exposure scope on deeper forensic review; and whether the open-banking-API build would clear the 2027 regulatory sandbox threshold.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Infrastructure**: *"Hybrid re-platform. Lift-and-shift on commodity, re-platform on core. Re-architect the mobile stack over Years 4–7."*

**The Head of Information Security**: *"Proactive on the S3. Regulator-first inside 72 hours. And the CloudFront-edge purge closes inside the six-month window."*

**The AWS Account Executive**: *"Reserved Instances. Thirty-five-percent unit-cost savings. Forecast accuracy above 85 percent is the precondition."*

**The Chief Executive Officer**: *"Cloud is a seven-year governance decision."*

## Options Under Consideration

Leguizamón-Vera had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Hybrid / Reserved-Instance / Remediation / Proactive / Aggressive:** the recommended envelope. OpEx reduction 22%. Uptime 99.95%. Data Sovereignty 98. Composite 73. Recommended.
- **Option B — Lift-and-Shift / On-Demand / Contest / Minimize / Deferred:** the minimalist envelope. Composite 14. Rejected — fine-exposure probability above 78% under a contest posture.
- **Option C — Full-Re-Architect / Three-Year-RI / Phased / Selective / Soft:** the premium envelope. Composite 52. Rejected — six-year calendar forecloses the 2028 innovation-pipeline.

Leguizamón-Vera's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single phase most correlated with the final Composite was Phase 3 — the SBS data-residency remediation — because it was the phase that decided whether the bank cleared the June 2026 supervisory window as a remediation event the group absorbed or as a fine-escalation that would price the 2027 capital plan.
