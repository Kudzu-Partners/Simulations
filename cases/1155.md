# Helio Labs: Sixty-Four Days Out

**Case Code:** OPS-2026-1155 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1155 — *Event Management — The Corporate Conference* · **v1.0 · September 2026**

**Time required: 90 minutes**

---

At 9:00 AM on Monday, April 6, 2026, Priya Raman, chief marketing officer of Helio Labs, closed the door of a small meeting room in Austin, Texas, and wrote three numbers on the whiteboard. The hotel that had hosted last year's conference wanted US$460,000 for the room block, food and meeting space. The headline keynote speaker, Marcus Thorne, wanted US$75,000 plus travel and a green-room rider. And the chief financial officer wanted a cost per attendee between US$900 and US$1,100, against last year's run rate of US$1,233.

Nadia Brennan, the senior producer who ran HelioConnect for Helio through a small Austin event agency, had sixty-four days until 600 people arrived for three days of sessions, June 9 to 11. Raman wanted the budget built and the first contracts signed by the end of Wednesday.

## The Protagonist

Brennan, thirty-six, had produced corporate events for eleven years, the last four as her agency's lead on technology conferences. She had run the 2025 edition of HelioConnect, two days at a premium downtown hotel. She knew Raman's habits: short questions, a demand for the number behind every recommendation, and ninety seconds for any defence of a decision. Raman was her client, and Raman answered to a board that had been promised a bigger conference for less money.

## The Decision

Brennan had to turn a budget that did not fit into a conference that did. She would settle the venue, the audio-visual (A/V) partner and the contract terms first; then the pricing, sponsor language and registration process that would fill the rooms; then the operation of the opening day; and finally the response to whatever the second day brought (see Exhibit 2). Each early choice would decide which options she still had when something went wrong on site.

## The Company and its Setting

Helio Labs, Inc. was a 280-person business-software company in Austin with about US$48 million in annual recurring revenue, growing 42% a year. Its product automated workflows for mid-market operations teams. HelioConnect, now in its third year, was its flagship conference for customers, prospects, partners and press, and the largest discretionary line in the marketing budget. The finance team wanted cost discipline. Raman wanted a larger audience and a keynote the trade press would cover.

Fourteen sponsor contracts were expected to bring in US$185,000 across four tiers. Sponsor renewals across the sector had fallen 15% that year. Sponsors who rated an event below 7.5 on the ten-point pulse the conference app collected renewed far less often than those who rated it higher, and Raman's own target for attendees on the same scale was 8.0.

## The Situation

The budget was US$740,000. About US$95,000 was already committed to fixed operating costs (registration platform, event staff, signage, security and insurance) whatever Brennan chose. The rest had to cover the venue, A/V, the keynote and any contingency she decided to buy (see Exhibit 1).

If Brennan simply renewed last year's arrangements, the arithmetic was already visible. The same hotel at US$460,000 and the premium A/V house at US$215,000, with the keynote fee and the fixed costs, came to about US$845,000 before a single registration was sold. The cheaper alternatives carried their own costs. The convention center meant shuttles and a less polished experience for sponsors who had paid for polish. The regional A/V firm staffed each room with one engineer.

Thorne's agent had offered two contracts: fee and travel only, at US$75,000, or a version at US$78,000 with force-majeure language, a kill fee and a replacement speaker supplied by his bureau. The closing event on Day 3 was a rooftop reception, and the long-range forecast gave a 30% chance of thunderstorms. The hotel would hold an indoor ballroom for US$12,000 in advance; booked on the day, if still free, it would cost US$18,000.

Registration would open in earnest at fifty days out, when the team expected about 110 people on the books against a target of 600.

## What Brennan Controlled

By Wednesday she had four contracts to settle. The **venue**: the premium hotel; a mid-tier downtown hotel at US$380,000 including food and meeting space; or Austin's convention center with a host hotel at US$255,000. The **A/V partner**: a national firm at US$215,000 with full redundancy; the regional independent at US$158,000; or a hybrid, the national firm on the main stage and the regional firm in the breakout rooms, at US$128,000. The **keynote contract**, basic or robust. And whether to pay for the **ballroom hold**.

Between fifty and seven days out, four more. The **registration tiers**: three tiers at US$595 early-bird, US$895 general and US$1,795 VIP; two tiers at US$895 and US$1,495; or free entry for existing customers with prospects paying US$895. The **sponsor contract language**: explicit booth coordinates and stage timings, or generic "premium placement." The **allergen handoff**, the route by which dietary and allergy flags captured at registration reached the catering captain: a checklist signed at each step, a daily email digest, or no formal handoff. And one **demand lever**: sponsors emailing their own lists, a US$18,000 paid campaign on a professional social network, a push by Helio's customer-success team, or an aggressive early-bird discount.

On Day 1, four operating calls. How many **registration stations** to open for the 7:30 AM doors: six with QR self-check-in, four with pre-printed badges, or two printing badges on demand. How thoroughly to **test A/V** at 8:00 AM before the 9:15 keynote: a full test of microphones, projector, confidence monitor, recording, livestream and sponsor-logo loop; a quick microphone and projector check; or no test, trusting the rehearsal. How many **lunch stations** to run for 600 people in a 45-minute window: nine, over the kitchen's objections; the captain's seven with lunch staggered by track; or the captain's seven as proposed. And how to answer a **Platinum sponsor** unhappy with its booth: hold the contract and offer a free logo overlay during a break, worth roughly US$3,000 of visibility; move it to a better booth by displacing another sponsor; or tell it the contract was the contract.

Day 2 carried four exposures that the risk register named, and Raman expected Brennan to own the response to each if it arrived. A **keynote cancellation**: a bureau replacement with attendee messaging within an hour, a fireside conversation between Helio's chief executive and a senior customer, or refunds and a cancelled slot. A **main-stage projector failure**: an audience question-and-answer fill while escalating to the A/V firm's regional director, a fifteen-minute break, or holding the speaker in silence until the fix. An **allergen** found in food near an attendee flagged at registration: pull the item with a calm announcement, an incident report and a briefing to Raman; pull it quietly, with no announcement or report; or move on if no one was hurt. And a worsening **Day 3 storm forecast**: pay the same-day rate and move indoors with an hour's notice, split the reception between ballroom and rooftop, or hold the rooftop.

Raman would watch six numbers throughout: the budget remaining, the mirror of cumulative spending; cost per attendee, meaning total spending divided by registered attendees; registrations; sponsor revenue; and the two app pulses, one for attendees and one for sponsors, which would not exist until the doors opened. She also scored each phase against her own checklist and kept a running total. Those numbers, not the vibe in the room, would frame her report to the board.

## Closing

At 9:40 AM Raman left for a board preparation call. Brennan put the three venue proposals on one screen and the A/V bids on the other. The hotel's hold on the June dates expired at 5:00 PM on Wednesday, the same hour Raman expected the budget.
