# Riverside Vale: Ninety Days to Embed the Bundle

**Case Code:** ORG-2026-1792 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1792 — *Evidence into Practice* · **v1.0 · September 2026**

---

On the morning of Monday, 5 October 2026, Grace Adeyemi read the coroner's report a second time, hoping the first reading had been wrong. It had not. A 34-year-old mother had died of undetected sepsis, and the coroner's Regulation 28 "Prevention of Future Deaths" report named Riverside Vale NHS Foundation Trust's failure to apply the nationally recommended Sepsis Six bundle within the first hour as a contributing cause. Adeyemi, the Trust's Deputy Director of Quality Improvement, had ninety days to show the coroner, the regulator and her own Medical Director a credible plan to get the bundle used, not merely written down, across the emergency department and two acute wards.

The evidence itself was not in question: Riverside Vale's own research-active sepsis team had published the bundle nationally. A first attempt to introduce it in 2024 had pushed compliance up to 28% before quietly lapsing, and 28% was still where it stood. Median time from recognition to antibiotics ran 127 minutes against the 60-minute standard the bundle existed to hit. The regulator wanted a credible trajectory to at least 75% compliance by day ninety; the national reliability benchmark for the bundle sat at 90%. Adeyemi would make four rounds of decisions over those ninety days, each reviewed by the Medical Director and a non-executive board member, with a fixed change budget of £180,000 and 0.6 WTE of protected clinical time — no more of either.

## The Protagonist

Grace Adeyemi, 43, had joined Riverside Vale five years earlier as a ward-based improvement lead and had spent the last two as Deputy Director of Quality Improvement, a post the board created after deciding safety improvement needed a full-time owner rather than a rotating committee. She reported jointly to the Medical Director and the Chief Nurse, and her standing rested on two kinds of trust at once: whether frontline staff believed she understood their shift patterns, and whether the executive team believed her numbers. She had not run the failed 2024 rollout — that had happened under her predecessor — but she had inherited its 28% ceiling and the night shift's memory of it.

## The Decision

Round one asked a single question: what actually stopped staff from using a bundle they already knew worked? Adeyemi's team weighed four explanations: an adoption problem rooted in workload and thin local ownership; a knowledge problem, staff not yet understanding sepsis well enough; a training problem, the bundle's steps never properly taught; or individual staff attitudes toward instructions already received. A parallel choice located the highest-leverage barrier: the culture, leadership and workload that differed unit to unit; whether the evidence itself was sufficiently proven for this Trust; facilitation delivered as a mandate from above; or that no real barrier existed and the 2024 launch needed repeating harder. A third setting, zero to a hundred, recorded how candidly the team named this an adoption problem rather than reaching again for training.

Round two translated the national bundle into something each unit would actually use. Four framings were available: showing each unit its own local compliance and timing data; the clinical narrative of the coroner's case, handled carefully; a mandate from above paired with hard auditing; or a Trust-wide email with an accompanying poster. Adeyemi also split the entire 0.6 WTE of protected champion time across three messengers: a respected night-shift charge nurse with peer credibility, the research consultant whose team had published the bundle, and a directorate manager who could clear organisational obstacles. Finally, she chose the artefact to carry the bundle into daily workflow: an electronic prompt in the patient record plus a pocket checklist card, at £90,000; a laminated checklist card on every trolley, at £25,000; or a freshly commissioned evidence review to re-establish the case, at £60,000.

Round three set the rollout's shape across the emergency department and the two acute wards. One option sequenced the launch as pilot-and-spread — starting on a willing ward, then extending to the other two; a second launched identically on all three units at once; a third began with the hardest, most short-staffed unit before any lessons had been learned elsewhere. A parallel choice set how much each unit could adapt the bundle to local workflow: flexing the how while protecting the clinical what, holding to 100% fidelity with no changes anywhere, or letting each unit rewrite the bundle however it wished. Two further settings closed the round: up to £60,000 more of the change budget for weekly audit-and-correction feedback loops, and a zero-to-hundred setting for how firmly protected backfill time was defended for the exhausted night shift.

In the final round Adeyemi chose what to measure for the board — real-time bundle steps combined with outcome measures such as time-to-antibiotics, a lagging compliance percentage reported to the regulator monthly, or a launch-week dashboard that then moved attention elsewhere — and who would own the bundle on each ward once her champion time ran out: a named ward owner built into induction and handover, continued reliance on the central champion role, or no named owner at all. She also decided how to answer the board's question of whether 75% meant real adoption or a paper trajectory: describe the figure using the leading-indicator evidence and the night-shift plan, order compliance and audit hard until the number arrived, or declare victory on a launch week whose second-week compliance had already reached 70%. A final setting, zero to a hundred, recorded how firmly she was prepared to defend that position.

## The Company and its Setting

Riverside Vale NHS Foundation Trust ran a 620-bed district general hospital in the Midlands, with an annual operating budget of £410 million and roughly 6,400 staff. The emergency department and the two acute medical wards employed around 240 frontline staff across day, evening and night shifts; the night shift carried a history of distrust toward "another initiative from management." The Trust's sepsis team had genuine standing: its research had helped establish the bundle nationally, which made 28% compliance harder, not easier, to explain to outsiders. Nothing about the underlying evidence was contested; what had failed, twice now if 2024 counted, was turning a bundle everyone accepted into a bundle everyone used.

## The Situation

The numbers Adeyemi inherited were unambiguous (see Exhibit 1). Bundle compliance stood at 28%, exactly where the 2024 attempt had peaked before it lapsed. Median time from recognition to antibiotics ran 127 minutes against the 60-minute standard. Two further measures described the quality of change rather than its headline size: an adoption-quality score, starting at 15 out of 100, meant to capture genuine frontline belief as distinct from compliance recorded on paper; and a sustainability score, starting at 10 out of 100, meant to capture whether a gain would survive once the champion time ran out. Both began low. Adeyemi had spent none of the £180,000 budget and none of the 0.6 WTE. If the next ninety days repeated 2024's pattern rather than breaking it, the arithmetic pointed to the same 28% already seen once: short of the regulator's 75% expectation, and the kind of shortfall that could move the Trust toward the "Inadequate" end of the regulator's scale.

## What Adeyemi Controlled

Across the four rounds (see Exhibit 2), Adeyemi committed a diagnosis and a candour setting; a framing choice, a three-way split of champion time, and an artefact; a sequencing choice, a fidelity stance, and two further budget-and-protection settings; and a measurement choice, an ownership model, a board-facing answer, and a conviction setting — thirteen decisions. She was measured on three moving numbers — bundle compliance against the 75% trajectory, adoption quality against the appearance of compliance, and sustainability against the day her champion time ran out — and against two fixed ceilings, the £180,000 budget and the 0.6 WTE, that no round could raise.

## Closing

Adeyemi closed the coroner's report and opened a blank page instead. Ninety days, £180,000 and 0.6 WTE stood between the 28% she had inherited and whatever she chose to call a credible trajectory. The Medical Director wanted her first move by the end of the week.
