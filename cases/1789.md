# Fluvento Digital SGPS: The Synergy Clock

**Case Code:** STR-2026-1789 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1789 — *The Acquisition Call* · **v1.0 · September 2026**

---

On the morning of Monday, 11 May 2026, Marta Salgado read the board resolution twice before reacting. Fluvento Digital SGPS's closest rival had gone live with an artificial-intelligence demand-forecasting module three weeks earlier and had already signed two companies Fluvento's sales team had courted for a year. Three of Fluvento's top-ten customers, worth €16 million of annual recurring revenue, had told their account managers that renewal now depended on Fluvento shipping the same capability by 31 May 2027.

The resolution gave Salgado, Fluvento's Head of Corporate Development, the mandate to close that gap by whatever means she judged, attaching one hard number: whatever she chose had to generate at least €12 million of run-rate value within twenty-four months of the move, or the board would treat the capital as wasted, claw it back, and reopen the search.

## The Protagonist

Salgado, 44, had run Corporate Development at Fluvento since 2022, after eight years in the company's strategy and finance functions, and earlier years advising software companies on transactions. She reported to the chief executive and sat, without a vote, in board sessions whenever a capital decision was tabled. The largest deal she had closed to date was a €15 million tuck-in; this mandate was far larger, and came with four colleagues seconded full time for its duration — the CFO, the chief operating officer, a newly named Head of Integration, and the Head of People & Culture. All five names would sit on the recommendation. Only Salgado's would be the one the board asked for when it wanted to know who had made the call.

## The Decision

Salgado's first task was to choose, and commit to, one of three mutually exclusive ways to get the AI capability into Fluvento's product (see Exhibit 2). She could acquire NordCast AS, a 60-person, research-led AI-forecasting specialist, outright for €95 million — roughly 9.5 times the target's revenue, for a company not yet profitable — and gain its technology and its people immediately. She could instead form a strategic alliance with NordCast, the same not-yet-profitable target: co-develop the module and share the resulting revenue for a committed €25 million over two years, without ever owning the intellectual property or fully controlling its roadmap. Or she could commit €35 million to building the capability inside Fluvento's own engineering organization, keeping full ownership of everything produced, at a realistic build time of twenty to twenty-four months.

Whichever path she picked, she had to state which single consideration had actually decided it — beating the renewal clock before the three at-risk customers renewed elsewhere; owning the technology and its roadmap outright; the decisiveness of committing to the largest, most binding option available; or preserving as much of Fluvento's €70 million cash position as possible — and how much weight she placed on being able to reverse or switch paths later, and how firmly, once chosen, she intended to hold the line.

## The Company and its Setting

Fluvento Digital SGPS, headquartered in Lisbon, sold business-to-business logistics software. It carried €180 million in annual recurring revenue, a 22% EBITDA margin, and €70 million in cash, and employed 1,150 people (see Exhibit 1). The board had approved the mandate because the company had the balance sheet to act on it, and because doing nothing while the rival closed the AI gap was itself a strategy — just not one anyone wanted to own. NordCast AS, the target common to two of the three paths, was based in Oslo: 60 people, almost all researchers and engineers, roughly €10 million in annual revenue, and no profit yet to its name.

## The Situation

The clock had two settings. Technically, whichever path Salgado chose had to put a working AI capability into customers' hands before 31 May 2027, about twelve months out, or the three at-risk customers — €16 million of ARR — would renew with the rival. Financially, within twenty-four months of committing capital, the move had to be generating at least €12 million a year of measurable extra value — new revenue the AI module could pull into Fluvento's base, savings from combined operations, net of integration cost — or the board's resolution treated the spend as destroyed. Four figures would eventually tell the story: how much of that €12 million had actually been captured; what share of the acquired or partnered engineers — who held the technology in their heads — were still at their desks; how much of the €16 million at-risk revenue had actually been saved; and the deal's net financial value once every euro of cost, premium and integration spend was weighed against what it produced.

If Salgado let the deadline arrive without a chosen, executed path, the outcome needed no imagination: the rival kept the two prospects it had already taken, the three renewal-conditional customers had no reason to stay, and the twenty-four-month test would run out on a company still unable to do what its closest competitor had shipped a year earlier.

## What Salgado Controlled

Beyond the initial path, its deciding factor, and her stated reversibility and conviction, Salgado had to put her own numbers on the deal. She had to name, in euros over the following two years, how much new revenue the AI capability could cross-sell into Fluvento's base and how much could be saved by combining infrastructure and functions — then recommend whether to proceed at the asking terms, adjust them, or walk away, measured against the €12 million target. A diligence detail would land mid-process: the engineer who had personally built roughly forty percent of NordCast's model, and held no contract binding him to stay, could leave the day after any deal closed. She would restructure the terms and pricing around that specific risk, hold the price but add guarantees to keep them, or treat it as immaterial and proceed with the deal.

Once any deal was signed, Salgado would set how deeply the acquired or partnered team was folded into Fluvento — from left almost entirely alone to fully absorbed within six months — and choose a retention and cultural plan for the sixty researchers involved, from no distinct plan, leaving retention and integration to resolve on their own, to standard onboarding, folding new engineers into Fluvento's existing HR processes and reviews, to combined packages, autonomy guarantees and joint leadership of the merged team, to autonomy guarantees alone, preserving the team's independence over its roadmap and hiring. She would choose the mechanism for combining the work itself — a joint team shipping the module together, a technical interface connecting the platforms while keeping staffs separate, full migration onto Fluvento's stack, tools and reporting lines, or keeping the target entirely separate, with no shared roadmap — and how aggressively to compress the timeline, from patient and multi-year to six months.

Eighteen months after closing, Salgado would stand in front of the board again, ready for the possibility that the engineer she was relying on had resigned just as the rival moved on whatever partner or target remained exposed. Her options ran from activating retention triggers and promoting internal successors, to renegotiating the deal terms to re-secure the capability, to converting an alliance into an acquisition or hiring the remaining team directly, to holding the existing plan unchanged, confident despite the shock. She would then have three minutes to reconcile synergy delivered, talent retained, and net deal value against the original target — walking the board through the arithmetic in full, leading with the strategic case regardless of the number, or defending the original price on its own terms — and to say, without hedging, whether the chosen mode was simply the right call, full stop, whether the real driver had been the cliff's timing, not boldness, or whether, in the end, only the price paid had actually mattered. Through all of it, as the board pushed back on every figure she gave them, she would have to choose how much of that pressure to visibly absorb and how much to meet head-on.

## Closing

Salgado closed the resolution and opened three folders on her desk, one per path, price already filled in. The board wanted an answer, not a study, and 31 May 2027 was already on the calendar in red.
