# Verdão Foods: Coherence Check

**Case Code:** STR-2026-2015 · **Companion simulation:** Express 2015 — *Coherence Check* · **v1.0 · August 2026**

---

On Monday 8 June 2026 Marta Sequeira set two documents side by side on the table of her firm's meeting room off Avenida da Liberdade in Lisbon. The first was Verdão Foods' Series A business plan, forty pages, asking a venture fund for €3.0 million to scale nationally and enter three export markets within eighteen months. The second was the single sheet the fund's analysts had returned with it, carrying one line in red across the top: *Strategy lacks coherence — fix or pass.*

The fund's investment committee sat on Thursday 11 June. Sequeira had those seventy-two hours to decide what to hand back — a judgement on the plan, a replacement for it, or a map of the places where the plan's own decisions contradicted one another — then to choose which two of those decisions to repair, which single realignment to put first, and what to say in the three minutes the committee would give her.

## The Protagonist

Marta Sequeira, 41, was a principal at Tejo Strategy Partners, an eleven-person Lisbon advisory firm. She had read industrial engineering and spent eight years at a chilled ready-meals manufacturer near Azambuja, the last three as head of production planning, before moving into advisory work in 2016. She had made principal in 2022 and since then had led most of the firm's diligence-support work for venture funds in Lisbon and Porto. Two of the partners who would read Verdão's repaired plan on Thursday had commissioned that work from her before. Verdão's founders had come to Tejo directly, on a fixed fee agreed in a single phone call.

## The Decision

Sequeira had to decide how to hand the plan back, what to repair in it, what to defend, and in what order — under a fee and a deadline that allowed one pass at each.

The hand-back itself was a choice of three. She could return a verdict, telling the committee the plan was ambitious and unfundable as written. She could return a full rewrite, flagging every flaw and proposing a new strategy across all dimensions. Or she could return a coherence map: the specific decisions in the plan that contradicted each other, named link by link. Whichever form she chose, she would put two contradictions at the top of it, drawn from what the analysts and her own reading had turned up — positioning against pricing, channel against capacity, ambition against resources, the milestone chart against the deck's formatting, the brand against the logo, the executive summary's tone against its length. She could also decide how much of the plan to trace before naming anything: her firm's scorecard held twelve internal links, and she could examine as few or as many as the three days allowed.

Repair was capped. Sequeira had told the founders in the first meeting that they would fix two decisions, not the company. Four repairs were open to her, and either of the two slots could be left unspent. She could align positioning with pricing by holding the premium range and dropping the modelled price cut, keeping the current margin. She could align positioning with pricing the other way, committing to mass-market and accepting the lower margin that came with it. She could align the channel with the capacity Verdão actually had, phasing the rollout to what one production line could serve and deferring the full listing target. Or she could align ambition with resources, moving export to a funded later phase and taking its unbudgeted cost out of this plan. Each repair moved numbers elsewhere, and Sequeira could assume those numbers held, re-run the headline margin alone, or re-run the whole chain — margin to monthly spend to cash runway to the size of the ask — re-checking each neighbouring decision.

## The Company and its Setting

Verdão Foods, Lda. was founded in Lisbon in 2024 by two food-science graduates and employed eight people. It made chilled, high-protein vegan ready-meals and sold them through one premium supermarket chain and a small direct-to-consumer subscription. Trailing-twelve-month revenue was €1.2 million at a 41% gross margin. Production ran on a single chilled line at 70% utilisation; no second site appeared in the plan's budget. The company was spending €95,000 a month and held about five months of cash.

The plan asked for €3.0 million. Its growth case modelled national distribution across 1,400 retail listings and three export markets inside eighteen months. It carried no regulatory, cold-chain or distributor cost for those markets; the fund's analysts had put the omission at roughly €600,000. It also kept the premium, chef-grade, clean-label language of the existing range on every page while modelling a 28% price cut to win supermarket volume (see **Exhibit 2**).

## The Situation

Tejo scored client plans on a 0–100 coherence scale — how far a plan's decisions reinforced one another across four headings: why the company existed, what it sold and to whom, how it produced and delivered, and who would do what by when. Verdão's plan scored 38. The fund's analysts kept their own 0–100 read of investability — how the committee read the plan as it stood — and put Verdão at 32. Sequeira kept a third number for herself, which she called buy-in: how firmly the two founders would stand behind a plan after it had been changed, on the same 0–100 scale. She put it at 55. Both founders had built the company on the export idea and talked about it in every meeting.

The vitals under those three scores were the ones the committee would recompute for itself: a 41% gross margin, five months of runway, and a repair allowance of two (see **Exhibit 1**). The margin was the number the chain moved through. At the price cut the plan modelled, the 41% margin fell toward 26%; monthly spending did not change with it, so the cash position and the size of the ask both moved. The fourth number the committee would look at was the funding need itself — the €3.0 million ask set against what the repaired unit economics would justify.

If the plan reached Thursday's committee as filed, it would be read as filed. Verdão had five months of cash at €95,000 a month, and a second raise assembled from a passed one would not close inside that window. Lisbon's venture network was small enough that the fund's decision would be known to the others within weeks.

## What Sequeira Controlled

Sequeira controlled the hand-back, the repairs, the sequence and the pitch, in that order and once each.

She would set the form of the hand-back, the two contradictions named at the top of it, the number of links traced, the two repairs and the treatment of the numbers downstream. She would then choose the single realignment to sequence first and defend hardest — the positioning–pricing repair, the channel–capacity phasing, or tidying the milestone chart and deck formatting. She would decide how to handle the founders' attachment to export: telling them it was off the table, leaving it unraised, or reframing it as deferred to a funded milestone. She would decide what to put in front of the lead investor, who had said margin durability was his question — the growth story, the €3.0 million ask and the eighteen-month timeline as written, or the repaired unit economics. And she would state, as a percentage, how firmly she held that one high-leverage fix beat five cosmetic ones.

Thursday itself was three decisions. What to open the three minutes with: the vision, an admission that the plan had been a mess, or the account of how the plan now hung together. How to answer the committee's probe on whether a plan that kept premium positioning still forecast mass-market shelf velocity: deflect it as a detail, concede the forecast, or resolve it. And what one-pager to leave behind: a glossy summary, a KPI sheet tracing each link, or a page of caveats.

She would be measured on four things, tracked round by round beside the move that produced them: the plan's coherence score, the committee's investability read, founder buy-in, and the funding need against the repaired economics.

## Closing

At 7:20 on Monday evening Sequeira wrote the plan's decisions onto the whiteboard in single words — premium, price, listings, line, export, ask — and drew lines between the ones that touched. Beside the board she wrote two empty boxes and numbered them. The committee sat on Thursday morning, and the founders were coming in at nine on Tuesday to hear what she had found.
