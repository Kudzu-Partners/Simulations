# Andina Forensic Services: The Phantom Supplier

**Case Code:** RSK-2026-1409 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1409 — *Forensic Audit — Fraud Detection, Digital Evidence and Expert Testimony* · **v1.0 · October 2026**

---

On the morning of Tuesday, April 7, 2026, Ana Patricia Solano-Vega opened an engagement letter that named her firm as independent forensic expert in a matter she had read about in a two-page internal memo the week before. Over 14 months, a Bogotá family-welfare fund called CABOCOM had paid COP 2,400 millones to a supplier that had not existed before January 2024, across 73 purchase orders that each sat just below the COP 80 million line above which a second signature was required.

The prosecutor for economic crimes in Bogotá expected Solano-Vega's expert report, a *dictamen pericial*, by May 30, 2026. Between now and an oral trial later in the year she had to decide how her team would find the pattern, preserve the evidence, interview the buyer who had approved the orders, and then defend every one of those choices from the witness stand.

## The Protagonist

Solano-Vega, 45, was a founding partner of Andina Forensic Services, a Bogotá boutique registered on the courts' official list of expert witnesses. A public accountant with a forensic specialisation and a Certified Fraud Examiner credential, she had spent 17 years in investigations, the last six at Andina. Two people worked the file with her: Andrés Calderón-Téllez, a senior accountant, and Carolina Bernal-Riveros, a digital-forensics analyst. Andina charged a fixed fee that did not depend on the result.

## The Decision

The engagement ran in four stages, each closing before the next opened.

**Detect.** Solano-Vega had to choose which of seven screens to run over CABOCOM's accounting data: a first-digit frequency test of payments above COP 5 millones against the distribution organic accounting data tended to follow; a concentration analysis of spend by supplier and month; a test of how closely orders clustered under the COP 80 million approval limit; a timing test for invoices dated on days 28 to 30 of the month, outside the treasury's fortnightly cycle; a cross-check of company registries against the conflict-of-interest declaration each employee signed; a gap analysis of evidence that the services had actually been delivered; and a scan for invoices in round amounts. She also had to set a working hypothesis and put a first figure on the loss.

The hypothesis had three advocates. Calderón-Téllez argued that the brother-in-law link and a false declaration already justified treating the matter as fraud and escalating at once. CABOCOM's administrative sub-director argued that it was a control deficiency and nothing more: an approval workflow untouched since 2022, a buyer with 11 clean years, and no proof yet that any service had gone undelivered. The internal audit director, Mauricio Pinilla-Granados, wanted both readings carried in parallel until the evidence separated them.

**Preserve.** Solano-Vega had to decide which steps to take to secure the digital evidence. The options were to image the buyer's workstation through a hardware write-blocker; to hash the image before and after with two algorithms; to have every handler sign CABOCOM's chain-of-custody form; to film each step with a timestamp; to place a legal hold on his corporate mailbox; to extract the approval and posting trail from the company's enterprise system; to request bank statements through the prosecutor under Article 244 of the criminal procedure code; to collect building-access and network-proxy logs; to preserve the desktop copy of his business messaging account; and to power on the workstation under administrator credentials and inspect it live. CABOCOM's head of IT favoured the live inspection, since an encrypted volume could lock and the team would see open files immediately. Bernal-Riveros favoured imaging first, so that nothing on the disk changed before it was copied.

**Interview.** The buyer, Wilson Camacho-Rojas, had agreed to be interviewed voluntarily. Solano-Vega had to choose which of six safeguards to adopt: a signed informed-consent form; the presence of his defence lawyer, Roberto Mejía-Caro; audio recording with a verbatim transcript; an independent observer; a rule against leading questions; and a rule against time pressure and implied threats. She also had to choose a method: a cognitive interview that used staged memory-retrieval techniques to draw out his recollection; a structured, non-accusatory interview that took his account and then tested it against the evidence; or an accusatory confrontation that put the allegation to him directly and pressed against his denials. Finally she had to set the goal: verifiable admissions of fact, such as signatures, dates and prior acquaintance, or a direct confession.

**Testify.** Mejía-Caro, 58, with 28 years in economic-crime litigation, was known to attack an expert on three lines: custody, method and impartiality. For each, Solano-Vega had three ways to answer. Asked who had access to the evidence between seizure and analysis, she could give a general assurance that the evidence had been held securely throughout, produce the custody log recording each handler and transfer, or object that the question fell outside the scope of her report. Asked whether the first-digit test was a screen rather than proof, she could argue that the deviation was sufficient evidence of fraud in itself, set the question aside and turn to her other findings, or agree that it was a screen and trace how it led to the cross-match and the preserved evidence. Asked about Andina's fees and its prior work for Rivas Mancilla Auditores, the statutory auditor's firm, she could disclose the fixed fee and every prior engagement, describe the prior engagements in general terms, or decline citing professional confidentiality. She would also choose how to frame her conclusion: in juridical terms, stating that Camacho-Rojas had committed embezzlement of public-purpose funds, or in technical-accounting terms, stating the economic facts and the size of the loss. The prosecutor's office had told her a plain conclusion was easier for a court to follow; Pinilla-Granados held that the legal classification belonged to the judge.

## The Company and its Setting

CABOCOM, the Caja Bogotana de Compensación Familiar, was a supervised non-profit founded in 1972. It collected a 4% payroll contribution from about 12,000 employers and served some 480,000 beneficiaries. Because its money was public-purpose money, a loss could lead to a criminal case, a supervisory sanction and questions in Congress.

## The Situation

The supplier, Suministros Andinos del Norte S.A.S., was registered in January 2024 with a single shareholder, Hernán Restrepo-Pinilla (see **Exhibit 1**). Pinilla-Granados's team had found that he was Camacho-Rojas's brother-in-law, although Camacho-Rojas had declared in March 2024 that he had no links to any supplier. The invoice descriptions repeated almost word for word, and the site they named already had a maintenance contract with another firm.

The data covered about 840 suppliers, 31,400 payables entries and 5,200 purchase orders. Camacho-Rojas, 47, had been purchasing coordinator for 11 years and had strong reviews. His wife had been diagnosed with stage III breast cancer in 2023, and the family had paid about COP 180 millones for treatment not covered by insurance.

If nothing changed, the outcome was clear. Without an admissible report, the prosecutor could not sustain charges, the COP 2,400 millones would be hard to recover, and the statutory auditor who had signed CABOCOM's 2024 and 2025 accounts would face her own disciplinary exposure.

## What Solano-Vega Controlled

At each stage Solano-Vega committed her choices before seeing results (see **Exhibit 2**). She was measured on five scores, each out of 100 and each starting at zero: detection quality, or how well her screening isolated the pattern; chain of custody, or how well the evidence would hold up as the same, authentic and unaltered material; interview yield, or how much usable and admissible fact the interview produced; cross-examination resilience, or how well her answers held under Mejía-Caro's questions; and the defensibility of the report as a whole, which rested on all four. Alongside them sat her own estimate of the loss in COP millones and, round by round, an overall score.

## Closing

By early afternoon the supplier file, the ledger and the order extract were on Solano-Vega's screens. Bernal-Riveros was asking whether she could go to CABOCOM that evening to collect the workstation. Calderón-Téllez wanted to know which screens to run first. The report was due in seven and a half weeks.
