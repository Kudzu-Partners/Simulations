## Abstract

The single-queue multi-server service system was, in the case universe, a quantitative abstraction of a LATAM retail-banking branch network that processed, across its 128 metropolitan-and-regional branches, roughly 840,000 customer visits per month through three customer types — Type A (fast, 60% of volume, average service time 4 minutes), Type B (standard, 28% of volume, 9 minutes), and Type C (complex, 12% of volume, 22 minutes) — against an average branch utilization of 89% and a network-median P90 wait of 18.4 minutes that had, across 2025, drawn an NPS compression of 11 points and a complaint volume above the Superintendencia Financiera soft threshold for two consecutive quarters. On Monday, March 2, 2026, the Head of Service Design, a 42-year-old former operations-research consultant who had joined the bank in June 2024, had opened the queue-lab commitment that would, through four controlled experiments, isolate the separate contributions of utilization, variability and pooling to the P90 wait, and would recommend a branch-design envelope that compressed the network-median P90 below 10 minutes at an unchanged headcount and an unchanged physical footprint. The case opens at 6:34 a.m. on Monday, March 9, 2026, at the bank's operations-analytics floor in the capital, as the Head of Service Design prepares the four-experiment protocol that will anchor the April 1 branch-design commitment to the Chief Operating Officer.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Experiment-1 baseline (single server, arrival rate 10/min, service rate 11/min, utilization 91%, P90 wait 18.4 minutes) and diagnose when the P90 wait is dominated by utilization rather than by mean service time.
2. Analyze the Experiment-2 pooling commitment (two servers at half the rate each; same total capacity) and recommend the pooling design consistent with Kingman's-formula decomposition of the P90 wait.
3. Diagnose the Experiment-3 arrival-variability effect (Cs² shift from low to high at held utilization) and quantify the quadratic variability term in Kingman's formula.
4. Design the Experiment-4 customer-mix pooling (three customer types into a single queue with pooled servers against three specialised queues) and recommend the pooling design that compresses the P90 wait at high utilization.
5. Recommend a branch-design envelope and write the COO-facing paragraph that anchors the four-experiment trajectory against the factory-physics principle that servers do not queue — variability does.

## The Protagonist

## The Decision

The Head of Service Design had to commit, by the April 1 branch-design commitment, the four-experiment queue-lab protocol and the corresponding design recommendation:

1. **Experiment 1 — Baseline** — whether to commit a 1,000-minute simulated run with a single server at an arrival rate of 10 customers per minute and a service rate of 11 customers per minute (utilization ρ = 0.91), low arrival and service variability (Cs² = 0.1 each), to establish the baseline P90 wait of 18.4 minutes under Kingman's formula W ≈ (ρ / (1 − ρ)) × ((Ca² + Cs²) / 2) × τ.
2. **Experiment 2 — Pooling** — whether to commit a second 1,000-minute run with two servers at 5.5 customers per minute each (same total capacity), utilization held at 0.91, low variability held, to isolate the pooling principle — the P90 wait halves from 18 to 9 minutes without a change in total service capacity.
3. **Experiment 3 — Variability Shift** — whether to commit a third 1,000-minute run with arrival variability shifted from Cs² = 0.1 to Cs² = 1.0, utilization held at 0.91, two servers held, to quantify the quadratic variability term in Kingman's formula — the P90 wait shoots from 9 to 22 minutes.
4. **Experiment 4 — Customer-Mix Pooling** — whether to commit a fourth 1,000-minute run with three customer types (Type A 40%, Type B 40%, Type C 20%), three specialised servers in three queues against three pooled servers in a single queue, utilization held at 0.91, to recommend the customer-mix pooling commitment that compresses the P90 wait from 18 minutes (three specialised queues) to 11 minutes (single pooled queue).

The four experiments would compound into a single branch-design recommendation. On the Head of Service Design's working model, a single-pooled-queue-multi-server-arrival-variability-reducing envelope delivered a network-median P90 wait of 8.4 minutes at unchanged headcount, unchanged physical footprint, and unchanged total service capacity — an envelope that no pair of the three levers acting alone had cleared.

## Process Background

The 128-branch network had been assembled across the 2019–2023 window through three acquisitions of regional retail-banking franchises and a subsequent consolidation of the back-office. Each branch operated a customer-facing service counter with between two and eight tellers, a separate queue for complex services (loans, disputes, investment-product inquiries), and a separate queue for fast services (withdrawals, deposits, balance inquiries). Across 2025, the network had processed 840,000 customer visits per month at an average branch utilization of 89% and a network-median P90 wait of 18.4 minutes.

The Q4 2025 branch-operations dossier had identified three specific observations the queue-lab envelope would investigate. First, the Q3 2025 staff-utilization analysis had, across a 28-branch observation window, documented a teller-utilization range of 82%–94% against a branch-utilization range of 82%–94% — a pattern that ruled out teller underutilization as the P90-wait driver. Second, the Q3 2025 service-time analysis had, across a 12,400-transaction sample, documented service-time distributions inside the designed envelope — a pattern that ruled out the mean-service-time hypothesis. Third, the Q3 2025 arrival-pattern analysis had, across the same 28-branch window, documented a coefficient of variation of arrivals at 1.1, against a designed 0.3 — the single variable that had not been audited before the queue-lab commitment.

## Industry and Market Context

The queuing-theory and service-design literature on single-queue multi-server systems in 2026 was anchored by three structural findings the queue-lab envelope sat inside. First, the 1960–2024 queuing-theory evidence base had documented that Kingman's formula — W ≈ (ρ / (1 − ρ)) × ((Ca² + Cs²) / 2) × τ — decomposed the expected wait into three terms (utilization, variability, service time) with utilization entering hyperbolically, variability entering linearly, and service time entering multiplicatively, a decomposition that made the service-time term the smallest lever at high utilization. Second, the 1990–2024 pooling-principle evidence base had, across a large sample of retail-banking, airport-service and healthcare studies, documented P90-wait compressions of 35%–50% under single-pooled-queue designs against specialised-queue designs at unchanged total capacity. Third, the 2005–2024 arrival-variability evidence base had, across a commercial summary of retail-banking branch redesigns, documented P90-wait compressions of 20%–35% under appointment-scheduling designs that had compressed the coefficient of variation of arrivals from 1.1 to 0.5.

Empirically, the post-1990 service-design literature had two findings the queue-lab had to respect. First, service systems at utilization above 85% had a P90 wait dominated by the variability term, not by the mean service time — a finding that made staffing solves queuing a false equivalence at the relevant utilization envelope. Second, pooled-queue designs at high utilization preserved the fairness-and-first-come-first-served customer experience against specialised-queue designs, a finding that made pooling the dominant envelope on the customer-experience dimension as well as the P90-wait dimension.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Chief Operating Officer had, in the December 2025 operating review, approved an April 1 branch-design-commitment deadline, a network-median P90-wait ceiling of 10 minutes, an unchanged headcount envelope, and an unchanged physical-footprint envelope. The Head of Retail Banking had, on February 10, argued for a headcount-increase envelope of 8%. The Head of Digital had, on February 18, argued for an appointment-scheduling commitment that would compress arrival variability. The Lean Program Director had, on February 25, argued for a pooling commitment that would eliminate the specialised-queue split.

### What the Head of Service Design knew

The team had modelled three branch-design envelopes. A Pooled-Plus-Appointment-Scheduling Envelope projected a network-median P90 wait of 8.4 minutes at unchanged headcount and unchanged physical footprint. A Headcount-Increase-Only Envelope projected a network-median P90 of 13.2 minutes at an 8% headcount increase. A Specialised-Queue-Redesign Envelope projected a network-median P90 of 14.6 minutes — above the COO ceiling.

### What she did not know

Whether the appointment-scheduling commitment would clear the customer-adoption barrier at the Q2 2026 launch; whether the pooling commitment would clear the teller-cross-training window inside the April 1 deadline; whether the Q3 2026 Superintendencia Financiera branch-network audit would land at the modelled soft threshold or a harder threshold; and whether the Chief Operating Officer would accept a four-experiment lab protocol or push for a single experiment.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Retail Banking**: *"Add tellers. The utilization is too high."*

**The Head of Digital**: *"Compress arrival variability. The appointment-scheduling platform is ready."*

**The Lean Program Director**: *"Pool the queues. Specialised queues at high utilization lose to pooled queues on P90 — always."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

The Head of Service Design had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Pooled-Plus-Appointment-Scheduling:** Single pooled queue per branch, cross-trained tellers, arrival variability compressed from Cs² = 1.1 to Cs² = 0.5 through an appointment-scheduling platform, utilization held at 0.89, no headcount change. Network-median P90 wait 8.4 minutes. Recommended.

- **Option B — Headcount-Increase-Only:** 8% headcount increase, specialised queues held, arrival variability held. Network-median P90 wait 13.2 minutes. Above COO ceiling. Rejected.

- **Option C — Specialised-Queue-Redesign:** Three specialised queues per branch, cross-trained tellers inside each specialisation, arrival variability held. Network-median P90 wait 14.6 minutes. Above COO ceiling. Rejected.

The Head of Service Design's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single lever most correlated with the network-median P90-wait target was the pooling commitment — the single-pooled-queue design that, on its own, halved the P90 wait under Kingman's decomposition against the specialised-queue design, before any variability-reduction contribution from appointment scheduling.
