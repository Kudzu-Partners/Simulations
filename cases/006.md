## Abstract

Hotel Pacifico, a 240-room upscale resort on the Pacific coast, entered 2026 with an ADR of USD 132, an occupancy of 71%, and a RevPAR of USD 94. Two competitors defined the market: Metropolis, an urban business property, priced at USD 145; and Conecta, a direct-competitor resort five kilometres down the coast, which had just announced a flash sale at USD 99 for the February low season. The Revenue Management Committee, led by Carolina Pardo, faced the first of five seasonal pricing decisions: set the leisure rate, the business rate, the inventory split, the channel mix, and the overbooking policy against an 8% no-show rate. The case ends with Pardo in the hotel's revenue-management room on the evening before the February rate goes live, weighing three responses to the Conecta move — match, hold, or raise — and modelling the five-season propagation of each.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate a competitor price move against reference-rate anchoring and the dynamics of customer price memory.
2. Analyze the interaction between leisure and business demand streams and the role of inventory fencing in yield capture.
3. Design a multi-season pricing response that trades short-run occupancy for long-run ADR.
4. Diagnose the difference between an elastic and an inelastic competitor action and propose a response to each.
5. Defend a pricing recommendation under challenge from operations (occupancy) and ownership (cash).

On the evening of **January 27, 2026**, **Carolina Pardo** stood at the window of the revenue-management office on the mezzanine of Hotel Pacifico and watched the lights of Conecta, five kilometres down the coast. Two hours earlier, Conecta had pushed a flash-sale notification across the regional OTAs: *"February escape — USD 99 per night — limited rooms."* Pardo's own February rate — USD 132 leisure, USD 178 business — was scheduled to go live at 6:00 a.m. the next morning. Her owner, Familia Vergara, had asked a single question in a two-line email at 4:12 p.m.: *"Do we match? Yes or no, by 9 tonight."*

---

## The Protagonist

Carolina Pardo, 38, had joined Hotel Pacifico in 2022 as Director of Revenue Management, after seven years at a regional hotel chain's revenue-management center. Before that, she had worked four years at a global OTA in distribution strategy. At Pacifico she had built a small RM team (three analysts, one distribution manager) and a weekly revenue-management committee — general manager, director of operations, director of sales, and herself — that met every Tuesday. The owner, Familia Vergara, held 78% through a family investment vehicle; the remaining 22% was held by a regional hospitality PE fund that had bought in at a 12x EBITDA multiple in 2020. Pardo's compensation carried a half-year bonus against RevPAR and a second bonus against a profit-conversion ratio.

---

## The Decision

Pardo had to finalize, by 9:00 p.m. on January 27, the Season 1 (February low-season) rate and inventory package. The package had five dimensions:

1. **Leisure rate** (currently USD 132).
2. **Business rate** (currently USD 178).
3. **Inventory split** between leisure and business (currently 70/30 against a 55/45 demand mix in February).
4. **Channel mix** across direct, OTA, wholesale, and group.
5. **Overbooking policy** against an 8% no-show rate.

A single email would lock the February rate into 12 OTA channels, a property-management system, and the hotel's direct channel. Pardo had two further decisions to make — the business rate and the inventory split — but the leisure-rate decision was the one the owner had asked about by 9:00 p.m.

---

## Company Background

Hotel Pacifico was a 240-room upscale resort, built in 1998 and substantially renovated in 2018 under the Vergara family's ownership. It sat on 18 hectares of beachfront, with three restaurants, a spa, two pools, and a 450-person conference facility opened in 2021. 2025 revenue was USD 22.8M; EBITDA was USD 6.1M (26.8% margin). Average length of stay was 3.1 nights in leisure and 1.8 nights in business. Room mix was 60% leisure, 30% business, 10% group. Loyalty-program guests accounted for 14% of occupied room-nights and booked, on average, 34% closer to arrival than non-loyalty guests — a pattern the RM team had tracked since 2023.

The hotel's P&L was sensitive to two ratios. First, RevPAR — revenue per available room — set the top-line. Second, profit conversion from RevPAR to EBITDA per room ran at roughly 38% on incremental room-nights after a fixed threshold, with a lower conversion on OTA-sourced rooms because of commission. The conference facility, still ramping, had added roughly USD 2.2M of group revenue in 2025 and had a committed pipeline of USD 3.8M for 2026 — almost all on business rates already negotiated.

## Industry and Market Context

The Pacific coast resort market had grown at 6.4% annually from 2021 to 2025. Demand came in two primary streams. Leisure demand was price-elastic (coefficient roughly −1.6 in the USD 95–160 band), seasonally peaked (July and December highs, February low, April and September shoulders), and increasingly OTA-dominated (54% of room-nights for the peer set in 2025). Business demand was price-inelastic (coefficient roughly −0.4 above an 80% occupancy threshold, steeper below), concentrated Sunday-through-Thursday, and increasingly direct and corporate-contracted.

The market had three relevant positioning anchors. Metropolis, an urban five-star property 35 minutes from the airport, was the business reference at USD 145 ADR. Conecta, a 190-room resort five kilometres from Pacifico, was the closest leisure comparable at a 2025 ADR of USD 118. A new entrant, a 320-room branded resort under construction 12 kilometres down the coast, was scheduled to open in Q4 2026.

Research on reference-rate anchoring in hospitality converged on two findings. First, a rate below the customer's prior observed rate at a given property or peer shifted the reference downward on a multi-quarter decay; a rate above did not shift it upward symmetrically. Second, flash sales in the bottom third of a competitor's peer band shifted peer-set customer reference rates by roughly 4–7% over the two quarters following the sale.

## The Situation

### How we got here

Conecta's flash sale was not unexpected. Its 2024 revenues had fallen 8% year-over-year; its new owner, a regional hospitality fund that had bought Conecta at distress in late 2024, had signaled in a November press statement that it would "recalibrate positioning." The USD 99 rate was, in Pardo's analysis, below Conecta's contribution-margin breakeven — a market signal of distress, not of a new pricing strategy.

### What Pardo knew

Pacifico's 2025 rate had cleared above Conecta's for eight of twelve months; where the two had crossed, occupancy flows had tilted toward Conecta but ADR at Pacifico had held. Loyalty-program guests were disproportionately concentrated at rate points above USD 120. A one-shot rate-memory decay model — validated against 2022 and 2023 competitive moves — suggested that a match to USD 99 in February would pull Pacifico's leisure reference rate downward by approximately 7% across all five 2026 seasons, not only February.

### What Pardo did not know

Whether Conecta's USD 99 rate would hold beyond February, or retract after the flash-sale window. Whether Metropolis would respond. Whether the owner — who had asked only "match or not" — would accept the five-season trajectory implications of a match.

### What others were saying

**General Manager Esteban Villa**, her direct superior, pressed for a match. "Occupancy first. We cannot let Conecta take the load. Match at USD 109, fence the business inventory, use the conference base."

**Director of Operations Ana Silvia Torres** pressed for a hold. "If we drop eight-day rates, we need four points of occupancy to break even. We will not get them. And the conference block locks in our business revenue regardless."

**Antonio Vergara**, family owner, called at 5:30 p.m. He said only: "Don't match to chase distress."

---

## Options Under Consideration

- **Option A — Match-and-Defend (the Villa plan):** Leisure rate USD 109, business rate USD 178, inventory split 65/35, channel mix 50/50 direct/OTA, overbooking 8%. Expected February occupancy 91%; ADR drops 17%; RevPAR roughly USD 99. Reference-rate decay of approximately 7% across Seasons 2–5.

- **Option B — Hold-the-Line (the Torres plan):** Leisure rate USD 128 (a USD 4 cut, presented as a minor recalibration), business rate USD 178, inventory split 55/45 (more business), channel mix 60/40 direct/OTA, overbooking 8%. Expected February occupancy 85%; ADR drops 3%; RevPAR roughly USD 109. Reference rate held.

- **Option C — Raise-and-Narrow (the "contrarian" plan):** Leisure rate USD 138, business rate USD 188, inventory split 50/50, channel mix 65/35 direct/OTA, overbooking 7%. Expected February occupancy 78%; ADR rises 5%; RevPAR roughly USD 108. Reference rate reinforced. Loyalty capture increased; Conecta-directed flow absorbed by distress rather than by Pacifico.
