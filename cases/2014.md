# Ribalume Executive Learning: The Capacity Ceiling

**Case Code:** STR-2026-2014 · **Companion simulation:** Express 2014 — *Portfolio Strategist* · **v1.0 · August 2026**

---

On Monday 14 September 2026, in her second week as Executive Director of Ribalume Executive Learning, Mariana Quintela received two documents an hour apart. The first, from the rector's office, confirmed the mandate the board had approved the previous Friday: the unit's annual contribution was to rise from €2.07 million to €2.57 million within four quarters, and the school's hiring freeze applied to the teaching pool without exception. The second was a letter from the procurement department of Banco Aurora, whose corporate academy Ribalume had run since 2018, stating that the contract would go to competitive re-tender and that the bank expected a 12% reduction at renewal.

The board would review the unit at the end of the fourth quarter. Until then Quintela had to find €0.50 million of additional contribution from a faculty pool fixed at 5,600 teaching-days a year, of which 96% was already committed. The rector's note set out the alternative: a unit that missed the number would be absorbed into the school's central marketing department, losing its autonomy and its right to reinvest what it earned.

## The Protagonist

Mariana Quintela, 44, had joined Ribalume in 2019 to run its in-company line, after nine years selling leadership programmes for a Lisbon consultancy. Most of that career had been spent opposite corporate procurement departments; she had closed or renewed the unit's twenty largest in-company contracts herself. She had never run the open-programme line. Her predecessor, who had built the Banco Aurora academy from a single cohort in 2018, had left in July for a school in Madrid. Quintela's variable pay and the budget the unit was permitted to reinvest both moved with realised contribution.

## The Decision

Quintela had to decide, quarter by quarter, how Ribalume's 5,600 teaching-days would be spent, and to keep deciding as the portfolio and the anchor relationship moved underneath her.

The unit sold through three families (see **Exhibit 2**): **open programmes**, sold seat by seat, carrying the highest margins and the marketing budget and filling unpredictably; **in-company programmes**, commissioned by one client at a time and priced down at every renewal by procurement; and **customised academies**, multi-year programmes designed around a single client that renewed reliably and absorbed teaching-days at a rate no other family matched.

She could not add capacity. Every day given to one programme was a day taken from another.

## The Company and its Setting

Ribalume Executive Learning was founded in 2009 as the executive-education unit of a mid-sized European business school in Porto, Portugal. In FY2025 it delivered 62 programmes, generated €9.4 million of revenue, and returned €2.07 million of contribution after central overhead — a margin of 22%. Its teaching was carried by senior academics and practitioner-instructors amounting to 5,600 teaching-days a year, a figure the hiring freeze fixed for the duration of the mandate.

The Banco Aurora academy was the single largest line in the portfolio: €2.6 million of revenue, 28% of the unit's total, consuming roughly 1,900 teaching-days and returning about €1,370 of contribution for each day it used. The unit's strongest open certificate, Board Effectiveness, returned about €2,100 per teaching-day.

## The Situation

Entering the mandate, contribution stood at €2.07 million against the board's €2.57 million, a gap of €0.50 million (see **Exhibit 1**). Utilisation stood at 96%, or roughly 5,380 of the 5,600 available days. Two senior instructors had told Quintela's predecessor in the spring that they intended to reduce their teaching days for FY2027; neither had said by how much. Programmes cancelled after enrolment opened carried refunds to participants and corporate sponsors.

Banco Aurora's 12% ask, applied to the academy as it stood, was worth approximately €320,000 of contribution — around two-thirds of the gap the board had asked her to close. The letter set the re-tender submission for the third quarter and named two competing schools as invited bidders.

Two open programmes had underfilled in FY2025. The Advanced Management Programme filled 71% of its seats; the Strategy in Practice certificate filled 64%. Client records showed that 40% of the customised-academy clients on the books had first encountered Ribalume through the Advanced Management Programme.

Four candidate growth sectors had come out of the previous year's market review and were still on the table: healthcare leadership, sustainability and ESG, artificial intelligence for managers, and family-business governance. The unit's launch planning standard put a credible new franchise at 300 to 450 teaching-days in its first year — enough cohorts, and enough marketing behind them, to be visible at all.

The review process was fixed. At the end of the fourth quarter the finance committee would name one disruption — the anchor client wavering, a competing school launching a cut-price programme in artificial intelligence for managers, or a senior instructor withdrawing 200 teaching-days — and ask the Executive Director to show that the plan still cleared €2.57 million inside the ceiling. She would then have three minutes with the rector.

If nothing in the portfolio changed, the arithmetic was already written. Contribution would hold near €2.07 million and finish €0.50 million short; the re-tender would remove roughly €320,000 more if signed as asked; utilisation would sit at 96% with no headroom for a launch or a withdrawal; and the review would take place with the mandate unmet.

## What Quintela Controlled

In the first quarter she settled the diagnosis: which metric she would rank the portfolio by — absolute revenue, total contribution margin, or contribution per teaching-day; which line she would name as the margin-dilutive consumer of capacity to challenge first; how she would record the 28% concentration; and what share of the quarter, from none of it to all of it, she would spend rebuilding the programme-level accounts from raw data rather than accepting them as reported.

In the second quarter she set the shape of FY2027: how to shift the revenue mix across the three families; which of three price positions to take on open programmes — cut price to fill seats, price against the way demand had responded to price, or price up on the unit's name; whether to close both underfilled flagships, leave both, or redesign and reprice one while keeping the other; and how many teaching-days, between 3,000 and 6,500, to book against the pool.

In the third quarter she committed the growth bet and answered the bank: whether to back one of the four sectors or all four; how many teaching-days, up to 1,400, to put behind that choice; whether to concede the 12%, hold the price and let the re-tender run, or restructure the academy into modular and partly digital delivery in exchange for terms of her own; and where those days would come from — booked on top of the pool, taken from the Aurora academy while it was still running, or reallocated from the lowest-yielding work in the calendar.

In the fourth quarter she took the plan to the board: the stance she would hold when the disruption was named — present the plan unchanged, absorb the disruption with whatever slack and spread of business she had kept, or cut the new sector back and re-price the academy to close the hole; what she would lead the three minutes with; what she would say when the rector pressed her on the trade-off; and a figure between 0 and 100 recording how firmly she held that contribution per teaching-day, rather than revenue, should govern the portfolio.

Each quarter's plan was committed before its results were known. She would be measured on four numbers: contribution in euro millions against the €2.57 million target, and the gap remaining; utilisation of the pool as a percentage of the ceiling, where anything above 100% meant days the faculty had not agreed to teach; the status of the Aurora relationship, recorded as at risk, defended, conceded at −12%, or lost; and a forward market-relevance index from 0 to 100, which stood at 50. A quarter-by-quarter record kept the move, the contribution, the utilisation, the relevance index and the anchor status side by side.

## Closing

On the Tuesday morning Quintela wrote the three family names down the left of a sheet of paper and the figure 5,600 at the top of it. The Aurora submission was due in the third quarter. The board review was four quarters out. Ninety-six per cent of the pool was already spoken for, and two of the people who taught it had said they wanted to teach less.
