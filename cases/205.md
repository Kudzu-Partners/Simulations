## Abstract

Ferrovia Industrial Group was, in the case universe, an Iberian railway-and-transport-infrastructure contractor headquartered in Madrid with EUR 320M in 2025 revenue, 1,820 employees across Spain and Portugal, three operating divisions (high-speed-rail civil works, signalling systems and rolling-stock maintenance services), a 2025 monthly EBITDA burn of EUR 5M against a 2023 monthly EBITDA margin of EUR 3M positive, a closing-cash balance of EUR 15M at April 15, 2026, EUR 180M in syndicated senior-secured debt (EUR 120M term loan, EUR 40M RCF, EUR 20M working-capital facility), an Altman Z-score of 1.10 against a distressed threshold of 1.81, an Employee Morale reading of 40% against a 2023 baseline of 68%, and a VALE Lender Confidence reading of 65%. The April 17, 2026 board session had appointed a Chief Restructuring Officer on a Sunday-emergency mandate with a four-round ninety-day charter — the TurnAround programme — against three overlapping pressures: a Q1 2026 covenant breach on the Debt-to-EBITDA and Interest-Coverage covenants, a projected cash-exhaustion date of July 16, 2026 at the current EUR 5M monthly burn, and a Q2 2026 EUR 40M senior-lender-acceleration risk on the term-loan facility following the covenant breach notice. The case opens at 5:42 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Ferrovia Industrial Madrid head office as **Beatriz Dorrego-Letamendi**, a 51-year-old Chief Restructuring Officer, prepares the Round-1 cash-track commitment. Ninety days separated her from the July 21, 2026 cash-exhaustion-and-standstill-close review.

Narrative second. EBITDA last."* Ninety days separated her from the July 21, 2026 cash-exhaustion-and-standstill-close review.

## The Protagonist

Dorrego-Letamendi, 51, had assumed the Chief-Restructuring-Officer role at Ferrovia Industrial on April 19, 2026, on a Sunday-emergency appointment, after twenty-two years across distressed-operator turnaround, thirteen-week-cashflow discipline and creditor-negotiation leadership at a Barcelona-based infrastructure-services group and an Amsterdam-based restructuring-advisory firm. She had, in 2021–2023, led a EUR 140M-debt Iberian construction-services turnaround that had moved Altman Z from 0.88 to 1.74 across sixteen months, had closed a EUR 32M non-core carve-out inside seventy-four days, and had sustained senior-lender confidence above 74% across three acceleration-event scenarios. The board chairman had recruited her in April 2026 specifically on two capabilities: her documented discipline of defaulting to thirteen-week-cash-forecast visibility over monthly-management-accounting postures that had priced two peer operators into unannounced cash-cliff events, and her willingness to default to negotiated-standstill-with-equity-kicker postures over litigate-the-acceleration postures that had consumed excessive capital and attention in adjacent Iberian restructurings. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 40% to the ninety-day Cash-and-Runway trajectory, 30% to the Altman-Z-recovery target and 20% to the VALE-Lender-Confidence floor.

## The Decision

Dorrego-Letamendi had to commit, by the 8:00 a.m. emergency finance-committee call, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–4.

1. **Round 1 — Cash track.** Three archetypes: thirteen-week-cash-forecast with discretionary-capex freeze (Composite 78, Runway +14 days in week one), payables-stretch-only (Composite 42, supplier-friction risk), or full-payables-plus-capex (Composite 64, moderate). Working envelope: forecast-and-capex-freeze.
2. **Round 2 — Creditors track after EUR 40M senior-lender acceleration.** Three vectors: negotiate-standstill with 5% equity kicker (Composite 78, VALE Confidence 74%, Runway to 100 days), litigate-the-acceleration (Composite 28, cash-consumed legal-cost risk), or accept-and-pay (Composite 16, immediate cash-cliff). Working envelope: negotiate-standstill.
3. **Round 3 — Operations track (non-core carve-out).** Three vectors: Iberian-Signalling-Systems carve-out at EUR 22M (Composite 78, Altman Z 1.10 → 1.68, Cash +EUR 22M), full-group hold and cost-out-only (Composite 34, Altman Z drift), or full-asset-fire-sale (Composite 46, reputational-and-going-concern risk). Working envelope: Signalling-carve-out.
4. **Round 4 — Recovery commitment.** Two vectors: cash-first-narrative-second-EBITDA-last archetype (Composite 76, ninety-day plan deliverable at July 21) or push-EBITDA-first (Composite 28, Cash collapse risk). Working envelope: cash-first archetype.

On Dorrego-Letamendi's working model, a Forecast-and-Freeze / Standstill / Signalling-Carve-Out / Cash-First envelope delivered Cash EUR 27M, Runway to December 2026, Altman Z 1.68, VALE Lender Confidence 74%, Composite 74.

## Company Background

Ferrovia Industrial Group had been founded in Madrid in 1978 and had, across forty-eight years, built a EUR 320M-revenue Iberian railway-infrastructure contractor organised into three operating divisions: a high-speed-rail civil-works division (EUR 180M revenue, AVE and CAV contract portfolio), a signalling-systems division (EUR 85M revenue, ERTMS-and-CBTC-signalling scope), and a rolling-stock-maintenance-services division (EUR 55M revenue, depot-and-yard services). The 2022–2025 cycle had left the group in a documented distress spiral: three consecutive contract-loss events in the high-speed-rail segment (two CAV tenders lost to a French-consortium competitor in 2023–2024, one AVE tender lost to a Spanish-German consortium in Q1 2025), cost-overrun events on two named civil-works projects that had consumed EUR 42M in unbudgeted completion capital, and a 2025 EBITDA margin of –EUR 60M against a 2022 reading of +EUR 36M. The April 17, 2026 Sunday-emergency board session had appointed Dorrego-Letamendi on a four-round ninety-day charter.

Three Q2 2026 events were named in the April 20 CRO-mandate dossier. First, the April 15 lender-syndicate letter had delivered the covenant-breach notice and the EUR 40M acceleration notice on the term-loan facility. Second, the April 18 employee Q2 pulse had priced Morale at 40% against the 2023 baseline of 68% following a March 2026 voluntary-departure wave of twenty-eight named engineers. Third, the April 20 indicative-bid log had surfaced three named buyers for the Iberian Signalling Systems division at indicative valuations in the EUR 18M–EUR 24M range.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2019–2025 European distressed-contractor literature had, across 38 documented railway-and-transport-infrastructure restructurings above EUR 100M debt, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, thirteen-week-cash-forecasts with discretionary-capex freezes had sustained Runway-to-next-decision above forty-five days in 78% of cases, against 32% for payables-stretch-only envelopes. Second, negotiated-standstill-with-equity-kicker postures had restored VALE Lender Confidence by 12–18 points in 68% of documented acceleration events, and had avoided named litigation in 74% of documented cases. Third, non-core-division carve-outs executed inside ninety days at EUR 18M–EUR 26M cash injection had moved Altman Z by 0.40–0.65 points in 62% of documented cases.

Empirically, the post-2022 Iberian-construction-restructuring literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, cash-first-narrative-second-EBITDA-last recovery archetypes had preceded sustainable going-concern postures in 72% of documented cases, against 22% for push-EBITDA-first postures that had consumed ninety-day cash reserves. Second, Altman-Z-to-above-1.60 recoveries at the ninety-day close had preceded subsequent six-to-twelve-month refinancing windows in 68% of documented cases.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the Sunday-emergency April 17 session, appointed Dorrego-Letamendi. The Head of Finance had, on April 18, delivered the twelve-week liquidity diagnostic. The Head of Operations had, on April 19, delivered the division-level carve-out scoping. The Head of Human Resources had, on April 20, delivered the Q2 Morale pulse. The senior-lender-syndicate agent had, on April 20, delivered the standstill-terms negotiation table.

### What Dorrego-Letamendi knew

The team had modelled three four-round envelopes. A Forecast-and-Freeze / Standstill / Signalling-Carve-Out / Cash-First envelope projected Composite 74. A Payables-Stretch-Only / Litigate / Hold / Push-EBITDA envelope projected Composite 12 with cash-exhaustion-by-day-sixty risk. A Full-Payables-Plus-Capex / Standstill / Hold / Cash-First envelope projected Composite 44.

### What she did not know

Whether the senior-lender syndicate would clear the standstill terms inside the two-week window the envelope assumed; whether the three indicative buyers for the Iberian Signalling Systems division would convert into a binding offer above the EUR 20M threshold; whether the CAV and AVE client counterparties would continue Stage-of-Completion payments through the standstill window; and whether the March-2026 voluntary-departure wave of twenty-eight named engineers would continue into Q2.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Finance**: *"Thirteen-week cashflow. Today. Every Sunday from this Sunday. Freeze discretionary capex. Seven-point-eight million a quarter out."*

**The Senior-Lender Syndicate Agent**: *"Standstill on forty million. Five-percent equity kicker. Ninety days. We need to see the non-core sale inside that window."*

**The Head of Operations**: *"Carve out Signalling. Twenty-two million cash. Keep the civil-works and maintenance divisions. That's the company you can actually run."*

**The Board Chairman**: *"Cash first. Narrative second. EBITDA last."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Dorrego-Letamendi had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Forecast-and-Freeze / Standstill / Signalling-Carve-Out / Cash-First:** the recommended envelope. Cash EUR 27M. Runway to December 2026. Altman Z 1.68. VALE 74%. Composite 74. Recommended.
- **Option B — Payables-Stretch-Only / Litigate / Hold / Push-EBITDA:** the defensive envelope. Composite 12. Rejected — cash-exhaustion probability above 80% inside sixty days.
- **Option C — Full-Payables-Plus-Capex / Standstill / Hold / Cash-First:** the partial envelope. Composite 44. Rejected — no cash injection, Altman Z drift.

Dorrego-Letamendi's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 3 — the Iberian Signalling Systems carve-out — because it was the round that delivered the EUR 22M cash injection that moved Altman Z above the 1.60 ninety-day-close threshold the refinancing window required.
