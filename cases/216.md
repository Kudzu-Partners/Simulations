## Abstract

Nimbus Games, Inc. was, in the case universe, a mid-size independent game studio headquartered in a secondary-tier creative-industry city with 4.2M monthly active users on its live-service flagship title, USD 2.80 ARPU across the platform, 420 employees (including 310 engineers and game-design specialists), three projects in active development (Project Apex — a live-service shooter with fourteen months to ship; Project Loom — a narrative single-player title with twenty-two months to ship; Project Riser — a mobile title with eight months to ship), USD 74M of cash, and a 2025 revenue base of USD 152M where the live-service flagship contributed USD 38M per quarter against a USD 46M quarterly burn. The 2024–2025 cycle had left the studio at a runway-constrained posture, a three-project slate that required thirty-six months to fully execute in sequence, and a live-service flagship that had entered its fifth year of operations with declining ARPU and flat concurrency. The Q1 2026 context had compounded three pressures: a hit-driven industry with no forgiveness for schedule slippage; a macro shift toward live-service monetisation that re-priced narrative-only titles at 28–42% of live-service per-user economics; and an organic competitive scene forming on Apex's alpha-test cohort. The case opens at 6:08 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Nimbus Games studio as **Selene Chávez-Oduwole**, a 46-year-old Chief Executive Officer, prepares the Period-1 slate commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Period-1 slate-discipline commitment (kill Project Loom at USD 8M sunk cost and redirect 22 engineers to Project Apex vs. hold Loom as a morale-and-narrative bet vs. accelerate Loom at the cost of Apex's ship date) and diagnose when the kill-and-redirect envelope dominates on the runway-preservation and ship-probability basis.
2. Analyse the Period-2 mobile-monetisation choice (battle-pass-only on Project Riser at D30 retention 18% vs. battle-pass + cosmetic hybrid at retention-dependent lift vs. neither) and recommend the monetisation envelope that the retention-you-have (not the retention-you-wanted) supports.
3. Diagnose the Period-3 launch-day response (ship Apex into 2.1M first-week concurrents, absorb USD 4M emergency capacity spend) and articulate why the worst problem a game can have — too much demand on day one — is also the problem that telegraphs a top-quartile retention outcome.
4. Respond to the Period-4 eSports-circuit-seeding commitment (USD 6M to seed an official circuit on Apex's organic competitive scene) and articulate why the studio that owns the competitive scene of its own game owns a decade-long revenue annuity the studio that cedes it does not.
5. Design the four-period studio-CEO archetype that couples a slate-kill discipline, a retention-based monetisation reflex, a launch-day capacity posture and a competitive-scene ownership commitment — anchored on the principle that in games every hit is luck and every studio that survives is discipline.

On the morning of **Wednesday, April 22, 2026**, **Selene Chávez-Oduwole** arrived at the Nimbus Games studio at 6:08 a.m., opened the four-period slate-and-launch workbook her Chief Creative Officer and her Chief Operating Officer had finalised at 1:14 a.m., and re-read the sentence she had pinned to her office whiteboard after the 2022 cancellation of her previous studio's narrative-only flagship: *"In games, every hit is luck. Every studio that survives is discipline."*

---

## The Protagonist

Chávez-Oduwole, 46, had assumed the Chief Executive Officer role at Nimbus Games on April 1, 2023, after nineteen years across game-design, product leadership and studio management at three mid-size independent studios and one AAA publisher. She had, in 2018–2021, led the ship of a live-service shooter that had delivered 1.4M first-week concurrents and had, in 2022, cancelled a narrative-only project at month twenty-eight that had consumed USD 11M of development cost — a decision that had preserved her former studio's runway at the cost of a publicly critical press cycle. The Nimbus board had recruited her in Q4 2022 specifically on three capabilities: her documented discipline of killing slate projects on ship-probability basis rather than sunk-cost basis, her command of live-service monetisation architecture that had, at the previous studio, generated ARPU climbs from USD 2.40 to USD 4.60 across eighteen months on a shooter flagship, and her willingness to spend aggressively on launch-day capacity to convert concurrency surges into retention cohorts rather than churn events.

---

## The Decision

Chávez-Oduwole had to commit, by the 8:00 a.m. executive-committee session, the Period-1 slate envelope and the directional intent for Periods 2–4.

1. **Period 1 — Slate discipline.** Three archetypes. A kill-and-redirect envelope (kill Project Loom at USD 8M sunk cost, redirect 22 engineers to Project Apex, ship Apex on schedule). A hold-all-three envelope (preserve Loom, preserve Apex's ship date, stretch cash by 4 months). An accelerate-Loom envelope (accelerate Loom to a 16-month ship, slip Apex by 4 months). Working envelope: kill-and-redirect.
2. **Period 2 — Mobile-monetisation choice.** Three vectors. A battle-pass-only envelope (Project Riser soft-launches in three markets at D30 retention 18%; battle-pass pricing anchors ARPU at USD 4.20 at the retention baseline). A hybrid envelope (battle-pass + cosmetic store; ARPU climbs to USD 5.80 at retention 24%; fails to support at retention 18%). A cosmetic-only envelope (cosmetic store only; ARPU anchors at USD 3.40 at retention 18%). Working envelope: battle-pass-only.
3. **Period 3 — Launch-day response.** Three vectors. A full-capacity-absorb envelope (absorb the 2.1M first-week concurrent surge, USD 4M emergency capacity spend, server stability within 72 hours). A throttle envelope (throttle concurrent access at 1.2M, preserve server stability, absorb a D7 retention drag of 6–8 points). A delayed-capacity envelope (scale capacity across the first 14 days, absorb a D3 churn event). Working envelope: full-capacity-absorb.
4. **Period 4 — eSports-circuit seeding.** Two vectors. A seed envelope (USD 6M to seed an official circuit — tournament organiser, prize pool, broadcast partner — on Apex's organic competitive scene). A cede envelope (allow a third-party tournament organiser to build the circuit; capture a share of the broadcast rights but cede scene-ownership). Working envelope: seed.

On Chávez-Oduwole's working model, a Kill-and-Redirect / Battle-Pass-Only / Full-Capacity-Absorb / Seed envelope delivered Period-4 MAU 9.4M, ARPU USD 4.10, Cash USD 118M and a Composite Survive-and-Compound rating — with the live-service flagship, Apex and the circuit all running as a connected portfolio.

## Company Background

Nimbus Games, Inc. had been founded in 2009 as a four-person independent studio, had shipped a critically-acclaimed narrative title in 2013, had pivoted to live-service in 2018 with the launch of its flagship title, and had, across seventeen years, built a mid-size studio organised into four creative and functional teams (Apex Live-Service, Apex Shooter, Loom Narrative, Riser Mobile) with a shared Engineering, Art, QA and Publishing backbone. The 2024–2025 cycle had produced three slate-management episodes: a February 2025 Loom milestone review that had flagged the project as ship-probability-low; a June 2025 Apex alpha-test that had telegraphed a top-quartile retention outcome; and an October 2025 Riser soft-launch in a single market that had produced a D30 retention of 16%.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2019–2025 live-service game-studio literature had, across 62 documented three-to-five-year slate cycles on USD 100M–USD 500M-revenue independent studios, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, slate-kill envelopes at month eighteen to month twenty-four of under-performing projects had preserved 18–34% of remaining development budget in 72% of documented cases, against hold-all-three postures that had fully consumed the budget and had delayed adjacent ship dates in 68%. Second, battle-pass-only monetisation architectures at mobile D30 retention of 16–20% had delivered ARPU bands of USD 3.80–USD 4.60 in 64% of documented cases, against hybrid architectures that had required D30 retention above 22% to be accretive. Third, launch-day capacity absorption at emergency-spend envelopes of USD 2M–USD 6M on first-week concurrency surges of 1.5M–3.0M had preserved D7 retention above 52% in 78% of documented cases, against throttle postures that had compressed retention by 4–9 points.

Empirically, the 2022–2025 eSports literature had two additional findings. First, studio-seeded official circuits had captured 64–82% of long-term broadcast-and-sponsorship revenue on their own titles in 72% of documented cases, against third-party-organiser cession that had delivered 12–24% revenue shares. Second, eSports-seed envelopes at USD 4M–USD 8M on organically-forming competitive scenes had out-performed over-invested USD 14M–USD 22M seed envelopes in 58% of cases, on the discipline-over-abundance basis.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The February 2025 Loom milestone had flagged the project. The June 2025 Apex alpha had validated the ship. The October 2025 Riser soft-launch had confirmed D30 retention of 16%. The Q4 2025 board had approved the slate commitment-to-decision window.

### What Chávez-Oduwole knew

Project Loom had consumed USD 8M of development cost across twenty months and had, on the Chief Creative Officer's most recent milestone review, received a ship-probability assessment of 38%. Project Apex had passed alpha with top-quartile retention metrics. Project Riser had soft-launched and was generating D30 retention of 18% in the three-market cohort. Apex's alpha cohort had organically formed a competitive scene across three streaming platforms.

### What she did not know

Whether the Loom team's departure would precipitate broader studio-morale losses; whether Riser's D30 retention would hold at 18% or drift to 14% over the first ninety-day cohort; whether the Apex server-stability engineering could absorb a 2.1M first-week concurrent load without a D3 churn event; and whether the eSports-circuit seed would support a USD 3M–USD 6M annual sponsorship-and-broadcast revenue baseline.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Creative Officer**: *"Kill Loom. Eight million is the tuition. We ship Apex or we don't ship anything."*

**The Chief Operating Officer**: *"Battle-pass-only on Riser. At eighteen retention, hybrid is aspirational."*

**The Head of Live-Services Engineering**: *"Four million to absorb a two-point-one million concurrent surge. We don't get a second launch day."*

**The Board Chair**: *"In games, every hit is luck. Every studio that survives is discipline."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Chávez-Oduwole had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Kill-and-Redirect / Battle-Pass-Only / Full-Capacity-Absorb / Seed:** the recommended envelope. Period-4 MAU 9.4M. ARPU USD 4.10. Cash USD 118M. Recommended.
- **Option B — Hold-All-Three / Hybrid / Throttle / Cede:** the abundance envelope. Period-4 MAU 5.8M. ARPU USD 3.10. Cash USD 24M. Rejected — runway compression and retention drag.
- **Option C — Accelerate-Loom / Cosmetic-Only / Full-Capacity-Absorb / Seed:** the narrative-preserving envelope. Period-4 MAU 7.6M. ARPU USD 3.40. Cash USD 62M. Rejected — Apex ship slip and ARPU compression.

Her private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the period most correlated with the final survive-and-compound rating was Period 1 — the slate discipline — because it was the period that decided whether the studio shipped the ship-probability-high project on schedule (Option A) or stretched its runway across three projects and shipped none (Options B and C).
