## Abstract

GreenEat S.A.S. was, in the case universe, a ten-month-old Bogotá pre-seed startup founded in June 2025 by three co-founders — **Camila Ospina-Ruiz**, 29, Chief Product Officer and the case protagonist; **Santiago Valdés-Barón**, 31, Chief Operating Officer and former chef; and **Luisa Mora-Ariza**, 28, Chief Executive Officer and former FMCG brand manager — testing a plant-based fast-food concept at an urban middle-class price point against the conventional Colombian burger. At the April 2026 decision point the startup had COP 48m of runway remaining against a burn rate of COP 4.8m per month, no revenue yet, and a Runway Meter visible on the shared dashboard. Two MVPs were planned: a two-Saturday Usaquén pop-up priced at COP 24,000, and a Rappi ghost-kitchen 30-day-retention test at the 35 % threshold. A Series A path required three consecutive months of ≥35 % MoM growth. An angel offer sat on the table: **Ángel González**, a Bogotá-based FMCG family-office principal, was offering COP 240m for 20 % at a COP 1.2bn post-money valuation. The case opens at 7:12 a.m. on **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at the three-co-founder coworking desk in Chapinero.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Apply Sarasvathy's effectuation logic — bird-in-hand, affordable-loss, crazy-quilt, lemonade, pilot-in-the-plane — to the Round 1 means-inventory the three co-founders must author before the business plan, articulate why expert entrepreneurs reason from means rather than goals, and articulate why the simulation refuses to advance to MVP-1 until the three co-founders have listed what each actually brings to the venture.
2. Design the Round 1 MVP-1 Usaquén pop-up as a single-riskiest-assumption test (the Bogotá middle-class consumer pays COP 24,000 for a plant-based burger), commit to revenue-per-stall-day as the actionable metric rather than Instagram followers or intent-survey responses, and apply the Ries Build-Measure-Learn loop with the strict pivot/persevere/zoom decision rule — no "run another Saturday to be sure" is permitted, because that is the founder's favourite way to burn runway.
3. Execute the Round 2 MVP-2 Rappi ghost-kitchen 30-day-retention test at the 35 % threshold, read the retention reading as the distinction between a transactional and a repeat model, and articulate why the bootstrap-versus-friends-and-family-versus-first-angel-conversation decision is forced by the six-month runway rather than chosen at leisure.
4. Trace the Round 3 Ángel González angel-offer against the Series A MoM-growth requirement — taking the COP 240m at COP 1.2bn post-money imposes dilution that compounds into Series A, declining the offer may starve the business before the MoM metric lands — and commit the defensible capital-stack posture against the Runway Meter.
5. Commit the Round 4 Series A positioning decision — consumer brand versus B2B food-service platform, with margins on corporate catering running at 2.3× retail — and articulate why the Fondo Verde LATAM valuation multiple differs between the two narratives. Articulate why the primer-entrepreneurship frame sim 303 occupies is distinct from Venture Builder (sim 008, multi-venture portfolio frame), CampoTech Agritech (sim 094, agri-tech venture frame), Iberian Ventures FounderLegal (sim 115, founder-legal frame) and HealthSpring Founder Runway (sim 081, healthtech venture frame).

On the morning of **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 7:12 a.m., **Camila Ospina-Ruiz** arrived at the three-co-founder coworking desk in Chapinero, set down the two-day Usaquén pop-up printed menu the designer had finalised the previous evening, poured the three mugs of cold brew she always poured at the start of the weekly founder meeting, and opened the GranMomento workbook on the shared-drive laptop. The Runway Meter at the top of the dashboard read *10 meses — 7 días — 3 horas*. Santiago was already at the desk sharpening the plant-based patty formulation spec. Luisa arrived six minutes late with the draft angel-term-sheet folder in her shoulder bag. The Usaquén pop-up was forty-two hours away. The Ángel González follow-up call was eighteen hours away. The Fondo Verde LATAM associate's introductory email had landed at 11:48 p.m. the previous night.

---

## The Protagonist

Ospina-Ruiz, 29, had co-founded GreenEat in June 2025, had completed an Ingeniería Industrial at the Universidad de los Andes in 2019 and was a 2024 MIT Sloan-Legatum Fellow, had spent four years at a Colombian consumer-goods firm in product-management and brand-strategy roles before leaving in May 2025 to co-found, and had met Santiago and Luisa at a 2023 plant-based-food-innovation hackathon at the Universidad Nacional where the three had co-written the six-page concept deck that became the GreenEat pitch. Her lean-startup instincts had been shaped by three teachings: the 2023–2024 MIT Sloan-Legatum exposure to Ries's *Lean Startup* and to Sarasvathy's effectuation canon; a 2022 failed MVP at her previous employer where the company had run "one more Saturday" of pop-ups for nine consecutive weeks without committing to pivot; and an April 2025 meeting with a Bogotá-based seed-stage investor who had told the three: *"No me traigan el business plan. Tráiganme el MVP y el número."*

---

## The Decision

Ospina-Ruiz had to commit, by the 9:00 a.m. co-founder stand-up, the four-round package.

1. **Round 1 — Effectuation and MVP-1.** Author the three-co-founder means-inventory across who-I-am, what-I-know, whom-I-know. Commit MVP-1: two-Saturday Usaquén pop-up priced at COP 24,000 per plant-based burger. Test the single riskiest assumption. Commit revenue-per-stall-day as actionable metric. Commit the Build-Measure-Learn pivot/persevere/zoom rule.
2. **Round 2 — MVP-2 and Capital-Stack.** Commit the Rappi ghost-kitchen MVP-2 with the thirty-day-retention ≥ 35 % threshold as go/no-go. Commit the capital posture: bootstrap, friends-and-family, or first-angel conversation with Ángel González.
3. **Round 3 — Series A Preparation.** Commit the three-consecutive-months ≥35 % MoM growth pre-Series-A metric target. Accept or decline the Ángel González COP 240m for 20 % offer at COP 1.2bn post-money.
4. **Round 4 — Series A Positioning.** Pitch GreenEat as consumer brand (lower multiple, retail-margin economics), or as B2B food-service platform (higher multiple, 2.3×-retail corporate-catering margin, different comparable set). Write the Series A deck from the defensible-with-data story.

Commitments: the Round 1 means-inventory and MVP-1 commitment; the Round 2 MVP-2 go/no-go and capital-stack posture; the Round 3 Ángel response and Series-A metric target; the Round 4 positioning; and a one-page co-founder memo that frames the four-round commitment as a single 18-month arc with the Runway Meter as the forcing function.

## Sectoral Background

The 2018–2025 Colombian plant-based-food category had, in the case universe, grown from approximately COP 42bn to COP 308bn in aggregate revenue, concentrated in three operator tiers (multinational branded-plant-based imports like Beyond and Impossible category extensions; Colombian specialty players like Frutopía and ProVegano; emerging startups at the pre-seed and seed stages). The 2024 Bogotá-middle-class consumer plant-based purchase intent panel had registered at 41 %, against a documented purchase-conversion rate of 9.2 % when the product was priced at a premium versus conventional and 28.4 % when priced at parity. The LTV/CAC band for Colombian urban-middle-class fast-food startups at the pre-seed stage ran approximately 4.2× to 11.8×; Fondo Verde LATAM had underwritten recent Series A investments at 6.5× to 9.0×, with B2B-food-service-platform comparable multiples running at approximately 2.1× the consumer-brand multiple on revenue.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2001–2025 entrepreneurship literature produced three findings the simulation had to respect. First, Sarasvathy's effectuation framework — expert entrepreneurs reason from means rather than goals, committing affordable-loss inventories and crazy-quilt partnership structures — had been validated across multiple Latin American SME-founder panels, with founders who authored explicit means-inventories showing documented 18-month-survival rates approximately 1.7× higher than control. Second, the Ries *Lean Startup* Build-Measure-Learn canon, operationalised as a strict pivot/persevere/zoom rule without the "run one more Saturday" relaxation, had produced documented runway-efficiency readings approximately 2.3× those of unstructured MVP cycles. Third, the venture-finance literature had documented that angel offers at pre-seed with 20 % dilution at COP 1.2bn post-money imposed approximately 6-to-9 percentage points of Series A cap-table dilution compounded against the founders, with the accept-or-decline decision dominated by the expected-value of landing the Series A MoM-growth metric before runway zero.

The 2015–2025 plant-based-food-venture literature added two findings. The 30-day-retention threshold at 35 % had emerged as the documented distinction between transactional-delivery and repeat-consumer models, with sub-35 % retention correlating at approximately 84 % with Series A rejection on public panels. And the consumer-brand-versus-B2B-platform positioning decision had emerged as the single largest driver of Series A valuation-multiple divergence in the plant-based-food pre-seed-to-Series-A cohort, with the 2.3× corporate-catering-margin finding correlating to a 2.1× valuation-multiple premium on comparable revenue.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The June 2025 founding; the seven-month product-formulation and concept-validation period; the February 2026 MVP-1 design; the March 2026 Ángel González introduction; the April 8 Fondo Verde LATAM associate outreach; and the April 23 co-founder stand-up commitment were the sequence.

### What Ospina-Ruiz knew

The Sarasvathy means-inventory template and the Ries Build-Measure-Learn discipline; the MVP-1 revenue-per-stall-day threshold; the Rappi ghost-kitchen 30-day-retention threshold; the Ángel González term sheet detail and the documented angel-to-Series-A cap-table compounding cost; the Fondo Verde LATAM 6.5-to-9.0× Series-A multiple range; and the consumer-versus-B2B-platform multiple divergence.

### What she did not know

Whether the Usaquén pop-up would hit the revenue-per-stall-day threshold or would slip and force the pivot; whether the Rappi ghost-kitchen would deliver 35 %+ 30-day retention or would read as transactional; whether Ángel González would accept a smaller check-size negotiation or would insist on the 20 %-at-post-money structure; whether Fondo Verde LATAM would underwrite at the B2B-platform multiple or would push the consumer-brand frame; and whether the three-co-founder means-inventory would reveal a shared-commitment gap the previous nine months of collaboration had masked.

### What others were saying

**Santiago Valdés-Barón (COO, 31, chef)**: *"Si el sabor no funciona el sábado, lo demás no importa. Si funciona, veremos si el precio aguanta."*

**Luisa Mora-Ariza (CEO, 28, brand)**: *"La pregunta no es el sábado. La pregunta es el segundo mes de retención en Rappi. Ahí decidimos si es marca o si es transacción."*

**Ángel González (Bogotá family-office, 56)**: *"Ciento veinte mil pesos por entrada a un ticket promedio de veinticuatro mil no es el negocio. Cuarenta y cinco pesos de margen en un corporativo de trescientos cubiertos, ese sí. Los miro como plataforma, no como marca."*

**The Fondo Verde LATAM associate (introduction letter)**: *"Tres meses de treinta y cinco por ciento mes-sobre-mes. Desde julio. Si tienen eso, los miramos. Si no, los miramos en la Series A de alguien más."*

**A previous mentor (the Bogotá seed-investor from April 2025)**: *"El plan es artefacto de lo aprendido. El aprendizaje es artefacto de lo construido. Y el Runway Meter sigue marcando."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Ospina-Ruiz had narrowed the pre-seed-through-Series-A space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — Sequenced MVPs + decline Ángel + push Series A on MoM + B2B-platform positioning (Recommended).** Respects Sarasvathy means-inventory; respects Ries Build-Measure-Learn; respects the 35 %-retention threshold; preserves cap-table for Series A; pitches into the higher-multiple comparable set with the 2.3×-retail B2B catering margin as anchor. The runway-six-month horizon is tight; the MoM-growth hit probability estimated at approximately 62 %.
- **Option B — Take Ángel González's COP 240m + bootstrap MVPs + consumer-brand positioning.** Rejected — the ángel dilution compounds into Series A at documented 6-to-9 pp cost; the consumer-brand positioning lands on the lower multiple; the bootstrap MVP cadence loses the professional-investor introduction sequence the MoM metric requires to land on Fondo Verde's Q3 underwriting calendar.
- **Option C — Skip MVP-1, launch MVP-2 directly, take Ángel + friends-and-family, consumer-brand positioning.** Defensible on speed grounds but violates the Build-Measure-Learn sequencing; forfeits the revenue-per-stall-day read; over-commits cap-table at pre-seed; and undersells the B2B-platform positioning on the Series A deck.

Ospina-Ruiz's private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the single decision most correlated with the Series A outcome was the B2B-platform positioning for Round 4, because Fondo Verde's underwriting multiple dominated all other levers on expected-enterprise-value at Series A, and because the documented 2.3× corporate-catering margin made the B2B narrative defensible with data the consumer-brand narrative could not match. The *Momento es Ahora* frame sits at the Colombian-primer-entrepreneurship tier in the 2026 Express catalogue, distinct from Venture Builder (sim 008, multi-venture portfolio), CampoTech Agritech (sim 094, agri-tech), Iberian Ventures FounderLegal (sim 115, founder-legal), and HealthSpring Founder Runway (sim 081, healthtech) — 303 is the plant-based-consumer-startup first-lab frame in Spanish with the Runway-Meter-as-forcing-function discipline.
