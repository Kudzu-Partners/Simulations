## Abstract

The Strategy Team Lead at the case-universe Santiago-headquartered Chilean-Peruvian mid-cap industrial-services firm — approximately USD 340m in 2025 revenue, 1,850 employees, a stagnating core losing three points of margin per year, three emerging competitive threats including a digital-platform disruptor, a regulator opening a consultation on pricing, and an activist investor accumulating a 4.2% position — had been asked, in the March 2026 CEO directive, to run the April 23 strategic-plan build-and-war-game exercise across the eight-person strategy team and deliver a revised three-pillar plan that would survive attack by four adversary personas. The fifteen-page dossier was deliberately ambiguous — three years of financials, ten pages of market data, a capability audit signed by the COO, and four pages of board minutes with the 2024 pillar-proliferation politics legible. The point was not that the team had incomplete data. The point was that the team had to pick the Five Forces that actually moved margin rather than all five, the VRIO capabilities that were both valuable and rare rather than all of them, and a Rumelt kernel with a diagnosis, a guiding policy and coherent action rather than a laundry list of goals. And then survive a stranger's attack. The case opens at 2:48 p.m. on Thursday, April 23, 2026, at the firm's Santiago strategy-office floor as **Camila Undurraga-Figari**, a 38-year-old Strategy Team Lead, prepares the three-phase StratCraft sequence.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Populate the four Phase-1 analytical canvases — Five Forces, VRIO, PESTLE, Value Chain — against the deliberately ambiguous fifteen-page dossier and articulate why an analytical canvas is only valuable to the extent it isolates the forces and capabilities that move margin and competitive position, and why populating all boxes equally is an analyst's failure rather than an analyst's achievement.
2. Design the Phase-2 strategic intent — vision, mission, three pillars, one guiding policy — against Rumelt's kernel (diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent action) and articulate why fluff and pillar-proliferation get stripped by the kernel test, why goals are not strategy, and why pillars that do not mutually reinforce each other belong on the reject pile rather than in the deck.
3. Prioritise the initiative portfolio against the impact × feasibility × risk matrix under the binding budget, headcount and executive-attention constraints and articulate why the constraint is the test — cutting half the initiatives before lunch is a sign of strategic discipline rather than a sign of risk aversion, and why every surviving initiative has to ladder back to a pillar.
4. Run the Phase-3 war-game with the four attacker personas — Incumbent-Defender, Digital-Disruptor, Regulator, Activist-Investor — and articulate why a coherent-looking plan that depends on a single hire, a replicable activity, or a funding dependence is a plan a competent adversary will break, and why Porter's activity-system test — does the plan survive if one activity is copied? — is the right structural-durability test.
5. Commit to the revised post-attack plan that reads the single strategic-vulnerability through the activity-system and funding-dependence lens and rebuilds around the vulnerability rather than patching the edges, articulating why a plan that survives a stranger's attack is a strategy and the rest is a document.

On the afternoon of **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, **Camila Undurraga-Figari** arrived at the firm's Santiago strategy-office floor at 2:48 p.m., opened the StratCraft workbook her senior associate had finalised at 9:50 p.m. the night before, and re-read the sentence she had pinned to her strategy-team wall: *"A plan that survives a stranger's attack is a strategy. The rest is a document."*

---

## The Protagonist

Undurraga-Figari, 38, had been Strategy Team Lead since 2023, had spent nine years across McKinsey's Lima and Santiago offices, an eighteen-month secondment inside a Chilean family-office turnaround in 2020–2021 where she had rebuilt a stagnating forestry operation's initiative portfolio, and a two-year stint as Corporate Development Director at a Colombian industrial-services firm in 2021–2023 where she had navigated the 2022 activist-investor engagement that ultimately redirected the firm's three-pillar plan. In 2024 she authored the firm's internal playbook *Kernel, No Laundry List*, which framed strategy as a Rumelt-kernel artefact with a diagnosis, a guiding policy and coherent action rather than a vision-and-mission-and-values laundry list. The CEO had assigned her the April exercise on three considerations: her command of the Rumelt kernel discipline; her comfort running a red-team against her own work; and her willingness to break and rebuild a plan rather than defend one.

---

## The Decision

Undurraga-Figari had to commit, by the 5:00 p.m. CEO debrief, the revised post-war-game strategic plan.

1. **Phase 1 — Analysis.** Five Forces, VRIO, PESTLE and Value Chain populated against the dossier. Forces that move margin identified; capabilities that are valuable and rare isolated; external trends over the planning horizon flagged; activities that constitute the current activity system mapped.
2. **Phase 2 — Strategic intent.** Vision, mission, three pillars, one guiding policy. Rumelt kernel: one-paragraph diagnosis, one-paragraph policy, three-to-five coherent actions. Initiative portfolio prioritised by impact × feasibility × risk under a USD 42m three-year investment envelope and a 60-person reallocation budget.
3. **Phase 3 — War-game.** Plan exchanged with a peer team. Four personas assigned: Incumbent-Defender, Digital-Disruptor, Regulator, Activist-Investor. Each builds a three-move attack in fifteen minutes. Attacks presented. The plan's single largest vulnerability identified. Ten minutes to revise. Some teams patch, some rebuild.

Commitments: a revised three-pillar plan with a Porter activity-system that survives replication of any single activity; a funding-structure that does not depend on a single hire or a single capital round; a Rumelt kernel that a board member can read in three minutes; and a Mintzberg emergent-strategy loop built into the QBR rhythm.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1990–2025 strategy-process literature produced three findings the exercise had to respect. First, war-gamed strategic plans produced documented three-year hit-rate readings approximately 35–55% higher than non-war-gamed plans on comparable 2010–2024 mid-cap cycles. Second, plans that passed Porter's activity-system test — mutual reinforcement among activities, non-replicability of the system as a whole — produced documented competitive-advantage durability readings roughly 2.1× those of single-activity advantages. Third, Rumelt-kernel plans — diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent action — produced documented executive-team alignment readings 40–60% higher than vision-and-values plans of comparable length.

The post-2010 red-team literature added two findings. Cross-team persona-based red-teaming produced documented vulnerability-detection rates approximately 2.3× those of internal strategy-team reviews. And post-attack revision, as opposed to post-attack documentation, was the single strongest correlate of the adopted plan's three-year survival.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The March 2026 CEO directive asked for a war-game-tested plan by April 23. The fifteen-page dossier was finalised April 7. The peer team was assigned April 10. The attacker-persona briefs were distributed April 18.

### What Undurraga-Figari knew

The digital disruptor threatens the mid-tier segment first; the regulator's pricing consultation is an optionality moment, not a certainty; the activist will publish a letter within six to eight weeks of the 13-D; the 2024 plan's pillar-proliferation is a political artefact that has to be stripped; and the board is split between pillar-retention and pillar-rebuild.

### What she did not know

Whether the attacker team would go for the obvious activity-replicability vulnerability or the quieter funding-dependence vulnerability; whether her three-pillar frame would survive the activist persona or require rebuild; whether the CEO would accept the kernel-length discipline or ask for the long-form vision; and whether the Mintzberg emergent-strategy loop would survive the QBR-redesign friction from the functional heads.

### What others were saying

**The CEO**: *"I want a plan that survives the activist investor's letter. Not a plan that only survives my own defence of it."*

**The CFO**: *"The USD 42m envelope is binding. The 60-person reallocation is binding. The pillars that do not fit inside both are not pillars."*

**The COO**: *"The activity system is what we build. If the war-game team can copy any one activity and kill the plan, we have a capability problem, not a strategy problem."*

**The Chief Commercial Officer**: *"The digital disruptor is already in the mid-tier. The war-game will go there first. We need to pre-empt."*

**The Head of People**: *"The reallocation reads as a reorganisation. Get the communication cadence right or the reorganisation becomes the story."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Undurraga-Figari had narrowed the space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — Revised three-pillar plan with Porter activity-system durability, Rumelt-kernel discipline and Mintzberg emergent-strategy loop (Recommended):** four-canvas analysis, three pillars, one guiding policy, initiative portfolio under binding budget and headcount, war-game-tested, revised around the single vulnerability the attacker persona isolates. Recommended.
- **Option B — Five-pillar plan retained from 2024 with supplementary war-game appendix:** rejected — keeps pillar proliferation, fails the kernel test, and treats the war-game as documentation rather than decision.
- **Option C — Strategy sprint replacing the annual plan with a rolling quarterly cadence:** rejected — useful complement but insufficient as the 2026 commitment because it loses the activity-system architecture the current shocks require.

Her private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the phase most correlated with the plan's three-year survival was Phase 3 — the war-game — because the war-game was the phase where the activity-system test and the funding-dependence audit most sharply dominated the vision-statement headline. StratComm handles the five-audience communication of the plan. HorizonMap handles the three-horizon portfolio allocation that the plan's initiatives must respect.
