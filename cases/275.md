## Abstract

The Principal Design Researcher at the case-universe Bogotá-headquartered service-design and design-research consultancy — approximately COP 48bn in 2025 revenue, 74 completed engagements, approximately 2,400 hours of field video in the archive, 86 design researchers and service-design practitioners, and a USAID post-conflict service-design contract at COP 1.8bn under review — had been asked, in the March 2026 USAID ethics-review letter, to deliver an April 22 methods-audit response across six flagged engagements whose fieldwork deficiencies were loud enough to justify freezing the contract. The Q1 2026 internal methods review had surfaced a recurring failure mode across the design-research function: project leads were signing off on engagement deliverables on the hours-of-video column, not on the reflexivity-and-consent column — deficit-lens field notes on financial-inclusion fieldwork, consent obtained from staff but not from patients in acute distress, observer-effect contamination from visible clipboards, over-reliance on single charismatic informants, video corpora coded by one analyst with no inter-rater reliability, and design frameworks presented without citation provenance. The USAID letter asked the Principal Design Researcher to work through all six flags and commit to a fieldwork-governance remediation that would either unfreeze the contract or forfeit it. The case opens at 2:28 p.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the firm's Teusaquillo head office as **Mariana Salcedo-Ruiz**, a 39-year-old Principal Design Researcher, prepares the six-flag commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Flag-1 Bancolombia San Andresito setup (financial-inclusion fieldwork, ethnographer from Chapinero embedded in a low-income informal-retail setting, field notes with 340 deficit-lens codings and zero adaptive-behaviour codings, personas that read as patronising, 68% onboarding abandonment, COP 2.4bn burned) and articulate why researcher positionality and reflexivity logging are the pre-analysis disciplines, and why deficit-lens coding is a positionality failure rather than a respondent-population failure.
2. Analyse the Flag-2 Sanitas emergency-department setup (staff consent obtained, patients in acute distress recorded without valid consent, Ley 1581 Habeas Data breach, 42 hours of video legally unusable, COP 920M frozen) and articulate why layered-consent protocols for vulnerable settings are a legal and ethical minimum in Colombia, and why staff-only consent is insufficient for patient-containing video corpora.
3. Diagnose the Flag-3 TransMilenio observer-effect setup (visible clipboards at fare gates, reported 3% fare-evasion versus an independent 18–22% from turnstile-counter audit, Movilidad COP 1.6bn spent solving the wrong problem) and articulate why the choice between overt and covert observation is a design-research decision that depends on whether observation itself distorts the behaviour being measured — and why fare-evasion plainly does.
4. Respond to the Flag-4 single-informant over-reliance (one charismatic informant quoted in every report, informant-triangulation failure), the Flag-5 inter-rater reliability failure (video coded by one analyst with no reliability check), and the Flag-6 citation-provenance failure (design frameworks presented to DPS traced to a competitor's published method with no citation, reputational landmine) and articulate why informant triangulation, inter-rater reliability, and citation provenance are three non-negotiable disciplines at the engagement-delivery level.
5. Design the six-flag commitment that reads each engagement through the reflexivity lens and commits to a fieldwork-governance framework — reflexivity logging, community co-design, layered consent, overt-versus-covert decision tree, inter-rater reliability, citation-provenance auditing — with a monthly ethics-committee review and a USAID-aligned escalation, articulating why ethnography is a discipline and the data is the discipline you brought.

## The Protagonist

Salcedo-Ruiz, 39, had been Principal Design Researcher since 2022, had spent eleven years across Colombian and Chilean design-research roles, a two-year masters appointment at Universidad Javeriana in 2013–2015 on visual ethnography in urban informal settlements, and a three-year stint at a São Paulo service-design firm in 2019–2022 where she had rebuilt the consent protocol for hospital and emergency-services fieldwork. In 2024 she authored the firm's internal playbook *El Campo Antes Que el Informe* ("The Field Before the Report"), which framed design research as a reflexivity-first discipline. The Managing Partner had assigned her the six-flag commitment on three considerations: her command of the 2013–2015 Javeriana work on positionality; her discipline of leading the engagement read with the reflexivity column rather than the video-hour column; and her willingness to recommend a USAID-facing commitment that the project-delivery team would find uncomfortable.

---

## The Decision

Salcedo-Ruiz had to commit, by the 4:30 p.m. Managing Partner pre-brief, the six-flag remediation commitment and the governance memorandum that would go to USAID, Bancolombia and Sanitas by Monday.

1. **Flag 1 — Bancolombia deficit-lens.** Re-code the corpus with adaptive-behaviour coding. Retrain the ethnographer roster on positionality. Community co-design sessions as a pre-field entry discipline.
2. **Flag 2 — Sanitas consent.** Re-consent on the archived footage where possible; delete where not. Layered-consent protocol for every patient-containing corpus.
3. **Flag 3 — TransMilenio observer effect.** Covert observation protocol for behaviour-distorting settings. Audit methodology against behavioural-evidence benchmarks.
4. **Flag 4 — Single informant.** Informant-triangulation rule: at least three informants per engagement with published methodology note.
5. **Flag 5 — Inter-rater reliability.** Dual-coder inter-rater reliability on every video corpus above 20 hours. Cohen's kappa threshold ≥ 0.70.
6. **Flag 6 — Citation provenance.** Citation-provenance audit on every framework presented externally. Methodology-provenance statement standard on every deliverable.

Combined flagged-engagement revenue exposure: approximately COP 12bn in direct fees plus approximately COP 1.8bn in USAID contract value plus approximately COP 920M of Sanitas frozen. Combined remediation spend: approximately COP 1,800M across re-fieldwork, consent cure, legal and ethics-committee rehabilitation.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1970–2025 ethnographic-methods literature produced three findings the commitment had to respect. First, reflexivity-logged fieldwork produced documented positionality-bias detection rates 3–5× higher than unreflexive fieldwork on comparable 2015–2024 design-research engagements. Second, overt-versus-covert observation decision frameworks produced documented observer-effect reduction of 40–70% on behaviour-distorting settings. Third, dual-coder inter-rater reliability produced documented coding-error detection rates approximately 4× higher than single-coder coding on comparable video-coded corpora.

The post-2010 research-ethics literature added two findings. Layered-consent protocols for vulnerable settings produced documented consent-failure detection rates 2–3× higher than single-layer consent in comparable hospital and social-service fieldwork. And citation-provenance auditing produced documented framework-provenance failure-detection rates approximately 60% on comparable design-consultancy portfolios.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The March 2026 USAID letter flagged six engagements. Salcedo-Ruiz's team reproduced every flag by April 15. The Bancolombia re-do request came April 17. The DPS framework-provenance question came April 18.

### What Salcedo-Ruiz knew

Deficit-lens field notes are a documented positionality failure; patient consent in acute-distress settings requires layered protocols under Ley 1581; visible clipboards distort evasion behaviour; single-informant dependence is a known failure mode; single-coder video coding is below the inter-rater reliability minimum; and framework-provenance failures are a reputational landmine.

### What she did not know

Whether USAID would unfreeze the post-conflict contract on the April 22 memorandum or require on-site ethics-committee inspection; whether Sanitas would accept re-consent or require deletion of the full 42-hour corpus; whether the DPS account would survive the framework-provenance disclosure; and whether the ethics-committee rehabilitation would be accepted at the USAID-required cadence.

### What others were saying

**The Senior Ethnographer**: *"Reflexivity logging and community co-design are standard practice in 2026. We fell behind the craft, not in front of it."*

**The Managing Partner**: *"USAID is the linchpin. If the memorandum holds, we keep the post-conflict contract and the firm's public-sector standing. If it does not, the practice contracts by a third."*

**The General Counsel**: *"The Ley 1581 Sanitas exposure is the litigation path. The commitment must name the re-consent protocol and the deletion procedure explicitly."*

**The Head of Private-Sector Accounts**: *"Bancolombia wants a re-do. The re-do has to produce adaptive-behaviour personas that land, or we lose the relationship permanently."*

## Options Under Consideration

Salcedo-Ruiz had narrowed the space to three remediation postures.

- **Option A — Six-flag remediation with fieldwork-governance framework (Recommended):** reflexivity logging, community co-design, layered consent, overt-versus-covert decision tree, inter-rater reliability, citation-provenance auditing; monthly ethics-committee review; USAID-aligned escalation. Cost COP 1,800M. Recommended.
- **Option B — Address the three major flags (Bancolombia, Sanitas, TransMilenio) and defer the craft-level flags:** rejected — leaves the DPS provenance exposure open and does not close the USAID review.
- **Option C — Narrow the practice to private-sector B2B and exit USAID and public-sector work:** rejected — destroys approximately 28% of revenue and abandons the firm's public-sector social-license capital.

Her private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the flag most correlated with USAID acceptance was Flag 2 — the Sanitas layered-consent failure — because it was the flag with the most direct legal and ethical exposure.
