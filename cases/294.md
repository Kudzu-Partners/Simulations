## Abstract

Rutas del Pacífico S.A.S. was, in the case universe, an eighteen-year curated-tourism operator running Pacific-coast Colombian destinations — approximately COP 11bn in 2025 revenue, 42 employees, a four-destination product line (Bahía Solano, Nuquí, Sanquianga and the Baudó river circuit), a customer base with a mean age of 47 and a high-income tilt acquired largely through travel-agent referral, no mobile application, an existing but not formally targeted millennial-adjacent segment (age 25-40) that the sales team had observed growing at approximately 14% year-on-year through direct web enquiries, and a Q1 2026 announcement from Booking.com and Airbnb that both platforms would open Colombian domestic-tour listings during Q3 2026. The eight-member board had, at its April 16 meeting, asked the new Strategy Director to produce a first-pass plan by the April 30 monthly review that met the four-decision standard (SWOT; SMART; Ansoff alternative; COP 800m three-year budget). The case opens at 2:36 p.m. on Thursday, April 23, 2026, at the firm's Cali headquarters as **Mateo Arboleda-Muñoz**, a 36-year-old Strategy Director, prepares the twenty-minute first-pass commitment.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Classify twelve candidate factors — eighteen years of Pacific-coast operational knowledge, no mobile app, a mean customer age of 47, a post-pandemic domestic-tourism surge, a Booking.com domestic-tour launch, and seven others — correctly across the four SWOT quadrants (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats), and articulate why a misclassification at the SWOT step propagates into a wrong TOWS-matrix generator downstream and wrong strategic-option seeds downstream of that.
2. Read the TOWS matrix as the SWOT's operational output — SO (Strengths × Opportunities), ST (Strengths × Threats), WO (Weaknesses × Opportunities), WT (Weaknesses × Threats) — and articulate why each quadrant seeds one or two initiative candidates and why the four quadrants constitute the starting option space rather than the final plan.
3. Distinguish SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) objectives from the vague aspirations they must replace, apply the Doran 1981 test to each of five candidate objectives, and articulate why three of the five qualify and two fail on the measurable or the time-bound criterion.
4. Select between the two Ansoff-grid alternatives — Premium Differentiation (product development in the existing segment) and Digital Platform (market development to the new segment) — given the budget envelope and the finite headcount, and articulate why the choice is mutually exclusive at this budget level and why picking both is the most common first-pass failure.
5. Commit the COP 800m three-year budget across four allocation categories (Digital, Premium Experiences, Brand, Operations), read the coherence score that rises when the allocation aligns with the chosen Ansoff alternative and falls when it contradicts, and articulate why the plan's four decisions must agree with each other — why incoherent beats average in every quadrant and why the plan is a system rather than four loosely-coupled decisions.

On the afternoon of **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, **Mateo Arboleda-Muñoz** arrived at Rutas del Pacífico's Cali headquarters in the San Fernando neighbourhood at 2:36 p.m., walked past the wall-sized relief map of the Pacific coast in the reception area, opened the PlanQueFunciona primer workbook his chief of staff had finalised at 7:20 p.m. the previous evening, and re-read the sentence the firm's founder had taped to the inside of the board-room door in 2008: *"El plan es un sistema. Las cuatro decisiones deben contar la misma historia."*

---

## The Protagonist

Arboleda-Muñoz, 36, had been Strategy Director since January 2026, had completed a Business Administration degree at the Universidad ICESI in Cali in 2011 and an MBA at INALDE Business School in 2018, had spent seven years at a Bogotá management-consulting firm on travel-and-leisure engagements, and had joined Rutas del Pacífico through a founder-to-successor conversation that had opened at a 2024 Bahía Solano family trip and closed at a February 2026 board-approved hire. His strategy instincts, the founder had noted in the hiring memo, had been shaped by three teachings: a two-year stretch inside the consulting firm's strategic-planning practice that had forced him to reject vision-statement-first deliverables in favour of decision-first deliverables; an eighteen-month secondment into a Cartagena hotel group's 2019 post-pandemic reboot that had forced him to compress a three-month planning cycle into twenty working days; and a lifelong habit of travel writing that had taught him to read tourism as a narrative product rather than as a commodity room-night. The board had assigned him the April primer exercise on three considerations: his comfort producing a first-pass rather than a fully-polished plan; his command of the four-decision coherence test; and his willingness to commit an Ansoff-grid alternative rather than hedge.

---

## The Decision

Arboleda-Muñoz had to commit, by the April 30 monthly-review deadline, a first-pass plan that met the four-decision standard.

1. **Decision 1 — SWOT classification.** Twelve candidate factors sit on the table. Eighteen years of Pacific-coast operational knowledge (Strength). No mobile application (Weakness). Mean customer age 47 (Weakness — narrowing segment). Post-pandemic domestic-tourism boom (Opportunity). Booking.com entering Colombian domestic tours (Threat). Airbnb entering (Threat). Growing 25-40 segment in direct web enquiries (Opportunity). Travel-agent dependency at 68% of bookings (Weakness). Colombian Pacific biodiversity recognition (Strength). Civil-conflict legacy in some destinations (Threat, dampening). Local community-partnership depth (Strength). Low brand recognition outside the travel-agent channel (Weakness).
2. **Decision 2 — SMART objectives.** Five candidates. *"Launch a slow-travel curated product line for the 25-40 urban-millennial segment at 14% gross margin by Q1 2027"* (SMART). *"Hold travel-agent-channel revenue at 2025 levels through 2028"* (SMART). *"Build brand awareness in Bogotá and Medellín"* (not SMART — no number, no deadline). *"Reduce customer acquisition cost 22% through direct-channel growth by Q4 2027"* (SMART). *"Become the leading Pacific-coast operator"* (not SMART — vague, no metric). Three of the five pass.
3. **Decision 3 — Ansoff alternative.** Two options. Alternative A — Premium Differentiation, product development in the existing high-income 47-year-old segment through deeper curation and slow-travel experiences. Alternative B — Digital Platform, market development to the 25-40 segment through a direct-booking app and a Bogotá-Medellín brand campaign. The budget envelope makes both mutually exclusive at full investment.
4. **Decision 4 — Budget allocation.** COP 800m three-year envelope across four categories: Digital, Premium Experiences, Brand, Operations. The coherence test reads whether the allocation matches the chosen Ansoff alternative.

Commitments: twelve SWOT factor classifications; three SMART objectives; one Ansoff alternative; four budget numbers that sum to COP 800m; and a one-page coherence statement the April 30 board can read in three minutes.

## Sectoral Background

The 2020–2025 Colombian Pacific-coast tourism category had, in the case universe, grown from approximately COP 94bn to COP 160bn in nominal revenue, concentrated in three operator tiers (international hotel chains in Tumaco and Buenaventura; regional tour operators like Rutas del Pacífico, Mar Pacífico and Nuquí Expediciones; individual guesthouse-and-lodge independents). The post-pandemic 2022–2024 domestic-tourism cycle had added approximately 28% to regional domestic bookings; the international inbound segment had recovered more slowly and remained below 2019 levels as of late 2025. The 2025 regional operator panel registered an average direct-channel share of 23%, an average net promoter score of +42, and an average customer-tenure of 2.1 years, with significant variance across operators driven primarily by brand-channel discipline rather than by destination attractiveness.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1957–2025 strategy-planning literature produced three findings the primer sim had to respect. First, Kenneth Andrews's SWOT framework, operationalised as a classification discipline rather than as a brainstorming tool, produced documented strategic-option quality readings approximately 24–41% higher than unstructured-brainstorming regimes on comparable mid-size-operator panels. Second, George Doran's SMART framework, applied to objective-setting as a filter rather than as a checklist, produced documented quarterly-review follow-through readings approximately 1.7× those of non-SMART regimes on equivalent planning cycles. Third, Igor Ansoff's growth-vector matrix, used as an alternative-selection rather than as a brainstorm-all-four discipline, produced documented portfolio-concentration readings approximately 34% higher than all-four-vectors regimes and was strongly correlated with follow-through budget integrity.

The post-2010 strategy-planning literature added two findings. The Porter activity-system-coherence reading — does the plan's allocation reinforce the chosen strategic position or contradict it — was the single strongest predictor of first-year follow-through across the 2020–2024 strategic-plan panel, with coherent-allocation plans out-performing incoherent-allocation plans by approximately 38% on year-one strategic-KR achievement. And the Mintzberg deliberate-versus-emergent reading of year-end actuals against year-start plans predicted that plans with strong coherence at Q1 retained deliberate-strategy character at year-end whereas incoherent plans emergent-drifted to ad hoc investment patterns.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The December 2025 board retreat had identified Booking.com and Airbnb entry as the firm's single largest Q3 2026 threat. The January 2026 founder-to-board succession brief had articulated that Rutas del Pacífico had run for eighteen years without a formal strategic plan and that the Booking.com-and-Airbnb announcement was the forcing function that demanded one. The April 16 board directive: *"Un primer plan en veinte minutos. Cuatro decisiones. Coherentes."*

### What Arboleda-Muñoz knew

The twelve candidate SWOT factors; the five candidate SMART objectives and which three met the Doran test; the two Ansoff alternatives and their respective budget profiles; the COP 800m envelope; the coherence test; and the board's appetite for a first-pass rather than a fully-polished deliverable.

### What he did not know

Whether the millennial-adjacent 25-40 segment would convert direct-web enquiries into bookings at rates comparable to the existing 47-mean segment; whether Booking.com would price Colombian Pacific tours aggressively enough to force a response before Q4 2026; whether the Bahía Solano and Nuquí community partnerships could scale into the volume a Digital Platform alternative would require; whether the firm's existing sales team could run both a premium-differentiation product line and a digital-platform channel during Q3–Q4 2026; and whether the board would commit to the Ansoff alternative or soften it into a hedge.

### What others were saying

**The Board Chair (founder)**: *"Elige una alternativa. No dos. Si eliges las dos, el presupuesto pierde."*

**The Head of Sales (18 years with the firm)**: *"Los agentes de viaje son el 68% de las reservas. No los abandonen por la app."*

**The Head of Product**: *"El slow-travel curado es donde tenemos dieciocho años de ventaja. Que el presupuesto lo refleje."*

**The Head of Digital (recently hired)**: *"La app es donde Booking y Airbnb atacan. Si no entramos ahí, el segmento 25-40 no nos conoce."*

**The CFO (fractional)**: *"El sobre de 800m tres años son 267m al año. El reparto parejo lo desperdicia."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Arboleda-Muñoz had narrowed the plan space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — Alternative A (Premium Differentiation) + budget coherent with A (Recommended).** SWOT correctly classified; three SMART objectives chosen; Ansoff A selected; budget allocated as Digital COP 120m / Premium Experiences COP 320m / Brand COP 280m / Operations COP 80m. The budget reinforces the premium-differentiation positioning; the coherence score is high; the Booking.com-and-Airbnb threat is addressed indirectly through a segment the platforms underserve rather than directly through a competing platform.
- **Option B — Alternative B (Digital Platform) + budget coherent with B.** Digital COP 360m / Premium Experiences COP 120m / Brand COP 240m / Operations COP 80m. Defensible but higher execution risk given the fifty-day runway to Q3 and the existing team's minimal app-development capability. The coherence score is high if the team can hit the Q3 launch; drops sharply if the launch slips past Q1 2027.
- **Option C — Hedge: split budget evenly COP 200m × 4.** Rejected — no category crosses the minimum threshold for competitive advantage; the plan is responsible-looking and delivers nothing; the coherence score collapses and the year-one strategic-KR achievement lands below the panel median.

Arboleda-Muñoz's private view was that Option A was the right commitment for the first-pass plan because the firm's eighteen-year Pacific-coast curation depth was the VRIO-valuable-and-rare capability that Booking.com and Airbnb could not replicate in twelve months, because the CMO-less digital-platform build in Option B would consume management attention through Q4 2026, and because the single decision most correlated with the year-one follow-through outcome was the budget-alternative coherence rather than the Ansoff choice itself. The *Plan que Funciona* primer sits at the undergraduate strategic-planning tier in the 2026 Express catalogue, distinct from StratCraft War Game (sim 282, MBA-grade Rumelt-kernel and competitive-war-game plan build) and ExecutionGap Strategy-to-Ops (sim 283, OKR cascade across four departments). Primer 294 emphasises the first-pass four-decision coherence for the undergraduate and primer-level student; 282 emphasises the Rumelt kernel and war-game attack-and-revise; 283 emphasises the execution cascade.
