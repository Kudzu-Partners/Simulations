## Abstract

Metalúrgica Andes S.A., an Andean automotive-parts manufacturer with USD 28M in 2025 revenue and 180 employees, received a letter on March 4, 2026, from its largest customer — a German Tier-1 supplier that represented 38% of Metalúrgica Andes' revenue — requiring that lead time on the critical structural-bracket family drop from 18 days to 5 within twelve months. At the letter's opening, the plant operated at an Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE) of 48%, work-in-progress (WIP) of 12 days, a defect rate of 4,200 parts-per-million (PPM), and a supervisor buy-in score of 35 out of 100. Andrés Salcedo, the Plant Director, had six rounds of the Toyota Production System (TPS) to deploy — value-stream mapping, 5S, kanban, SMED, cellular flow and poka-yoke, and management-system redesign — against a USD 2.4M deployment budget and the known Valley of Despair (an OEE dip during Round 3) that would test every decision he made in Round 1. The case opens on April 19, 2026, as Salcedo prepares the Round-1 package for the April 27 operations committee.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Apply the value-stream-mapping (VSM) framework to scope a lean deployment in a multi-product plant under a customer deadline.
2. Evaluate the trade-off between an aggressive future-state target (fast customer recovery) and a moderate target (higher probability of sustained improvement).
3. Analyze the interaction of the management-system decision (utilization metric versus OEE/lead-time/defect metric) with lean-tool deployment.
4. Diagnose the Valley of Despair risk and design Round-1 communication commitments that sustain the program through the Round-3 dip.
5. Recommend a Round-1 package and articulate the two specific supervisor behaviors that would signal a re-tune in Round 2.

Budget USD 2.4M.* Below those numbers, in the Controller's handwriting: *"Cliente alemán: five days or the contract walks in April 2027."*

## The Protagonist

Andrés Salcedo, 48, had been Plant Director of Metalúrgica Andes since 2021. A mechanical engineer from the Universidad de Chile and a certified Lean Six Sigma Black Belt with fifteen years at a Japanese automotive assembly plant in Chile, he had led three lean deployments in his career: two that reached sustained improvement and one that reverted eighteen months after the consultants left. He had joined Metalúrgica Andes with an explicit mandate — renegotiated with the family-owned board — to treat the Toyota Production System as a management system, not as a tool kit, and he had, in his first two years, built a small internal lean function of four people who had coached (but not executed) the first lean pilots in 2024.

---

## The Decision

Salcedo had to decide, by 9:00 a.m. on April 27, the Round-1 package: four decisions the simulation would treat as Round 1 inputs.

1. **VSM Scope.** Three options: a *single product family* (structural brackets — 58% of volume, 38% of revenue), *two product families* (brackets plus hinges), or the *whole plant* (six product families). Narrower scope delivered faster early results; wider scope required more consultant time and risked Round-1 overreach.
2. **Waste-Walk Format.** Three options: *engineering-led* (two industrial engineers walking the floor), *cross-functional team walk* (eight-person team from engineering, quality, production, maintenance, planning, and supervision), or *shop-floor-led* (supervisors leading the walk with operators participating). The cross-functional team walk carried the highest waste-identification yield; the shop-floor-led walk carried the highest ownership yield.
3. **Future-State Target.** Three options: *conservative* (lead time 10 days), *moderate* (lead time 7 days), or *aggressive* (lead time 5 days). The customer's target was 5 days at Round 6; the question was whether to commit to 5 days in Round 1 or to stage the target.
4. **Workforce Communication Posture.** Three options: *full all-hands with Q&A* (140 people in the plant cafeteria, Salcedo speaking, cross-functional team answering questions), *written memo plus supervisor cascade*, or *silent execution* (announce lean deployment only when Round 2 visible actions begin). The 2020–2025 empirical base rate for lean programs that began with an all-hands plus Q&A carried a 1.6x higher probability of surviving the Round-3 Valley of Despair.

Five subsequent rounds would follow on a pre-committed cadence: Round 2 — 5S deployment with shadow boards and a fifteen-minute daily audit routine. Round 3 — two-card kanban, pull signals, and operator simulation-based training (the Valley of Despair window). Round 4 — SMED on the bottleneck press line. Round 5 — cellular reorganization, heat-treatment in-house feasibility, poka-yoke on the five most-frequent defect modes. Round 6 — management-system redesign: tiered accountability and the retirement of the utilization metric.

## Company Background

Metalúrgica Andes had been founded in 1976 by the Salazar family as a metal-stamping shop serving agricultural-equipment producers in the Chilean Central Valley. Over five decades the company had migrated into automotive-parts manufacturing — structural brackets, hinges, mounts, and welded sub-assemblies — serving three Tier-1 customers (one German, one Japanese, one Brazilian) and two Chilean OEM assembly plants. 2025 revenue was USD 28M with an EBITDA margin of 11.8% and an operating margin of 6.4%. The plant occupied 14,000 square meters in a two-cell layout with a total of eighteen major pieces of equipment, including three presses (of which one — *Press Three* — carried 42% of monthly changeovers), a heat-treatment pre-shipment step currently outsourced to a third-party within three days truck distance, and a final inspection area.

The company employed 180 people directly: 132 production operators, 18 supervisors and production planners, 12 quality and engineering, 10 maintenance, and 8 commercial and corporate. Supervisory tenure averaged fourteen years. The production-floor culture was stable, proud, and — on the Salcedo team's repeated diagnostic — oriented around equipment utilization as the single most-watched metric. The senior production supervisor, *Jorge Martínez*, had been with the company twenty-eight years and was widely respected; he had participated in the 2024 lean pilot, had not publicly opposed it, and had — Salcedo's private assessment — not converted.

The family-owned board consisted of five seats: two Salazar family members, two independents from the Chilean manufacturing sector, and Salcedo. The board had, in February 2026, approved the USD 2.4M lean-deployment budget on a single condition — that Salcedo present, at the April 27 operations committee, a six-round plan with a pre-committed Round-3 decision for the Valley of Despair.

## Industry and Market Context

The Latin American automotive-parts sector entered 2026 under four simultaneous pressures. First, Tier-1 customers had, post-2022 supply-chain disruption, tightened lead-time requirements on strategic suppliers by an average of 38% across the 2023–2025 contract-renewal cycle. Second, the SMED-and-kanban benchmark for plants of Metalúrgica Andes' size had shifted: the 2025 sector survey reported median lead times of 7.2 days for plants with the Andes' product complexity, against the Andes' 18 days. Third, supervisor-led lean programs in similar plants had, in the 2020–2025 cohort, sustained improvement in 68% of cases against 34% for consultant-led deployments. Fourth, the specific sector's Valley-of-Despair pattern — an OEE dip of 6–10 percentage points during the kanban-introduction phase — was, in the 2020–2025 empirical record, the single most common point at which lean programs reverted.

Empirically, plants that had, in the 2020–2025 cohort, started lean with a cross-functional team walk plus an all-hands communication event had reached Round-6 sustained OEE of 72–78% and Round-6 lead times below 6 days; plants that had skipped the communication event or that had cut VSM scope to a single product family had reached Round-6 OEE of 58–64% and lead times of 8–10 days.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The March 4 customer letter had arrived at a moment when Salcedo's internal lean diagnostic — running since late 2023 — had already identified the bracket family as the natural first VSM target, SMED on Press Three as the single highest-leverage Round-4 move, and the utilization metric as the management-system obstacle. The 2024 pilot had run on a different, smaller product family and had produced a documented but small improvement that had not materially changed plant-level KPIs. The 2026 program would be the first plant-wide deployment.

### What Salcedo knew

The internal lean office had modeled four Round-1 packages. A conservative-scope package (single family, engineering-led walk, conservative 10-day target, written memo) delivered Round-6 OEE of 62 and lead time of 8 days — customer requirement missed. A balanced package (single family, cross-functional team walk, moderate 7-day target, full all-hands) delivered Round-6 OEE of 74 and lead time of 6.2 days — customer requirement nearly met. An aggressive package (two families, cross-functional team walk, aggressive 5-day target, full all-hands) delivered Round-6 OEE of 78 and lead time of 5.0 days — at a materially higher Round-3 Valley-of-Despair risk. A safe-scope-with-aggressive-comms package (single family, shop-floor-led walk, moderate 7-day target, full all-hands) delivered Round-6 OEE of 72 and lead time of 6.5 days with the highest supervisor buy-in.

### What he did not know

Whether Jorge Martínez would, as lead floor supervisor, publicly advocate for the program in the Round-1 all-hands — or merely not oppose it; whether the customer audit scheduled for Round 5 would be performed by the Japanese or the German procurement team (the two teams scored slightly different criteria); and whether the Round-3 Valley of Despair would, on this plant's equipment base, be 6 points or 10.

### What others were saying

**Jorge Martínez**, Senior Production Supervisor, had not made a public statement but had been observed adjusting his schedule to attend the Round-1 planning meeting.

**Isabel Carrasco**, Quality Manager, argued for the **balanced package**: "Single family. Cross-functional walk. Moderate target 7 days at Round 6. The German customer's real test is the sustained lead time at Round 9, not the advertised one at Round 6. Aim to sustain."

**Matías González**, Production Manager, argued for the **aggressive package**: "Commit to 5 days at Round 6. The customer letter is not a negotiation. We will reach the number. The question is whether we will teach the plant to sustain it."

**Consultancy partner**, external lean advisor, argued for the **safe-scope-with-aggressive-comms**: "Start narrow. Start loud. The first 48 hours of a lean program decide the first 24 months."

---

## Options Under Consideration

Salcedo had narrowed the space to three concrete Round-1 packages.

- **Option A — Balanced Deployment (the Carrasco plan):** Single product family (brackets), cross-functional team walk, moderate 7-day target, full all-hands with Q&A. Round-6 OEE 74, lead time 6.2 days. Round-3 Valley estimated at 7 points.

- **Option B — Aggressive Deployment (the González plan):** Two families (brackets + hinges), cross-functional team walk, aggressive 5-day target, full all-hands. Round-6 OEE 78, lead time 5.0 days. Round-3 Valley risk elevated to 9–10 points.

- **Option C — Safe-Scope / Aggressive-Comms (the consultancy plan):** Single family, shop-floor-led walk led by a named supervisor, moderate 7-day target, full all-hands with Q&A plus a two-day lean-foundations course for supervisors. Round-6 OEE 72, lead time 6.5 days. Round-3 Valley estimated at 6 points. Highest supervisor buy-in of any option.

Salcedo's own instinct, not yet shared with the executive team, was that the single most consequential decision of Round 1 was not the scope or the target but whether Jorge Martínez would lead the shop-floor-led walk — and whether that decision was made before or after the April 27 committee. He had drafted, in his notebook, the sentence with which he intended to open the all-hands: *"Lean is not a toolkit. It is a sequencing problem."*
