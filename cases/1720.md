# Bauxia Andes Resources: Six Weeks at Pampacolca

**Case Code:** SUS-2026-1720 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1720 — *The Social License — Mining, Communities and Conflict* · **v1.0 · September 2026**

---

At 06:14 on Wednesday, April 29, 2026, while Esperanza Quiroga-Tapia was in a taxi on Avenida Javier Prado, a two-page memorandum reached the secure distribution list at Bauxia Andes Resources S.A. Twenty-three Quechua and Aymara communities of the upper Colca and Castilla valleys had resolved the previous night to suspend all company transit, exploration and operating activity across communal land. The memorandum set five conditions, gave Bauxia ten working days to answer in writing, and stated that until then the federation would not meet the company without outside guarantors.

By 07:42 the chief financial officer had forwarded it with three sentences of her own. The Quebrada Yanaorco haul road carried 71% of Bauxia's 2026 bauxite tonnage to the port at Pisco; closed beyond fourteen days it would breach the cash-sweep covenant in the middle of a bondholder roadshow opened that Monday. Rodrigo Paniagua-Echenique, the chief executive, gave Quiroga-Tapia, his Director of Community Affairs, until Friday, May 1, to recommend how Bauxia would answer the five conditions.

## The Protagonist

Quiroga-Tapia, 41, was a sociologist with a master's in environmental governance and eleven years in extractives: Hatunpampa, then a mid-tier copper producer, and for the last eighteen months Bauxia. She was the fourth person in seven years to hold the post, and the first to report to the chief executive rather than through the legal department. Since November she had written three memoranda to the executive committee: on the conduct of the 2019 consultation at Pampacolca, on the divergent readings the company and the comunidad's leadership gave the agreement, and on the prospect of escalation to federation level. All three were minuted.

## The Decision

Quiroga-Tapia had six weeks and three moments at which something had to be committed in writing: Friday's diagnosis of who was on the other side and what of their demands was negotiable; a consultation designed, staffed and paid for inside the window, with terms for a renegotiated framework agreement; and, on June 9, a memorandum to the board's sustainability committee on what had been settled and what the company would change so that its other three community-affected sites did not arrive at the same place.

## The Company and its Setting

Bauxia Andes Resources S.A., a Peruvian-Chilean bauxite and aluminium group headquartered in Lima, recorded revenue of USD 2.1 billion in 2025 with roughly 3,400 employees (see **Exhibit 1**). It carried USD 780 million of senior secured debt facing a refinancing window in 2027, on a facility with an environmental-and-social clause in which a development-finance institution held 12%.

The Comunidad Campesina de Pampacolca — about 4,200 people in the highlands of Castilla province, Arequipa — held title to the land the haul road crossed. Its asamblea had voted 874 in favour, 121 against, with 67 abstentions, to block the road at kilometre 47 of the Aplao–Andagua highway. The federation of 23 communities ratified the vote the following night and issued the memorandum.

## The Situation

Four of the five conditions reached back to one event (see **Exhibit 2**). The 2019 consultation had been held after the environmental impact assessment was already approved, on a 142-page Spanish-language document circulated three working days before the asamblea. The Convenio Marco signed at the end of it bore the signatures of three community representatives whose authority the federation now contested, and fixed the community's share at PEN 1.8 million a year, plus a development fund Bauxia released project by project.

Since 2024 the federation's technical team had recorded elevated turbidity and dissolved aluminium at the Yanaorco spring; Bauxia's monitoring, on other protocols at other points, recorded lower levels. Neither set had been examined by anyone both parties accepted.

Her department reported four measures monthly to the chief executive. A social-licence index scored 0 to 100 stood at 22, in the band labelled "Withdrawal". Community trust stood at 18, a board-confidence measure kept by the corporate secretary at 50, and covenant headroom — blockade days the balance sheet could absorb — at 14.

Augusto Marchena-Ríos, VP Operations, held that the Convenio Marco was a signed contract the company had paid under for six years, and that reopening it under a blockade would bring the same demand at the other three sites; he had draft pleadings ready. Javier Loayza, the bishop of Aplao, had offered in February to convene talks.

If nothing changed, the blockade held and the covenant breached past the fourteenth day, with the roadshow still running.

## What Quiroga-Tapia Controlled

The first week settled three things. How the company would engage: run the consultation again, convened by the federation and settled by a vote of the asamblea; the same process with the regional ombudsman, the bishop and the lender's standards specialist seated as guarantors; a bilateral table with Pampacolca's elected leadership alone, the title being the comunidad's; or a separate approach to the minority that had voted against a blockade the asamblea had not carried unanimously. What she told Marchena-Ríos, who wanted that separate approach authorised: refuse in writing, citing the representativeness requirement of the ILO convention and the lender's indigenous-peoples standard; authorise it and run it as a parallel channel; or leave him without an answer. And what the company said publicly: no comment until the diagnosis was done; a release setting out the company's record at Pampacolca and its right of transit; or a statement acknowledging that the federation spoke for the communities.

The middle weeks belonged to the consultation and the money. She would fix the information protocol: a bilingual technical package with 21 days and a reviewer chosen by the federation; the 142-page Spanish assessment with a three-day reading window; or that document with a 28-page Quechua summary and seven days. She would decide what happened at the Yanaorco face during the consultation: a cut to half rate as a partial signal; full suspension, verified monthly by the ombudsman's office, at some USD 4.2 million of second-quarter EBITDA; or full production through the window. She would name a figure for the canon comunal: 2.0% of gross with a floor of PEN 4.2 million; 2.5% with a floor of PEN 4.5 million, the federation's figure; or 1.5% with a floor of PEN 3.5 million, the figure in the plan. And she would dispose of the legal track: back the injunction and let operations carry the file; decline it and record no reason; or decline it in writing to the chair of the sustainability committee.

The last week was the memorandum. She would propose an annual figure for the systems the company lacked: USD 2.4 million for six measures, among them a grievance mechanism and dual sign-off on community land; USD 3.6 million for those six and a rotating roster of external auditors; nothing, the renegotiated Convenio being itself the system; or USD 1.2 million for a monitoring board on its own. She would say whether her reporting line stayed an informal term of her own contract or became a governance-code provision, direct to the chief executive with a dotted line to the sustainability committee. She would choose how to answer a paper Marchena-Ríos might put before a different board committee: a point-by-point rebuttal circulated internally; a request to the committee's chair to rule on which committee owned the question; or that request with an appendix benchmarking peer operations in the region. And she would decide whether the 2026 sustainability report described the industry council's principles, the lender's performance standards and the United Nations guiding principles as voluntary guidance applied at the company's discretion, or as contractual commitments whose breach was a breach of the Convenio.

## Closing

At 08:55 Quiroga-Tapia took the lift to the eighteenth floor, the memorandum printed and the four measures written on the back. Marchena-Ríos was at the window, on a call to Aplao. The federation's ten working days and the fourteen days of covenant headroom ran out on the same Wednesday in May; the first answers were due on Friday.
