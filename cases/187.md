## Abstract

VidaPlena Servicios was, in the case universe, a Bogotá-headquartered LatAm senior-living group operating across Colombia, Mexico, Peru and Ecuador with USD 380M in 2025 revenue, a 2025 EBITDA margin of 11% against a LatAm-senior-living peer-benchmark of 14%, a 2025 Care-Quality Index reading of 68 on a composite scale against a peer-benchmark of 72 (composite captures clinical-outcome, family-satisfaction and staffing-ratio readings), a 2025 Mission-Alignment reading of 72 against a peer-benchmark of 64, a 2025 portfolio spanning 22 facilities across five operating personas — Premium Independents, Assisted Living, Memory Care, Home-Based and Affordable — and a 2024 Q4 board-approved three-city expansion target across Medellín, Quito and Lima. The Q4 2025 board had approved a USD 54M five-round longevity-strategy envelope — the SilverWave programme — and had positioned it as the group's response to three overlapping pressures: a Q1 2026 Colombian Ministry-of-Health minimum-care-standard amendment that had raised the staffing-ratio floor and had triggered an estimated USD 4.2M compliance cost, a Q4 2025 competitor-diagnostic showing two regional operators closing Memory-Care share on a lower-margin but higher-Mission-Alignment posture, and a Q1 2026 demographic-forecast refresh that had repriced the 2030 seventy-five-plus population across the four-country footprint at +38% versus the 2022 forecast. The Q1 2026 board had reaffirmed the USD 54M envelope and a Chief-Executive-Officer-led five-round commitment cycle against a Care-Quality floor above 72, a Mission-Alignment floor above 68, an EBITDA floor above 10%, and a Composite SilverWave Score above 70. The case opens at 6:46 a.m. on Tuesday, April 21, 2026, at the VidaPlena Bogotá head office as **Rodrigo Mendieta-Luján**, a 53-year-old Chief Executive Officer, prepares the Round-1 persona-segmentation commitment. Sixty-eight days separate him from the June 28 board strategy review.

## The Protagonist

Rodrigo Mendieta-Luján, 53, had assumed the Chief-Executive-Officer role at VidaPlena Servicios on January 1, 2023, after eighteen years across healthcare, senior-living and services leadership at two pan-LatAm healthcare groups and a Brazilian hospital network. He had, in 2020–2022, led a Memory-Care pivot at a Peruvian senior-living operator that had, across a 30-month window, lifted Care-Quality from 62 to 78 and Mission-Alignment from 58 to 76 while preserving EBITDA in a 9–12% band. The board had recruited him in October 2022 specifically on two capabilities: his documented discipline of treating Care-Quality as a gating variable rather than as an output, and his willingness to hold mission-aligned pricing against capital-partner pressure. His 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the Care-Quality floor and 20% to the Mission-Alignment floor.

## The Decision

Mendieta-Luján had to commit, by the May 12 board pre-review, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–5.

1. **Round 1 — Persona segmentation.** Three archetypes: three-persona-focus (concentrate capital across Premium Independents, Assisted Living and Memory Care, Composite 76), five-persona-spread (sustain all five personas under one brand — Composite 30, care-quality-dilution probability), or two-persona (only Assisted Living and Memory Care — Composite 54). Working envelope: three-persona-focus.
2. **Round 2 — Tiered-pricing-and-waiver policy.** Three archetypes: fifteen-percent-waiver (means-tested waiver set at 15% of beds with published ledger, Composite 76), zero-waiver (full-commercial pricing — Composite 30, Mission-Alignment collapse), or five-percent-waiver (modest waiver — Composite 56). Working envelope: fifteen-percent-waiver.
3. **Round 3 — Staffing-and-technology balance.** Three vectors: balanced-ratio (geriatric-nurse ratio above regulatory floor plus fall-detection-and-assistive-technology layer, Composite 78), tech-only (sensors substitute for staffing — Composite 28, Care-Quality collapse), or staffing-only (no technology layer — Composite 56). Working envelope: balanced-ratio.
4. **Round 4 — Regulatory-shock absorption.** Three vectors: invest-and-comply (absorb the USD 4.2M compliance cost as baseline spend, use the shock to document competitive differentiation, Composite 78), defer-and-appeal (delay compliance — Composite 26, facility-closure probability), or phased (compliance on three facilities first — Composite 54). Working envelope: invest-and-comply.
5. **Round 5 — Three-city expansion.** Three vectors: gated-three-city (Medellín, Quito, Lima expansion gated on Care-Quality floor above 65 at each launch, Composite 78), aggressive-five-city (add two more markets — Composite 32, over-extension probability), or two-city (defer one city — Composite 56). Working envelope: gated-three-city.

On Mendieta-Luján's working model, a Three-Persona / Fifteen-Percent / Balanced / Invest / Gated envelope delivered Revenue +34%, Care Quality 74, Mission-Alignment 74, EBITDA 11%, Composite 74.

## Company Background

VidaPlena Servicios had been founded in Bogotá in 2004 and had, across twenty-two years, built a USD 380M-revenue LatAm senior-living portfolio of 22 facilities across five operating personas and the four-country footprint. The 2020–2024 operating cycle had sustained Care-Quality at 68 against the 72 peer-benchmark and Mission-Alignment at 72 against the 64 peer-benchmark — a pattern the November 2025 internal review had read as structurally sound on the mission axis but structurally stretched on the quality axis due to the five-persona-spread posture. The Q4 2025 board-approved USD 54M SilverWave envelope had been positioned explicitly as a five-round sharpening of persona focus, pricing discipline, staffing-and-technology balance, regulatory absorption and expansion gating.

Three Q1 2026 events were named in the March 2026 board dossier. First, the January 2026 Colombian Ministry-of-Health minimum-care-standard amendment had raised the geriatric-nurse staffing-ratio floor by 18% across all residential senior-care facilities, with a June 2026 compliance horizon and an estimated USD 4.2M cumulative compliance cost across the VidaPlena Colombian footprint. Second, the November 2025 competitor-diagnostic had documented two regional operators closing Memory-Care market share at a 1.4× Mission-Alignment reading versus VidaPlena. Third, the March 2026 demographic-forecast refresh had repriced the 2030 seventy-five-plus population across the four-country footprint at +38% versus the 2022 forecast, tightening the windows across which expansion-site economics cleared the IRR hurdle.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2020–2025 LatAm senior-living literature had, across 24 documented operators, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, operators on three-persona-focus postures had documented Care-Quality readings 2.1× more durable than operators on five-persona-spread postures. Second, operators that had codified means-tested waiver policies at 10–20% of beds had preserved Mission-Alignment readings above 68 in 76% of cases against 24% for zero-waiver operators, with long-horizon-demand readings materially stronger in the waiver cohort. Third, operators that had absorbed regulatory-floor shocks on an invest-and-comply basis had, across the 2019–2024 LatAm regulatory cycle, avoided facility-closure events in 82% of cases against 28% for defer-and-appeal responses.

Empirically, the post-2020 longevity-economy literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, balanced staffing-and-technology postures had sustained Care-Quality readings above 72 in 74% of cases against 22% for tech-only postures that had substituted sensors for staffing. Second, expansion programmes that had gated each launch on a Care-Quality floor at the receiving facility had preserved brand-trust readings in 78% of cases against 24% for ungated expansions.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the December 2025 session, approved the USD 54M envelope and the June 28 review calendar. The Chief Operating Officer had, on March 3, delivered the persona-economics diagnostic. The Chief Medical Officer had, on March 10, delivered the Care-Quality pack. The Chief Financial Officer had, on March 17, delivered the regulatory-shock model. The Chief Strategy Officer had, on March 24, delivered the three-city expansion brief.

### What Mendieta-Luján knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. A Three-Persona / Fifteen-Percent / Balanced / Invest / Gated envelope projected Composite 74. A Five-Persona / Zero-Waiver / Tech-Only / Defer / Aggressive envelope projected Composite 24. A Hybrid envelope projected Composite 48.

### What he did not know

Whether the Ministry-of-Health amendment would generalise to Mexico inside the envelope window; whether the fifteen-percent-waiver policy would clear a capital-partner posture shift at the Q3 distribution review; whether the Memory-Care persona would absorb the competitive posture from the two closing competitors without a price-and-mission spiral; and whether the Lima market-entry would clear the Care-Quality-gate at the target launch date.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Medical Officer**: *"Balanced staffing-and-technology. Sensors never replace a nurse. They extend one."*

**The Chief Operating Officer**: *"Three-persona focus. Five personas dilutes the training, the rotation and the culture."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"Invest-and-comply. The defer-and-appeal path closes facilities."*

**The Board Chair**: *"Longevity rewards operators, not optimisers. Pay the EBITDA give-back."*

## Options Under Consideration

Mendieta-Luján had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Three-Persona / Fifteen-Percent / Balanced / Invest / Gated:** the recommended envelope. Revenue +34%. Care Quality 74. Mission-Alignment 74. EBITDA 11%. Composite 74. Recommended.
- **Option B — Five-Persona / Zero-Waiver / Tech-Only / Defer / Aggressive:** the margin-maximising envelope. Composite 24. Rejected — Care-Quality and facility-closure probability above 70%.
- **Option C — Hybrid / Phased / Phased / Phased / Phased:** the partial envelope. Composite 48. Rejected — three-persona architecture fragments under partial gating.

Mendieta-Luján's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 3 — the staffing-and-technology balance — because it was the round that anchored Care-Quality against the regulatory-floor shock of Round 4 and the expansion-launches of Round 5.
