# Dom & Styl: The Board's Return Mandate

**Case Code:** MKT-2026-1963 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1963 — *Digital Pivot* · **v1.0 · August 2026**

---

On the afternoon of 9 March 2026, Marta Zielińska left the fourth-quarter board review of Dom & Styl S.A. with two numbers written on the back of her agenda: 11 and 20. The first was the share of the Warsaw home-and-lifestyle group's revenue that came through its own website. The second was where the board had just instructed her to put it within four quarters, on a marketing budget frozen at PLN 64 million.

The instruction carried a name. Bartosz Rejman, appointed Chair that quarter after fifteen years as an operating partner in private equity, had reduced it to a sentence: prove that marketing spending earns its return, or the board would cut the budget by 20% and hand channel decisions to an outside agency. He had also set an interim gate. If digital revenue share stood below 16% at the half-year review on 30 September 2026, the cut and the handover would trigger then, not at year end.

## The Protagonist

Zielińska, 44, had joined Dom & Styl in 2014 from a Warsaw media agency. She ran brand and trade marketing for six years. The previous board promoted her to chief marketing officer in 2022 on a three-word mandate: protect the brand. The 71% unaided recall the company reported among Polish homeowners was in part a record of her own campaigns. Her variable pay had followed brand health and revenue growth until January 2026, when Rejman's board rewrote it around margin marketing could attribute to itself. Two of her four direct reports had built their careers in television and catalogue. The other two had been hired since 2023 to build the online business.

## The Decision

Zielińska had to decide, quarter by quarter, how the frozen PLN 64 million would be divided. Part of that money made customers want Dom & Styl; part of it converted them. She also had to place a separate PLN 12 million the board had ring-fenced for the transformation, and account for both at the year-end review. The first commitment was due before the marketing committee met in April.

She could not ask for more money. Rejman had told the board that a marketing function unable to fund its own priorities out of PLN 64 million had not yet earned an argument about the size of the number.

## The Company and its Setting

Dom & Styl S.A. was founded in Warsaw in 1994. It sold furniture, décor and small appliances through 38 owned stores across Poland and a wholesale network of 240 independent retailers. It employed 2,600 people and had generated PLN 920 million of revenue in FY2025 at a 9% operating margin (see **Exhibit 1**).

The category was moving underneath it. Online accounted for 18% of national sales in the category and was growing 22% a year. Dom & Styl's own e-commerce sat at 11% of revenue. Its platform converted 1.4% of visits into orders, against a category benchmark of 2.6%. The average online order was PLN 410; in a store it was PLN 690. The chief executive had summarised the position in three words that Rejman repeated back to her: famous but not found.

## The Situation

Of the PLN 64 million, 62% flowed to television, catalogue and trade promotion. Marketing could not say what any of it returned. The board read that 62% as a line item it could cut without knowing what it had cut.

The traceable numbers were not comfortable either. Blended customer acquisition cost — total acquisition spending divided by the new customers it won — stood at PLN 240, up 34% in two years. At the traffic the site already carried, each additional tenth of a percentage point of conversion was worth roughly PLN 7.8 million of annual online revenue. Replacing the platform had been quoted at PLN 9–14 million against a six-to-nine-month build.

Inside the department the argument had hardened before Zielińska could frame it. Ewa Sadowska, who ran brand and trade marketing, held that the 71% recall was what made acquisition affordable. Spending it down to buy quarterly orders, she said, was selling an asset to fund a bonus. Piotr Lenart, head of digital and e-commerce, held that a brand nobody could buy from was a museum. Two senior brand managers had told Sadowska they would resign if performance marketing was allowed to eat the brand.

Rejman had said he would judge the year on five numbers and would ask which decision moved each one. Digital revenue share was the mandate itself. Blended acquisition cost was what a customer cost to win. Online conversion was the proportion of visits that ended in an order. Unaided awareness was the proportion of Polish homeowners who named Dom & Styl unprompted. The fifth he called transformation return: the incremental margin the pivot generated, set against the money it consumed.

Nothing in the current plan moved any of them. On the existing mix and the existing platform, the category would go on adding online share at 22% a year while Dom & Styl's own 11% held. Acquisition cost would keep climbing at the rate of the past two years. And 30 September would arrive with the interim gate unmet.

## What Zielińska Controlled

In the first quarter she had to commit a diagnosis. She could read the budget three ways: as measurable in substance, since television and catalogue drove online searches the company could not tag; as 62% unattributable and therefore the board's to cut; or as two kinds of spending, one traceable to orders and one that built demand harvested later. She had to name where the largest leak in the purchase funnel sat — at awareness, at the top of the funnel where traffic was bought, or between consideration and purchase online. She had to name the one metric the team would manage: total spending against the freeze, unaided awareness, or transformation return and digital share. And she had to set what proportion of the pivot marketing owned outright, against the portion run by information technology and the agency, anywhere from none to all.

In the second quarter she had to move money inside the frozen envelope, and four commitments went with it. The share of the television, catalogue and trade budget redeployed into digital, from none to all of it. Whether brand investment had a floor beneath it — none, a token amount of television retained, or a floor of roughly 40% of the budget. Where the redeployed money went first: into the cheapest clicks available through discount-led paid search, into more television and catalogue, or into a mix of paid search, social, email and marketplace weighted toward buyers already in market. And a conversion target between the present 1.4% and 3.0%.

In the third quarter she had to place the PLN 12 million against four quoted investments that together cost more than the envelope (see **Exhibit 2**). She also had to say what she would sequence first if she could not fund all four: the Allegro launch while the onboarding discount was open, a portion of each of the four, or the replatform and the attribution work ahead of the rest.

In the fourth she had to face the board. The account she gave of the year: the headline number, the constraints the company had worked against, or a decision-by-decision reconstruction of what moved each metric. The measure she had managed alongside acquisition cost: acquisition cost alone, digital share alone, or order value and repeat purchase. How the brand-and-digital conflict had been handled: settled in favour of performance, referred to human resources, or addressed by putting both teams on one profit-and-loss and one metric. And a conviction, from 0 to 100%, attached to her second-year budget request.

## Closing

Zielińska pinned the four-quarter calendar above her desk that evening and marked 30 September in red. It was 205 days out. She had PLN 64 million she could not add to, PLN 12 million she could spend once, and 71% recall she could spend down. Rejman had said he would not accept assertion in place of proof.
