## Abstract

METRO Land 14 was a 0.7-hectare transit-oriented parcel in Colina Norte, the secondary LATAM capital in which Valverde & Partners had developed three prior mixed-use buildings. On March 26, 2026, the landowner — a Colina-Norte family office — opened a best-and-final round with a deadline of Friday, April 3. Four bidders had signalled intent, with pre-qualifying bids ranging from USD 32M to USD 41M. Valverde's internal model priced the land at USD 34M–USD 38M at a blended hold-and-sell strategy, and required a 25% levered IRR and a 2.0× equity multiple across a thirty-six-month hold from closing to core-fund sale. The capital stack was pre-identified — a 65% loan-to-cost construction loan at SOFR plus 350 basis points, and a 35% equity check with a JV institutional partner at a 20% preferred return. Daniel Valverde, Managing Partner, opened the case at 6:20 a.m. on Monday, March 30, 2026, as he prepared the Round-1 bid-and-design package. Four rounds would unfold: land bid and entitlement, capital stack, lease-up, and exit.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the three land-bid prices (USD 34M, USD 36M, USD 38M) on project-level IRR sensitivity under the two most plausible downside scenarios.
2. Analyze the residential-allocation trade-off (55%, 70%, 85%) against market absorption, zoning constraints, and exit-cap-rate sensitivity.
3. Diagnose the community-engagement decision as an entitlement-risk decision and explain how Minimal engagement triggers a 90-day environmental review.
4. Design a capital-stack package (LTC, interest reserve, rate hedge, contract type) that survives a 50-basis-point Fed surprise inside the construction window.
5. Recommend a complete Round-1 package and identify the single Round-3 tenant-default event most likely to require a vacancy-mitigation re-tune.

## The Protagonist

Daniel Valverde, 44, was Managing Partner and Head of Development at Valverde & Partners, a Colina-Norte-based real-estate developer he had co-founded in 2014 with his mother, an architect by training, and two former colleagues from a regional bank's real-estate credit desk. The firm had delivered three mixed-use buildings between 2017 and 2024 — one 14,000 m² apartment-plus-retail building in San Felipe, one 9,800 m² mid-market residential building in the Puerto district, and one 22,000 m² Class A office-plus-retail in the Centro financial zone — producing a composite levered IRR of 26% and an equity multiple of 2.1× across the three projects. The firm had six employees in Colina Norte, one structural-engineering lead, and a roster of six subcontracted project managers the firm engaged deal-by-deal. METRO would be the largest single project in the firm's eleven-year history.

## The Decision

Valverde had to commit, by 5:00 p.m. on Friday, April 3, the Round-1 bid-and-design package:

1. **Land Bid Price** — USD 34M, USD 36M, or USD 38M.
2. **Residential Allocation** — 55% (retail-heavy, higher ground-floor NOI), 70% (zoning-favored and master-plan-aligned), or 85% (residential-heavy, lower retail exposure).
3. **Feasibility Study Depth** — Light (single external consultant, USD 140K), Standard (two consultants plus market study, USD 340K), or Deep (three consultants, environmental pre-review, traffic study, USD 620K).
4. **Community Engagement** — Minimal (public-hearing attendance only, USD 90K), Standard (three neighborhood-association meetings plus one open house, USD 240K), or Deep (six-month engagement program with an appointed community liaison, USD 520K).
5. **Architect** — Starchitect (signature firm, USD 3.8M design fee, schedule risk +4 months), Boutique (local-regional firm with two prior mixed-use references, USD 2.1M, schedule risk +1 month), or Local (Colina-Norte firm with three prior buildings on the same block, USD 1.2M, schedule risk flat).

The five sub-decisions would interact and would compound across four rounds. A higher bid price raised the acquisition hurdle but avoided the 60% probability of losing the parcel to the best-resourced competing bidder; residential allocation drove the blended absorption schedule and the exit-cap-rate; feasibility depth reduced the execution-surprise probability; community engagement was the entitlement-risk decision that the sim had embedded a Round-1 event around.

## Company Background

Valverde & Partners was a Colina-Norte-incorporated, LLP-structured development firm with 2025 firm-level revenue of USD 3.4M and development-phase assets under management of USD 38M as of March 2026 (the 22,000 m² office building mid-construction). The firm's track record had been built on a single underwriting principle: Valverde did not bid on land without a capital-stack and an exit-buyer shortlist in the file. METRO was the first parcel on which he would deploy a JV institutional equity partner rather than a family-office LP syndicate.

The JV institutional partner, a regional pension-fund separate-account vehicle, was prepared to commit USD 25M of equity on a 20% preferred return, a 65/35 promote structure above the preferred, and full approval rights on the Round-1 design package and the Round-3 lease-up strategy. The pension fund had, in their February 24, 2026 investment-committee meeting, approved the commitment subject to the Round-1 package landing within the residential-allocation band and within the feasibility-depth floor.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM secondary-capital mixed-use market in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Round-1 decision sat inside. First, the 2023–2025 development-yield compression had bottomed at 6.4%–7.1% on stabilized NOI in the Colina Norte corridor, with exit-cap-rates for Class A mixed-use at 5.0%–5.5%; the 50-basis-point range between low-end and high-end exit-cap rates was, on the METRO underwriting, a USD 7M swing on gross-sale price. Second, the entitlement-risk literature had converged on a clear finding: transit-oriented parcels with Minimal community engagement faced a 38% probability of a triggered environmental review or a public-hearing delay of sixty days or more; parcels with Deep engagement faced 8%. Third, the construction-cost escalation profile in 2024–2025 had produced 6.2% average annual hard-cost inflation in the Colina Norte market, with GMP-with-contingency contracts closing schedule overruns at 14% of projects and cost overruns at 9% of projects; hard-bid contracts closing schedule overruns at 26% and cost overruns at 18%.

Empirically, the lease-up research had two robust findings. First, ground-floor retail in the 800 m²–2,400 m² range in Colina Norte produced, at stabilized occupancy, 31% higher NOI-per-square-meter than residential floors, but absorbed 28% slower; F&B anchor tenants had, across a 2020–2024 sample, defaulted inside the first twelve months at 9.4% of projects. Second, residential-lease absorption at the 55-to-70% allocation band had tracked Colina-Norte baseline absorption of approximately 4.2% of total units per month; the 85% allocation — which would deliver simultaneously against two competing developments on the same corridor — was projected to absorb at 3.1% per month.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The landowner had retained an advisor in November 2025 and run a structured process across five bidder groups. Valverde had been one of three short-listed bidders through the January 2026 financial-qualification round. The best-and-final had opened on March 24 with a one-page term sheet. The pension fund had approved the equity commitment on February 24 and had, on March 21, signalled that they would execute on any Round-1 package inside the envelope.

### What Valverde knew

The internal team had modeled three Round-1 packages. A Low-Cost Package (USD 34M bid, 85% residential, Light feasibility, Minimal engagement, Local architect) projected USD 640K environmental-review carrying cost (from triggered review), project IRR 19.4%, equity multiple 1.7×, does not win the bid in 55% of simulations. A Balanced Package (USD 36M bid, 70% residential, Standard feasibility, Standard engagement, Boutique architect) projected project IRR 26.1%, equity multiple 2.1× at base case, wins the bid in 72% of simulations, no environmental review triggered. A Premium Package (USD 38M bid, 70% residential, Deep feasibility, Deep engagement, Boutique architect) projected project IRR 24.8%, equity multiple 2.0×, wins the bid in 94% of simulations, no environmental review triggered, highest first-year carrying cost.

### What he did not know

Whether the landowner's advisor would, in a late-round telephone call, signal that the bid-ceiling was closer to USD 37M than USD 38M; whether the April 7 Federal Reserve meeting — which the fixed-income desk of the pension fund was pricing at a 40% probability of a surprise 50-basis-point increase — would land inside the construction window and reprice the un-hedged portion of the construction loan; whether the Round-3 F&B anchor-tenant selection (which Valverde had provisionally routed through an international-fast-casual chain) would default inside the first twelve months as the 9.4% sector base rate implied; and whether the eventual exit-cap-rate at Round 4 would land at 5.25% (base), 5.5% (downside), or 5.0% (upside).

### What others were saying

**Isabel Montes**, Chief Financial Officer: *"Thirty-six million. Deep feasibility. Standard engagement. Boutique architect. The Premium Package protects the bid but compresses the IRR by one-point-three points on the downside case. Standard engagement is the minimum the pension-fund term-sheet will accept without a follow-up."*

**The JV institutional partner**, LP representative: *"Standard engagement is the floor. Minimal engagement is a bid-termination condition for our commitment. We saw that movie in 2019. It cost the last developer eighteen months and the exit spread."*

**Andrés Kreutzer**, Chief Construction Officer (contracted): *"GMP with three percent contingency, eighteen-month interest reserve, SOFR cap at six. If the Fed surprises by fifty basis points in April, the cap pays for itself inside the first quarter."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Valverde had narrowed the space to three bid-and-design packages.

- **Option A — Low-Cost Runway:** USD 34M bid, 85% residential, Light feasibility, Minimal engagement, Local architect. Wins-the-bid 45%. Community-opposition-triggered review 38%. IRR 19.4%. Equity multiple 1.7×. Does not clear the pension-fund floor.

- **Option B — Balanced Block Operator:** USD 36M bid, 70% residential, Standard feasibility, Standard engagement, Boutique architect. Wins-the-bid 72%. IRR 26.1% base, 21.3% downside. Equity multiple 2.1× base, 1.9× downside. Clears the pension-fund envelope.

- **Option C — Premium Entitlement Protection:** USD 38M bid, 70% residential, Deep feasibility, Deep engagement, Boutique architect. Wins-the-bid 94%. IRR 24.8% base. Equity multiple 2.0× base. Thick margin on the entitlement risk; thin margin on the bid ceiling.

Valverde's private view, not yet shared with Isabel Montes, was that Option B was the right Round-1 package and that the marginal USD 280K of Standard-to-Deep engagement spending on Round-1 Option-C logic was better redeployed into the Round-3 leasing-broker co-op budget that would carry the F&B-anchor-default vacancy-mitigation plan.
