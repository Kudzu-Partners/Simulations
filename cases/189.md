## Abstract

Andina Infra Concessions was, in the case universe, a Lima-headquartered LatAm infrastructure sponsor operating through a special-purpose vehicle — the InfraVerde Highway Concession — shortlist-confirmed in December 2025 for a USD 900M, 32-year highway concession across a 310-km Andean corridor, with a 2025 sponsor-equity envelope of USD 100M, a sponsor-board IRR target of 12%, a 2025 Risk-Score reading of 58 on a composite scale combining traffic, construction, currency and political-risk sub-scores, a 2024 track record of five closed LatAm infrastructure PPPs with cumulative Debt-Service-Coverage-Ratio readings inside a 1.10–1.45 band across the 2016–2024 window, and a 2025 Capital-Markets-Funding posture that had secured a USD 540M senior-debt indicative term-sheet from a four-lender club and a USD 120M mezzanine indicative term-sheet from a pan-LatAm infrastructure-debt fund. The Q4 2025 concession-award tender had, after a nine-month RFQ, placed Andina Infra Concessions at the shortlist top with a 2026 financial-close calendar targeting July 2026. The Q1 2026 board had approved the USD 100M sponsor-equity envelope and a Chief-Financial-Officer-led five-phase commitment cycle against an IRR floor above 11.8%, a DSCR floor above 1.20, a Covenant-Health floor above 60, and a Composite InfraVerde Score above 72. The case opens at 6:47 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Andina Infra Concessions Lima head office as **Diego Arellano-Figueroa**, a 48-year-old Chief Financial Officer, prepares the Phase-1 capital-stack-design commitment. Sixty-seven days separate him from the June 28 board financial-close pre-review.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Phase-1 capital-stack-design commitment (balanced-tranche envelope anchored on equity, senior debt, mezzanine and government-guarantee layers at an envelope-clearing IRR-and-DSCR trade-off vs. equity-heavy envelope) and diagnose when the balanced envelope dominates the equity-heavy envelope on the IRR-DSCR frontier.
2. Analyze the Phase-2 risk-allocation-matrix commitment (principled-allocation envelope that routes traffic risk to the entity that can best predict demand, construction risk to the entity that can best build, currency risk to the hedging party and political risk to the sovereign vs. residual-to-sponsor envelope) and recommend the principled envelope on the contract-durability basis.
3. Diagnose the Phase-3 government-negotiation commitment (tariff-for-term envelope that absorbs a 6% base-tariff reduction in exchange for a concession-term extension from 25 to 32 years vs. lump-sum subsidy envelope) and recommend the tariff-for-term envelope on the IRR-restoration basis.
4. Respond to the Phase-4 unexploded-ordnance construction-crisis commitment (contract-anchored envelope that applies the Phase-2 risk-allocation matrix to a kilometre-47 unexploded-ordnance discovery with an 8-month delay and a USD 14M overrun vs. sponsor-pays envelope) and recommend the contract-anchored envelope on the allocation-discipline basis.
5. Design the Phase-5 operations-covenant-discipline commitment that converts InfraVerde from a structuring exercise into an operational covenant-compliance capability with quarterly DSCR-reporting, covenant-amber-to-green discipline and a published concession-performance dashboard.

## The Protagonist

Diego Arellano-Figueroa, 48, had assumed the Chief-Financial-Officer role at Andina Infra Concessions on July 1, 2023, after fifteen years across project finance, infrastructure-debt and sponsor-equity leadership at two LatAm development-bank programmes and a Madrid-based infrastructure-debt fund. He had, in 2018–2022, led the financial structuring of three closed LatAm toll-road concessions that had, across the 2020–2024 operating window, preserved DSCR readings inside a 1.22–1.46 band through pandemic-era traffic-shock cycles. The sponsor board had recruited him in May 2023 specifically on two capabilities: his documented discipline of principled risk-allocation rather than sponsor-residual risk-absorption, and his willingness to trade tariff for term rather than accept lump-sum subsidies that compromised the operating-period covenant package. His 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the IRR floor and 25% to the Covenant-Health floor.

## The Decision

Arellano-Figueroa had to commit, by the May 13 board pre-review, the Phase-1 envelope and the directional intent for Phases 2–5.

1. **Phase 1 — Capital-stack design.** Three archetypes: balanced-tranche (USD 100M sponsor equity / USD 540M senior debt / USD 120M mezzanine / USD 140M government guarantee envelope, DSCR 1.28 / IRR 12.1%, Composite 76), equity-heavy (USD 180M equity / USD 540M senior debt — DSCR 1.42 but IRR 8.6% — Composite 32), or debt-heavy (USD 60M equity / USD 620M senior debt — DSCR 1.12 fails the covenant — Composite 30). Working envelope: balanced-tranche.
2. **Phase 2 — Risk-allocation matrix.** Three archetypes: principled-allocation (traffic risk to private, construction risk to private, currency risk to hedging party, political risk to sovereign, Composite 78), residual-to-sponsor (all risks default to sponsor — Composite 24, contract-durability collapse), or phased (principled on construction and traffic, residual on currency and political — Composite 52). Working envelope: principled-allocation.
3. **Phase 3 — Government negotiation.** Three vectors: tariff-for-term (absorb 6% base-tariff reduction in exchange for term extension from 25 to 32 years, Composite 78), lump-sum subsidy (accept USD 180M upfront subsidy without term extension — Composite 36, operating-period covenant compression), or hold-the-line (reject counter-proposal — Composite 28, award-risk). Working envelope: tariff-for-term.
4. **Phase 4 — Unexploded-ordnance construction crisis.** Three vectors: contract-anchored (apply the Phase-2 risk-allocation matrix, route the 8-month delay and the USD 14M overrun according to the construction-risk-to-private architecture with sovereign-bearing on the ordnance-specific political risk, Composite 78), sponsor-pays (sponsor absorbs the full USD 14M — Composite 28, allocation-discipline collapse), or phased (split 50-50 — Composite 52). Working envelope: contract-anchored.
5. **Phase 5 — Operations covenant discipline.** Three vectors: integrated-architecture (quarterly DSCR reporting, covenant-amber-to-green protocol, published concession-performance dashboard, lender-committee engagement cadence, Composite 78), quarterly-only (numerical reporting without lender-committee cadence — Composite 34), or phased (reporting on years 1–3 — Composite 54). Working envelope: integrated-architecture.

On Arellano-Figueroa's working model, a Balanced / Principled / Tariff-for-Term / Contract-Anchored / Integrated envelope delivered IRR 12.1%, DSCR 1.28, Covenant-Health 68, Composite 74.

## Company Background

Andina Infra Concessions had been founded in Lima in 2014 and had, across twelve years, structured five LatAm infrastructure PPPs with USD 3.6B in cumulative invested capital across highway, port and urban-rail concessions. The 2016–2024 operating track record had produced DSCR readings inside a 1.10–1.45 band and had exited two concessions on schedule at end-of-concession-period. The Q4 2025 concession-award tender for the USD 900M, 310-km, 32-year InfraVerde highway concession had placed Andina Infra Concessions at the top of a four-sponsor shortlist, with the January 2026 concession-award confirmation triggering the Q1 2026 financial-structuring programme and the July 2026 financial-close calendar.

Three Q1 2026 events were named in the March 2026 board dossier. First, the January 2026 government counter-proposal to the Andina Infra Concessions indicative bid had proposed a 6% base-tariff reduction against the bid figure in exchange for a term extension from 25 to 32 years — a counter-proposal with an IRR-recovery profile if absorbed inside the right capital-stack design. Second, the February 2026 four-lender club indicative senior-debt term-sheet had confirmed covenant architecture with a 1.20 DSCR covenant and a 1.35 cash-sweep trigger — constraints the Phase-1 capital-stack design had to clear with margin. Third, the March 2026 construction-preparation geotechnical survey had surfaced a kilometre-47 unexploded-ordnance risk with a forecast 8-month construction delay and a USD 14M overrun if confirmed — a risk the Phase-2 risk-allocation matrix would route either to the sovereign or to the sponsor depending on the drafting.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2018–2025 LatAm infrastructure-PPP literature had, across 34 documented concessions, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, concessions closed on balanced-tranche capital stacks had preserved DSCR readings above 1.20 in 78% of cases against 28% for equity-heavy or debt-heavy closes. Second, concessions anchored on principled risk-allocation matrices had avoided sponsor-absorption of catastrophic overruns in 82% of cases against 26% for residual-to-sponsor architectures. Third, concessions that had traded tariff for term had preserved IRR readings inside a ±120-basis-point band through counter-proposal negotiations in 74% of cases against 22% for lump-sum-subsidy closes.

Empirically, the post-2018 project-finance literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, covenant-amber-to-green disciplines anchored on lender-committee engagement cadences had avoided covenant waivers in 76% of cases against 28% for quarterly-numerical-reporting-only postures. Second, published concession-performance dashboards had preserved sponsor-credibility readings through stress events in 72% of cases against 26% for concessions without published disclosure.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the December 2025 session, confirmed the USD 100M sponsor-equity envelope and the July 2026 financial-close calendar. The Head of Project Finance had, on March 5, delivered the capital-stack model. The Chief Risk Officer had, on March 12, delivered the risk-allocation matrix. The Head of Government-Affairs had, on March 19, delivered the negotiation pack. The Chief Operating Officer (Construction) had, on March 26, delivered the geotechnical-and-ordnance diagnostic.

### What Arellano-Figueroa knew

The team had modelled three five-phase envelopes. A Balanced / Principled / Tariff-for-Term / Contract-Anchored / Integrated envelope projected Composite 74. An Equity-Heavy / Residual-to-Sponsor / Lump-Sum / Sponsor-Pays / Quarterly envelope projected Composite 22. A Hybrid envelope projected Composite 44.

### What he did not know

Whether the government counter-proposal would hold through the pre-close ministerial-ratification step; whether the four-lender club would maintain the 1.20 DSCR covenant through the term-extension amendment; whether the geotechnical kilometre-47 ordnance risk would be confirmed or mitigated in the Phase-0 pre-construction survey; and whether the sovereign would accept the Phase-2 political-risk allocation on the ordnance-specific clause.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Project Finance**: *"Balanced tranche. Equity-heavy kills the IRR. Debt-heavy kills the covenant."*

**The Chief Risk Officer**: *"Principled allocation. Residual-to-sponsor is how you turn a PPP into a bilateral guarantee."*

**The Head of Government-Affairs**: *"Tariff-for-term. Lump sum compresses the operating-period covenant room."*

**The Board Chair**: *"In PPP, the risk matrix is the contract. Pay the tariff give-back."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Arellano-Figueroa had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Balanced / Principled / Tariff-for-Term / Contract-Anchored / Integrated:** the recommended envelope. IRR 12.1%. DSCR 1.28. Covenant-Health 68. Composite 74. Recommended.
- **Option B — Equity-Heavy / Residual-to-Sponsor / Lump-Sum / Sponsor-Pays / Quarterly:** the risk-concentrating envelope. Composite 22. Rejected — sponsor-absorption-and-covenant-waiver probability above 70%.
- **Option C — Hybrid / Phased / Phased / Phased / Phased:** the partial envelope. Composite 44. Rejected — risk-allocation architecture fragments under partial gating.

Arellano-Figueroa's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single phase most correlated with the final Composite was Phase 2 — the risk-allocation matrix — because every subsequent phase (the tariff-for-term negotiation, the ordnance-discovery crisis and the operations covenant) would route its economic consequences through whichever allocation architecture Phase 2 had committed.
