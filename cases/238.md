## Abstract

Northwind Industries Inc. was, in the case universe, a Chicago-headquartered industrial-technology group with USD 2.6B 2025 consolidated revenue, 6,820 employees, and a portfolio of commercial contracts that spanned simple product sales, bundled equipment-plus-service deals, multi-year software licenses, long-term construction contracts and pharma-adjacent licensing transactions inherited from a 2022 life-sciences-tools acquisition. The 2024–2025 cycle had left the controllership at a standards-adopted but application-inconsistent posture: the Q3 2025 external-audit review had left two revenue-recognition informational findings traceable to over-time-vs-point-in-time application on software licenses and to variable-consideration constraint application on volume-rebate counterparties, and the Q4 2025 talent review had flagged that the incoming-controller cohort had arrived able to recite the five-step model but unable to apply it to bundled or variable-consideration contracts without senior-manager review. The Q2 2026 context had compounded three pressures: a reformed revenue-recognition retraining workbook that centred on an eight-contract archetype portfolio; a Q3 2026 pipeline of two pharma licensing deals that would trigger Contract-8-grade three-component recognition; and an investor-relations request for a sales-or-usage-based-royalty disclosure in the 10-Q revenue footnote. The case opens at 7:50 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Northwind Industries Chicago controllership floor as **Aliyah Brennan-Castellucci**, a 43-year-old Corporate Controller, prepares the Contract-1 simple-product-sale commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Apply the IFRS 15 / ASC 606 five-step model (identify the contract, identify the performance obligations, determine the transaction price, allocate the transaction price to POs, recognise as POs are satisfied) to the Contract-1 simple-product sale that collapses into a single point-in-time recognition event at delivery of control.
2. Analyse the Contract-2 equipment-plus-warranty case (two distinct performance obligations, transaction-price allocation by stand-alone selling price, equipment recognised upfront, warranty ratably over two years) and the Contract-4 volume rebate (variable consideration via expected-value or most-likely-amount, constrained to the highly-probable-not-to-reverse envelope).
3. Diagnose the Contract-5 software-license split (right-to-use as point-in-time at code delivery, right-to-access as over-time ratable across the license period) and articulate why one word — use vs. access — rewrites the recognition timing.
4. Respond to the Contract-6 long-term-construction over-time recognition (input-method cost-to-cost percentage vs. output-method milestones) and the Contract-8 pharma licensing structure (upfront fee, milestone payments, sales-based royalties recognised under the sales-or-usage-based-royalty exception as the underlying sales happen).
5. Design the eight-contract five-step revenue-recognition archetype that couples a point-in-time opener, a multi-PO allocation, a variable-consideration constraint, a right-to-use vs. right-to-access split, an over-time method choice and a multi-component pharma structure — anchored on the principle that revenue is not a cash event but a control transfer.

Revenue is a control transfer."*

## The Protagonist

Brennan-Castellucci, 43, had assumed the Corporate-Controller role at Northwind on August 1, 2023, after seventeen years across audit, technical-accounting and controllership leadership at a Big-Four firm and a Minneapolis industrial-technology group. She had, in 2018–2022, led the IFRS 15 / ASC 606 adoption at a predecessor group and had, in 2021, co-authored with the external audit firm a practitioner memorandum titled *Five Steps, Eight Contracts* that the predecessor group had subsequently adopted as its required-reading orientation document for all incoming technical-accounting hires. She had, in 2022, delivered an internal workshop on the sales-or-usage-based-royalty exception that had become the reference document on the pharma-licensing treatment at three Midwestern industrial-pharma groups. The Northwind Chief Financial Officer had recruited her on three capabilities: her documented discipline of teaching the five-step model through eight archetype contracts rather than twenty; her willingness to apply the variable-consideration constraint conservatively; and her command of the right-to-use vs. right-to-access toggle that the 2023–2026 software-license portfolio required.

## The Decision

Brennan-Castellucci had to commit, by the 9:30 a.m. controllership-retraining session, the eight-contract revenue-recognition envelope.

1. **Contract 1 — Simple product sale.** A single envelope: point-in-time at delivery of control; five steps collapse into one recognition event. Working envelope: accept as opener.
2. **Contract 2 — Equipment plus warranty.** A single envelope: two POs, allocation by stand-alone selling price, equipment upfront, warranty ratably over two years. Working envelope: accept.
3. **Contract 4 — Volume rebate.** Two estimation options: expected value (probability-weighted) and most-likely-amount; apply the highly-probable-not-to-reverse constraint. Working envelope: expected-value on portfolio rebates, most-likely-amount on binary-outcome rebates, constraint enforced on both.
4. **Contract 5 — Software license.** Two options. Right-to-use (point-in-time at code delivery). Right-to-access (over-time ratably over license period). Working envelope: right-to-use on perpetual, right-to-access on subscription.
5. **Contract 6 — Long-term construction.** Two options. Input-method cost-to-cost percentage-complete. Output-method milestone-based. Working envelope: cost-to-cost on labour-intensive contracts, milestone on output-specified contracts.
6. **Contract 8 — Pharma licensing.** A single envelope: upfront fee at license grant; milestone payments recognised when substantive and probable; sales-based royalties under the sales-or-usage-based-royalty exception as underlying sales happen. Working envelope: accept.

On Brennan-Castellucci's working model, an Accept / Accept / Expected-Value-or-Most-Likely-with-Constraint / Right-to-Use-or-Right-to-Access / Cost-to-Cost-or-Milestone / Accept envelope delivered an eight-contract archetype portfolio that every incoming controller could apply to a representative commercial contract without senior-manager review. A counterfactual "teach the standard, skip the archetypes" envelope would preserve theoretical fluency but leave the application gap the Q3 external-audit findings had identified.

The Q2 2026 context had compounded three pressures: the reformed retraining workbook, the Q3 pharma-licensing pipeline, and the 10-Q revenue-footnote disclosure request.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2015–2025 revenue-recognition post-adoption literature had, across 58 documented retraining cycles at industrial and industrial-technology groups, produced three findings the function had to respect. First, archetype-portfolio retraining had, on twelve-week windows, reduced external-audit revenue-recognition informational findings by 58% on average versus standards-recitation retraining. Second, the variable-consideration constraint application had accounted for 28–36% of post-adoption revenue restatements in the 2020–2024 SEC-review sample, making it the single most-scrutinised application dimension. Third, the right-to-use vs. right-to-access software-license toggle had, in 72% of surveyed industrial-technology groups, been the most frequently-re-litigated application question in the 2022–2025 window.

Empirically, the 2020–2025 pharma-licensing literature had two further findings. First, the sales-or-usage-based-royalty exception had, in 84% of surveyed pharma-adjacent contracts, produced royalty-recognition timing that lagged the upfront-fee recognition by twelve to thirty-six months. Second, milestone-payment recognition had stabilised at the substantive-and-probable-threshold standard in 78% of surveyed Big-Four audit engagements.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Q3 2025 external-audit review had left two revenue-recognition informational findings. The Q4 2025 talent review had flagged the controller application gap. The Q3 2026 pharma-licensing pipeline had scheduled two three-component deals.

### What Brennan-Castellucci knew

The five-step model; the stand-alone-selling-price allocation on bundled contracts; the expected-value vs. most-likely-amount variable-consideration toolkit and the highly-probable-not-to-reverse constraint; the right-to-use vs. right-to-access software-license toggle; the cost-to-cost input method and the milestone output method on over-time construction; the sales-or-usage-based-royalty exception on pharma licensing.

### What she did not know

Whether the Q3 pharma-licensing pipeline would settle on upfront-plus-milestone-plus-royalty or on a simpler upfront-plus-royalty split; whether the volume-rebate variable-consideration application would require a constraint-threshold re-review at year-end; whether the eight-contract archetype would cover all the 2026–2027 commercial-contract variations or require a ninth archetype; and whether the 10-Q sales-or-usage-based-royalty footnote would trigger a re-review of the 2024–2025 royalty-recognition timing.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Technical Accounting**: *"Eight archetypes. Five steps. A controller who can apply the model to all eight is a senior-manager-ready hire."*

**The Head of Commercial Contracts**: *"Right-to-use versus right-to-access is the one-word that moves the P&L by twenty-four months."*

**The External-Audit Liaison**: *"The variable-consideration constraint is the SEC's favourite pressure point. Document the expected-value math. Document the constraint rationale."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"Revenue is not a cash event. Revenue is a control transfer."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Brennan-Castellucci had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Eight-archetype retraining with explicit constraint, right-to-use/access toggle, cost-to-cost/milestone choice and sales-or-usage-based exception:** the recommended envelope. Audit findings resolvable inside twelve months. Controller application gap closable. Recommended.
- **Option B — Standards-recitation retraining, no archetype portfolio:** the theory-heavy envelope. Rejected — application gap persists.
- **Option C — Twenty-contract retraining:** the over-specified envelope. Rejected — retraining-window compression risk.

Her private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the contract most correlated with the external-audit findings trajectory was Contract 4 — the variable-consideration constraint — because it was the contract that decided whether Northwind committed to an archetype-portfolio retraining with documented constraint application (Option A), stayed in a standards-recitation mode that would not close findings (Option B) or over-specified the workbook and failed the retraining-window compression test (Option C).
