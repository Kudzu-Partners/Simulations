## Abstract

VERADA MERIDIAN was, in the case universe, a premium beverage being launched by a regional CPG platform with USD 1.6B in 2025 revenue, a nine-brand beverage portfolio, and an in-house consumer-insights function that had, across 2023–2025, built a partnership with two academic neuroscience laboratories and one commercial neuromarketing vendor. The Q3 2026 launch window, budgeted at an incremental marketing envelope of USD 14M against a Year-1 revenue target of USD 32M, was anchored on a four-round neuromarketing lab that would, across Q1 2026 through Q2 2026, interpret four neuroscientific data streams (EEG frontal asymmetry for approach motivation; GSR skin-conductance for emotional arousal; IAT implicit-association scores for implicit brand associations; eye-tracking heatmaps for visual attention) to commit ad creative, pricing frame, store-layout-and-digital-UX architecture, and loyalty-program architecture. On Monday, February 16, 2026, Sofía Lagos, a 44-year-old former academic neuroscientist and ten-year consumer-insights director, had opened the four-round commitment inside an ethics envelope that required consent-first design across any biometric-gated decision. The case opens at 6:08 a.m. on Tuesday, February 24, 2026, at the platform's consumer-insights lab, as Lagos prepares the Round-1 ad-creative commitment that will anchor the full four-round envelope.

## The Protagonist

Sofía Lagos, 44, had joined the platform in January 2022 from a regional academic neuroscience laboratory where she had, across eight years, run EEG-and-GSR consumer-behavior studies at an R1 university neuroeconomics lab. Before that, she had been a postdoctoral researcher at a European decision-neuroscience institute for four years. The Chief Marketing Officer had recruited her specifically on the strength of two capabilities: her experience running EEG-frontal-asymmetry studies on advertising creative with published peer-reviewed calibration, and her reputation for refusing to commit biometric-gated marketing interventions without a consent-first ethical envelope. Her long-term incentive plan was tied 50% to the launch-success score (LSS) at Year-1 launch close and 15% to the ethics score at Year 1 — a single KPI that the CMO had explicitly protected inside the envelope.

## The Decision

Lagos had to commit, by the March 10 executive-committee commitment, the Round-1 ad-creative envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2 through 4.

1. **Round 1 — Ad-Creative Optimization** — whether to commit the launch creative to Variant B (EEG frontal-asymmetry score +2.3, GSR arousal moderate, predicted purchase intent high), Variant D (EEG +1.1, GSR arousal peak, predicted purchase intent moderate), or to commission a fifth variant inside the four-week window. The EEG frontal-asymmetry signal predicted approach motivation; the GSR arousal signal predicted attention but not direction. On the launch literature, the two signals disagreed roughly 28% of the time and, when they disagreed, EEG frontal-asymmetry predicted purchase intent at 2.1x the GSR arousal signal.
2. **Round 2 — Pricing-Frame Selection** — whether to commit the launch pricing to a scarcity frame (loss-aversion activation; implicit premium-quality association reinforced), an anchoring frame (reference-price manipulation), a bundling frame (complexity-aversion activation), or a values frame (sincerity-dimension reinforcement). The IAT implicit-association score had placed the premium-quality association at the 78th percentile of the relevant beverage category — a pattern that framed the scarcity commitment.
3. **Round 3 — Store-Layout and Digital-UX Operationalization** — whether to commit the default-option placement to the premium SKU at eye-level in the physical retail layout and at the top-of-funnel in the DTC flow, or to commit a choice-architecture design that spread the default across three SKUs.
4. **Round 4 — Loyalty-Program Architecture** — whether to commit the loyalty-program rewards gate to an EEG-biometric-gated reward structure (loyalty reward unlocked by verified EEG purchase-intent signal; technically effective, ethically contested), a consent-first design (loyalty reward unlocked by explicit purchase action; ethical envelope intact), or a hybrid design (opt-in biometric augmentation).

The four rounds would compound into a single launch commitment. On Lagos's working model, a Variant-B-plus-Scarcity-plus-Default-plus-Consent-First envelope delivered Round-4 DQS 48, calibration 21, ethics 13, LSS 82. A Variant-D-plus-Anchoring-plus-Spread-Default-plus-Biometric-Gated envelope delivered DQS 31, calibration -8, ethics -14, LSS 62. A Variant-B-plus-Scarcity-plus-Default-plus-Biometric-Gated envelope delivered DQS 48, calibration 21, ethics -22, LSS 68 — a launch that maximised short-term conversion but that tanked the ethics score and forfeited the CMO's protected floor.

## Brand Background

VERADA MERIDIAN had been developed across 2023–2025 as the platform's premium beverage entry into the USD 1.2B regional ready-to-drink-beverage segment, a segment that had grown 18% in the relevant 2025 window and that was expected to grow 16%–22% in 2026 on a premiumisation tailwind and a modern-trade expansion cycle. The platform's pre-launch consumer research had, across an 11,200-shopper stated-preference study, placed VERADA MERIDIAN at a concept-preference of 62% against a category benchmark of 48% — a signal that had justified the USD 14M incremental launch envelope.

The Q4 2025 consumer-insights dossier had identified three specific pressures the four-round envelope would inherit. First, the Q3 2025 Advertising Research Foundation (ARF) methodological review had, across a commercial summary of 22 neuromarketing-augmented launches, placed EEG frontal-asymmetry as the single highest-predictive-validity biometric signal for purchase-intent prediction at the pre-launch window — a pattern that framed the Round-1 Variant-B commitment. Second, the Q4 2025 IAT implicit-association tracker had, across a 2,800-shopper sample, placed VERADA MERIDIAN's premium-quality association at the 78th percentile of the category — a pattern that framed the Round-2 scarcity commitment. Third, the Q1 2026 regulatory-scrutiny calendar had, on the LATAM data-privacy-and-biometrics front, confirmed a Q4 2026 LATAM biometric-consent regulation update that would tighten oversight on biometric-gated commercial applications — a calendar that placed the Round-4 consent-first commitment squarely inside the envelope.

## Industry and Market Context

The neuromarketing and decision-neuroscience landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2015–2024 EEG-frontal-asymmetry evidence base had, across fifty-eight peer-reviewed consumer-neuroscience studies, documented a predictive validity of 0.72 for purchase-intent prediction against 0.34 for GSR arousal — a pattern that anchored the Round-1 commitment. Second, the 2010–2024 IAT-based pricing-frame evidence base had, across twenty-four commercial launches, placed scarcity framing at a conversion lift of 12–18% against anchoring and values frames on category premium-quality positioning — a pattern that anchored the Round-2 commitment. Third, the 2015–2024 default-effect evidence base had, across thirty-seven retail-and-DTC operationalizations, placed premium-default placement at a DTC conversion lift of 22–34% against choice-architecture-spread defaults — a pattern that anchored the Round-3 commitment.

Empirically, the post-2015 neuromarketing literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, launches that had sequenced consent-first loyalty-program architectures ahead of biometric-gated loyalty architectures had, across a 2019–2024 sample of 14 premium beverage launches, preserved ethics scores above the ARF Guidelines floor at every launch window against 39% of biometric-gated launches. Second, launches that had committed to EEG-frontal-asymmetry as the purchase-intent-prediction signal had, across the same sample, closed a DQS at Round 4 that was 1.6x higher than launches that had sequenced GSR-arousal-as-primary-signal.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Chief Marketing Officer had, in the December 2025 off-site, approved a four-round neuromarketing lab, an LSS target of 80, a DQS floor of 45, a calibration floor of 15, and an ethics floor of 10. The Senior Neuroscientist had, on February 2, circulated the Round-1 sheet that proposed the Variant-B commitment on the EEG frontal-asymmetry signal. The Senior Brand Strategist had, on February 8, signed off on the Round-2 scarcity frame conditional on the Round-1 Variant-B commitment holding through the ad-testing window. The Chief Legal Officer had, on February 14, flagged the Q4 2026 LATAM biometric-consent regulation and argued for a consent-first Round-4 commitment. The Chief Ethics Officer had, on February 19, protected the ethics floor at 10 inside the envelope.

### What Lagos knew

The team had modelled three four-round envelopes. A Variant-B-plus-Scarcity-plus-Default-plus-Consent-First Envelope projected Round-4 DQS 48, calibration 21, ethics 13, LSS 82. A Variant-D-plus-Anchoring-plus-Spread-Default-plus-Biometric-Gated Envelope projected DQS 31, calibration -8, ethics -14, LSS 62. A Variant-B-plus-Scarcity-plus-Default-plus-Biometric-Gated Envelope projected DQS 48, calibration 21, ethics -22, LSS 68 — a launch that failed the ethics floor.

### What she did not know

Whether the Q4 2026 LATAM biometric-consent regulation would clear at the modelled timeline or slip to Q1 2027; whether the Variant-B ad-creative would reproduce the modelled EEG frontal-asymmetry signal across the Round-1 testing cohort or show a weaker +1.4 signal; whether the scarcity pricing frame would clear the Chief Legal Officer's anti-manipulation review; and whether the default-placement commitment would survive the Grupo Éxito planogram negotiation inside Q2 2026.

### What others were saying

**The Senior Neuroscientist**: *"Variant B. Frontal asymmetry predicts approach motivation. GSR arousal predicts attention — not direction."*

**The Senior Brand Strategist**: *"Scarcity frame. The IAT premium-quality association is already there — we are activating it, not creating it."*

**The Chief Legal Officer**: *"Consent-first Round 4. The biometric-consent regulation will land inside the envelope."*

**The Chief Ethics Officer**: *"Ethics floor at 10 is non-negotiable. Every round must clear it."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Lagos had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Variant-B-plus-Scarcity-plus-Default-plus-Consent-First:** Round 1 ad Variant B (EEG +2.3). Round 2 scarcity pricing frame. Round 3 default premium SKU at eye-level and top-of-funnel. Round 4 consent-first loyalty with opt-in biometric augmentation. DQS 48. Calibration 21. Ethics 13. LSS 82. Aligns with SN, SBS, CLO, CEO. Recommended.

- **Option B — Variant-D-plus-Anchoring-plus-Spread-Default-plus-Biometric-Gated:** Round 1 ad Variant D (GSR peak). Round 2 anchoring frame. Round 3 spread default across three SKUs. Round 4 EEG-biometric-gated loyalty. DQS 31. Calibration -8. Ethics -14. LSS 62. Rejected.

- **Option C — Variant-B-plus-Scarcity-plus-Default-plus-Biometric-Gated:** Round 1 Variant B. Round 2 scarcity. Round 3 default placement. Round 4 EEG-biometric-gated loyalty. DQS 48. Ethics -22. LSS 68. Fails ethics floor. Rejected.

Lagos's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the Round-4 LSS was Round 1 — the Variant-B commitment on the EEG frontal-asymmetry signal that anchored the purchase-intent-prediction trajectory and that compounded through the Round-2 scarcity activation, the Round-3 default operationalization, and the Round-4 consent-first architecture.
