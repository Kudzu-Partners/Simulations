## Abstract

FinNova Digital was, in the case universe, a São Paulo-headquartered LatAm digital fintech operating across Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, Chile, Peru, Argentina and Uruguay, with EU-processing exposure through a Dublin-based analytics processor, 4.8M active customers, USD 620M in 2025 revenue, a 2025 Compliance-Score reading of 58 on a GDPR-and-LGPD-composite scale against a LatAm-fintech peer-benchmark of 72 (composite captures PIA coverage, transfer-mechanism completeness and breach-readiness scoring), a 2025 Trust-Index reading of 62 against a peer-benchmark of 74, a 2025 Risk-Index reading of 71 against a peer-benchmark of 38 (higher means more risk), a 2024 post-breach remediation file still open with the Brazilian ANPD, and a 2025 cross-border-transfer architecture that had relied on a derogation basis for 62% of Dublin-processor flows. The Q4 2025 board had approved a USD 24M eighteen-month privacy-engineering-rebuild envelope — the DataShield programme — and had positioned it as the company's response to three overlapping pressures: a Q3 2025 suspicious-access incident on the customer-service platform that had escalated into a Q1 2026 breach-notification event exposing 2.0M customer records, a Q4 2025 Irish Data-Protection-Commission correspondence questioning the Dublin-processor transfer basis, and a Q1 2026 LGPD amendment tightening consent-granularity expectations. The Q1 2026 board had reaffirmed the USD 24M envelope and a Chief-Data-Protection-Officer-led five-round commitment cycle against a Compliance-Score floor above 78, a Trust-Index floor above 72, a Risk-Index ceiling below 30, and a Composite DataShield Score above 74. The case opens at 6:49 a.m. on Monday, April 20, 2026, at the FinNova Digital São Paulo head office as **Isabela Figueiredo-Costa**, a 46-year-old Chief Data Protection Officer, prepares the Round-1 privacy-impact-assessment-scope commitment. Sixty-nine days separate her from the June 28 board risk-review session.

3. Diagnose the Round-3 72-hour breach response commitment (notify-contain-investigate envelope that executes the parallel notify-contain-investigate sequence inside the 72-hour window vs. investigate-first envelope) and recommend the parallel-sequence envelope on the multiplier-fine basis.

Respond to the Round-4 consent redesign commitment (granular-no-dark-pattern envelope that redesigns the consent surface with per-purpose toggles, pre-unticked defaults and a documented dark-pattern audit vs. unchanged envelope) and recommend the redesign envelope on the Trust-Index recovery basis.
5. Design the Round-5 privacy-by-design embedding commitment that converts DataShield from a remediation programme into a permanent privacy-by-design capability with DPO-independence, board-reporting line and SDLC-integrated stage gate.

## The Protagonist

Isabela Figueiredo-Costa, 46, had assumed the Chief-Data-Protection-Officer role at FinNova Digital on November 1, 2024, from a European fintech where she had, across ten years, led two enterprise privacy-engineering programmes and had closed a 2022 GDPR-class remediation across a 12M-customer book that had delivered a Compliance-Score uplift from 54 to 82 and had exited a Data-Protection-Authority open-file process inside fourteen months. The Chief Executive Officer had recruited her in September 2024 specifically on two capabilities: her documented discipline of parallel-sequencing notify-contain-investigate under the 72-hour clock, and her willingness to block product ship on privacy-by-design failures against revenue-pressure. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the Compliance-Score floor and 25% to the Risk-Index ceiling.

## The Decision

Figueiredo-Costa had to commit, by the May 11 board pre-review, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–5.

1. **Round 1 — Privacy-Impact-Assessment scope.** Three archetypes: full-flow (map every data flow between LATAM operations and the Dublin processor, scored risk rubric per flow, Composite 76), sampling (PIA applied on a 20% flow sample — Composite 32, Dublin-processor blind spot), or phased (full-flow on two markets — Composite 54). Working envelope: full-flow.
2. **Round 2 — Cross-border-transfer architecture.** Three archetypes: SCCs-plus-TIA (2021-class Standard Contractual Clauses coupled with a transfer-impact assessment, Composite 76), derogation-reliance (Article-49 derogation basis — Composite 22, Irish-DPC enforcement probability), or BCRs (Binding Corporate Rules — Composite 58, 24-month approval horizon). Working envelope: SCCs-plus-TIA.
3. **Round 3 — 72-hour breach response.** Three vectors: notify-contain-investigate (parallel sequence inside the 72-hour window, Composite 78), investigate-first (serial sequence — Composite 24, multiplier-fine probability), or phased (notify to supervisory authority, deferred customer notice — Composite 48). Working envelope: notify-contain-investigate.
4. **Round 4 — Consent redesign.** Three vectors: granular-no-dark-pattern (per-purpose toggles, pre-unticked defaults, documented dark-pattern audit, Composite 78), unchanged (preserve legacy surface — Composite 24, LGPD-amendment exposure), or phased (granular on two product lines — Composite 54). Working envelope: granular-no-dark-pattern.
5. **Round 5 — Privacy-by-design embedding.** Three vectors: integrated-architecture (DPO independence, direct-to-board reporting line, SDLC-integrated stage gate, privacy-by-design audit register, Composite 78), compliance-memo-only (annual compliance memo — Composite 30), or phased (gate integrated on two products — Composite 54). Working envelope: integrated-architecture.

On Figueiredo-Costa's working model, a Full-Flow / SCCs / Parallel / Granular / Integrated envelope delivered Compliance-Score 80, Trust 74, Risk 28, Composite 76.

## Company Background

FinNova Digital had been founded in São Paulo in 2017 and had, across nine years, built a USD 620M-revenue LatAm digital-fintech portfolio across payments, lending and neobanking products with 4.8M active customers across seven markets. The 2019–2023 growth cycle had built the data-and-analytics architecture on a Dublin-processor anchor that had, at scale, produced a cross-border-transfer architecture reliant on Article-49 derogations for 62% of flows — a posture the September 2025 Chief-Legal-Officer review had flagged as inconsistent with post-2020 EU-enforcement trajectories. The Q4 2025 board had approved the USD 24M DataShield envelope as a comprehensive privacy-engineering rebuild.

Three Q1 2026 events were named in the March 2026 board dossier. First, the Q3 2025 customer-service-platform suspicious-access incident had, through December 2025 forensics, been confirmed as a breach exposing 2.0M customer records — triggering the January 2026 ANPD and supervisory-authority notification sequence under the 72-hour clock. Second, the December 2025 Irish Data-Protection-Commission correspondence had questioned the Dublin-processor derogation basis and had requested a documented transfer-impact assessment. Third, the February 2026 LGPD amendment had tightened consent-granularity expectations, bringing the Brazilian consent regime closer to the GDPR-granular standard and implying a June 2026 compliance deadline for existing consent surfaces.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2020–2025 EU-and-LATAM privacy-enforcement literature had, across 42 documented enforcement events, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, entities on SCCs-plus-TIA architectures had documented enforcement-risk readings 3.6× lower than entities on derogation-reliance postures. Second, entities executing parallel notify-contain-investigate sequences inside the 72-hour window had documented fine-multiplier readings 2.2× lower than entities on investigate-first sequences, with supervisory-authority trust-readings 22–28 points higher. Third, entities that had redesigned consent surfaces with per-purpose toggles and documented dark-pattern audits had delivered Trust-Index rebounds of 14–22 points within a 6–9-month window.

Empirically, the post-2022 privacy-by-design literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, DPO-independence architectures with direct-to-board reporting had reduced repeat-breach probability by 64% relative to compliance-memo-only architectures. Second, SDLC-integrated privacy stage gates had sustained Compliance-Score readings above 78 at the eighteen-month mark in 76% of cases against 22% for programmes without SDLC integration.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the December 2025 session, approved the USD 24M envelope and the June 28 review calendar. The Head of Privacy Engineering had, on March 4, delivered the PIA inventory. The Chief Legal Officer had, on March 11, delivered the Irish-DPC correspondence pack. The Chief Information Security Officer had, on March 18, delivered the breach-response diagnostic. The Chief Product Officer had, on March 25, delivered the consent-surface redesign brief.

### What Figueiredo-Costa knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. A Full-Flow / SCCs / Parallel / Granular / Integrated envelope projected Composite 76. A Sampling / Derogation / Investigate-First / Unchanged / Memo-Only envelope projected Composite 22. A Hybrid envelope projected Composite 46.

### What she did not know

Whether the Irish-DPC correspondence would escalate into a formal enforcement notice inside the envelope window; whether the ANPD open file from the Q1 2026 breach would return a fine posture that the Trust-Index and customer-retention trajectory would absorb; whether the June 2026 LGPD-amendment compliance deadline would move; and whether the SDLC-integrated gate discipline would hold against Q3 product-ship pressure.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Privacy Engineering**: *"Full-flow PIA. Dublin alone is three flows scoring above seventy."*

**The Chief Legal Officer**: *"SCCs-plus-TIA. The derogation basis does not survive a written DPC question."*

**The Chief Information Security Officer**: *"Parallel notify-contain-investigate. Every hour of serial delay multiplies the fine. That's the math."*

**The Chief Executive Officer**: *"Compliance is not a document. It is a clock we already lost once. Build the architecture. Pay the ship-date give-back."*

## Options Under Consideration

Figueiredo-Costa had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Full-Flow / SCCs / Parallel / Granular / Integrated:** the recommended envelope. Compliance-Score 80. Trust 74. Risk 28. Composite 76. Recommended.
- **Option B — Sampling / Derogation / Investigate-First / Unchanged / Memo-Only:** the legacy envelope. Composite 22. Rejected — enforcement-and-repeat-breach probability above 72%.
- **Option C — Hybrid / Phased / Phased / Phased / Phased:** the partial envelope. Composite 46. Rejected — privacy-by-design architecture fragments under partial gating.

Figueiredo-Costa's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 3 — the 72-hour breach response — because it was the round in which the fine-multiplier math, the ANPD trust posture and the supervisory-authority correspondence all resolved inside the same clock.
