# Biscayne Executive Learning: Hold the Line

**Case Code:** OPS-2026-1936 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1936 — *Cohort Operations* · **v1.0 · August 2026**

---

The floor of Room 300 had been carpet on Friday. Nadège Toussaint stood in the doorway at 7:40 a.m. on Monday, April 13, 2026, looking at what a failed supply line above the ceiling had done to it over the weekend. The facilities superintendent had already costed the dry-out and the ceiling replacement: nine days. Room 300 was the only classroom at Biscayne Executive Learning that seated 50 people with the cameras, microphones and displays a hybrid programme required.

Two cohorts needed it that week. Cohort A — the MeridianHealth Leadership Academy, 48 clinical managers — opened Day 1 on Wednesday, 48 hours away, and its contract specified the hybrid-capable room. Cohort B — the Global Finance Certificate, 30 international executives already on campus and halfway through the programme — had been promised Room 300 on Thursday for a guest-faculty session advertised since January. By the time Toussaint reached her desk two faculty had also changed their availability. She had four planning sessions with her team between that morning and the end of the week to rebuild the plan, and one instruction from her director that had not moved: both cohorts had to finish at or above 8.5 out of 10 on the Net Learner Experience score.

## The Protagonist

Nadège Toussaint, 43, was the lead academic support coordinator at Biscayne Executive Learning and had run its academic operations team since 2021. She had come to the unit in 2014 from conference operations at a Miami Beach hotel group, where the cycles ran three days rather than three weeks. Her team did the work that never appeared on a programme brochure: the right faculty member in the right room with the right materials, catering, technology and floor support, about sixty times a year. Her own review, and her team's, was written against delivery — cohorts that ran clean and clients who came back.

## The Decision

Toussaint had to decide, over four planning sessions that week, where each cohort would meet, when each would start, who would staff it, and what she would spend — and to keep deciding as the week moved under her.

## The Company and its Setting

Biscayne Executive Learning was the custom- and open-programmes unit of a large urban public university's business school in Miami, Florida. It billed roughly US$14 million a year across about sixty executive cohorts — leadership academies, certificate seminars and bespoke corporate programmes — for clients in Florida, Latin America and the Caribbean. It was self-funded: it earned what it spent.

The unit measured every cohort on a Net Learner Experience score from 0 to 10, collected from participants and reported to the client. That number travelled. It went into renewal conversations, and a corporate sponsor quoted it back when deciding whether to run the next intake.

MeridianHealth's chief human resources officer (CHRO) had described Cohort A in writing as "the audition" for a US$3.2 million renewal. Cohort B's thirty executives belonged to a different client in a different market, whose programme reviews were read by a different set of buyers.

## The Situation

Besides the scores it collected at the end of a programme, the unit carried a projected figure for each cohort on the calendar, revised whenever the plan changed. Cohort A's projection and Cohort B's both read 5.5 as the week opened, against the 8.5 both had to clear (see **Exhibit 1**). The unit's own operational resilience index — its internal measure of whether a plan could absorb a second disruption without a further drop in service — stood at 20 out of 100. Nothing had been committed against the US$40,000 left in the quarterly support budget, and no top-up was available before July.

The two changed faculty windows narrowed it further. Cohort A's lead faculty was now available Wednesday morning only, not the afternoon slot printed on the master calendar. Cohort B's guest faculty flew in Thursday and could not move (see **Exhibit 2**).

Three senior coordinators and four juniors were available that week. Both cohort teams had asked for Maria Solano, the senior who had run MeridianHealth's pilot programme the previous autumn; Solano had also closed out the prior week over her standard hours, and any further hours she worked billed at 1.5 times her standard rate. Junior coordinators cost less and had not yet run a flagship client alone.

The calendar as printed committed both cohorts to a room that was out of service: Cohort A's hybrid Day 1 on Wednesday afternoon in Room 300, in the slot its lead faculty had removed from her availability, and Cohort B's guest session in the same room on Thursday. Held to that calendar, both projections stayed at 5.5 against the 8.5 both had to clear, the resilience index at 20, and none of the US$40,000 committed. Closing that gap required a change to the plan.

## What Toussaint Controlled

In the Monday-morning session Toussaint had to name the one constraint every other decision would bend around — the hybrid room out for nine days, the two changed faculty windows, the US$40,000 left in the budget, or the coordinator roster. She had to set how her team worked the cascade: trace every downstream break before committing any move, take the most urgent conflict first and handle what it broke afterwards, or give each function its own piece to solve in parallel and reconcile at the end. And she had to state, as a percentage, how much of the new faculty availability the team had absorbed before it started re-sequencing.

Monday afternoon set the week's shape. Cohort A's hybrid Day 1 could run in a hotel conference suite at US$4,200 a day — four days, US$16,800, or 42% of the remaining quarterly budget; in two smaller campus rooms in parallel at about US$1,800, which put a second coordinator on the floor; mostly virtually at about US$600, which changed the format the 48 managers had been sold and the catering with it; or in Room 300 at no cost, on the chance the dry-out finished early. Cohort A could start Wednesday morning inside its lead faculty's stated window, hold the Wednesday afternoon slot on the master calendar, or slide to Thursday. Cohort B's Thursday session could keep a room of the promised standard, move to a smaller room, or move off Thursday altogether.

Tuesday morning was people and money. Solano could take a single cohort with a junior beside her and a named backup covering elsewhere, take Cohort A only paired with a junior, or take both flagship cohorts at the overtime rate. The three seniors could be spread as senior cover on each cohort with juniors backstopped, as two seniors and the better room on Cohort A with one junior on Cohort B, or as one senior across both with the juniors carrying more. Catering, printed materials and on-site supplies for both cohorts were a single figure Toussaint set; the standing plan for a week of this size was US$6,000, and it drew on the same US$40,000 as any room.

Tuesday afternoon brought the CHRO's email — *"Is Day 1 fully handled?"* — and a junior coordinator calling in sick. Toussaint could write to both clients ahead of the question, answer MeridianHealth now and tell Cohort B if they raised it, or say nothing while the plan worked. She could put a pre-named backup on the floor, spread the remaining coordinators wider on overtime, or run one coordinator short. She could name a single decision-maker and keep an issue log, ask the whole team to watch and decide together, or run no formal routine. And when the CHRO pressed, she had to say how firmly she stood behind the plan.

She was measured on four things: Cohort A's experience score, Cohort B's experience score, the resilience index she started at 20, and how much of the US$40,000 she had committed.

## Closing

Toussaint carried the printed master calendar into the first planning session with the room inventory and the staff roster on top of it. She opened with the question she wanted settled before anything moved: *what has to be true by Wednesday morning.* Room 300 was out for nine days. The MeridianHealth cohort opened in 48 hours.
