## Abstract

BancoMontaverde S.A. was, in the case universe, the fourth-ranked universal bank in the Montaverde financial system, with 2025 year-end assets of USD 28.0B, a CET1 ratio of 13.2%, risk-weighted assets of USD 19.0B, a group ROE of 11.4%, and a peer ranking of four out of six on the Montaverde Banking Association composite. The bank operated three principal business books: retail, contributing 46% of 2025 revenue; corporate mid-market, contributing 38%; and markets and treasury, contributing 16%. On Thursday, February 12, 2026, the Board of Directors approved a three-year capital-allocation envelope that the Group CEO would commit in a Year-1 package. The Montaverde economy, heavily exposed to the copper and iron-ore export corridor, carried a 2026 GDP growth projection of 2.8% and an inflation projection of 3.5%, with a Group Risk Office base-case probability of 47% on a commodity-driven Year-2 recession. Esteban Alarcón, appointed Group CEO on September 1, 2025 after eight years as the bank's Head of Corporate Banking, faced three annual rounds, a Year-2 commodity-crash scenario the Risk Office had identified as the dominant downside path, and a Year-3 recovery window. The case opens at 6:04 a.m. on Monday, February 16, 2026, as Alarcón prepares the Year-1 capital-allocation package for the March 4 Board meeting.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Year-1 capital-allocation choice between retail expansion, corporate mid-market growth, markets/treasury deployment, digital-platform CapEx, CET1 buffer, and buyback, and diagnose when each dominates under a given cycle probability.
2. Analyze the countercyclical-provisioning decision — whether to provision above the required model ahead of a modelled downturn — and recommend a posture consistent with the CET1 buffer and the shareholder-return commitment.
3. Diagnose the compound effect of a Year-1 mid-market SME tilt, a Year-2 corporate de-risking, and a Year-3 selective re-expansion on a three-year peer-ranking outcome.
4. Design a digital-platform CapEx envelope that pays no measurable Year-1 benefit and is defensible against a Board member's Year-1 buyback proposal.
5. Recommend a complete Year-1 capital-allocation package and identify the Year-3 move most likely to convert a Year-2 defensive posture into a shareholder-value outcome.

On the morning of **Monday, February 16, 2026**, **Esteban Alarcón** walked into the Group CEO office on the twenty-seventh floor of the BancoMontaverde headquarters in the Montaverde financial district at 6:04 a.m. and opened the Year-1 capital-allocation workbook his Head of Group Strategy had finalized at 11:14 p.m. Sunday. Seventeen days separated him from the Board meeting. The peer-ranking dashboard on the wall read four — with the three-year-ranking trajectory beneath it blank.

## The Protagonist

Esteban Alarcón, 52, had joined BancoMontaverde in 2018 after eleven years at a Spanish universal bank and had served as Head of Corporate Banking from 2018 through August 2025, when the Chairman had presented his name to the Board succession committee. A graduate of the Pontificia Universidad Católica de Montaverde with an MBA from INSEAD and an executive program on bank balance-sheet management at Harvard, he had, in his prior role, led the 2019–2022 de-risking of BancoMontaverde's mining-corridor exposure — a campaign that had, in the 2023 peer-ranking cycle, moved the bank from six to four. His reputation inside the system was as a cycle reader. The Chairman's September written mandate had been: "Run the bank that will be peer-ranking one in three years. Not the bank that will be peer-ranking one next quarter."

---

## The Decision

Alarcón had to commit, by 9:00 a.m. on Wednesday, March 4, the Year-1 capital-allocation envelope the Board would treat as the three-year operating commitment:

1. **Retail Expansion** — whether to allocate 40% of the incremental-capital envelope to retail deposit growth and digital-channel acquisition, whether to lean toward an affluent-wealth proposition or a mass-market deposit push.
2. **Corporate Mid-Market** — whether to allocate 30% to a disciplined mid-market SME diversification, and whether to hold mining-corridor exposure at the current 11% of the corporate book or taper it further ahead of the cycle.
3. **Markets/Treasury, Digital-Platform CapEx, CET1 Buffer, Buyback** — whether to maintain a 10% digital-platform CapEx line, whether to hold 20% as CET1 buffer, and whether to commit no Year-1 buyback.

The four sub-decisions would interact and would compound across three rounds. A Year-1 package that allocated 40% retail deposit growth, 30% corporate mid-market SME, 10% digital CapEx, 20% CET1 buffer, and zero markets-expansion and zero buyback produced, on the internal scenario model, a Year-2 corporate NPL path of 3.4% against a peer-average projection of 4.8%, a Year-2 ROE drop to 9.1% under heavy provisioning, and a Year-3 ROE recovery to 15.8% on a re-expansion into mid-market diversified and infrastructure. A Year-1 package that leaned into a markets-expansion and a Year-1 buyback program produced a higher Year-1 ROE but opened a Year-2 CET1 path that the Montaverde regulator had signalled, in its January 2026 thematic review, as below the preferred thirteen-percent supervisory floor under a commodity-shock scenario.

## Company Background

BancoMontaverde had been founded in 1962 as the retail-banking-and-trade-finance arm of a Montaverde industrial group and had, across sixty-four years, built the four-book universal-banking franchise the case studied. 2025 group assets were USD 28.0B distributed as retail (USD 12.9B, 46%), corporate (USD 10.6B, 38%), and markets/treasury (USD 4.5B, 16%). The 2025 group P&L showed revenue of USD 2.10B, an EBITDA-equivalent operating margin of 34%, a cost-to-income ratio of 52%, and a group ROE of 11.4%. The corporate book's 11% mining-corridor exposure was a legacy of the 2016–2018 commodity-boom-era lending cycle and had been tapered from a peak of 19% through the 2019–2022 de-risking campaign the case protagonist had led. The 2025 group NPL ratio was 2.1% (retail) and 1.9% (corporate), against a peer-average 2.4% and 2.3% respectively.

The digital-platform investment the Year-1 package proposed was, in the internal roadmap, a USD 340M three-year commitment to rebuild the retail channel on a cloud-native architecture, with Year-1 and Year-2 spend carrying no measurable top-line contribution and Year-3 expected uplift concentrated in a retail-wealth proposition that would launch against the Year-3 recovery window. The Board had, in the December 2025 strategy off-site, approved the digital-platform architecture; the Year-1 allocation decision was the explicit CapEx sizing.

## Industry and Market Context

The Montaverde banking landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Year-1 decision sat inside. First, the Montaverde central bank had, in its January 2026 policy statement, held the policy rate at 6.0% and signalled a posture of rate-cut readiness if the commodity corridor entered an export-price correction — the Risk Office's downside scenario placed a Year-2 GDP outcome at minus 0.8% and a rate-cut cycle of 100 basis points. Second, the Montaverde regulator had, in the January 2026 thematic review, reaffirmed a preferred CET1 operating range of 13.0% to 14.5% and had signalled that banks whose CET1 would drop below 13.0% under a modelled stress would face a dividend-approval review — a signal BancoMontaverde's shareholder-return program could not ignore. Third, the LATAM peer-group fintech-JV cycle had, across 2024–2025, produced mixed outcomes — of twenty-two announced JVs, nine had been unwound within thirty-six months on governance and integration drag, eight were in a Year-3 scaling phase, and five were still in Year-1 soft-launch. The Year-1 envelope did not include a fintech JV; the question would sit on the Year-3 table.

Empirically, the post-2016 bank-cycle research had two robust findings the Year-1 package had to respect. First, banks that had provisioned 40–60 basis points above the required model in the six-to-twelve months preceding a commodity-driven credit downturn had, across a 2018–2024 sample of thirty-one LATAM cycle events, exited the cycle with Year-3 ROE 280 basis points above peer average and with peer-ranking movements averaging plus-two positions. Second, banks that had expanded consumer-lending during a pre-downturn window in the pursuit of Year-1 ROE optics had, across the same sample, entered the following cycle with Year-2 provisioning 110 basis points above the pre-expansion trajectory and had required, on average, a Year-3 CET1-preserving pause on shareholder returns.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Chairman had, in the December 2025 off-site, committed to the Board a three-year agenda that would test BancoMontaverde's cycle-reading capability under Alarcón's leadership. The Board Risk Committee had, on January 28, approved the Risk Office's downside-probability-47% commodity-shock scenario as the Year-2 operating assumption. The CFO had, on February 2, circulated a Year-1 capital-allocation pre-reading that proposed a USD 420M buyback program funded from excess CET1 — a proposal the Chairman had sent to Alarcón with the handwritten note "do not reject without argument." The Head of Group Strategy had, on February 6, modelled three Year-1 packages against the commodity-shock path.

### What Alarcón knew

The team had modelled three Year-1 packages. A Growth-Optimistic Package (35% retail, 40% corporate — including a 3-point mining-corridor re-expansion, 10% markets, 0% digital CapEx, 5% CET1 buffer, 10% buyback) projected a Year-1 ROE of 13.8%, a Year-2 NPL ratio of 4.2% (corporate), a Year-3 peer ranking of five, and a Year-3 composite of 72. A Disciplined Package (40% retail, 30% corporate mid-market SME diversified, 0% markets-expansion, 10% digital CapEx, 20% CET1 buffer, 0% buyback) projected a Year-1 ROE of 12.6%, a Year-2 NPL of 3.4%, a Year-3 peer ranking of one, a Year-3 composite of 86. A Defensive Package (30% retail, 15% corporate, 0% markets, 15% digital CapEx, 40% CET1 buffer, 0% buyback) projected a Year-1 ROE of 10.8%, a Year-2 NPL of 2.9%, a Year-3 peer ranking of two, a Year-3 composite of 78.

### What he did not know

Whether the commodity shock would land at the Risk Office's modelled 47% probability or at the alternate 62% path that two of the sell-side analysts had published; whether the Montaverde regulator, under a 13.0%-CET1-floor signal, would approve a Year-3 buyback of material size; whether the Chairman's handwritten note was a directive or a test; and whether the digital-platform investment would survive a Year-2 CET1-preserving cost-review once the shock hit.

### What others were saying

**The CFO**: *"Small buyback, fund it from the Year-1 excess CET1. The stock trades at 0.91 book. It is a rational capital return, not a cycle call."*

**The Chief Risk Officer**: *"No buyback, no markets expansion. Corporate mid-market SME with mining tapered to 8%. Provision 50 basis points above the required model through the second half of 2026. The auditor will like you. Compliance will understand it."*

**The Chairman of the Board**: *"Peer-ranking one in three years. I do not need Year-1 ROE to be at the top of the league table. I need the bank to be the one standing in Year 3."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Alarcón had narrowed the space to three Year-1 envelopes.

- **Option A — Growth-Optimistic:** 35% retail, 40% corporate with mining re-expansion, 10% markets, 0% digital CapEx, 5% CET1 buffer, 10% buyback. Year-1 ROE 13.8%, Year-3 peer ranking five, composite 72. Rewards Year-1 optics. Exposed to the shock.

- **Option B — Disciplined:** 40% retail, 30% corporate mid-market SME, 0% markets, 10% digital CapEx, 20% CET1 buffer, 0% buyback. Year-1 ROE 12.6%, Year-3 peer ranking one, composite 86. Aligns with CRO and Chairman. CFO's buyback proposal rejected or deferred.

- **Option C — Defensive:** 30% retail, 15% corporate, 0% markets, 15% digital CapEx, 40% CET1 buffer, 0% buyback. Year-1 ROE 10.8%, Year-3 peer ranking two, composite 78. Over-capitalized for the cycle. Under-deploys the digital opportunity.

Alarcón's private view, not yet shared with the Chairman, was that Option B was the right Year-1 package and that the single subsequent decision most correlated with the Year-3 composite was the Year-3 capital reset — specifically, a selective re-expansion into mid-market diversified and infrastructure, a retail-wealth launch on the digital platform, and a small selective buyback sized at USD 180M against the stock trading below book, with the fintech JV deferred to a Year-4 review rather than rushed into a Year-3 announcement.
