## Abstract

Helix, a five-year-old B2B SaaS provider of workflow and document-automation software, reached the start of 2026 with 612 paying customers (600 SMB, 12 enterprise), USD 8.4M in cash, a blended monthly burn of USD 282K (roughly 30 months of runway), and four unit-economics metrics that, at the quarter's end, fell short of investor-diligence norms: net revenue retention of 103% (vs. a 115% benchmark), LTV:CAC of 2.2 (vs. 3.0), CAC payback of 22 months (vs. 18), and a net-new ARR trajectory that had flattened across the prior three quarters. The co-founder and CEO, Rafael Vianna, faced six quarters to Series-B readiness. A Round 1 decision — ICP choice, CAC strategy, customer-success intensity, pricing model, and product investment — would set the trajectory. The case ends with Vianna at his desk on a Sunday evening, a board off-site fourteen hours away, weighing three internally coherent Round-1 commitments against a known macro event modelled to hit in Q3.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the compounding effect of ICP choice on unit economics over a multi-quarter horizon.
2. Analyze the interaction of customer-success intensity, pricing model, and product investment on Net Revenue Retention.
3. Design a Series-B-ready unit-economics trajectory from a starting position that does not yet meet investor benchmarks.
4. Diagnose when a narrowing is a strategic focus and when it is a retreat.
5. Recommend a Round 1 commitment set and defend it under a hostile Series-B-style diligence review.

On the night of **January 11, 2026**, **Rafael Vianna** sat in the glass-walled corner of the Helix office on Avenida Faria Lima, São Paulo, with four printed dashboards fanned across the floor. The dashboards showed the four metrics — NRR, LTV:CAC, CAC payback, cash runway — with the Series-B thresholds drawn as horizontal lines. Two of the four metrics were above the lines. Two were not. The board off-site would start at 9:00 a.m. Monday. A term sheet from Helix's existing Series-A lead, MarVentures Brasil, had arrived on Friday; it contained a Series-A+ extension at flat valuation, conditional on Vianna committing to "a narrow ICP strategy" in writing.

---

## The Protagonist

Rafael Vianna, 37, had co-founded Helix in 2021 with Julia Borges, 38. Vianna had spent eight years at a regional enterprise software company before founding; Borges had been a senior product manager at a Brazilian fintech. The two had raised a USD 3.2M seed in 2022 and a USD 18M Series A in 2023. Vianna held 24% of the common stock; Borges held 22%; the ESOP pool was at 14% fully diluted; Series A investors held 28% and Seed investors held 12%. Vianna's compensation was a salaried base; his wealth was concentrated in Helix common. His board was five seats: Vianna, Borges, MarVentures (lead), a seed-round super-angel, and an independent director recruited in Q4 2024.

---

## The Decision

Vianna had to commit, by 9:00 a.m. on Monday, January 12, the Helix Round 1 (Q1 2026) strategic package covering:

1. **ICP:** broad (current), law firms, accounting practices, or enterprise.
2. **CAC strategy:** aggressive (continue current paid-acquisition mix), balanced (mix shift toward inbound and partner), conservative (inbound and partner, reduced paid).
3. **Customer-success model:** current (1 CSM per 80 accounts), intensive (1 CSM per 40 accounts), tech-touch (1 CSM per 200 accounts plus product-led nudges).
4. **Pricing model:** per-seat (current), usage-based, hybrid, or flat-tier.
5. **Product investment:** current roadmap, workflow-automation focus, vertical-depth focus, or platform-extensibility focus.

The board would review the package at the Monday off-site. The MarVentures term sheet required a letter of commitment — attached to the Q1 package — by close of business Monday.

---

## Company Background

Helix was incorporated in São Paulo in 2021 as a B2B SaaS provider of workflow and document-automation software for professional-services firms. The initial wedge was legal-document-review automation for mid-sized Brazilian law firms; the product had expanded over 2023 and 2024 into broader workflow and document management, and the customer base had broadened to include accounting practices, boutique consultancies, and — in 2025 — twelve enterprise customers (7 in financial services, 5 in manufacturing). 2025 revenue was USD 4.1M (32% year-over-year growth); gross margin was 74%; ARR at Q4 was USD 5.2M. Helix employed 46 people across São Paulo and a remote engineering team distributed across four Latin American countries.

The customer base had bifurcated. Law firms and accounting practices (approximately 430 of the 600 SMB customers) showed a churn rate of 2.4% monthly, average contract size of USD 4,100 ACV, and organic expansion (land-expand) averaging 9% year-over-year. The remaining 170 SMB customers, a mix of boutique consultancies and small marketing firms, showed churn of 6.1% monthly, ACV of USD 3,400, and negative net retention. The twelve enterprise customers showed churn of 0.8% monthly, ACV of USD 62,000, and 23% year-over-year expansion — but CAC for each had averaged USD 140,000, a 2.4-year payback.

## Industry and Market Context

The B2B SaaS market in Latin America in 2026 had entered its sixth year of double-digit growth, but the venture-funding environment had compressed. Series B rounds in regional SaaS had fallen from a 2021 peak of approximately 40 per year to an estimated 14 in 2025; median post-money valuations had compressed from USD 120M to USD 72M. Series-B investor diligence had, through 2024 and 2025, sharpened on four metrics: NRR (benchmark 115%), LTV:CAC (3.0), CAC payback (18 months), and net-new ARR monotonicity (no sequential quarterly declines). Companies not meeting three of the four had, in the 2025 sample, priced down or not closed.

Research on SaaS unit economics, principally out of the U.S. and adapted for the Latin American context, converged on two findings. First, ICP choice dominated all other strategic levers as a predictor of NRR: a narrow-ICP cohort typically outperformed a broad-ICP cohort by 15–25 NRR points within 12 quarters. Second, customer-success intensity was a second-order driver: at intensive ratios, NRR improved 7–12 points but at a CAC-payback cost of 3–5 months.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Helix broad-to-narrow debate had been running inside the founder team since Q3 2025. Borges had argued throughout 2025 for narrowing to law firms. Vianna had held the broad position, on the argument that a broad base created optionality for enterprise expansion. Revenue had grown — from USD 3.1M to USD 4.1M in 2025 — but the unit economics had deteriorated quarter over quarter as the boutique-consultancy and small-marketing segments had absorbed marketing spend without producing retention.

The MarVentures term sheet was, in effect, Borges's argument made by an external party with capital. A Series-A+ extension at flat valuation — rather than a priced-up Series B — would be dilutive but would extend runway by approximately 18 months and would signal a focused commitment.

### What Vianna knew

The internal cohort model, rebuilt in October 2025, showed the law-firm cohort at NRR 119%, LTV:CAC 3.4, CAC payback 14 months — all four metrics above benchmark — and the boutique-consultancy cohort at NRR 82%, LTV:CAC 1.1, CAC payback 31 months. Narrowing to law firms would produce an immediate 18-month revenue-growth deceleration (a projected USD 1.6M ARR cut, as boutique customers churned off without replacement) and a 24-month recovery.

### What Vianna did not know

Whether the MarVentures commitment language could be softened (he had not yet asked). Whether the known macro event in Q3 — a Brazilian regulatory change affecting law-firm workflow data retention — would, in fact, accelerate law-firm spending on Helix-category software (thesis held by Borges) or dampen it (thesis held by MarVentures's due-diligence advisor). Whether the twelve enterprise customers, acquired largely through outbound and founder-led deals, would sustain their 23% expansion at a reduced CAC investment.

### What others were saying

**Julia Borges**, Co-Founder and President, pressed for a Law-Firm ICP narrowing. "Law firms. Intensive CS. Usage-based pricing. Workflow-automation product focus. Twelve quarters and we are investor-dream."

**Camila Machado**, Chief Revenue Officer, pressed for an enterprise push. "Enterprise has the expansion. Hire a second enterprise AE. Keep SMB flat. Platform investment."

**Pedro Silva**, MarVentures partner (the lead investor), in a Sunday-morning call: "Narrow. Law firms. I will support twelve quarters of slower revenue for a three-point-four LTV to CAC. I cannot support a broad strategy at A-plus."

---

## Options Under Consideration

- **Option A — Broad and Accelerate (the Machado plan):** ICP broad; CAC strategy aggressive; CS model current; pricing per-seat; product investment platform-extensibility. Expected Q1 ARR growth 5.2% QoQ; six-quarter trajectory leaves NRR at 106%, LTV:CAC at 2.4 — below Series-B benchmark on three of four metrics at Q6.

- **Option B — Narrow to Law Firms (the Borges/Silva plan):** ICP law firms; CAC strategy balanced; CS model intensive; pricing usage-based; product investment workflow-automation focus. Expected Q1 ARR decline 2.1% QoQ; six-quarter trajectory reaches NRR 121%, LTV:CAC 3.5, CAC payback 15 months, cash-runway positive at Q6.

- **Option C — Enterprise-Led Hybrid (Vianna's own draft):** ICP enterprise + law firms (drop boutique, keep accounting); CAC strategy balanced; CS model intensive for law firms, tech-touch for accounting; pricing hybrid; product investment vertical-depth focus. Expected Q1 ARR growth 1.8% QoQ; six-quarter trajectory reaches NRR 116%, LTV:CAC 3.1, CAC payback 17 months — just above benchmark.

Vianna looked at the four printed dashboards one last time and drew a fifth — blank — on a sheet of paper beside them. The MarVentures term sheet sat on the desk with an unsigned commitment line. Borges had texted at 8:42 p.m.: *"What are you going to do?"* He had not yet replied. The board off-site would open at 9:00 a.m. Six quarters stood between the current Helix and the Helix that would, or would not, raise a Series B.
