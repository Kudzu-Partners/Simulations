## Abstract

Apex Manufacturing Corp was, in the case universe, a Cleveland-headquartered industrial components manufacturer with USD 3.6B in 2025 revenue across twelve SKU families (bearings, precision castings, electro-mechanical assemblies, motion-control subsystems and fluid-handling components) serving automotive, aerospace, industrial-automation and energy-infrastructure end-markets, a 2025 Cost Index reading of 100 against an Asia-heavy benchmark of 92 (a cost-advantage reading on a rebased-2025 index), a 2025 Risk Score reading of 72 (higher is worse) against a diversified-footprint benchmark of 46, a Delivery Score of 81 against a benchmark of 86, a Flexibility Index of 34 against a benchmark of 62, and a 68% China-Vietnam supplier concentration across the twelve SKU families. The 2023–2025 operating model had been anchored on a decade-long Asia-low-cost sourcing discipline that had delivered unit-cost leadership inside each SKU family but had, by 2025, accumulated tariff, logistics-disruption and customer-demanded-dual-source exposures the Q1 2026 customer scorecards had, for the first time, flagged as a contract-eligibility risk across three of Apex's top-ten customers. The Q1 2026 board had approved a USD 220M resilience envelope and a Chief-Operating-Officer-led five-round commitment cycle against a Risk Score ceiling below 48, a Delivery floor above 86, a Flexibility floor above 58, a Cost Index ceiling below 106 and a composite NearShore Score above 68. The case opens at 5:42 a.m. on Monday, April 13, 2026, at the Apex Manufacturing Corp Cleveland East-Side operations office as **Tomás Reyes**, a 52-year-old Chief Operating Officer, prepares the Round-1 footprint diagnosis. Seventy-four days separate him from the June 26 board operations-and-resilience review.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Round-1 footprint-diagnosis commitment (expected-value-weighted envelope across tariff, geopolitical and logistics scenarios vs. cost-only envelope) and diagnose when the expected-value envelope dominates the cost-only envelope on the Risk-and-Delivery composite.
2. Analyze the Round-2 first-tariff-shock commitment (dual-source pre-positioning envelope vs. wait-and-see) and recommend the pre-positioning envelope against the wait-and-see envelope on the volume-retention and contract-eligibility basis.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 Mexico-vs-Asia trade-off (Mexico-plus-retained-Asia footprint vs. full-Asia vs. full-Mexico) and recommend the Mexico-plus-retained envelope on the cost-and-flexibility trade-off basis.
4. Respond to the Round-4 dual-sourcing build (certified-dual-source on critical SKU families vs. single-source) and recommend the dual-source envelope on the customer-contract-eligibility basis.
5. Design the Round-5 resilience-posture commitment that converts the footprint rebuild from a defensive tariff response into a commercial asset.

It's the line item you survive."* Seventy-four days separated him from the June 26 board operations-and-resilience review.

## The Protagonist

Tomás Reyes, 52, had assumed the Chief-Operating-Officer role at Apex Manufacturing Corp on October 1, 2024, from a Tier-1 North-American industrials group where he had, across fifteen years, led a 2019–2022 Mexico footprint build across six plants and had closed a 2023 dual-sourcing rebuild across a motion-control portfolio that had held delivery-performance above 92% through the 2024 Asia logistics disruption. Before that he had spent eight years as a manufacturing director at a Tier-1 automotive supplier. The Chief Executive Officer had recruited him in August 2024 specifically on two capabilities: his documented discipline of treating footprint decisions as expected-value problems under geopolitical uncertainty rather than as cost-variance problems, and his willingness to commit dual-source qualification capex ahead of the tariff curve. His 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the Risk Score ceiling and 20% to the Flexibility Index floor.

## The Decision

Reyes had to commit, by the May 5 board pre-review, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–5.

1. **Round 1 — Baseline footprint diagnosis.** Three archetypes: expected-value-weighted (tariff, geopolitical and logistics-disruption scenarios weighted across the twelve SKU families, risk-adjusted landed-cost view, Composite 76), cost-only (rebased landed-cost view without scenario weighting — Composite 34, exposure-trigger), or phased (expected-value on four SKU families only — Composite 54). Working envelope: expected-value-weighted.
2. **Round 2 — First tariff-shock response.** Three archetypes: dual-source-pre-position (initiate qualification on top-six SKU families across Mexico-plus-Vietnam before the H2 2026 tariff window, Composite 76), wait-and-see (retain full-Asia footprint, respond after shock — Composite 34, volume-retention floor trigger), or phased (pre-position on two SKU families — Composite 54). Working envelope: dual-source-pre-position.
3. **Round 3 — Mexico-vs-Asia trade-off.** Three vectors: Mexico-plus-retained-Asia (anchor production on Mexico for four SKU families, retain Asia for two cost-sensitive families, dual-source the remaining six, Composite 76), full-Asia (retain 68% concentration — Composite 32, risk-ceiling trigger), or full-Mexico (relocate all twelve SKU families — Composite 52, cost-ceiling trigger). Working envelope: Mexico-plus-retained-Asia.
4. **Round 4 — Dual-sourcing build.** Three vectors: certified-dual-source (certified-dual-source on top-six SKU families, qualification cycle compressed to nine months, Composite 76), single-source (single-source after footprint shift — Composite 36, delivery-floor trigger), or phased (dual-source on top-three SKU families — Composite 56). Working envelope: certified-dual-source.
5. **Round 5 — Resilience posture.** Three vectors: commercial-asset (published dual-source coverage, fast-response lead-time commitments, tariff-pass-through clauses with customers, Composite 78), defensive-compliance (dual-source held as internal risk measure only — Composite 42), or phased (commercial-asset on automotive and aerospace only — Composite 58). Working envelope: commercial-asset.

On Reyes' working model, the full envelope delivered Cost Index 104, Risk Score 44, Delivery 88, Flexibility 64, Composite 72.

## Company Background

Apex Manufacturing Corp had been founded in Cleveland in 1962 as a bearings house and had, across sixty-four years, grown into a twelve-SKU-family industrial-components group with USD 3.6B in 2025 revenue and a 68% China-Vietnam supplier concentration. The 2015–2023 operating record had delivered unit-cost leadership inside each SKU family but had, across 2024–2025, accumulated three specific exposures the February 2026 operations-review had named explicitly.

First, the 2024 H2 Asia-route logistics disruption had forced a twelve-week delivery delay on four SKU families that had triggered a USD 28M contractual-penalty exposure and a customer-scorecard downgrade at three top-ten accounts. Second, the 2025 Q3 tariff announcement across three of the twelve SKU families had triggered a 14% landed-cost increase that the incumbent cost-only diagnostic had not anticipated. Third, the Q1 2026 customer-scorecard cycle had, for the first time in Apex's forty-year relationship history, flagged "single-source geography" as a contract-eligibility risk across three top-ten customers.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2020–2025 nearshoring literature had, across 212 North-American industrial-components footprint decisions, documented three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, full-Asia footprint had delivered a 6–10-point Cost Index advantage inside each SKU family but had sustained a Risk Score of 68–78 and a Flexibility Index below 40 across the sample. Second, Mexico-plus-retained-Asia footprint had delivered a 2–4-point Cost Index penalty but had compressed Risk Score into a 42–50 band and had lifted Flexibility above 58. Third, full-Mexico footprint had delivered a 6–8-point Cost Index penalty across cost-sensitive SKU families.

Empirically, the post-2022 dual-sourcing literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, programmes that had certified dual-source on top-six SKU families had sustained Delivery above 86 in 84% of disruption episodes against 32% for single-source programmes. Second, programmes that had priced tariff-pass-through clauses with customers had preserved gross-margin above 28% in 78% of tariff-shock episodes.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the October 2025 operations-review, approved the USD 220M envelope and the June 26 review calendar. The Head of Supply Chain had, on February 5, delivered the 68% concentration map. The Head of Manufacturing Engineering had, on February 19, delivered the qualification-cycle blueprint for certified-dual-source. The Chief Financial Officer had, on March 3, confirmed the USD 220M envelope against the 2028 landed-cost trajectory. The Chief Commercial Officer had, on March 17, delivered the customer-scorecard eligibility matrix.

### What Reyes knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. An Expected-Value / Dual-Source-Pre-Position / Mexico-plus-Retained / Certified-Dual-Source / Commercial-Asset Envelope projected Composite 72. A Cost-Only / Wait-and-See / Full-Asia / Single-Source / Defensive Envelope projected Composite 28. A Hybrid Envelope projected Composite 54.

### What he did not know

Whether the Mexico Saltillo and Monterrey plant builds would clear against the 2026 labour-market compression; whether the Q3 2026 anticipated tariff window would compress to the pre-positioning window the team had modelled; whether the compressed nine-month qualification cycle would hold in aerospace SKU families under customer-approval constraints; and whether the tariff-pass-through commercial posture would survive the H2 2026 renewal cycle at the three at-risk top-ten accounts.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Supply Chain**: *"Dual-source pre-position on the top-six SKU families. Wait-and-see is a volume-retention floor we cannot defend."*

**The Head of Manufacturing Engineering**: *"Mexico-plus-retained. Full-Mexico is a cost-ceiling trigger on the cost-sensitive families."*

**The Chief Commercial Officer**: *"Commercial-asset posture. The three at-risk accounts will extend if we publish dual-source coverage before the Q3 renewal window."*

**The Chief Executive Officer**: *"Resilience isn't a line item. It's the line item you survive."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Reyes had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Expected-Value / Dual-Source-Pre-Position / Mexico-plus-Retained / Certified-Dual-Source / Commercial-Asset:** the recommended envelope. Cost Index 104. Risk Score 44. Delivery 88. Flexibility 64. Composite 72. Recommended.
- **Option B — Cost-Only / Wait-and-See / Full-Asia / Single-Source / Defensive:** the status-quo envelope. Composite 28. Rejected — risk-ceiling and customer-eligibility trigger.
- **Option C — Hybrid / Phased / Full-Mexico / Phased / Defensive:** the partial envelope. Composite 54. Rejected — cost-ceiling and flexibility forfeited.

Reyes' private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 2 — the first-tariff-shock pre-positioning — because it was the round that converted the subsequent footprint and qualification commitments from a defensive reaction into an ahead-of-the-curve customer commitment.
