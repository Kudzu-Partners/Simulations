# Caribia Mar: The Recovery Playbook

**Case Code:** STR-2026-1721 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1721 — *The Tourism Recovery Playbook* · **v1.0 · September 2026**

---

On the morning of Monday, June 22, 2026, Eugenio Ferrín-Esquivel read the May revenue report twice and stopped on a line he had passed over every month for fifteen months. Published room rates at Caribia Mar had never been higher; revenue had not moved. The forecast built the previous October still put the property's 2027 total revenue at US$51.2 million — what Caribia Mar had produced in 2025, after three years of what everyone in the building called the recovery.

In two weeks, on Monday, July 6, Ferrín would sit in the Caribia Resorts boardroom in the capital and hand the chief executive, Mercedes Caraballo de Ferrán, one document: the operating plan for the next eighteen months at the chain's flagship beachfront property. The chief financial officer had already fixed the numbers it had to reconcile to. Total revenue of US$55.3 million by the end of 2027 — the 2025 figure plus 8%. A property-level EBITDA margin of 23.6%, 120 basis points above the 22.4% Caribia Mar earned in 2025. A capital expenditure envelope of US$4.8 million, and nothing beyond it.

## The Protagonist

Ferrín-Esquivel, 44, had taken the general manager's chair at Caribia Mar in March 2025, recruited from a competitor's flagship on the Mexican Caribbean where he had been resident manager. His predecessor had run the property for nine years and left an operating playbook tuned against the demand curves of 2019: stable forecast shapes, group blocks booked far out, and a channel mix in which the property's own website and its trade accounts did most of the selling.

The board's brief when it hired him had been one sentence — rebuild the playbook. In fifteen months he had rebuilt the morning stand-up, the outlet schedules and half the middle-management bench. His standing in the firm rested on the document he would carry to the capital.

## The Decision

Ferrín had to commit, in one sitting and in a fixed order, to a reading of what had changed in the property's demand, then to the channel mix, the segment priorities, the capital programme and the wage structure that followed from it. Mercedes would take the plan line by line, asking whether each commitment was consistent with the one before. Whatever she signed was the operating spine through December 2027.

## The Company and its Setting

Caribia Resorts S.A. was a family-controlled regional resort operator headquartered in the capital of Caribia, a Caribbean-coast country whose tourism sector had passed its 2019 arrival volumes and settled slightly below them. Caribia Mar, on the North Coast, was the chain's flagship: 412 rooms, two beaches, a spa, two conference rooms and five food-and-beverage outlets, of which the main buffet carried the volume and three à la carte restaurants carried the price. Selva Verde, one of the three, had run a reduced-cover schedule for most of a year because the property could not keep line cooks.

The North Coast competitive set ran from international chain-managed resorts down to regional family operators. Caribia Mar sat between them, with a published rate close to the chains' and an online-agency dependency closer to the regional operators'.

## The Situation

The 2026 demand pattern did not resemble 2019 (see **Exhibit 1**). The property had 412 rooms in 2019 and 412 in 2025. The median booking had come in 84 days ahead in 2019 and 31 days ahead in 2025, with 28% of bookings landing inside the final seven days. Against 2019, the United States share of arrivals was up 12 points and the European share down 11; the average stay had fallen to 4.1 nights and the all-inclusive upgrade take-rate to 56%. Variable cost per occupied room was up 23% in nominal terms.

Online travel agencies took 41% of room nights in 2025, against 17% for the property's own website, 14% for travel-trade, 19% for group and wholesale, and 9% for walk-ins. The economics of the two largest channels were not close. A booking made on the property's own site netted US$300 a night, stayed 5.2 nights and came back 38% of the time. An agency booking netted US$256, stayed 3.6 nights and came back 14% of the time. Published research on resort markets put each point of agency share migrated to direct at US$0.45 to US$0.55 of net average daily rate, and the sustainable pace of that migration at no more than two points a year.

Inside the property, food and beverage produced 24% of revenue against 64% from rooms and 12% from everything else. The buffet took 71% of covers in 2025 against 58% in 2019, and food-and-beverage revenue per occupied room had fallen from US$80 to US$57. The rooms were due: the chief financial officer's note required their renovation by the third quarter of 2027, at US$1.8 million, ahead of any other capital item (see **Exhibit 2**).

The labour line was the one Ferrín handled every morning. Back-of-house turnover stood at 38% against a 2019 baseline of 19%: housekeeping at 47% and paid 3% below the sector, food-and-beverage back-of-house at 41% and paid 2% below. Sales and management were paid above sector. Industry work put the replacement cost of a trained line cook at 1.4 times annual wage, and found that wage adjustments of 12% to 18%, paired with structured career paths, cut resort back-of-house turnover by 35% to 45% within two years.

Total revenue, property EBITDA margin, capital committed against the envelope and back-of-house turnover were the four measures Ferrín would answer for. Entering the session they stood at US$51.2 million, 22.4%, nothing of the US$4.8 million, and 38%. Held flat, none of them moved, and the envelope went on the room renovation and whatever the remaining US$3.0 million bought.

## What Ferrín Controlled

The plan had five parts, taken in sequence.

**The reading.** He had to name which two of four movements were the binding constraints on 2026 demand, and in which order: booking lead time, source-market mix, channel migration, variable-cost inflation.

**The channel plan.** A twelve-month target for agency share, between 20% and 50% of room nights, and for direct share, between 15% and 40%. Then one posture toward the agencies: hold the existing parity-rate agreements, renegotiate to a 14% commission with a volume floor, or move to net-rate contracting.

**The segment map.** One stance on Europe: fund a European recovery campaign out of the brand budget, accept a structurally smaller European share and move the money to US premium demand, or defend only the highest-value European cells. One priority segment from four — US premium leisure couples, US premium leisure families, European long-haul leisure, or corporate group wholesale. And one segment to step back from: regional Latin American, the sub-1% corporate-group cells, US mainstream leisure families, or Canada.

**The mix and the capital programme.** A 2027 food-and-beverage ratio: hold at 24%, lift to 28% by re-energising the à la carte outlets and the upgrade take-rate, or take 22% and move the capacity into spa and water sports. Then a capital selection from a twelve-item menu inside the US$4.8 million envelope, the US$1.8 million room renovation already fixed (see **Exhibit 2**). And a sustainability posture: stay at the Global Sustainable Tourism Council's awareness level with no certification, take the basic hotel certification, or commit to full certification with a measured carbon baseline.

**The talent plan.** A wage adjustment between 0% and 25% for housekeeping and, separately, for food-and-beverage back-of-house. Then any combination of five retention programmes: tenure bonuses of US$200 a month after twelve months, referral bonuses of US$150 per successful hire, a cross-functional training and career-path programme, a family-leave and shift-flexibility programme, and a recognition and career-pathing programme.

## Closing

At 7:20 that morning Ferrín laid the May report, the capital menu and the channel table side by side on the desk. Four of his directors would take a section each; the document carried his name. Mercedes convened on July 6.
