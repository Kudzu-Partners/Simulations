## Abstract

Imprenta Creativa S.R.L. was, in the case universe, a three-product commercial print operation in the Ciudad Vieja of Montevideo — approximately UYU 38m in 2025 revenue, eleven employees, one 240-square-metre production facility on Calle Juan Carlos Gómez, a three-SKU product line (Flyers accounting for 58% of revenue at an average unit price of UYU 50 and an estimated variable cost of UYU 39; Brochures accounting for 27% at UYU 180 average price and UYU 106 variable cost; Large-Format prints accounting for 15% at UYU 1,200 average price and UYU 470 variable cost), three years of aggregate profitability that had run at 6–9% net margin, and a founder-controller-accountant triangle in which no one had built a per-product P&L. The March 2026 quarterly management meeting had revealed a founder hypothesis that Flyers were subsidising Brochures; the controller had run a parallel hypothesis that the actual subsidy direction was the reverse; and the April 2026 primer exercise had been commissioned to resolve the disagreement empirically. The case opens at 3:18 p.m. on Thursday, April 23, 2026, at the firm's Ciudad Vieja controller's desk as **Luciana Torres**, a 33-year-old controller, prepares the three-product diagnostic.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Classify each of twenty April cost-line items as a true unit-variable cost (consumable inks, paper stock, per-click printer-lease charges, sales commissions) versus a period fixed cost (rent, salaried designer payroll, basic utilities, insurance) versus a mixed cost that must be decomposed, and articulate why misclassifying a fixed cost as variable biases the contribution-margin calculation downward and corrupts every downstream profitability inference.
2. Calculate the per-product contribution margin (price − variable cost) and contribution-margin ratio (CM divided by price) for each of the three products — Flyers at 22% CM ratio, Brochures at 41%, Large-Format at 61% — and articulate why the volume leader (Flyers at 58% of revenue) is the worst margin earner and why revenue-share-of-mix is a different thing from profit-share-of-mix.
3. Calculate per-product break-even volumes under the fixed-cost-allocation assumption — Flyers at 12,700 units, Brochures at 3,900 units, Large-Format at 620 units — and articulate why each product sits differently relative to its break-even (Flyers at 14,000 units barely past break-even; Large-Format at 900 units deep past) and why the allocation rule itself is a choice rather than a fact.
4. Refuse the naive "discontinue Flyers" decision through Colin Drury's relevant-cost principle, trace why the fixed costs allocated to Flyers do not disappear if Flyers are discontinued and are therefore sunk from the decision's perspective, and articulate why any product that covers its variable cost and contributes positively to the fixed-cost pool should generally be retained under the short-horizon decision even if its allocated-cost P&L shows it as "unprofitable".
5. Apply the Cooper-and-Kaplan Activity-Based Costing overlay to the three products, trace how Large-Format's per-unit true cost increases by approximately 38% once setup time, customer service and quality inspection are allocated on activity-consumption rather than on volume, and articulate why ABC reshuffles the per-product true-profit ranking from the volume-based apparent ranking and why this primer frame is distinct from the CFO Command (sim 210) and Cost Lab (sim 209) frames elsewhere in the catalogue.

Utilidad es atribución. Antes de discontinuar, relevancia."*

## The Protagonist

Torres, 33, had been Controller at Imprenta Creativa since 2022, had completed a Contador Público degree at the Universidad Oriental del Plata in Montevideo in 2014, had spent five years at a Big-Four Montevideo audit office in the commerce-and-manufacturing group, and had joined the firm as its first in-house controller when the founder had decided to end the outsourced-accounting relationship that had run from 2008 through 2021. Her controller instincts, she had told the founder at the 2024 annual review, had been shaped by two teachings: an audit engagement at a Montevideo textile exporter during which she had watched a naive product-discontinuation decision cost the client approximately USD 340,000 in unrecoverable fixed-cost absorption; and a two-semester managerial accounting sequence she had audited in 2019 at the Universidad Ciudadela in which Colin Drury's relevant-cost framework had been taught as the controller's single most important intellectual tool. The founder had assigned her the April primer exercise on three considerations: her willingness to disagree with him about the flyer-subsidy hypothesis; her comfort applying the Drury relevant-cost test to a live decision; and her command of the Cooper-Kaplan ABC overlay against a small-operator cost structure.

---

## The Decision

Torres had to commit, by the 4:30 p.m. founder meeting, a three-product profitability diagnostic with four clear outputs and a single recommendation.

1. **Output 1 — Cost classification.** Twenty April cost-line items tagged fixed, variable or mixed. The mixed items (mainly utilities and one hybrid-lease printer) decomposed into their fixed and variable components. Any misclassification that would bias the contribution-margin calculation flagged.
2. **Output 2 — Per-product contribution margin.** Flyers 22% CM ratio; Brochures 41%; Large-Format 61%. Revenue-share-of-mix versus profit-share-of-mix reconciled and presented as two separate pie charts rather than one.
3. **Output 3 — Per-product break-even.** Flyers 12,700 units against actual volume 14,000; Brochures 3,900 against 3,500 (below break-even); Large-Format 620 against 900. The fixed-cost-allocation method disclosed (volume-based for the baseline calculation).
4. **Output 4 — Refusal of naive flyer-discontinuation and ABC overlay.** Drury's relevant-cost test applied. The founder's proposed discontinuation modelled: fixed costs unchanged, flyer contribution margin lost, total firm profit drops approximately 30%. Cooper-Kaplan ABC overlay run: Large-Format true cost up 38%, its apparent 61% margin reduces to approximately 40%; Brochures essentially unchanged; Flyers true cost down marginally.

Commitments: the four outputs; the refusal of the flyer-discontinuation decision; a one-page recommendation memo for the founder; and a three-sentence paragraph the founder can read at the Friday team-stand-up that explains why the best-selling product is not the worst product even though it is the worst margin earner.

## Sectoral Background

The 2020–2025 Uruguayan commercial-print category had, in the case universe, grown from approximately UYU 3.4bn to UYU 4.6bn in nominal revenue, concentrated in three segments (mass-production flyer and direct-mail; specialty brochure and marketing collateral; large-format signage and exhibition graphics) and four operator tiers (national chains; regional multi-facility independents; small single-facility independents like Imprenta Creativa; micro-operators and copy shops). The 2025 single-facility-independent segment registered an average gross margin of 31%, an average net margin of 6%, and an average product-portfolio count of 2.4 products, with significant variance across operators driven primarily by per-product cost visibility rather than by volume or pricing.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1923–2025 managerial-accounting literature produced three findings the primer sim had to respect. First, the cost-volume-profit tradition — traceable to James O. McKinsey's 1922 cost-analysis writing and codified in Charles Horngren's mid-20th-century management-accounting texts — treated contribution margin as the per-unit economic lever rather than as a residual, and produced documented product-rationalisation readings approximately 22–38% more accurate than gross-margin-only regimes on comparable multi-SKU panels. Second, Colin Drury's relevant-cost framework, applied to discontinuation decisions in the 1985 managerial-accounting text and subsequent editions, produced documented avoid-the-naive-discontinuation rates approximately 3.4× those of allocated-cost-only regimes on panel decisions. Third, Robin Cooper and Robert Kaplan's Activity-Based Costing, introduced in the 1988 *Harvard Business Review* article and operationalised in the 1991 book, produced documented per-product true-cost readings that reshuffled the apparent-profit ranking in approximately 44% of the panel's products and was the single largest source of strategic product-portfolio re-evaluation in mid-market operators during the 1990–2010 cycle.

The post-2010 managerial-accounting literature added two findings. The time-driven ABC variant — Kaplan and Steven Anderson's 2004 simplification — produced documented implementation-cost reductions of approximately 58% against classical ABC on equivalent small-operator engagements. And the controller-as-business-partner tradition elevated the per-product-P&L visibility to a recurring-management-meeting deliverable in the 2018–2024 cycle, which redistributed decision authority on product-portfolio questions from the founder's instinct to the controller's analytic output.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The March 2026 quarterly management meeting had included a founder remark that the flyers were "eating the brochures' margin" and a recommendation to discontinue flyers. The controller's March 24 memo had flagged that the hypothesis was inverted relative to what per-product data would show and had requested a one-cycle diagnostic. The founder had approved the diagnostic on April 2. The April 23 primer exercise was the diagnostic's delivery session.

### What Torres knew

The twenty-line April cost-classification list; the three-product contribution-margin estimates from the March panel; the per-product break-even volumes against actual volumes; the Drury relevant-cost test and its application to the flyer-discontinuation proposal; the Cooper-Kaplan ABC activity-driver estimates from a preliminary March run; and the founder's appetite for a clear recommendation rather than a hedge.

### What she did not know

Whether the founder would accept a "refuse the discontinuation" recommendation against his own hypothesis or would push back; whether the ABC activity-driver estimates for Large-Format setup-time and customer-service had been measured accurately enough to support a 38% true-cost increase with confidence; whether the sales commissions structure needed to be redesigned in light of the per-product true-margin ranking; whether the Brochures below-break-even reading was a one-period drop or a structural change; and whether the founder would accept a second-cycle diagnostic on the sales-commission question or would treat it as out of scope.

### What others were saying

**The Founder**: *"Los folletos matan el margen de los brochures. Sácalos del catálogo. Tres meses."*

**The Head of Production**: *"El gran formato ocupa tres veces el tiempo de setup que los folletos. Eso nunca lo hemos visto en el costo."*

**The Senior Designer**: *"Los brochures son el producto estrella del portafolio creativo. El CM alto es porque los diseñamos una vez y los imprimimos veinte."*

**The Sales Manager**: *"Las comisiones son parejas por ingreso. Si el verdadero ranking de margen es otro, la compensación está mal."*

**The External Accountant (Montevideo CPA firm)**: *"El costo variable de los flyers incluye el papel y la tinta. El costo asignado incluye también un prorrateo de alquiler. Si discontinúas flyers, el alquiler sigue ahí."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Torres had narrowed the diagnostic-output space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — Full CM analysis + Drury relevant-cost refusal of discontinuation + ABC overlay + reshuffled ranking + sales-commission flag (Recommended).** The four outputs produced cleanly; the discontinuation refused on relevant-cost grounds; the ABC overlay run and the per-product ranking reshuffled; the sales-commission design flagged as a follow-up cycle. Coherent with the managerial-accounting literature; coherent with the founder's desire for a clear recommendation rather than a hedge.
- **Option B — Full CM analysis + ABC overlay + *accept* the discontinuation recommendation on the volume-based allocated P&L.** Rejected — the volume-based allocation shows Flyers as a loss-maker on the allocated P&L, but the relevant-cost analysis shows that discontinuation reduces total firm profit by approximately 30% because the allocated fixed costs do not disappear. Accepting the recommendation would land on the single largest panel-documented naive-discontinuation error.
- **Option C — Full CM analysis only; skip ABC and skip the discontinuation test.** Rejected — the diagnostic's core deliverable is the discontinuation-decision defensibility and the per-product true-ranking; stopping at CM is the standard first-year-student exercise rather than the primer's full arc.

Torres's private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the single output most correlated with the founder's accept-or-reject response was the Drury relevant-cost demonstration of the 30% profit drop under the naive-discontinuation path, because a founder will accept a recommendation against his own hypothesis if the recommendation is presented as a preventable unforced error rather than as a disagreement. The *Dónde Está el Dinero* primer sits at the undergraduate managerial-accounting tier in the 2026 Express catalogue, distinct from AndinaConsumerProducts Cost Lab (sim 209, CFO-level multi-SKU cost re-architecture) and AndinaIndustrial CFO Command (sim 210, full CFO command-desk multi-quarter). Primer 295 emphasises the first-pass CM-plus-relevant-cost-plus-ABC diagnostic for the controller without a full CFO stack; 209 emphasises the CFO-level cost-lab multi-SKU re-architecture; 210 emphasises the multi-quarter CFO command desk.
