## Abstract

FlexSpace was, in the case universe, a LATAM co-working operator with USD 4.08M in cash, five locations across two cities, 300 desks in total, a hot-desk occupancy of 70%, a dedicated-desk occupancy of 61%, a private-office occupancy of 43%, a blended occupancy of 58%, a monthly churn of 20% (implying an average member tenure of five months), an NPS of 62, an annual revenue run-rate near zero at start, and a technology level of 1 (basic). On Monday, January 12, 2026, the Board approved a three-round strategic commitment inside which the Chief Executive would commit pricing-and-community architecture in Round 1, expansion architecture in Round 2, and B2B-partnership architecture in Round 3 — against a post-pandemic co-working market fragmented by twelve peer operators in the same two cities, three of them VC-funded. Natalia Duarte, a 41-year-old former hospitality operator who had founded FlexSpace in 2021, faced a Board mandate that the three-round trajectory had to lift blended occupancy above 75%, compress monthly churn below 10%, lift member tenure above 12 months, open a B2B-enterprise-flex revenue line above 25% of total revenue, and close Round 3 on a composite above 80. The case opens at 5:32 a.m. on Monday, January 19, 2026, at the FlexSpace flagship location on the creative-industries corridor, as Duarte prepares the Round-1 pricing-and-community commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Round-1 pricing-and-community commitment (hot-desk at USD 250 flat, dedicated at USD 750 flat, office at USD 3,500 flat, community budget doubled to USD 4K per location per month, marketing at USD 180K, technology to level 2) and diagnose when a price-hold-plus-community-double commitment dominates price-flex on churn compression.
2. Analyze the Round-2 expansion commitments (USD 30 price lift on dedicated, USD 200 price lift on office, hot-desk held, community to USD 5K per location, two new locations at USD 820K capex in member-commute neighborhoods, technology to level 3 with coaching-and-mentorship matching) and recommend the expansion-as-same-product trajectory consistent with an NPS headroom signal.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 B2B-partnership commitments (three anchor enterprise clients at 40 desks each on six-month pilots, community to USD 6K per location, two more locations at USD 780K capex, technology to level 4 with enterprise portal, pricing held) and design the enterprise-flex launch that holds individual-member community while opening B2B as 32% of revenue.
4. Design a three-round integrated sequence that pays for expansion with the community-driven churn compression and pays for B2B with the expansion that made nine-location coverage credible.
5. Recommend a complete three-round sequence, identify the round most correlated with the Round-3 composite under the twenty-percent-monthly-churn starting constraint, and write the Board-facing paragraph the Round-1-to-Round-3 trajectory would anchor.

On the morning of **Monday, January 19, 2026**, **Natalia Duarte** arrived at the FlexSpace flagship location on Calle de las Artes at 5:32 a.m., opened the Round-1 pricing-and-community sheet her Head of Member Experience and Head of Growth had finalized at 11:16 p.m. the night before, and re-read the sentence she had pinned to the flagship community-bar in December 2022: "We do not win on month one. We win on month twelve — and the only lever that moves month twelve is community." Twelve days separated her from the January 31 Board commitment.

---

## The Protagonist

Natalia Duarte, 41, had founded FlexSpace in June 2021 after ten years as a hospitality operator (two boutique hotel general-manager roles plus a regional director role at a serviced-apartment operator) and two years as an independent hospitality consultant. A graduate of the Universidad de Hotelería Internacional with a hospitality-operations certification from Wrenfield University's Hotel School, she had, across the first four years of FlexSpace, built the five-location 300-desk footprint the case studied on a sequence of a USD 600K founder-and-family round (Q3 2021), a USD 1.4M angel round led by a regional hospitality investor (Q2 2022), and a USD 2.0M seed extension in Q4 2024 that had funded the fifth-location opening and built the USD 4.08M cash balance the case opened with.

## The Decision

Duarte had to commit, by the January 31 Board commitment, the Round-1 sheet and the Round-2 and Round-3 intent:

1. **Round-1 Pricing and Community** — whether to hold hot-desk at USD 250 flat (the churn data says community-not-price moves the twelfth-month decision), dedicated at USD 750 flat, office at USD 3,500 flat, double the community budget from USD 2K to USD 4K per location per month (USD 24K monthly across five — the line that funds events, programming, and the community-manager role), lift marketing to USD 180K from USD 150K, open zero new locations, and move technology from level 1 to level 2 (access control, booking system, member app).
2. **Round-2 Expansion** — whether to lift pricing lightly (USD 30 on dedicated to USD 780, USD 200 on office to USD 3,700, hot-desk held) on the NPS-headroom signal, lift community to USD 5K per location, lift marketing to USD 220K, open two new locations at USD 820K capex total in neighborhoods the Round-1 data showed members commuting from (not new markets), and lift technology to level 3 with a coaching-and-mentorship-matching feature (the data says one mentorship connection cuts churn by 70%).
3. **Round-3 B2B Partnerships** — whether to launch enterprise-flex with three anchor clients at 40 desks each on six-month renewable pilots (dedicated office area plus access to all nine locations), lift community to USD 6K per location, lift marketing to USD 280K, open two more locations at USD 780K capex total, lift technology to level 4 with an enterprise-portal for the anchor-client HR to manage membership, and hold pricing.

The three sub-decisions would compound. On Duarte's working model, Round 1 delivered blended occupancy at 68% (from 58), monthly churn at 14% (from 20), NPS at 71 (from 62), annual revenue at USD 3.21M run-rate, annual profit at USD 380K, cash at USD 3.6M, and member tenure at 7.1 months (from 5). Round 2 delivered seven locations, 420 desks, blended occupancy at 71% (new locations hit 60% in Q1, faster than industry average because the Round-1 playbook transferred), churn at 11%, NPS at 74, annual revenue at USD 5.8M, profit at USD 1.1M, cash at USD 3.4M, and tenure at 9.2 months. Round 3 delivered nine locations, 540 desks, blended occupancy at 78%, churn at 8% (from 20), NPS at 76, annual revenue at USD 9.2M, profit at USD 2.4M, cash at USD 2.6M, B2B revenue at 32% of total (from 0), tenure at 12.4 months (nearly tripled), and a composite of 83.

## Company Background

FlexSpace had been founded in June 2021 in the creative-industries corridor of the capital's metropolitan area as a single-location 62-desk hospitality-driven co-working operator and had, across four-and-a-half years, built the five-location 300-desk two-city footprint the case studied. The 2025 location portfolio comprised three flagship locations in the capital and two neighborhood locations in the second city, with a desk mix of 45% hot-desk, 38% dedicated, and 17% private office. The 2025 community programming included a weekly industry-speaker series, three monthly networking events per location, one quarterly cross-location member retreat, and a member-mentorship pilot that had run in only one location and that the data suggested had driven the 62 NPS.

The Board's December 2025 strategy dossier had identified three specific pressures the three-round envelope would inherit. First, the 2025 twelve-peer-operator competitive map had, across the two cities, placed FlexSpace at the middle of the pricing range (neither the lowest nor the highest) and at the top of the NPS range on a community-programming-driven differentiation — a pattern that framed the Round-1 price-hold-plus-community-double commitment. Second, the 2025 churn-driver analysis had, across a 600-member exit-interview sample, placed community-as-reason-for-leaving-or-staying at 58% of stated reasons against price-as-reason at 14% — a pattern that framed the Round-1 community-commitment. Third, the Q4 2025 post-pandemic enterprise-flex commercial press had, across eighteen LATAM enterprise-flex commitments at mid-sized corporates, placed the 40-desk-six-month-renewable-pilot structure as the anchor commitment that had converted 78% of pilots into multi-year contracts — a pattern that framed the Round-3 B2B commitment.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM co-working operator landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2021–2024 community-driven-retention evidence base had, across a commercial-summary sample of 16 LATAM-and-peer co-working operators that had committed community budget above USD 3.5K per location per month, documented a monthly-churn compression of 6–10 percentage points within 12 months and an NPS lift of 8–14 points — a pattern that monetized the Round-1 commitment. Second, the expansion-as-same-product evidence base had, across a 2020–2024 sample of 11 LATAM co-working operators that had expanded into member-commute neighborhoods (rather than new markets), produced a new-location-Q1 occupancy of 55–65% against a 35–45% median for new-market expansions — a pattern that anchored the Round-2 commitment. Third, the enterprise-flex evidence base had, across a 2022–2024 sample of 9 LATAM co-working operators that had launched six-month renewable pilots with 40-desk-or-more anchors, produced a Year-1 B2B-revenue-share of 22–35% of total revenue at operators that had cleared the nine-location coverage threshold — a trajectory that anchored the Round-3 commitment.

Empirically, the post-2020 co-working literature had two robust findings the envelope had to respect. First, operators that had sequenced community (Round 1) ahead of expansion (Round 2) ahead of B2B (Round 3) had, across a 2020–2024 sample of 17 LATAM peers, closed the composite 2.0 times higher than operators that had sequenced expansion-first. Second, operators that had held member tenure above 9 months through a B2B-launch window had, across the same sample, preserved NPS above 72 against a 58 median for tenure-below-7-months peers — a signal that the B2B-launch had not diluted the individual-member experience.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Board had, in the December 2025 off-site, approved a three-round composite target of 80, a Round-3 blended-occupancy floor of 75%, a monthly-churn ceiling of 10%, a member-tenure floor of 12 months, and a B2B-revenue-share floor of 25%. The Head of Member Experience had, on January 5, circulated a Round-1 pre-reading that proposed the price-hold-plus-community-double envelope. The Head of Growth had, on January 8, signed off on the Round-1 price-hold conditional on the Round-2 NPS-headroom-driven price lift. The Chair of the Board (a hospitality-investor-turned-director) had, on January 12, argued for the Round-3 B2B launch conditional on the Round-2 nine-location coverage credibility.

### What Duarte knew

The team had modeled three three-round envelopes. A Community-Expansion-B2B Envelope (Round-1 price-hold-plus-community-double; Round-2 two-locations-plus-mentorship-matching; Round-3 B2B-at-three-anchors) projected a Round-3 composite of 83. A Price-Cut Envelope (Round-1 hot-desk at USD 225, dedicated at USD 700) projected a composite of 58 — churn moved modestly but NPS compressed and expansion capex was unfunded. A Expansion-First Envelope (Round-1 two new locations before community-double) projected a composite of 62 — member tenure stalled and B2B launch deferred.

### What she did not know

Whether the three anchor-enterprise-flex counterparties would sign pilots inside the Round-3 window or slip; whether the coaching-and-mentorship-matching feature would deliver the modeled 70%-churn-cut or a lesser signal; whether the two Round-3 new locations would clear the member-commute-neighborhood lease-and-permitting framework; and whether the enterprise-portal software build would ship inside the Round-3 window on the Head-of-Growth-and-CTO roadmap.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Member Experience**: *"Round-1 community-double. Twenty-percent churn is a community problem, not a price problem."*

**The Head of Growth**: *"Round-2 expansion-as-same-product. New locations in member-commute neighborhoods hit 60% in Q1. New markets do not."*

**The Board Chair**: *"Round-3 B2B-at-nine-locations. Enterprise flex needs nine-location coverage. We cannot sell eight."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Duarte had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Community-Expansion-B2B:** Round-1 price-hold-plus-community-double-plus-tech-level-2; Round-2 expansion-plus-mentorship-matching; Round-3 B2B-at-three-anchors-plus-enterprise-portal. Round-3 composite 83. Aligns with HoME, HoG, Board Chair. Recommended.

- **Option B — Price-Cut:** Round-1 hot-desk USD 225, dedicated USD 700. Round-3 composite 58. Churn moved; NPS compressed; expansion unfunded. Rejected.

- **Option C — Expansion-First:** Round-1 two new locations before community-double. Round-3 composite 62. Tenure stalled; B2B deferred. Rejected.

Duarte's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the Round-3 composite was Round 1 — the community-double commitment, which compressed churn from 20% to 14% in five months, lifted tenure from 5 to 7.1 months, and created the NPS headroom that funded every subsequent pricing, expansion, and B2B commitment.
