## Abstract

EnvíoRápido was, in the case universe, a LatAm e-commerce fulfillment operator serving marketplace-and-D2C retailers across Mexico and selected Colombia corridors, with USD 220M in 2025 revenue, a 2025 year-end operating fleet of 140,000 monthly active SKUs across four secondary facilities, a 2025 cost-per-order reading of USD 2.40 against a category benchmark of USD 1.95, a 2025 fulfillment-time reading of 22 hours against 14 hours, a 2025 accuracy reading of 94% against 98%, a 2025 customer-NPS reading of 35 against 52, and a Q3 2025 real-estate commitment of USD 32M to a 25,000-sqm greenfield fulfillment-centre in Guadalajara opening in Q3 2026. The Q4 2025 board had approved a USD 18.6M twelve-month operations-build envelope — the WarehouseOps programme — and had positioned it as the operator's response to three overlapping pressures: a 2025 Comprandia-Mexico expansion that had moved the category cost-per-order benchmark from USD 2.10 to USD 1.95 and accelerated the automation-capex threshold, a 2025 Buen-Fin-plus-Black-Friday Q4 peak that had priced the category demand multiple at 4.0× baseline against a 2.8× legacy-infrastructure ceiling, and a 2025 returns-rate benchmark of 18% against a legacy 11% that had priced reverse-logistics as the single largest 2026 operating-risk exposure. The case opens at 6:48 a.m. on Thursday, April 23, 2026, at the EnvíoRápido Mexico City head office as **Alejandra Quintanilla-Bravo**, a 41-year-old Vice President of Operations, prepares the Round-1 facility-design commitment. Sixty-six days separate her from the June 28 board operations-review session.

## The Protagonist

Quintanilla-Bravo, 41, had assumed the Vice-President-of-Operations role at EnvíoRápido on March 1, 2024, after thirteen years across fulfillment-centre operations, automation-capex and last-mile-network leadership at a Mexican e-commerce operator and a Monterrey-based 3PL. She had, in 2021–2023, co-led the 30,000-sqm Monterrey-facility launch that had cleared the Year-1 cost-per-order at USD 2.08 against a USD 2.30 plan and had absorbed a 3.6× peak-multiple inside the first Buen-Fin cycle. The Chief Executive Officer had recruited her in January 2024 specifically on two capabilities: her documented discipline of anchoring facility design on hybrid-zone philosophies rather than bulk-anchored defaults, and her willingness to defend integrated-automation-capex envelopes against milestone-phased pressure. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the cost-per-order ceiling, 25% to the fulfillment-time ceiling and 20% to the NPS floor.

## The Decision

Quintanilla-Bravo had to commit, by the May 14 operations-council pre-review, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–5.

1. **Round 1 — Facility design.** Three archetypes: four-zone-92 (Storage 40% / Picking 25% / Packing 17% / Returns 10%, four dock doors, hybrid-storage, Composite 78), bulk-anchored (Composite 46, forward-pick bottleneck), or forward-pick-anchored (Composite 42, storage-depth fragility). Working envelope: four-zone-92.
2. **Round 2 — Automation capex.** Four archetypes: integrated (USD 4M, 50% throughput uplift, goods-to-person + sortation + pick-to-light, Composite 78), goods-to-person-only (USD 3.5M, 40% uplift — Composite 58), sortation-only (USD 2.8M, 25% uplift — Composite 48), or pick-to-light-only (USD 1.2M, 18% uplift — Composite 38). Working envelope: integrated.
3. **Round 3 — Peak-season response.** Three vectors: temporary-capacity-plus-wave-picking (290K-per-day absorption on a 200K baseline, Composite 78), overtime-only (Composite 44), or defer (Composite 22, NPS collapse). Working envelope: integrated.
4. **Round 4 — Last-mile network.** Three vectors: hybrid-plus-micro-fulfillment (own-fleet core, 3PL surge, micro-fulfillment on same-day corridors, cost-per-order USD 1.95, Composite 78), own-fleet (Composite 56, higher fixed cost), or 3PL-only (Composite 52, SLA fragility). Working envelope: hybrid-plus-micro-fulfillment.
5. **Round 5 — Returns-tsunami archetype.** Three vectors: integrated-reverse-logistics (18%-return-rate absorption, automated sortation, restock-cycle discipline, Composite 78), contracted (Composite 48), or deferred (Composite 18, NPS minus-14). Working envelope: integrated.

On Quintanilla-Bravo's working model, a Four-Zone / Integrated-Capex / Temporary-Plus-Wave / Hybrid-Micro / Integrated-Reverse envelope delivered Cost per Order USD 1.95, Fulfillment Time 14h, Accuracy 98%, NPS 45, Composite 74.

## Company Background

EnvíoRápido had been founded in Guadalajara in 2016 and had, across ten years, built a USD 220M-revenue e-commerce fulfillment operator with four secondary facilities, a customer base of 48 marketplace-and-D2C retailers, and a 2025 package-throughput of 36M annualised shipments. The 2020–2025 operating cycle had left the group on a cost-per-order reading 23% above the Comprandia-Mexico-anchored category benchmark and a fulfillment-time reading 57% above the category — the November 2025 diagnostic had priced the cumulative gap at USD 11.8M in annual forgone operating margin. The Q4 2025 board-approved USD 18.6M WarehouseOps envelope had been positioned explicitly as a five-round rebuild of facility design, automation capex, peak-response, last-mile-network and reverse-logistics architecture.

Three Q1 2026 events were named in the March 2026 operations dossier. First, the December 2025 real-estate-close had finalised the 25,000-sqm Guadalajara greenfield facility with a Q3 2026 go-live target. Second, the January 2026 Buen-Fin-plus-Black-Friday-2026 forecast had priced the Q4 demand multiple at 4.0× baseline — 290K packages per day against the 200K baseline capacity. Third, the February 2026 returns-rate benchmark had priced the Q4 2026 return-rate at 18% against a legacy 11% reading.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2019–2025 LatAm fulfillment-operations literature had, across 28 documented greenfield facilities above 20,000 sqm, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, four-zone facility designs at 88–94% utilisation with hybrid-storage philosophies had delivered cost-per-order readings 18–26% below bulk-anchored or forward-pick-anchored envelopes in 72% of cases. Second, integrated-automation-capex envelopes above USD 12M had delivered throughput uplifts of 42–56% against single-system envelopes of 20–40% and had cleared 12–18 month payback windows in 74% of cases. Third, hybrid-plus-micro-fulfillment last-mile networks had delivered cost-per-order readings 12–22% below own-fleet envelopes and 6–14% below 3PL-only envelopes in 68% of cases.

Empirically, the post-2022 reverse-logistics literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, integrated-reverse-logistics envelopes on 15–20% Q4 return rates had preserved NPS readings inside a plus-eight band in 68% of cases, against minus-14 bands for deferred envelopes. Second, wave-picking peak-response protocols had absorbed 3.5–4.5× peak multiples on a single-facility basis without triggering sustained-period fulfillment-time erosions in 62% of cases.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the December 2025 session, approved the USD 18.6M envelope and the June 28 operations-review calendar. The Head of Facility Engineering had, on March 5, delivered the facility-design workbook. The Head of Automation had, on March 12, delivered the automation-capex stack. The Head of Last-Mile Operations had, on March 19, delivered the last-mile-network brief. The Head of Returns had, on March 26, delivered the reverse-logistics dossier.

### What Quintanilla-Bravo knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. A Four-Zone / Integrated-Capex / Temporary-Plus-Wave / Hybrid-Micro / Integrated-Reverse envelope projected Composite 74. A Bulk-Anchored / Sortation-Only / Overtime / 3PL / Contracted envelope projected Composite 32. A Forward-Pick / Goods-to-Person-Only / Defer / Own-Fleet / Deferred envelope projected Composite 26.

### What she did not know

Whether the Buen-Fin 2026 peak would exceed the 4.0× forecast under a Comprandia-Mexico price-war scenario; whether the 3PL surge capacity would clear the Q4 window at contracted SLAs; whether the Colombian corridor expansion would clear the Q2 2027 threshold; and whether the hybrid-storage philosophy would absorb a marketplace-client SKU-expansion beyond 180K.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Facility Engineering**: *"Four-zone, ninety-two percent utilisation, hybrid storage. The geometry of day-one decides every subsequent round."*

**The Head of Automation**: *"Integrated. Goods-to-person plus sortation plus pick-to-light. Four million. Fifty-percent throughput uplift. Payback under eighteen months if volume shows up."*

**The Head of Last-Mile Operations**: *"Hybrid plus micro-fulfillment. One ninety-five per order. The routing software is the earn-it line."*

**The Chief Executive Officer**: *"Every shortcut ships."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Quintanilla-Bravo had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Four-Zone / Integrated-Capex / Temporary-Plus-Wave / Hybrid-Micro / Integrated-Reverse:** the recommended envelope. Cost per Order USD 1.95. Fulfillment Time 14h. Accuracy 98%. NPS 45. Composite 74. Recommended.
- **Option B — Bulk-Anchored / Sortation-Only / Overtime / 3PL / Contracted:** the minimalist envelope. Composite 32. Rejected — NPS collapse probability above 68% under Q4 peak.
- **Option C — Forward-Pick / Goods-to-Person-Only / Defer / Own-Fleet / Deferred:** the partial envelope. Composite 26. Rejected — facility-geometry fragility forecloses Q3 go-live.

Quintanilla-Bravo's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 5 — the returns-tsunami archetype — because it was the round that decided whether EnvíoRápido cleared the Q4 peak as an NPS-plus-eight event or as an NPS-minus-14 crisis.
