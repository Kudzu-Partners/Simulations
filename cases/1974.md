# Siam Pantry Co., Ltd.: The Promotion That Broke the Chain

**Case Code:** OPS-2026-1974 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1974 — *Bullwhip Buster* · **v1.0 · August 2026**

---

On Monday, 9 March 2026, Naruemon Sirithorn put two lines from the same twenty-six weeks onto one page. The first was what Thai shoppers had taken off the shelf: instant noodles, ready-to-drink coffee and sauces moving within a band of ±6% a week, about as steady as a consumer signal ever got. The second was what Siam Pantry's factory in Chonburi had been told to make. It swung ±48% in an ordinary week, and around a four-week price promotion on the flagship noodle line it had spiked 62% above plan before collapsing into warehouses nobody could empty. The promotion had lifted demand at the till by 14%.

Anucha Boonliang, the chief executive, gave her thirteen weeks. In the first week of June she was to sit across from him and from the supply-chain director of Baan Mart — one of two modern-trade accounts that had put delisting on the table over fill rates — and account for four numbers: how violently factory orders swung, what inventory cost the company each year, what share of retail orders were filled on time, and how wrong the factory's forecast was. Whatever she built had to fit inside THB 18 million a year of coordination and systems spending — no unlimited safety stock, no free data platform.

## The Protagonist

Naruemon Sirithorn, 43, had spent her career in demand planning — first at a Bangkok brewer, then at Siam Pantry, where she had run the planning team before Boonliang named her Operations Strategist for the network. Her planners knew a proposal reached her with its arithmetic attached or came back. The appointment carried the CEO's mandate and almost no line authority: the plant reported to manufacturing, the centres to logistics, the promotion calendar to commercial, and the wholesalers to nobody at all.

## The Decision

Naruemon had to decide how the four tiers would order from one another, what each tier would be allowed to see of the others' demand, and what the commercial calendar would be permitted to do to that demand — then hold the design together against conditions already in the network.

## The Company and its Setting

Siam Pantry Co., Ltd. was a Bangkok-based Thai manufacturer and distributor of packaged food and beverages, with revenue of THB 4.2 billion. It made instant noodles, ready-to-drink coffee and sauces at Chonburi and moved them through four tiers: factory to regional distribution centres at Chiang Mai, Khon Kaen and Hat Yai; centres to wholesalers; wholesalers to modern-trade chains and to the traditional grocers that still carried the bulk of Thai volume. Every tier ran its own planner, held its own safety stock, and ordered against the orders it received. Nobody upstream of the shelf planned against the shelf (see **Exhibit 2**).

## The Situation

The promotion had left an itemised bill. Siam Pantry carried THB 620 million of inventory across the network; at a 9% cost of capital that was THB 55.8 million a year of carrying cost before anything went wrong. The promotion added THB 240 million of excess stock and THB 31 million of write-offs on slow-moving and expiring goods, taking the annual inventory line to THB 86.8 million (see **Exhibit 1**).

For that money the network still failed to deliver. Service level — the share of retail orders filled complete and on time — stood at 89% against a target of 96%, and during the promotion the noodle line had been out of stock in 11% of outlets. The stock existed; it sat in the wrong buildings. Forecast error at the factory — the average percentage gap between what Chonburi planned to make and what the network turned out to need — ran at 38%, against the 15% good demand planners in the category achieved. Planning had circulated a target of holding factory order swings to no more than ±18%.

Three conditions were already live. Social-video traffic had begun producing genuine step changes in Thai snack demand; the planning team's scenario set now carried a real surge of up to +40% on a single line. Congestion at the container port had added two weeks to the lead time on imported film and packaging. And Thawee Trading, the largest wholesaler at roughly a third of volume, had told the commercial director that its point-of-sale data was commercially sensitive and would not be opening.

Inside the company the argument had already sorted itself: manufacturing wanted capacity to absorb the swings, the distribution-centre managers wanted more safety stock at each site, Finance wanted the inventory line cut, and Commercial wanted the calendar left alone. If nothing changed, the arithmetic was undramatic: the next deep promotion would run the same cycle, the THB 86.8 million line would hold or grow, forecast error would stay near 38%, and the June meeting would happen on the fill rates that had prompted it.

## What Naruemon Controlled

She controlled, first, the diagnosis. She had to say where the swing grew fastest — at the shelf, cumulatively upstream, only at the factory, or nowhere in particular — and rank the driver among four candidates: each tier forecasting from the orders below it rather than the shelf, the size and infrequency of order batches, the plant lacking the capacity to keep up, and variability in the material coming from suppliers. She had to state an opening hypothesis for the fix — safety stock at every tier, sharing the shelf signal across tiers, or more factory capacity — and decide how much of her first fortnight to spend costing the current state.

Second, the design. On ordering she could leave every tier on its own reorder point, cut order sizes and order more often, run base-stock replenishment with a smoothing rule that damped the reaction to each week's movement, or put one synchronised replenishment rule across all four tiers. On information she could leave each tier forecasting from the orders it received, push point-of-sale demand upstream so that every tier planned against the shelf, add a single collaborative forecast on top of that data, or move to vendor-managed inventory with joint planning and replenishment across partners. On pricing she could keep the deep periodic discounts the calendar was built on, move to promotions scheduled and sized in advance with the plant, or shift toward a standing low price. Each choice drew on the THB 18 million, and she set how much to commit then and how much to hold back.

Third, how the design met the three live conditions. Against a genuine surge she could hold the smoothing rule and treat the jump as noise, release the tiers to order freely until it passed, or test the jump against shared shelf data and lift base stock deliberately if it was real. Against the longer packaging lead time she could leave it to the replenishment rule, hold extra packaging at every tier, or place one buffer at the node the delay constrained and replan around it. Against Thawee she could keep the design as drawn and expect them to come around, drop that channel back to independent ordering, or estimate its demand from downstream signals and offer terms to earn the data. She also set how much of the design had to keep working where data never arrived.

Fourth, the June meeting. She could argue that Siam Pantry had bought enough buffer to stop stocking out, that it had made every tier react faster, or that coordination and shared information had done the work. She could make the data a condition of supply for Baan Mart, run without it, or trade fill rates, fresher stock and joint promotions for it. When Boonliang pressed on what had been given up, she could lead with the number that had moved most, show what the calendar and Thawee's refusal had cost the plan, or take the four measures in turn and account for the THB 18 million. She also had to decide how firmly to hold her position.

## Closing

That afternoon Naruemon printed the four tiers down one side of a sheet and wrote three headings across the top: what each tier ordered on, what each tier could see, and what the price would be doing meanwhile. Thirteen weeks. Thawee's data was not coming, the packaging was two weeks late, and the calendar already had the next promotion in it.
