## Abstract

Montaverde Hospitality Group S.A. was, in the case universe, a three-property Montaverde hospitality portfolio comprising Ciudad Central, a 220-room business hotel in the commercial core of the capital; Playa Vista, a 340-room coastal resort on the Pacific shore; and Valle Verde, a 68-room boutique lifestyle property in the country's wine-producing mountain region. On Monday, January 12, 2026, the group carried 2025 revenue of USD 96.4M, a blended GOPPAR of USD 82, a blended RevPAR of USD 119, an OTA-channel dependency of 62%, and a thirty-eight-thousand-member loyalty program with limited tier differentiation. Rafael Olivera, appointed Chief Executive Officer on October 1, 2025 after six years as the group's Chief Revenue Officer, faced three annual decision rounds, a Year-2 competitive pressure scenario the Board's revenue-intelligence office had flagged as more-likely-than-not (Luxury Riviera's fifteen-percent coastal-resort rate cut plus a new boutique entry in the secondary city near Valle Verde), and a Year-3 portfolio-expansion window in a corridor between Ciudad Central and Playa Vista. The case opens at 7:08 a.m. on Wednesday, January 21, 2026, as Olivera prepares the Year-1 portfolio package for the February 3 Board meeting.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Year-1 property-level rate-strategy choice across a business-hotel, a coastal-resort, and a boutique-lifestyle property, and diagnose when a loyalty-differentiated rate architecture dominates a uniform-market-match posture.
2. Analyze the Year-2 coastal-resort price-war scenario — Luxury Riviera's fifteen-percent cut — and recommend a non-price response that holds ADR and GOPPAR while conceding two occupancy points.
3. Diagnose the Year-1 loyalty-program investment that produces no measurable Year-1 ADR uplift and compounds as a distribution channel by Year-2 and Year-3.
4. Design a Year-3 portfolio-expansion decision that converts a Year-1 loyalty-data foundation into a multi-stay travel pattern across a fourth property.
5. Recommend a complete Year-1 portfolio package and identify the Year-2 move most correlated with the Year-3 composite outcome.

On the morning of **Wednesday, January 21, 2026**, **Rafael Olivera** sat in the CEO office on the nineteenth floor of the Montaverde Hospitality Group headquarters in Ciudad Central at 7:08 a.m. and opened the Year-1 portfolio workbook his Chief Revenue Officer had finalized at 11:22 p.m. Tuesday. Thirteen days separated him from the Board meeting. The portfolio dashboard on his wall carried three tiles — Ciudad Central 220 rooms, Playa Vista 340 rooms, Valle Verde 68 rooms — and a single distribution line at the top that read "OTA 62% of demand; direct 27%; corporate 11%."

---

## The Protagonist

Rafael Olivera, 47, had joined Montaverde Hospitality in 2020 as Vice-President of Revenue Management and had been promoted to Chief Revenue Officer in 2022, a role in which he had, across 2022–2024, repositioned the group's revenue architecture and reduced OTA dependency from 71% to 62%. A graduate of the Cornell University School of Hotel Administration with an MBA from IE Business School, he had, in his Chief Revenue Officer role, launched the group's first tiered-loyalty architecture and had co-authored a 2024 industry paper on loyalty-data-as-pricing-instrument. The Board's October 2025 appointment letter had read: "Run the group that has the lowest OTA dependency in the Montaverde market in three years. Do not buy that position by discounting."

---

## The Decision

Olivera had to commit, by 9:00 a.m. on Tuesday, February 3, the Year-1 portfolio package the Board would treat as the three-year operating envelope:

1. **Property-Level Rate Strategy** — whether to price Ciudad Central as corporate-rate-premium with negotiated corporate contracts, whether to hold Playa Vista's rate and invest in loyalty-only rate access, and whether to position Valle Verde as premium-lifestyle with zero-OTA on select high-demand dates.
2. **Loyalty-Program Investment** — whether to launch tiered benefits (breakfast, late checkout, room upgrades) funded by a modest ADR increase, whether to invest in a co-brand credit card in Year-1 or defer to Year-3, and whether to build a tiered loyalty-data platform that would not generate measurable Year-1 direct-booking uplift.
3. **OTA Contract Posture, CapEx Allocation, and Service Quality** — whether to renegotiate OTA contracts to parity-rate-plus-loyalty-only-lower-price, whether to tilt CapEx to Valle Verde's guest-experience refresh or to Playa Vista's beach-club, and whether to absorb the service-quality-training cost at USD 680K across Year-1.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across three rounds. A Year-1 package that priced Ciudad Central corporate-rate-premium, held Playa Vista's rate with loyalty-only access, positioned Valle Verde at premium-lifestyle with select-date zero-OTA, launched tiered loyalty benefits, deferred the co-brand card to Year-3, renegotiated OTA contracts to parity-plus-loyalty-lower, and tilted CapEx to Valle Verde produced, on the internal model, a Year-1 RevPAR uplift of 6%, a Year-2 GOPPAR resilient through the Luxury Riviera cut, and a Year-3 composite of 85 out of a 100-point envelope. A Year-1 package that matched Luxury Riviera's projected rate moves and deferred the loyalty-program investment to a post-shock window produced a higher Year-1 occupancy but opened a Year-2 GOPPAR exposure of 11 percentage points and a Year-3 composite of 70.

## Company Background

Montaverde Hospitality Group had been founded in 2006 through a family-office acquisition of two legacy Montaverde hotels and had, across twenty years, added the boutique Valle Verde property in 2014 and the coastal Playa Vista property in 2018. 2025 group revenue was USD 96.4M distributed as Ciudad Central 31%, Playa Vista 51%, Valle Verde 18%. Blended 2025 occupancy stood at 74%, blended ADR at USD 172, and blended RevPAR at USD 119. The group's loyalty program — launched in 2021, rebranded in 2023 — counted 38,000 active members with 34% one-year repeat stay and a tier architecture that had, in the 2024 Chief Revenue Officer's review, been flagged as underdifferentiated. OTA dependency of 62% reflected a 2018–2020 over-reliance on booking.com and Expedia to absorb Playa Vista's opening-phase occupancy and had been tapered from a 2020 peak of 71%.

The Montaverde hospitality competitive set carried three principal actors. Luxury Riviera, the dominant regional portfolio with seven properties including three coastal resorts, competed on a scale-led pricing program and a broader loyalty base. Casa del Mar, a smaller boutique operator with properties adjacent to Valle Verde's catchment, had, in December 2025, announced an additional boutique opening in the secondary city Valle Verde served. A cluster of OTA-dependent independents occupied the remainder of the market.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM hospitality landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Year-1 decision sat inside. First, the post-2022 OTA-commission environment had stabilized in the 15–22% band, with loyalty-only direct-pricing below OTA posted rates permitted under the 2023 revised OTA contract terms — a structural shift that had, across a 2023–2025 sample of thirty-one LATAM portfolios, enabled OTA-dependency reductions of 9–14 percentage points at a measured ADR cost of 50–80 basis points. Second, the loyalty-program cycle had, across 2023–2025, re-sorted around tier differentiation and guest-data-as-pricing-instrument, with top-15% member share-of-wallet uplifts of 22–35% against prior flat-architecture cohorts. Third, the co-brand credit card window had, in LATAM specifically, produced outcomes that varied sharply with Year-1 loyalty-data depth — portfolios that had launched co-brand cards on a shallow data base had typically produced program-level ROI below 60% by Year-3, against portfolios that had deferred to a post-data-foundation Year-3 launch, which had produced program-level ROI above 140% on average.

Empirically, the post-2020 hospitality research had two robust findings the Year-1 package had to respect. First, non-price responses to coastal-resort price wars — specifically, loyalty-only all-inclusive upgrade packages that preserved posted ADR while delivering a perceived value of 60–80% of the competitor's cut — had, across a 2020–2024 sample of twenty-eight coastal-resort rate-war events, preserved GOPPAR at a measured occupancy cost of 1.5–2.5 percentage points. Second, OTA-contract renegotiation to parity-rate-plus-loyalty-lower had, across a 2022–2024 sample, moved direct-booking shares 6–11 percentage points over a two-year window at no measurable ADR cost.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Board had, in the November 2025 strategy off-site, committed to a three-year OTA-dependency target of below 40% and a GOPPAR uplift of 20% cumulative. The Revenue-Intelligence office had, on January 5, 2026, flagged the Luxury Riviera Year-2 coastal-resort pricing cycle as more-likely-than-not and had modeled the expected rate cut at 12–18% depending on the Luxury Riviera Q4 2025 RevPAR trajectory. Casa del Mar had, on December 18, 2025, announced the additional Valle Verde-catchment boutique opening. The Board's Year-3 portfolio-expansion mandate had referenced, in broad terms, a corridor opportunity between Ciudad Central and Playa Vista.

### What Olivera knew

The team had modeled three Year-1 packages. A Rate-Match Package (match Luxury Riviera's projected rate moves on Playa Vista, defer the loyalty-program investment, maintain OTA contract posture) projected Year-1 occupancy lift 4pp, Year-2 GOPPAR drag 11pp, Year-3 composite 70. A Portfolio-Loyalty Package (corporate-rate-premium Ciudad Central, hold Playa Vista with loyalty-only access, premium-lifestyle Valle Verde with select-date zero-OTA, tiered loyalty benefits, OTA renegotiation to parity-plus-loyalty-lower, CapEx to Valle Verde) projected Year-1 RevPAR uplift 6%, Year-3 composite 85. A Defensive Package (hold rate structure, minimal loyalty investment, minimal OTA-contract change, CapEx to maintenance) projected Year-1 RevPAR uplift 2%, Year-3 composite 74 — stable but share-losing.

### What he did not know

Whether the Luxury Riviera Year-2 cut would land at the Revenue-Intelligence 12–18% band or at a deeper 22% that the Pacific-corridor trade-association gossip had referenced; whether the Casa del Mar new-boutique launch would open in the April 2026 window (their public announcement) or slip to the late-2026 window (their supplier-network consensus); whether the Ciudad Central corporate-travel rebound would materialize at the expected 11% Year-1 level; and whether the Year-3 portfolio-expansion opportunity — a boutique urban property in the Ciudad Central-Playa Vista corridor — would remain available at the current 2025 valuation or move toward a higher 2027 valuation on the expected corridor growth.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Revenue Officer**: *"Portfolio-Loyalty Package. Do not match Luxury Riviera's cut on Playa Vista. Package the loyalty-only all-inclusive upgrade. The math works."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"Defer the co-brand card to Year-3. A Year-1 card launch on a thirty-eight-thousand-member base would produce a Year-3 ROI under 60%. The data foundation is the Year-1 investment."*

**The General Manager, Valle Verde**: *"Zero-OTA on select high-demand dates. Our guest mix cannot absorb a Casa del Mar-induced OTA-discount spiral and maintain the chef-programming cost base."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Olivera had narrowed the space to three Year-1 portfolio packages.

- **Option A — Rate-Match:** Match Luxury Riviera Year-2 projected moves, defer loyalty investment, maintain OTA posture. Year-1 occupancy lift 4pp, Year-3 composite 70. Year-2 GOPPAR exposure 11pp.

- **Option B — Portfolio-Loyalty:** Corporate-rate-premium Ciudad Central, loyalty-only access Playa Vista, premium-lifestyle Valle Verde with select-date zero-OTA, tiered loyalty, OTA parity-plus-loyalty-lower renegotiation, CapEx to Valle Verde. Year-1 RevPAR uplift 6%, Year-3 composite 85. Aligns with CRO, CFO, and Valle Verde GM.

- **Option C — Defensive:** Hold rate structure, minimal investment, CapEx to maintenance. Year-1 RevPAR uplift 2%, Year-3 composite 74. Stable. Share-losing.

Olivera's private view, not yet shared with the Board, was that Option B was the right Year-1 package and that the single subsequent decision most correlated with the Year-3 composite was the Year-2 Playa Vista rate response — specifically, hold posted ADR, package a loyalty-only all-inclusive upgrade worth a perceived USD 70 for a tariff-card equivalent of USD 15, renegotiate the two OTA primary contracts to parity-plus-loyalty-lower, and double Valle Verde's chef-led weekend programming to defend against the Casa del Mar entry.
