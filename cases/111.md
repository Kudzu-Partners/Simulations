## Abstract

CORVENNA PRECISION MANUFACTURING was, in the case universe, a mid-sized precision machined-components plant inside a European industrial group with €380M in 2025 revenue, three production lines delivering CNC-machined aerospace and automotive subassemblies, 412 plant employees across two shifts, and an OEE that had flattened at 64% across the 2023–2025 window against a parent-group benchmark of 78%. The Q1 2026 board envelope had committed €2.4M to a three-quarter SmartFactory transformation that would, across 2026, lift OEE, reduce Cost/Unit, compress unplanned downtime, raise the Digital Maturity Index (DMI) from 52 to a target above 70, and hold the Operational Engagement Score (OES) above 55 — on the evidence that plants which had starved workforce inside IoT rollouts had, across a 2019–2024 sample, forfeited up to 40% of projected OEE uplift. On Monday, February 2, 2026, Marta Esteve, a 46-year-old mechanical-engineer-turned-operations-leader, had opened the Q1 allocation commitment that would set the sensor-edge-analytics-workforce split, define the TPM-plus-RCM operating model, and wire the ISA/IEC 62443 cybersecurity envelope. The case opens at 5:42 a.m. on Tuesday, February 10, 2026, at the Corvenna Precision plant gate as Esteve prepares the Q1 commitment that will anchor the full three-quarter trajectory.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Q1 budget-allocation commitment (sensors, edge computing, advanced analytics, workforce enablement) and diagnose when a workforce-protected allocation dominates a sensor-maximalist allocation on the downstream OES score that gates every later round.
2. Analyze the Q2–Q3 connected-operations trajectory under a Line-3 vibration-anomaly event and recommend the escalation path (MES queue vs. shift-lead ownership) that honors the TPM-plus-RCM operating model.
3. Diagnose the IT/OT cybersecurity probe inside the ISA/IEC 62443 zone architecture and quantify the OEE protection from zone isolation against line shutdown.
4. Design the Q4 advanced-analytics optimization that lifts the DMI from 52 to 71 when Q1 under-investment in analytics has already capped the Round-4 upside, and recommend the mitigating analytics build.
5. Recommend the Q1 commitment and write the board-facing paragraph that anchors the three-quarter trajectory against the principle that connected factories run on the people who trust the sensors, not on the sensors alone.

Se sostienen con la gente que confía en ellos."* Eleven days separated her from the February 21 executive-committee commitment.

## The Protagonist

Marta Esteve, 46, had joined Corvenna Precision in January 2019 from a Tier-1 automotive supplier where she had, across nine years, run TPM and lean transformations on three machining lines. Before that, she had been a manufacturing engineer at a European aerospace prime for six years. The Group Chief Operating Officer had recruited her specifically on the strength of two capabilities: her experience running TPM-plus-RCM operating models through a digitalisation window, and her reputation for refusing to commit IoT budgets that starved the operator enablement line. Her long-term incentive plan was tied 45% to the Year-1 OEE lift and 20% to the Operational Engagement Score floor — a KPI that the Group COO had explicitly protected inside the transformation envelope.

---

## The Decision

Esteve had to commit, by the February 21 executive-committee commitment, the Q1 allocation and the directional intent for Q2 through Q4.

1. **Q1 — Transformation Planning** — how to split the €2.4M envelope across sensors (instrumentation density, vibration, temperature, energy, vision), edge computing (line-side compute and protocol translation), advanced analytics (predictive models, digital-twin scaffolding), and workforce enablement (TPM training, shift-lead RCM ownership, operator dashboards). Under-investing workforce below 20% had, in the plant's own 2024 pilot, crashed OES from 46 to 28 and stalled sensor adoption at the line level.
2. **Q2–Q3 — Connected Operations** — how to route a forecast Line-3 vibration anomaly: through the MES alert queue (central-maintenance routing) or through a shift-lead-owned TPM loop (line-level ownership). The RCM priority score ranked the anomaly as predictive-actionable; the TPM operating model said operator-owned.
3. **Q2–Q3 — Cybersecurity Probe Response** — how to handle an expected ISA/IEC 62443 probe at the IT/OT boundary: isolate the affected zone and conduit (preserving line throughput), or hold the full line down pending SOC review.
4. **Q4 — Advanced-Analytics Optimization** — whether to activate the advanced-analytics tile (predictive-maintenance scoring, digital-twin scenario runs) at full envelope or at the reduced envelope consistent with the Q1 analytics under-investment.

The four moves would compound into a single transformation commitment. On Esteve's working model, a Sensors-30 / Edge-28 / Analytics-20 / Workforce-22 envelope — with shift-lead-owned Line-3 escalation, zone-isolation cybersecurity response, and a reduced Q4 advanced-analytics build — delivered Q4 OEE 82%, Cost/Unit €17.80, DMI 71, OES 58 and a final SmartFactory score of 78. A Sensors-45 / Edge-25 / Analytics-20 / Workforce-10 envelope delivered Q4 OEE 72%, DMI 62, OES 28 — a rollout that maximised short-term data capture but forfeited the OES floor. A Sensors-25 / Edge-20 / Analytics-35 / Workforce-20 envelope delivered a higher Q4 DMI of 74 but a Q2 OEE that collapsed to 68% inside the vibration-anomaly window because the edge layer was under-provisioned.

## Plant Background

Corvenna Precision had been built in 1987 as a single-line CNC turning shop and had, across thirty-eight years, grown into a three-line precision machined-components plant delivering aerospace and automotive subassemblies across a European customer book of eighteen OEMs. At the start of 2026, the plant had 412 employees across two shifts, a 2025 revenue of €142M, a 2025 plant EBITDA margin of 9.4% (against a parent-group benchmark of 13.2%), and a Q4 2025 OEE of 64% against a parent-group benchmark of 78%. Cost/Unit had held at €19.60 across the 2023–2025 window; unplanned downtime had averaged 11.8% against a parent-group benchmark of 6.4%.

The Q4 2025 plant-transformation dossier had identified three specific pressures the three-quarter envelope would inherit. First, a 2025 external benchmarking study had, across twenty-two European precision-machining peers, placed DMI at a median of 66 against Corvenna Precision's 52 — a gap that anchored the €2.4M envelope. Second, the plant's 2024 SmartFactory micro-pilot had demonstrated a 14-point OEE lift on Line 2 under a workforce-protected allocation but only a 6-point lift under a sensor-maximalist allocation — an internal signal that framed the Q1 OES-floor commitment. Third, the Q1 2026 European cybersecurity-scrutiny calendar had, inside the industrial-OT segment, confirmed a Q3 2026 NIS2-aligned supervisory pass that would require evidence of ISA/IEC 62443 zone-and-conduit segmentation — a calendar that placed the Q2–Q3 cybersecurity response squarely inside the envelope.

## Industry and Market Context

The connected-manufacturing landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2019–2024 TPM-plus-IoT evidence base had, across fifty-one European precision-machining rollouts, documented an OEE uplift of 12–18 percentage points under workforce-protected allocations against 4–7 points under sensor-maximalist allocations — a pattern that anchored the Q1 workforce-22 commitment. Second, the 2020–2025 RCM implementation evidence base had, across thirty-four operating companies, placed shift-lead-owned escalation loops at an MTTR reduction of 34–42% against central-MES-queue routings — a pattern that framed the Q2–Q3 Line-3 escalation path. Third, the 2021–2025 ISA/IEC 62443 incident-response literature had, across eighteen OT-security incidents, shown that zone-isolation responses preserved 88% of operating throughput against 34% for full-line-down responses — a pattern that framed the Q2–Q3 cybersecurity commitment.

Empirically, the post-2020 connected-manufacturing literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, plants that had under-invested in workforce enablement below 15% in Q1 had, across a 2019–2024 sample of 27 machining plants, forfeited 38% of projected OEE uplift and cratered OES scores by Q3 in 71% of cases. Second, plants that had deferred advanced-analytics investment to Q4 after under-funding it in Q1 had, across the same sample, recovered only 60% of the modelled DMI uplift — a pattern that told Esteve the "seventy-eight final score" in her base case was, as the sim engine would score it, a strong but not world-class outcome.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Group Chief Operating Officer had, in the December 2025 off-site, approved a €2.4M envelope, a Q4 OEE target of 80%, a DMI target above 70, a Cost/Unit target below €18, and an OES floor of 55. The Head of Production had, on January 14, argued for the workforce-22 allocation on the 2024 pilot evidence. The Head of Digital had, on January 22, argued for a workforce-18 / analytics-24 allocation to front-load the DMI trajectory. The Chief Information Security Officer had, on February 2, flagged the Q3 2026 NIS2 supervisory pass and argued for a dedicated €180K line item inside the edge allocation for zone-and-conduit architecture. The Works Council had, on February 6, protected the OES floor at 55 inside the envelope.

### What Esteve knew

The team had modelled three three-quarter envelopes. A Sensors-30 / Edge-28 / Analytics-20 / Workforce-22 Envelope projected Q4 OEE 82, Cost/Unit €17.80, DMI 71, OES 58 and a final score of 78. A Sensors-45 / Edge-25 / Analytics-20 / Workforce-10 Envelope projected Q4 OEE 72, DMI 62, OES 28. A Sensors-25 / Edge-20 / Analytics-35 / Workforce-20 Envelope projected a DMI of 74 but a Q2 OEE dip to 68% under the vibration-anomaly window.

### What she did not know

Whether the Line-3 vibration anomaly would arrive at the forecast week eleven or slip to week fourteen; whether the ISA/IEC 62443 probe would land as a low-severity reconnaissance event or escalate to a credential-spraying attempt; whether the analytics vendor would hit the Q4 deployment window against a six-week contractor backlog; and whether the Works-Council OES floor of 55 would hold through a compressed training calendar.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Production**: *"Workforce twenty-two. Sensors only work if the operators trust them — and the 2024 pilot proved it."*

**The Head of Digital**: *"I want more analytics in Q1. Deferring it to Q4 caps the DMI upside — the sim remembers."*

**The Chief Information Security Officer**: *"Zone isolation on the 62443 probe. We do not take Line 3 down for a reconnaissance event."*

**The Works Council representative**: *"OES floor fifty-five is non-negotiable. Training inside Q1, not bolted on in Q3."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Esteve had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Sensors-30 / Edge-28 / Analytics-20 / Workforce-22 with Shift-Lead Escalation and Zone Isolation:** Q1 allocation as above. Q2–Q3 Line-3 vibration routed to shift lead on RCM priority. Zone-and-conduit isolation on 62443 probe. Q4 advanced-analytics at reduced envelope. Q4 OEE 82, Cost/Unit €17.80, DMI 71, OES 58. Final 78. Aligns with HP, CISO, Works Council. Recommended.

- **Option B — Sensors-45 / Edge-25 / Analytics-20 / Workforce-10 with MES-Queue Escalation and Line-Down Response:** Sensor maximalist. Q4 OEE 72, DMI 62, OES 28. Fails OES floor. Rejected.

- **Option C — Sensors-25 / Edge-20 / Analytics-35 / Workforce-20 with Shift-Lead Escalation and Zone Isolation:** Analytics front-loaded at the expense of edge. Q2 OEE dips to 68% under vibration-anomaly window. Final 72. Rejected as the edge under-provisioning is the single biggest OEE drag under the event calendar.

Esteve's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single quarter most correlated with the final score was Q1 — the workforce-protected allocation that anchored OES through the Q2–Q3 events and that compounded through the Q4 analytics build, even at its reduced envelope.
