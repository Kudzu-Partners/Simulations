## Abstract

Grupo Verdia Global was, in the case universe, a multinational manufacturing group with a 2025 revenue of USD 3,380M, a cash position of USD 5,000M, a two-market active footprint (Mercado Central and Región Andina) and a six-market dormant addressable set (Europa, Asia Pacífico, Norteamérica, MENA, África del Sur, and Oceanía), an ESG score of 45 (sector-peer 60th percentile), and a carbon-intensity index of 100. On Monday, April 13, 2026, the Board approved a twelve-quarter integrative strategic commitment inside which the Chief Executive Officer would commit, each quarter, four operating levers — sustainability investment as a percent of revenue, pricing strategy across active markets, production mode (standard, green, or hybrid), and market-expansion decisions. Inés Mondragón, a 51-year-old former Chief Sustainability Officer promoted to Chief Executive Officer on December 1, 2025, faced a Board mandate that the twelve-quarter trajectory had to carry both a financial composite above 70 and an ESG composite above 80, inside a regulatory environment in which six of the eight addressable markets were modeled to pass carbon-tax legislation across the 2026–2028 window. The case opens at 5:45 a.m. on Monday, April 20, 2026, at the Grupo Verdia Global headquarters, as Mondragón prepares the Q1 2026 commitment package.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

3. Diagnose the Year-3 rival ESG-scandal event and the Asia-Pacífico entry, and design the share-absorption and premium-pricing sequence that converts a governance failure at a rival into a Year-3 composite lift.
4. Design a twelve-quarter market-expansion sequence that couples ESG investment to entry timing such that the markets that reward ESG pay for the ones that do not, inside a Board-approved capital envelope.
5. Recommend a complete twelve-quarter envelope, identify the single quarter most correlated with the Q12 ESG composite, and write the shareholder-letter paragraph that frames the ESG-as-demand-signal argument.

On the morning of **Monday, April 20, 2026**, **Inés Mondragón** sat at the center of the Grupo Verdia Global boardroom at 5:45 a.m., opened the Q1 2026 commitment package her Chief Strategy Officer and the Chief Sustainability Officer had finalized at 11:28 p.m., and pinned to the top of the document the single sentence the Board had committed to in December: "Every ESG point costs a basis point of margin until the carbon regulation makes the opposite true — and the opposite is priced two regulatory cycles ahead." Twelve days separated her from the May 2 Board commitment.

## The Protagonist

Inés Mondragón, 51, had joined Grupo Verdia Global in 2014 as Head of Corporate Sustainability and had been promoted to Chief Sustainability Officer in 2019. A graduate of the Universidad Politécnica de Madrid in chemical engineering with an MBA from INSEAD, she had, in her CSO tenure, built the company's first abatement-curve decomposition, the first supplier ESG-scoring protocol, and the 2022 Mercado Central carbon-tax response that had, under her predecessor's operating direction, preserved operating margin through the pass-through window. Her December 2025 appointment as CEO had carried a Board mandate that the 2026–2028 arc would demonstrate whether the ESG-as-demand-signal thesis — a paper she had co-authored at the 2024 WBCSD annual meeting — held inside a live commercial scorecard. Her January 2026 CEO-letter opening line had read: "ESG is not a reporting line. It is a demand signal priced two regulatory cycles ahead."

## The Decision

Mondragón had to commit, by the May 2 Board meeting, the Q1 2026 commitment package and the Year-1 intent:

1. **Sustainability Investment as Percent of Revenue and Production Mode** — whether to enter Q1 at a sustainability investment of 3% of revenue (the sector-peer median) with a standard production mode, or to enter at 4% with a hybrid-mode commitment that would lift to green by Q5.
2. **Pricing Strategy and Market-Expansion Commitment** — whether to run balanced pricing across the two active markets through Year-1 and commit to a Q5 Europa entry, or to run premium pricing in Mercado Central from Q1 and defer Europa to Year-2.
3. **Dual-Scorecard Gating and Regulatory-Horizon Tracking** — whether to commit to a Q12 financial-composite floor of 70 and an ESG-composite floor of 80 as the Board-gating rule, and whether to track the regulatory-horizon calendar as a monthly instrument inside the operating committee.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across twelve quarters. A Q1 package that anchored the 3%-sustainability-investment, standard production, balanced pricing, a Q5 Europa entry, and the dual-scorecard gating produced, on Mondragón's working model, a Year-1 revenue run-rate of USD 3,400M, an ESG climb from 45 to 52, a carbon intensity fall from 100 to 96, a Q4 profit of USD 85M per quarter, and a twelve-quarter trajectory that closed at a financial composite of 71 and an ESG composite of 82 — including a Year-3 share-price premium of 14% against unregulated peers.

## Company Background

Grupo Verdia Global had been founded in 1968 as a Central-market chemical-and-materials operator and had, across fifty-eight years, built the two-market active and six-market dormant footprint the case studied. The 2025 revenue of USD 3,380M was distributed as Mercado Central 58%, Región Andina 42%. The manufacturing footprint comprised eleven plants across the two active markets, an R&D center, a central sustainability-and-technical-services function, and an M&A-and-market-development function that carried the dormant-market options. The company's competitive position had rested, since 2018, on a Mercado Central brand premium of 6%, a gross margin of 28%, and a sector-peer 60th-percentile ESG score that had, in 2024, climbed to the 72nd percentile under the CSO's abatement-curve execution.

The Board's April 2026 strategy dossier had identified three specific pressures the twelve-quarter envelope would inherit. First, the Mercado Central carbon-tax legislation was on a Year-2 implementation calendar at a modeled USD 40 per ton of CO2 equivalent — a pass-through envelope that would compress Year-2 margin by 1.2 points unless offset by the Year-1 abatement investment. Second, the Europa addressable market — a four-country premium-buyer footprint of USD 1,280M — had, in Q4 2025, published a supplier-ESG-score procurement rule that would take effect in Q3 2026, creating a sensitivity to Q1–Q2 ESG positioning. Third, a regional rival (Rival 1) was, in the Board's competitive-intelligence briefing, assessed as carrying a 62% probability of a Year-3 governance-scandal event that would detach three share points across three markets — a probability the case modeled as a Q8 event.

## Industry and Market Context

The global multinational-manufacturing landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the twelve-quarter envelope sat inside. First, the 2024–2025 global carbon-tax legislative wave had, across a commercial-summary sample of forty-four national jurisdictions, produced an implementation trajectory in which six of eight global-peer markets were modeled to carry active carbon-tax legislation by end-2028 at an average rate of USD 42 per ton — a trajectory that monetized the Year-1 abatement curve across the Year-2 and Year-3 windows. Second, the Europa supplier-ESG-score procurement rule had, across 2023–2025, rolled into 73% of the tracked Europa mid-to-large procurement offices, with a commercial-summary finding that suppliers ranked in the top quartile on the published scoring protocols had captured procurement volumes 2.4-times the sector median; a finding that monetized the Q1–Q2 ESG positioning. Third, rival ESG-scandal events had, across a 2019–2024 sample of thirty-eight multinational-peer governance events, produced an average share detachment of 2.4 points across three contiguous markets, with a commercial-summary finding that competitors running a 7%-or-higher sustainability investment at the event window had absorbed 62% of the detached share at a premium price.

Empirically, the post-2018 sustainability-and-internationalization literature had two robust findings the twelve-quarter envelope had to respect. First, CEOs who had sequenced ESG investment ahead of premium-market entry had, across a 2020–2024 sample of nineteen multinational ESG-entry events, captured Europa-category share 1.7-times faster than CEOs who had sequenced entry ahead of ESG investment. Second, CEOs who had maintained sustainability investment through a competitor-scandal window had, across the same sample, absorbed detached share at a premium price of 1.3 points against the composite-market price — a revenue-and-margin lift that compounded across the window.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Board had, in the March 2026 strategy off-site, approved a twelve-quarter revenue target of USD 5,120M, a financial-composite floor of 70, and an ESG-composite floor of 80. The Chief Sustainability Officer had, on April 8, circulated a Year-1 pre-reading that proposed the 3%-sustainability Q1 entry with a Q5 rise to 6% under the carbon-tax trigger. The Chief Financial Officer had, on April 11, signed off on the envelope with a note that the Europa Q5 entry capex of USD 180M would hold only if the Year-2 carbon-tax pass-through closed at the modeled 70% rate. The Chief Sales Officer had, on April 15, requested a Q7 Asia Pacífico entry to match a Rival-3 regional aggression — a position the CEO had held under review.

### What Mondragón knew

The team had modeled three twelve-quarter envelopes. An ESG-Sequenced Envelope (Q1 3%, Q5 6% + green mode + Europa entry, Q8 7% + Asia Pacífico entry) projected a Q12 revenue run-rate of USD 5,120M, a profit of USD 148M per quarter, an ESG of 77, a carbon intensity of 54, a financial composite of 71, and an ESG composite of 82. A Profit-First Envelope (Q1 2%, minimal green commitment, Europa deferred to Year-3) projected a Q12 ESG of 58 and an ESG composite of 63 — below the Board-gating floor. An ESG-First Envelope (Q1 7%, green mode from Q1, Europa Q2 and Asia Pacífico Q5) projected a Q12 ESG composite of 88 but a financial composite of 64 — below the Board-gating floor.

### What she did not know

Whether the Mercado Central carbon-tax rate would implement at the modeled USD 40 per ton or at a deeper USD 55 that the second-source regulatory intelligence had referenced; whether the Europa supplier-ESG-score procurement rule would hold through a Year-3 political cycle; whether Rival 1's Year-3 governance event would land at the modeled Q8 or would delay to Q10 and compress the share-absorption window; and whether the Asia Pacífico entry cost would come in at the modeled USD 140M or at a deeper USD 180M under the second-source M&A intelligence.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Sustainability Officer**: *"ESG-Sequenced Envelope. Q1 3%, Q5 6% with green mode, Q8 7% with Asia entry. The curve bends where regulation bends."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"Europa Q5 is the capital decision. If the pass-through closes at 70%, the envelope funds itself. Below 70%, we pause the Asia entry to Year-3."*

**The Chief Sales Officer**: *"Asia Pacífico at Q7, not Q9. Rival 3 regional aggression opens the window. If we wait, the window closes."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Mondragón had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — ESG-Sequenced:** Q1 sustainability 3%, Q5 6% + green + Europa, Q8 7% + Asia Pacífico, premium pricing in Europa and Asia. Q12 financial 71 + ESG 82. Aligns with CSO and CFO. Recommended.

- **Option B — Profit-First:** Q1 2%, minimal green, Europa deferred to Year-3, Asia off-table. Q12 ESG 63 — below Board-gating floor. Margin-preserving. Fails the dual-scorecard.

- **Option C — ESG-First:** Q1 7%, green mode from Q1, Europa Q2, Asia Q5. Q12 financial 64 — below Board-gating floor. ESG-maximizing. Fails the dual-scorecard on financial.

Mondragón's private view, not yet shared with the Board, was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single quarter most correlated with the Q12 composite was the Q5 carbon-tax response — specifically, the simultaneous sustainability lift to 6%, the production-mode switch to green, the Mercado Central pricing lift to absorb 70% of the pass-through, and the Europa entry at premium pricing that would compound across Q6 through Q8 as the procurement-rule implementation rolled through Europa's mid-market.
