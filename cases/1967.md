# Noordveld Components: Placing the Buffer

**Case Code:** OPS-2026-1967 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1967 — *Stock Smart* · **v1.0 · August 2026**

---

On the morning of Monday 9 March 2026, Sanne Vermeulen sat at the far end of the boardroom at Noordveld Components B.V. in Tilburg while the maintenance director of its largest account read two figures off one sheet. The first was 91.4%, the share of order lines Noordveld had filled from stock within 24 hours against the 97% printed in his framework contract. The second was two: consecutive quarters missed. A third, he said, and his company would buy elsewhere.

The finance director's numbers came after lunch and pointed the other way. Inventory on hand stood at €31.0 million, up from roughly €27 million, and inventory turns — cost of goods sold divided by average inventory, or how often a year the stock was sold and replaced — had fallen from 4.1× to 3.6×. Before the meeting broke up Vermeulen had a mandate and a clock: one planning cycle, six weeks, to bring the fill rate back above the contract without adding a euro of working capital. Failing that, the board would hand planning to a third-party logistics provider, and the nine roles in her team with it.

## The Protagonist

Sanne Vermeulen, 41, had come to Head of Central Planning from inside the network rather than from above it. Four years as the regional inventory analyst in Eindhoven had taught her to set safety stocks the way all four analysts still set theirs: from experience, from the memory of the last time a line ran out, and from the assumption that nobody forgives a planner an empty shelf. She had been hired centrally, fourteen months earlier, to give the network a single owner of stock levels; the regions still ordered what they judged they needed and Tilburg still replenished whatever they ordered.

## The Decision

Vermeulen had to decide where Noordveld's safety stock would sit — how much in Tilburg, how much at the four regional points, for which parts of the range — and have the policy running before the cycle closed six weeks later. Inside that placement sat four further calls: the service target each segment was planned to, the rule by which reorder points were built, where the money came from, and who owned the numbers afterwards.

## The Company and its Setting

Noordveld Components distributed industrial drive components — bearings, couplings, gearboxes, motor spares — to machine builders and maintenance operations across the Benelux and western Germany, on revenue of €148 million a year, and held stock at two levels (see **Exhibit 2**). Tilburg carried the full catalogue of 6,200 stock-keeping units, replenished by suppliers on a three-week lead time. Four regional stocking points — Rotterdam, Eindhoven, Antwerp and Düsseldorf — carried the faster-moving part of the range, were replenished from Tilburg with a day in transit, and quoted the 24-hour delivery promise.

Three accounts produced 38% of revenue, all on framework contracts written on fill rate rather than price, each carrying a penalty clause that fired if the fill rate stayed below 95%.

## The Situation

Vermeulen had twelve months of demand history for a representative basket of 40 stock-keeping units across fast, medium and slow movers, with each line's safety stock, reorder point, supplier lead time and measured fill rate beside it. The aggregate position sat in **Exhibit 1**, and its two halves did not sit together: service below contract, stock above plan, at the same time.

An internal scan had put €5.8 million of the €31 million into one category — stock at the regional points that did not turn, accumulated a buffer at a time, each addition defensible on the day. Finance was explicit that this was the only money in the room, and that cash-neutral was a condition, not a target: inventory was not to end the cycle above the €31.0 million it began at.

The building held two settled views. Katrin Weber, the analyst in Düsseldorf, noted that a line held in Tilburg could not be on a customer's machine next morning, and that every euro she had added went in because a customer waited. Pieter Nooteboom, who ran the Tilburg warehouse, noted that the same slow line sat in four buildings at once, and that Tilburg saw all four regions' demand while each region saw only its own.

Unchanged, the sequence was not dramatic. Fill rate would sit near 91.4%, a third consecutive quarter below the 97% contract and under the 95% penalty trigger, and the largest account had said what it would do then. Inventory would drift above €31.0 million as each analyst covered the last shortage locally, turns would fall further from 4.1×, and the third-party proposal would reach the board unopposed.

## What Vermeulen Controlled

The six weeks held four blocks, each closing on a commitment she could not cheaply undo. The first settled the read of the data. She could rank the basket by the euros each line moved in a year; by euros moved and, alongside it, how erratic each line's demand had been month to month; or by gross margin, taking the most profitable lines first. She could locate the idle €5.8 million in Tilburg, where the catalogue was deepest; in slow, erratic lines held separately at each of the four regions; or nowhere, every euro covering some customer somewhere. She had to name what was breaking the fill rate — the slow lines, which ran out most often; the fast lines at the regions the promise was written on; or every line equally — and decide how much of the six weeks to spend reading before committing.

The second turned the read into a policy. One service target on every line, set high; one on every line, set low; or different targets by segment. Reorder points built on average demand across an average lead time; on the spread of demand with the lead time held at three weeks; or on the spread of demand across the lead-time window with the lead time itself allowed to move. The cost of an additional point of service read as roughly the same on every line, or as rising steeply on the slow, erratic ones. And a figure between 80 and 99 for the service level the fast contract lines would be planned to.

The third placed the stock. The buffer could sit forward at all four regional points; entirely in Tilburg; or split, with the slow erratic lines held centrally and only the fast steady lines pushed forward. She could fund the move by buying additional stock, by liquidating the €5.8 million of idle regional stock and repositioning it, or by cutting stock across every line. Each site could keep setting its own levels, or one owner could set both. And she had to fix the share of slow, erratic volume moving to Tilburg, from none to all.

The fourth was a stress test and a board meeting. A bearing supplier's lead time doubled from three weeks to six: hold the reorder points as written, recompute the exposed ones and stage a temporary buffer on that family, or bring the family in everywhere at once. Düsseldorf's demand rose 40%: ship in from Tilburg and the neighbouring regions, build a permanently larger buffer there, or hold the targets and let Düsseldorf run short until demand settled. In three minutes with the board she could lead with the fill rate, the working capital, or both with turns beside them. And she had to name the rule that would outlast her: raise maximum stock levels whenever in doubt; leave each region owning its own buffer; or set safety stock across both levels together, by segment, sized to how erratic demand was.

Four numbers closed every block: the fill rate, order lines filled from stock within 24 hours, in per cent; inventory on hand, in euros; inventory turns; and a quarterly service figure, in euros, netting penalty exposure and lost contribution from stockouts against the cost of carrying the stock she held.

## Closing

Vermeulen returned to her office and laid two sheets side by side: the customer's fill-rate history and the finance director's inventory ledger. Six weeks, one policy, and €5.8 million she had not been given but had been told she already had. She wrote one line at the top of a clean page — *how much protection, and where* — and opened the demand file.
