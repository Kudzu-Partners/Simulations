## Abstract

The Banco Central de Quisqueda was, in the case universe, the inflation-targeting central bank of the Republic of Quisqueda, an upper-middle-income LATAM economy of 18.2M people with a 2025 GDP of USD 226B and a floating exchange-rate regime in effect since 2008. On Monday, March 3, 2026, Dr. Lucía Medrano took office as the new Governor, inheriting an inflation rate of 8.4% year-on-year (against a 3.0% target), a policy rate of 6.25%, a credibility index of 58 (between a crisis line of 40 and a safety line of 70), reserves of USD 24.6B, and a QZQ/USD exchange rate of 52.40. Medrano's mandate — price stability — was codified in the 2004 Central Bank Organic Law; her renewal — at the end of her five-year term — was a decision of the government. Eight quarters separated her from the first inflation-path credibility test and the unavoidable confrontation with a Finance Minister whose public communication on monetary policy had, in the two years prior, diverged from the Board's. The case opens at 6:00 a.m. on Tuesday, March 3, 2026, as Medrano prepares her Quarter-1 policy-rate decision for the March 19 Board meeting.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Quarter-1 policy-rate change (−50, 0, +25, +75, +150 basis points) against the real-rate gap, the inflation expectations anchor, and the output cost.
2. Analyze the four forward-guidance tones (Dovish, Neutral, Hawkish, Data-Dependent) as credibility instruments rather than rate-path commitments.
3. Diagnose the political-pressure event — a Finance Minister publicly calling for 200 basis points of cuts — and design the press-conference statement that preserves credibility without institutional conflict.
4. Design a response to a commodity shock (oil +18%, food +12%) that separates supply-side headline inflation from second-round effects in the wage data.
5. Recommend a complete Quarter-1 package and identify the single forward-guidance phrase most likely to compound a two-quarter credibility gain rather than a one-quarter rate gain.

Headline CPI at 8.4% year-on-year. Core services at 7.1%. The QZQ at 52.40 against the dollar, down 4.2% over the prior six months. The Board met on March 19. Eight quarters separated her from the first renewal-decision window.

## The Protagonist

Dr. Lucía Medrano, 54, was a University-of-Quisqueda-trained economist with a Ph.D. in monetary economics from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. She had served as Deputy Governor of the Banco Central de Quisqueda from 2019 to 2024, as Chief Economist of a Quisqueda commercial bank from 2024 to 2026, and had been appointed Governor by the Executive Branch on February 10, 2026, with Congressional ratification on February 26. She had written, in her 2012 M.I.T. dissertation, on inflation-targeting credibility in emerging markets — a literature she now had to operate inside. The Governor before her had served a full five-year term; the Governor before that had resigned in Q3 2017 under a public disagreement with the Executive Branch over a 200-basis-point rate decision.

---

## The Decision

Medrano had to commit, by 11:00 a.m. on Thursday, March 19, the Quarter-1 monetary-policy package the Board would vote on and the Communications Department would release at 3:30 p.m. the same day:

1. **Policy Rate Change** — −50 basis points (cut), 0 basis points (hold), +25 basis points (modest hike), +75 basis points (front-loaded hike), or +150 basis points (shock-and-awe hike).
2. **Forward Guidance Tone** — Dovish, Neutral, Hawkish, or Data-Dependent.

The two sub-decisions would interact and would compound across eight quarters. A +75-basis-point hike with a Hawkish tone would, on the internal macroeconomic model, close the real-rate gap to 5.5 percentage points above neutral and pull the yield curve forty basis points across the front end inside forty-eight hours; a +150-basis-point shock-and-awe hike would pull the curve sixty-eight basis points but would, on the credibility-index model, cost 4 points at Q1 (reading as a panic move) before recovering 7 points at Q2 (reading as a credibility investment) — a net gain but at the cost of an output-gap contraction of 0.8 percentage points that the Finance Minister's office was already framing.

## Company Background

The Banco Central de Quisqueda had been formally established in 1949 under a fixed-exchange-rate regime and had moved to inflation targeting, via the 2004 Organic Law, in 2006. The inflation target band was 2.0%–4.0% with a 3.0% point target. The Board comprised seven members — the Governor, three Deputy Governors, and three external directors — with five-year terms, staggered, and with policy decisions requiring a simple majority. The operational framework was a corridor-system with the policy rate as the midpoint and a ±100-basis-point window for repo and reserve-deposit facilities. Reserves stood at USD 24.6B at end-February 2026, approximately 8.9 months of imports.

The 2022 inflation spike — driven by global commodity prices and the currency depreciation of that year — had pushed headline CPI to 12.4% by mid-2023. The prior Governor had responded with 775 basis points of cumulative tightening across 2022–2024, bringing the policy rate from a COVID-era low of 2.25% to the 10.0% peak of September 2024 before beginning the easing cycle that had produced the 6.25% Medrano now inherited. The inflation-path from 12.4% (2023 peak) to 8.4% (February 2026) was a decelerating-but-slower-than-target disinflation, principally because core services had remained sticky and because a 4.2% currency depreciation in H2 2025 had re-opened imported-inflation pressure.

## Industry and Market Context

The macroeconomic landscape in Quisqueda in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Quarter-1 decision sat inside. First, the Fed's rate-path expectations had, between January and March 2026, been re-priced from one cut in 2026 to an uncertain path contingent on U.S. core services; the yield differential between Quisqueda and the U.S. was the single largest determinant of the QZQ capital-account flow. Second, the 2022–2025 inflation-targeting-emerging-market literature had converged on a finding: central banks that front-loaded tightening and coupled it with Hawkish-tone forward guidance preserved credibility-index scores above 65 in 78% of cases; banks that tightened slowly and used Neutral or Dovish guidance preserved 41%. Third, political-pressure events — defined as a Finance Minister or Executive-Branch public intervention on a specific rate decision — had, in an eight-country 2019–2025 sample, produced a measurable credibility-index decline on the day of the event in 14 of 18 cases, with recovery within three weeks in 9 of 14.

Empirically, the commodity-shock response research had two robust findings. First, central banks that distinguished in their communications between supply-side headline inflation and second-round wage effects preserved 11 index points of credibility on average versus those that responded symmetrically to headline and core. Second, forward-guidance tone shifts — Hawkish to Data-Dependent, Data-Dependent to Neutral, Neutral to Dovish — were read by markets as commitment-level signals rather than rate-path signals; a premature shift to Dovish, even with unchanged policy-rate decisions, cost, on average, 3.2 points of credibility inside one quarter.

## The Situation

### How we got here

Medrano had, between her appointment on February 10 and the first Board meeting on March 19, held one-on-one briefings with each of the six other Board members, reviewed the staff's Quarter-1 inflation and output forecasts, and received, on March 14, the staff recommendation of a +75-basis-point front-loaded hike with a Hawkish tone. The Finance Minister's office had requested a courtesy meeting on March 17 and had, in that meeting, pressed for a hold-or-cut decision on employment-support grounds. The Minister's public communication on monetary policy was expected to escalate if the Board delivered a +75 hike.

### What Medrano knew

The internal team had modeled three Quarter-1 packages. A Conservative Package (hold at 6.25%, Neutral tone) projected inflation at 5.8% at Q4 and 4.4% at Q8, credibility index 52 at Q4 (below the safety line and near the crisis line). A Balanced Package (+75 basis points to 7.00%, Hawkish tone) projected inflation at 5.1% at Q4 and 3.4% at Q8, credibility index 68 at Q4 and 72 at Q8. An Aggressive Package (+150 basis points to 7.75%, Hawkish tone) projected inflation at 4.6% at Q4 and 3.0% at Q8, credibility index 64 at Q4 and 74 at Q8, with output gap at −0.9% at Q8 versus −0.6% for the Balanced Package.

### What she did not know

Whether the Fed's March 19 meeting — scheduled the same day as the Banco Central's — would deliver a surprise pause or a surprise hike, either of which would move the QZQ and re-price the capital-account flow inside the hour; whether the Finance Minister would go on television within seventy-two hours of a +75 decision and whether a Finance Minister intervention at that scale would produce the kind of institutional-conflict escalation that had, in 2017, resulted in the prior Governor's resignation; whether the Q7-projected commodity shock (oil +18%, food +12%) would materialize on the staff's base case or shift the inflation path into a second hike; and whether the Board's three external directors — each appointed at different points in the prior administration — would vote with the Governor on a +75 decision or would split.

### What others were saying

**Deputy Governor Rodolfo Ayala**, responsible for monetary operations: *"Plus seventy-five. Hawkish. The real-rate gap at 6.25% is too wide to defend. The market will move thirty basis points on the front end by the close of trading. The credibility investment at Q1 pays for itself at Q3."*

**Deputy Governor Pilar Villalobos**, responsible for financial stability: *"Plus twenty-five with Data-Dependent. Front-loading on inflation surprises the exchange-rate on the upside; the import-price pass-through is not symmetric. We have been through this cycle. The Q7 commodity shock is the one we cannot model away."*

**External Director Dr. Federico Hidalgo**, economist: *"Plus seventy-five with Hawkish is the textbook. Plus one-fifty is a panic signal that a Board majority will not sustain at Q5 when the cut becomes defensible on the data."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Medrano had narrowed the space to three Quarter-1 packages.

- **Option A — Conservative Hold:** 0 basis points, Neutral tone. Q4 inflation 5.8%, Q8 inflation 4.4%. Credibility 52. Does not hit the 3.0% target inside eight quarters. Finance Minister's political cost low; institutional-credibility cost high.

- **Option B — Balanced Front-Load:** +75 basis points to 7.00%, Hawkish tone. Q4 inflation 5.1%, Q8 inflation 3.4%. Credibility 72 at Q8. Soft-landing path. Finance Minister conflict likely but manageable.

- **Option C — Shock-and-Awe:** +150 basis points to 7.75%, Hawkish tone. Q4 inflation 4.6%, Q8 inflation 3.0%. Credibility 74 at Q8 but 58 at Q1. Output gap −0.9% at Q8. High risk of a Board 4–3 split at Q1 that itself costs credibility.

Medrano's private view, not yet shared with Deputy Governor Villalobos, was that Option B was the right Quarter-1 package and that the single sentence in the press-conference statement — "the central bank's mandate is price stability, and the path to employment is through credible disinflation" — was the communication instrument that would carry the Finance-Minister-confrontation event through the following quarter without an institutional-conflict escalation.
