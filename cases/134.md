## Abstract

MERIDIA HEALTH SYSTEMS was, in the case universe, a private urban primary-care network of twelve clinics across Bogotá, Lima and Santiago with USD 36M in 2025 revenue, 540 employees, 380,000 annual outpatient visits, and a 2024–2025 appointment-adherence trend that had, across two successive technology cycles, failed to move the no-show rate below 34% against a regional peer benchmark of 18%. Two prior attempts — a 2024 SMS-reminder app and a 2025 scheduling-software replacement — had, at a combined USD 330K spend, delivered a no-show reduction of 0.9 percentage points against a modelled 8-point target. The Q1 2026 executive committee had approved a USD 240K human-centered-innovation envelope, 120 team-hours of dedicated capacity, and a four-round Design-Thinking cycle — Empathize, Define, Ideate-and-Prototype, Test — scored on Insight Depth (30%), Prototype-Problem Fit (35%), Stakeholder Alignment (20%) and Process Discipline (15%), modulated by the Learning Yield of the final user test, with scripted penalties for the Speed Trap (−15), Analysis Paralysis (−10) and the Wrong Problem (−20). The case opens at 6:16 a.m. on Monday, June 8, 2026, at the Meridia central office in Bogotá as **Valentina Reyes**, a 39-year-old Directora de Experiencia del Paciente e Innovación, prepares the Round-1 Empathize commitment. Fifty-six days separate her from the August 3 sponsor review.

## The Protagonist

Valentina Reyes, 39, had joined Meridia Health Systems in August 2023 as Directora de Experiencia del Paciente e Innovación from a Santiago-based primary-care operator where she had, across seven years, run three Design-Thinking cycles on patient onboarding and chronic-condition adherence, and had led the 2021 service-design note that re-classified the operator's adherence problem from a reminder-frequency problem to a logistics-and-trust problem. Before that, she had spent five years as a service-design consultant at a healthcare-innovation studio in Mexico City. The CEO had recruited her specifically on the strength of two capabilities: her record of refusing to ship an innovation pilot that had not cleared a problem-fit diagnostic, and her record of shipping low-fidelity service pilots that beat six-figure app builds on measured outcome. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the no-show reduction floor and 20% to the final Innovation Score.

## The Decision

Reyes had to commit, by the June 22 executive pre-review, the Round-1 Empathize envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–4.

1. **Round 1 — Empathize (research-hour allocation and segment focus).** The envelope priced three research methods against the 120-hour capacity: patient interviews at USD 2,000/hr (high-yield on stated needs), clinic-waiting-room observation at USD 1,500/hr (highest yield on latent behavior), and desk review of claims and survey archives at USD 800/hr (low yield). Segment focus was a forced choice: low-income urban patients (42% no-show, the largest volume), middle-income working patients (29%), or elderly patients in supported housing (61%, small volume). Across the interviews her team scripted, four stated signals dominated: *"se me olvidó"* — I forgot (44%), *"I had work and couldn't leave"* (27%), *"the bus was late"* (16%), and *"I had nobody to watch my kids"* (9%). The observation protocol surfaced what the interviews did not: patients at the front desk **declining to book follow-up appointments at all** — because they could not predict, weeks out, whether transport and childcare would both be available on the day. The latent signal was a booking-confidence problem — logistics and social capital, not memory.
2. **Round 2 — Define ("How Might We" selection, convergence hours, stakeholder update).** Four candidate HMW framings: *make appointment reminders more engaging* (symptom — the 2024 failure restated), *reduce transport and childcare barriers to visits* (the validated root cause), *redesign the scheduling interface for usability* (symptom — the 2025 failure restated), and *build a support network so patients plan visits confidently* (a strong adjacent framing). Convergence time (5–30 hours at USD 1,000/hr) sharpened whichever framing was chosen; the stakeholder update (full briefing USD 5K, summary email USD 1K, or skip) governed sponsor alignment.
3. **Round 3 — Ideate and Prototype (approach, fidelity, sponsor expectations) plus the URGENT event.** Ideation: broad brainstorm (20 hrs), focused sprint (10 hrs), or expert consultation (15 hrs, USD 15K). Prototype: storyboard (USD 5K, low fidelity), role-play scenario (USD 8K, medium), physical service mock-up (USD 25K, high), or digital wireframe (USD 35K, high) — with the working rule that **fidelity should match validated clarity**, and that a digital prototype for a non-digital behavioral problem forfeits fit. In the working scenario the CEO, under board pressure, demanded a mid-cycle pivot back to a digital reminder app; the response set was push back with research evidence (USD 8K), compromise on a small parallel digital pilot (USD 4K), or comply. A quarterly cost review could cut the remaining envelope by 25% in the same round.
4. **Round 4 — Test (method, participants, final decision).** User interviews (15 hrs, USD 12K), observation sessions (10 hrs, USD 8K) or a survey (USD 3K, 5 hrs), across 5–50 participants at USD 200 each; then persist, pivot, or redefine, presented to the CEO sponsor. The test's Learning Yield — method quality times sample adequacy — modulated the final Innovation Score.

On Reyes's working model, an Observation-Weighted / Barrier-Reframe / Matched-Fidelity / Interview-Tested envelope delivered Insight Depth 88, Prototype-Problem Fit 95, Stakeholder Alignment 82, Innovation Score ≈ 90. A Rush-to-Ideate / Reminder-App / Digital-Wireframe envelope delivered Insight Depth ≈ 10, the Speed Trap (−15) and Wrong Problem (−20) penalties, and an Innovation Score below 20 — the two prior failures, reproduced at speed. An Empathize-Heavy / Barrier-Reframe / CEO-Compliant-Digital envelope delivered Insight Depth 88 but a clarity-degraded, mismatched prototype and an Innovation Score in the mid-40s — the trajectory in which good research was overridden in the room where the prototype was chosen.

## Company Background

Meridia Health Systems had been founded in Bogotá in 2011 as a two-clinic ambulatory operator and had, across fifteen years, grown into a twelve-clinic urban network — five clinics in Bogotá, four in Lima, three in Santiago — with USD 36M in 2025 revenue, 540 employees, and a 380,000-annual-visit footprint concentrated in primary care and chronic-condition follow-up. The 2024–2025 operational dashboard had placed the no-show rate at a persistent 34% against a regional benchmark of 18%. The Q1 2026 CFO memo had priced the aggregate revenue leakage and idle-capacity cost at USD 4.1M across the 2026 operating year.

The March 2026 executive dossier had identified three pressures the four-round envelope would inherit. First, both prior adherence cycles had reproduced the same symptom-anchored problem statement — "patients forget" — and the post-mortem had flagged compressed empathy work as the root cause of the reproduction. Second, the clinic advisory council had pre-committed voluntary participants for an interview-and-shadowing protocol across all three cities. Third, the CEO had committed to the board a scalable solution within six months, with pilot results in eight weeks — a deadline that made a mid-cycle demand for visible digital progress foreseeable.

## Industry and Market Context

Missed appointments were a USD 150B+ global problem, with 20–30% of scheduled healthcare appointments missed in developing economies and root causes that the 2019–2025 evidence base placed as predominantly behavioral and structural rather than technological. The failure pattern of digital reminder apps in healthcare — where adoption is driven by administrators rather than patients — illustrated the classic design failure the envelope had to avoid: solving the visible symptom rather than the underlying barrier.

Empirically, the post-2022 human-centered-innovation literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, design teams that weighted direct observation alongside interviews on multi-stakeholder clinical problems surfaced latent behavioral root causes at roughly three times the rate of interview-only designs — stated preference reliably over-reported "forgetting" and under-reported logistics dependency. Second, teams that matched prototype fidelity to the validated clarity of their problem definition preserved learning-per-dollar at two to three times the rate of teams that bought polish early; Design Thinking's application in LATAM healthcare remained nascent, with no Spanish-language simulation-based training equivalent in competitor catalogs.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The CEO had, in the March 2026 executive committee, approved the USD 240K envelope and the four-round Design-Thinking cycle. The Service-Design Lead had, on April 27, pre-scripted the observation-weighted research allocation. The Patient-Experience Analyst had, on May 11, published the 2024–2025 prior-cycle post-mortem. The advisory-council chair had, on May 18, confirmed voluntary shadowing participation in all three cities. The Lima Clinic Operations Lead had, on May 25, priced a physical front-desk service mock-up — a "confirmed-transport booking" station with a childcare-corner pilot — at USD 25K. The CFO had, on June 1, confirmed the USD 4.1M annual leakage estimate and flagged that the Q3 cost review could claw back up to a quarter of any unspent envelope.

### What Reyes knew

The team had modelled three four-round envelopes. An Observation-Weighted / Barrier-Reframe / Matched-Fidelity / Interview-Tested envelope projected Innovation Score ≈ 90. A Rush-to-Ideate / Reminder-App / Digital-Wireframe envelope projected below 20, with both scripted penalties. An Empathize-Heavy / Barrier-Reframe / CEO-Compliant-Digital envelope projected the mid-40s.

### What she did not know

Whether the booking-confidence signal would generalise from the shadowing sample to the full 380,000-visit population; whether the CEO's board pressure would erupt mid-cycle into a demand for visible digital progress, and what pushing back would cost her politically; whether the Q3 cost review would cut the envelope before the prototype was funded; and whether eight weeks was enough to carry a physical service pilot to measurable results in three cities.

### What others were saying

**The Service-Design Lead**: *"Weight observation. The front desk is where the truth is. Do not rush Define."*

**The Patient-Experience Analyst**: *"Both prior cycles reproduced the same symptom statement. Only the reframe changes the trajectory."*

**The Lima Clinic Operations Lead**: *"A physical mock-up at the front desk, twenty-five thousand. We can fake the service tomorrow and learn more than any wireframe."*

**The CFO**: *"Four-point-one million in leakage. And if the quarter turns, I take a quarter of whatever you haven't spent."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Reyes had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Observation-Weighted / Barrier-Reframe / Matched-Fidelity / Interview-Tested:** the recommended envelope. Insight Depth 88. Prototype-Problem Fit 95. Stakeholder Alignment 82. Innovation Score ≈ 90. Recommended.
- **Option B — Rush-to-Ideate / Reminder-App / Digital-Wireframe:** the conventional envelope. Speed Trap −15; Wrong Problem −20; Innovation Score < 20. Rejected — reproduces the 2024–2025 failures at speed.
- **Option C — Empathize-Heavy / Barrier-Reframe / CEO-Compliant-Digital:** the capitulation envelope. Research survives; the prototype does not. Innovation Score mid-40s. Rejected — forfeits the fit layer that converts insight into outcome, though it buys short-term sponsor comfort.

Reyes's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final score was Round 1 — the observation hours that surfaced the booking-confidence signal. Without that signal, Round 2 defaulted to a symptom framing; with the framing wrong, no prototype fidelity could rescue the fit. And if the CEO's demand came, she would spend the eight thousand dollars and push back with the evidence — because the alignment lost in one uncomfortable meeting was cheaper than the clarity lost in a compliant pivot.
