# Cumeeira Educação Executiva: The Aratu Brief

**Case Code:** MKT-2026-1796 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1796 — *The In-Company Brief* · **v1.0 · September 2026**

---

On the morning of 2 June 2026, Renata Bastos read Siderúrgica Aratu's request for proposal twice, then set it down beside the transcript of the discovery call her team had run the week before. The RFP asked for a leadership development program for 180 senior and middle managers, an all-in budget fixed at BRL 4.2 million, and a launch inside five months, ahead of the steelmaker's strategic offsite. The discovery call, twelve pages of it, described something else: a transformation stalling on the shop floor, middle managers who had stopped believing in it, and two safety incidents that traced back to supervisors nobody had taught to lead. Aratu's chief human resources officer, Marisa Andrade, had invited two other bidders to quote against the same one-page brief.

Bastos had five months to turn the gap between the page and the transcript into a diagnosis, an architecture, a price, and a decision Andrade would actually sign — without Cumeeira's quote reading like either of the two bids already on the table.

## The Protagonist

Renata Bastos, 43, had directed Custom Programs at Cumeeira Educação Executiva since 2020, after eleven years designing leadership curricula inside two multinational manufacturers. She had built her standing on a single insistence: a proposal was only as good as the diagnosis underneath it, and she would not let a client's own wording of its problem go into a contract unexamined. Her compensation moved with the division's contribution margin and with the renewal of accounts the size of Aratu's.

## The Decision

Bastos had to decide, in turn: what business problem Cumeeira would actually design against; how to architect the learning journey around that problem; how to price the proposal inside Aratu's ceiling and Cumeeira's own margin floor; and how to hold that position once procurement, the client, and her own faculty roster all moved against her in the same month.

## The Company and its Setting

Cumeeira Educação Executiva was the custom, in-company solutions unit of a Brazilian business school headquartered in Nova Lima, Minas Gerais, with a delivery hub in São Paulo. Founded in 2004, the unit ran roughly 48 tailored corporate programs a year through a roster of 140 affiliated faculty and a core team of 22 designers and relationship partners, billing BRL 96 million in fiscal 2025 at a 34% contribution margin.

Two facts shaped how Bastos worked the bid. First, Cumeeira's price depended on the claim that its programs were diagnosed, not assembled from a catalogue; a proposal built to the literal brief would look like anyone else's. Second, the unit's contribution-margin floor was 30% on any engagement — below that, Cumeeira declined to staff the work, whatever the top line said.

## The Situation

Siderúrgica Aratu S.A. was a BRL 14 billion steelmaker and, across its relationship with several Cumeeira-affiliated units, worth roughly BRL 11 million a year — an anchor account, not a one-off sale (see **Exhibit 1**). Procurement had invited a global brand-name business school, Halstead Global Institute, quoting BRL 5.8 million for an off-the-shelf leadership program, and a low-cost domestic provider, Prática Corporativa, quoting BRL 2.5 million. Aratu's own ceiling of BRL 4.2 million worked out to roughly BRL 23,300 per participant across the 180 managers.

On the morning the RFP landed, Cumeeira's own proposal dashboard read the way every new bid did. Diagnosis Fit, a measure of how closely the problem Cumeeira designed against matched the real one rather than the brief's own words, stood at 20 out of 100. Design Fit, how tightly the learning architecture would tie back to that diagnosis, stood at 15. Projected Impact, the behaviour-change and business metrics Aratu's own board would judge the program by, stood at 10. Contribution Margin had no reading yet; it would not exist until a price did, against the unit's own 34% benchmark. If Cumeeira designed and quoted the program exactly as the RFP described it, the result would be a fluent version of what Prática Corporativa was already quoting at little more than half the price — with no board-level metric behind it beyond how many of the 180 sat through every module.

## What Bastos Controlled

The first decision was diagnostic. Four readings of the brief were available to take to Andrade: that a stalling transformation had left middle managers disengaged and frontline leadership unable to prevent safety incidents; that managers needed leadership skills — communication, delegation, strategic thinking; that the brief meant exactly what it said, a leadership program for 180 people; or that the core issue was safety compliance, calling for mandatory frontline safety training. Alongside it sat the question of which metric Aratu's board would actually judge the program by — business measures such as transformation milestones, engagement, and safety incidents; participant satisfaction and a net-promoter score; how many of the 180 completed every module; or post-course scores on leadership theory — and a separate call on how hard to challenge and re-contract the brief with Andrade before designing anything, set anywhere from accepting her request as written to an outright challenge to its premise.

The second decision was architectural. Bastos could anchor the journey on frontline leadership behaviour tied to engagement and safe operations, on a grounding in established leadership theory, or on a "better leadership" competency set — then split a hundred points of design effort across four kinds of learning: projects built around Aratu's own live transformation problems, immersive in-person modules, peer coaching and simulation, and digital modules for reinforcement between sessions. A separate call set the measurement plan: behaviour-change and business-result measures baselined before launch, end-of-module satisfaction surveys, or no formal measurement.

The third decision was commercial. The three faculty best suited to the transformation-and-safety modules were only 70% committed for the coming quarter; Bastos could keep them on the highest-stakes modules and staff the rest with mid-tier faculty, put senior best-fit faculty on premium retainer across every module, or staff the program entirely with junior, lower-cost faculty. Content could be reused for generic layers and built new only for Aratu's specific core, built entirely from scratch, or drawn entirely off the shelf. In-person cohort days per participant, across the five-month journey, could run anywhere from two to twelve. And the proposal itself could be priced near the BRL 4.2 million ceiling, dropped near BRL 2.6 million to sit against Prática Corporativa, or thinned to clear comfortably under the ceiling.

The fourth decision arrived after the price was already in front of the client (see **Exhibit 2**). Procurement asked for a 12% cut to match "market"; Bastos could hold the price and offer a scope-linked restructuring — deferring the digital layer, phasing a cohort — hold it flat with the scope unchanged, or concede the full cut with the scope unchanged. Andrade separately asked to add 40 participants, to 220, inside the same budget; Bastos could redesign the cohort structure and shift more of the load onto digital and peer formats, adjusting the cost basis to match; absorb all 40 into the existing cohorts with no change to design or price; or keep the price and reduce what each participant received without changing the quoted scope. Within the same week, the lead faculty member's availability dropped from 70% to 50%; Bastos could pair the lead with a co-delivering understudy on the critical modules, swap the lead out for another faculty member, or compress the lead's full module list into the reduced availability. Last came four minutes in front of Andrade, in which Bastos had to lead with the business outcome her managers' behaviour and metrics would show, with the features of the design itself — modalities, faculty CVs, the digital platform — or with the price against the two rivals, and to decide how firmly to hold that framing under her questions, anywhere from a passing mention to an unhedged stand.

## Closing

At 8:15 that first morning, Bastos printed the RFP on one side of a page and the discovery-call summary on the other, and set them side by side on the desk in front of her. Andrade wanted an answer inside five months. Prática Corporativa already had a number on the table at BRL 2.5 million, and it was for exactly the program the RFP had asked for.
