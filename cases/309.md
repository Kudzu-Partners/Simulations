## Abstract

Constructora MetroAndes S.A. de C.V. was, in the case universe, a 28-year-old Mexico-City-headquartered infrastructure-construction contractor founded in 1998 serving public-sector clients in the Valley of Mexico, specialising in urban-market and civic-infrastructure rehabilitation — approximately MXN 640m 2025 revenue, 420 employees including 280 field and 140 office-professional staff, a portfolio of twelve concurrent public-sector contracts ranging from MXN 28m to MXN 380m, and — at the April 2026 moment — the largest active contract in the firm's pipeline: the rehabilitation of Mercado Tepito for the Gobierno de la Ciudad de México (CDMX) at a fixed price of MXN 187m with a fourteen-month schedule, an MXN 8m on-time-handover bonus, a 6.4 % contingency reserve, a CDMX Bicentennial-handover-date forcing function, eight work packages (demolition / structural / electrical / plumbing / market-stalls / heritage-facade / commissioning / handover), a 420-day critical path with zero float, a near-critical path with 115-day float, and Monte Carlo schedule simulations showing only 50 % probability of hitting month 14 and 85 % probability of hitting month 15.5. On **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 6:48 a.m., **Esteban Navarrete-Iturbide**, a 49-year-old Project Director, arrived on site for the Month-4 EVM-review meeting.

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

2. Execute the DuPont 1956 Critical-Path-Method analysis across the eight work packages, identify the 420-day critical path and the 115-day-float near-critical path, compute the crash-cost table (the structural-reinforcement 15-day crash at MXN 4.2m), and articulate why concrete-curing physics imposes a physical floor on crash feasibility below 70 days.
3. Execute the Round 2 Earned-Value-Management recovery at Month 4 — PV 53m, EV 46m, AC 49m, SV -7, CV -3, SPI 0.87, CPI 0.94, EAC = BAC / CPI = MXN 199m — articulate the twelve-million projected overrun against the MXN 187m budget, and articulate why every EVM number is objective, honest and painful.

Manage the Round 3 risk-register activation as R-001 (archaeological finding, 30 % probability, +45 days impact) fires alongside the CDMX heritage-authority scope-change request for MXN 14m colonial-facade restoration, apply the Iron-Triangle trade-off discipline to the take-or-refuse change decision, and articulate why the refusal cascades into the documented political-relationship break that forecloses the next five CDMX contracts.

On the morning of **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 6:48 a.m., **Esteban Navarrete-Iturbide** arrived on site at the Mercado Tepito perimeter gate, greeted the Secretaría-de-Obras supervisor who had already signed in at 6:10 a.m., walked past the temporary hoarding that displayed the projected-handover date of August 12, 2027, and entered the site office where the Month-4 Earned-Value-Management review was scheduled for 8:00 a.m. The GranTriángulo workbook was open on the whiteboard screen. The risk register sat at the right. The structural subcontractor's Month-3 closeout report — which had flagged the lower-than-surveyed structural condition at the southwestern corner — sat in the stack. The CDMX heritage-authority scope-change letter, dated April 21, lay at the top of the stack with the yellow flag. The vendor-association delegate had requested a meeting at 11:30 a.m. The Bicentennial handover date was 378 days away. The Monte Carlo schedule simulation had just re-run at 6:02 a.m. and read 47 % probability of month-14 hit, down from 50 % the previous week.

## The Protagonist

Navarrete-Iturbide, 49, had been Project Director at MetroAndes since June 2019, had completed an Ingeniería Civil at the Instituto Politécnico Nacional Mexico in 1999 and a Master in Project Management at the Universidad Panamericana in 2008, had earned his PMP certification in 2010 and his PMI-RMP (Risk-Management) in 2015, had spent eleven years at a multinational infrastructure contractor in Mexico-City project-manager roles before joining MetroAndes in 2016 as Senior Project Manager, and had been elevated to Project Director at the 2019 internal promotion round. His project-management instincts had been shaped by three teachings: the 2007–2008 Universidad Panamericana exposure to the DuPont-Atkinson-PMBoK canonical project-management literature; a 2015 airport-expansion project in Querétaro where the unowned-change pattern had driven an eighteen-month schedule slip; and a 2023 CDMX hospital rehabilitation where the INAH-archaeological-finding had fired in the final trimester and the Project-Director-of-record at the time had converted the finding into a documented positive scope-change case-study rather than a schedule-break.

---

## The Decision

Navarrete-Iturbide had to commit, by the 5:00 p.m. client-status call, the four-round project-management package.

1. **Round 1 — CPM and Iron-Triangle.** Commit the eight-work-package Critical-Path-Method baseline. Establish the Iron-Triangle trade-off log-and-write discipline — every change request written, every trade documented, no unowned changes.
2. **Round 2 — EVM Recovery.** Accept the Month-4 EVM numbers. Commit a recovery plan — the twelve-million projected overrun closed through structural-reinforcement crash, material-cost negotiation on the steel input, and productivity targeting on the near-critical work packages.
3. **Round 3 — Risk Activation.** Absorb the R-001 archaeological-finding +45-day-critical-path impact. Respond to the CDMX heritage-authority colonial-facade scope-change at MXN 14m — accept-with-scope-change-order-and-compensation, or refuse.
4. **Round 4 — Final Sprint.** Crash the remaining critical path at cost, or extend and lose the bonus. Absorb the vendor-association-protest risk at the week-52 meeting. Defend the Bicentennial handover-date.

Commitments: the Round 1 CPM baseline and trade-off log; the Round 2 EVM recovery plan; the Round 3 scope-change posture and archaeological response; the Round 4 final-sprint posture; and a one-page client-status memo that frames the four-round commitment as a single Bicentennial-handover arc.

## Sectoral Background

The 2015–2025 Mexican public-sector infrastructure-construction category had, in the case universe, run approximately MXN 280bn to MXN 420bn in aggregate annual contract-award volume, concentrated in three operator tiers (large-infrastructure players like ICA, GMD and Mota-Engil serving the national-airport and highway portfolios; mid-market players like Constructora MetroAndes, Idinsa and Urbanizadora del Valle serving urban-rehabilitation and civic-infrastructure portfolios; smaller specialty contractors). The 2024 CDMX-commissioned Mercado-rehabilitation program had awarded twenty-two contracts across the city's public-market portfolio with a median contingency reserve of 8.2 %; MetroAndes had underbid the median at 6.4 % contingency to win the Tepito contract on political-credibility-and-prior-delivery-track-record grounds. The INAH-law archaeological-finding activation patterns across CDMX-downtown construction had documented approximately 38 % historical activation rate on Tepito-area excavations on panels going back to 2005, with typical schedule impact at 35-to-60 days.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1956–2025 project-management literature produced three findings the simulation had to respect. First, the DuPont 1956 Critical-Path-Method discipline, operationalised as a written-trade-off-log-and-every-change-owned execution, produced documented on-time-delivery rates approximately 2.9× higher on comparable fixed-price public-sector contracts than on un-logged-change-control regimes. Second, the PMI Earned-Value-Management formulation with SPI and CPI indices, applied monthly as an objective-honest-painful number discipline, had produced documented budget-discipline readings approximately 2.4× stronger on panels that accepted the Month-4 EAC reading and committed recovery plans than on panels that deferred acceptance to Month 8 or later. Third, the Atkinson 1999 Iron-Triangle frame with Quality at centre, combined with the PMBoK risk-register discipline, had produced documented change-control readings approximately 3.1× more disciplined on written-trade-off regimes than on verbal-agreement regimes.

The 2000–2025 Mexican public-sector-construction literature added two findings. The INAH-law archaeological-finding activation on CDMX-downtown sites had documented approximately 38 % historical rate with 35-to-60 days schedule impact, and the activation probability had risen in Monte-Carlo-informed 2024–2025 panels to approximately 42 %. And the CDMX-Bicentennial political-forcing-function, combined with the vendor-association-protest risk on large public-market rehabilitations, had documented that the refuse-scope-change posture on political-motivated change-requests cascaded into the documented next-five-contracts break at approximately 78 % of observed panel cases.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The 2024 contract award at 6.4 % contingency; the January 2026 kick-off; the Month-3 structural-subcontractor closeout flagging lower-than-surveyed structural condition; the April 8 archaeological finding on the southwestern excavation; the April 15 INAH formal notification; the April 21 CDMX heritage-authority colonial-facade scope-change letter; and the April 23 Month-4 EVM review and 5:00 p.m. client-status-call commitment were the sequence.

### What Navarrete-Iturbide knew

The eight-work-package CPM baseline; the structural-reinforcement crash-cost table and the concrete-curing-physics 70-day floor; the Month-4 EVM dashboard with SPI 0.87 and CPI 0.94 and EAC MXN 199m; the Monte Carlo schedule-simulation 47 % month-14 probability and 85 % month-15.5 probability; the risk-register with five R-codes and their probabilities and impacts; the CDMX political-calendar-lock on the Bicentennial handover; the fixed-price cost-lock; the vendor-association-protest history; and the client's appetite for a specific four-round commitment with written trade-offs.

### What he did not know

Whether the Monte Carlo simulation's 47 %-probability month-14 reading would hold as the archaeological-finding absorbed or would compress below 30 %; whether the INAH archaeological work would release the southwestern excavation at the median 45-day or extend to the 60-day tail; whether the CDMX heritage authority would accept a negotiated MXN 8m partial-colonial-facade scope-change compromise or insist on the MXN 14m full-scope; whether the vendor-association-protest meeting at week 52 would land in the relocation-plan-accepted outcome or would fire the +30-day-schedule-impact R-002; and whether the steel-input cost negotiation would yield the projected 4 % material-cost reduction or would hold at current market.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Executive Officer of MetroAndes (58)**: *"Esteban, el bono de ocho millones no nos cambia la vida. El contrato siguiente sí. Perder la relación con CDMX es perder cinco contratos en cinco años."*

**The CDMX Secretaría-de-Obras supervisor (on-site, 14 years at CDMX)**: *"El gobierno no negocia la fecha del Bicentenario. Todo lo demás se conversa."*

**The INAH archaeological-liaison (Instituto Nacional de Antropología)**: *"El hallazgo es real. La ley es la ley. Cuarenta y cinco días es el piso, no el techo."*

**The vendor-association delegate (representing 1,847 Mercado Tepito stalls)**: *"Si el plan de relocalización no es respetuoso, hay protesta. Si hay protesta, hay treinta días mínimo."*

**The structural-subcontractor lead (22 years of CDMX-downtown experience)**: *"Puedo crashear quince días. Más allá de eso el concreto no cura. La física no negocia."*

**The CDMX heritage-authority director (cultural-patrimony counterparty)**: *"La fachada colonial es patrimonio. Si la rehabilitamos, entra al legado del Bicentenario. Si no la rehabilitamos, entra al expediente administrativo."*

## Options Under Consideration

Navarrete-Iturbide had narrowed the project-management space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — CPM baseline + Month-4 EVM recovery + archaeological-finding absorbed + scope-change negotiated-accepted at MXN 11m partial + crash on near-critical + vendor-association relocation-plan (Recommended).** Respects Iron-Triangle log discipline; accepts Month-4 EVM numbers and commits the recovery plan; absorbs the R-001 +45-day impact through float on near-critical and partial crash; negotiates the CDMX scope-change at MXN 11m partial-colonial-facade-rehabilitation (85 % of the heritage-authority's request); crashes the near-critical at MXN 3.8m; executes the vendor-association relocation-plan with stalls formally consulted. Projected hit-month-14 probability 64 %; EAC at MXN 196m; CPI recovered to 0.97.
- **Option B — Full scope-change accept at MXN 14m + minimal crash + accept month-15.5 completion + forfeit bonus.** Rejected — forfeits the MXN 8m bonus; delivers the CDMX heritage-authority the full MXN 14m but commits to an outcome that publicly misses the Bicentennial-handover target; the documented political-relationship-break pattern still fires at 23 % probability despite full-cooperation posture.
- **Option C — Refuse CDMX scope-change + maximum crash + accept vendor-protest + push month-14 at all costs.** Defensible on budget-discipline grounds but breaks the CDMX heritage-authority political relationship on the 78 %-cascade pattern; the vendor-association-protest fires and adds +30 days anyway; the MXN 4.2m structural-reinforcement crash below 70 days breaches the concrete-curing physics and raises the quality-at-centre Iron-Triangle violation.

Navarrete-Iturbide's private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the single decision most correlated with the Bicentennial outcome was the Round 3 CDMX scope-change negotiated-partial-acceptance, because the documented panel had shown that the refuse-entirely posture cascaded into the next-five-contracts-break pattern at 78 % probability, and because the full-accept posture forfeited the bonus and still risked the 23 % political-relationship-break residual. The *Triángulo de Fuego* frame sits at the LATAM-Mexican-public-sector-market-rehabilitation-Spanish-primer tier in the 2026 Express catalogue, distinct from Andino Torres Construction Iron Triangle (sim 036, generic-construction frame).
