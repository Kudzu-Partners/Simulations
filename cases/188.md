## Abstract

Cementos Pacífico was, in the case universe, a Callao-headquartered LatAm cement-and-building-materials group operating across Peru, Chile, Ecuador and Bolivia with USD 1.1B in 2025 revenue, a 2025 EBITDA margin of 6% against a LatAm-cement peer-benchmark of 11% (and a global peer-benchmark of 16%), a 2025 Differentiation Index reading of 23 on a composite scale against a peer-benchmark of 48 (composite captures offering-feature differentiation, buyer-perceived value and brand-preference readings), a 2025 Price-Pressure Index reading of 82 against a peer-benchmark of 58 (higher means more pressure), a 2025 industry-rivalry-intensity reading of 91 and a substitute-threat reading of 68 on a post-2022 trajectory that had seen two new entrants from prefab-and-3D-printed-structural-systems close 3 points of addressable share. The Q4 2025 board had approved a USD 36M five-round strategy-redesign envelope — the BlueShift programme — and had positioned it as the group's response to three overlapping pressures: a structural red-ocean pattern in the core ten-ton-and-above contractor segment, a Q3 2025 small-contractor-and-prefab-factory diagnostic surfacing an un-addressed rental-of-outcomes demand pattern, and a Q4 2025 competitor-diagnostic showing two regional peers pricing cement as a service rather than as a ton. The Q1 2026 board had reaffirmed the USD 36M envelope and a VP-Strategy-led five-round commitment cycle against a Differentiation Index floor above 48, a Price-Pressure Index ceiling below 58, an EBITDA-margin floor above 10%, and a Composite BlueShift Score above 70. The case opens at 6:48 a.m. on Monday, April 20, 2026, at the Cementos Pacífico Callao head office as **Mauricio Salazar-Rengifo**, a 42-year-old Vice-President of Strategy, prepares the Round-1 Five-Forces-diagnosis commitment. Sixty-nine days separate him from the June 28 board strategy review.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Round-1 Five-Forces-diagnosis commitment (full-force envelope that scores all five forces with annotated evidence vs. rivalry-only envelope) and diagnose when the full-force envelope dominates the rivalry-only envelope on the strategy-set-richness basis.
2. Analyze the Round-2 Four-Actions redesign commitment (eliminate-reduce-raise-create envelope that executes the full four-action grid vs. raise-only envelope that defends the legacy value curve) and recommend the full-grid envelope on the value-curve-shift basis.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 noncustomer-targeting commitment (three-tier envelope that names refused, unwilling and unexplored tiers and evidences prefab-factory and small-contractor demand vs. existing-customer envelope) and recommend the three-tier envelope on the blue-ocean-addressable-market basis.
4. Respond to the Round-4 subscription-and-outcome pricing commitment (per-project envelope that prices pour-and-cure outcomes on a subscription basis vs. per-ton envelope that preserves the legacy price-per-weight anchor) and recommend the per-project envelope on the Differentiation-Index and exit-red-ocean basis.
5. Design the Round-5 tipping-point-scale-up commitment that converts BlueShift from a pilot-at-one-plant into a six-plant rollout anchored on four identified internal champions, protected pilot-to-scale instrumentation and a governance-and-credibility dashboard.

## The Protagonist

Mauricio Salazar-Rengifo, 42, had assumed the VP-Strategy role at Cementos Pacífico on March 1, 2024, after twelve years across strategy, corporate-development and commercial leadership at two LatAm building-materials groups and a Spanish cement multinational. He had, in 2021–2023, led a value-curve-redesign programme at a Chilean building-materials distributor that had, across a 22-month window, lifted Differentiation Index from 29 to 58 and reduced Price-Pressure Index from 74 to 44 through a subscription-anchored model change. The Chief Executive Officer had recruited him in December 2023 specifically on two capabilities: his documented discipline of building a full four-action grid rather than settling for a raise-only value-curve, and his willingness to anchor strategy change on noncustomer-addressable markets rather than on incumbent-share defences. His 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the Differentiation Index floor and 20% to the EBITDA-margin floor.

## The Decision

Salazar-Rengifo had to commit, by the May 11 board pre-review, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–5.

1. **Round 1 — Five-Forces diagnosis.** Three archetypes: full-force (score all five forces with documented evidence and benchmark, Composite 76), rivalry-only (diagnose rivalry without supplier, substitute, entrant, buyer — Composite 28, strategy-set narrowing), or three-force (rivalry-substitute-entrant only — Composite 52). Working envelope: full-force.
2. **Round 2 — Four-Actions redesign.** Three archetypes: full-grid-four-actions (eliminate ten-ton minimum, reduce sales-rep coverage, raise carbon-transparency, create pour-and-cure SLA, Composite 78), raise-only (defend the legacy curve by raising carbon-transparency alone — Composite 32), or three-action (no create action — Composite 56). Working envelope: full-grid-four-actions.
3. **Round 3 — Noncustomer targeting.** Three vectors: three-tier (refused, unwilling and unexplored tiers with prefab-factory and small-contractor evidence, Composite 78), existing-customer (preserve focus on incumbents — Composite 28, red-ocean entrenchment), or phased (one noncustomer tier only — Composite 54). Working envelope: three-tier.
4. **Round 4 — Subscription-and-outcome pricing.** Three vectors: per-project (price pour-and-cure outcomes on a three-year subscription basis for small-contractor tier, Composite 78), per-ton (preserve legacy price-per-weight — Composite 26, Differentiation-Index ceiling), or hybrid (per-project on pilot only — Composite 54). Working envelope: per-project.
5. **Round 5 — Tipping-point scale-up.** Three vectors: four-champion-six-plant (four identified internal champions, pilot-to-scale across six plants, governance dashboard, Composite 78), all-plant-bigbang (simultaneous 18-plant rollout — Composite 30, adoption collapse), or one-plant (pilot only — Composite 52). Working envelope: four-champion-six-plant.

On Salazar-Rengifo's working model, a Full-Force / Full-Grid / Three-Tier / Per-Project / Four-Champion envelope delivered Differentiation Index 54, Price-Pressure Index 52, EBITDA margin 10.4%, Composite 74.

## Company Background

Cementos Pacífico had been founded in Callao in 1942 and had, across eighty-four years, grown into a USD 1.1B-revenue LatAm cement-and-building-materials group with 18 producing plants, 3.2M tons of annual cement capacity and a legacy go-to-market model anchored on per-ton pricing to the ten-ton-and-above contractor segment. The 2020–2024 operating cycle had seen EBITDA margin compressed from 11% to 6% across a red-ocean pricing spiral with two regional peers, a substitute-threat reading rising from 48 to 68 driven by prefab-structural-systems adoption, and a Differentiation Index reading falling from 38 to 23. The Q4 2025 board-approved USD 36M BlueShift envelope had been positioned explicitly as a strategic exit from the red ocean rather than as a marginal-value-curve refresh.

Three Q1 2026 events were named in the March 2026 board dossier. First, the December 2025 industry-structure diagnostic had documented rivalry-intensity at 91 and a price-pressure-index at 82 — both the highest readings in the 2020–2025 window. Second, the Q3 2025 small-contractor-and-prefab-factory diagnostic had surfaced a 24%-of-addressable-demand pattern in customers that had been historically unreachable through the per-ton-to-ten-ton-minimum model, with surveyed buyers expressing preference for an outcome-based purchase frame. Third, the Q4 2025 competitor-diagnostic had documented one regional peer executing a per-project pilot in Colombia and a second peer launching a subscription-anchored mixer-delivery contract in Ecuador — signals that the window for differentiation was closing.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2015–2025 blue-ocean-strategy literature had, across 36 documented value-curve redesigns, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, redesigns anchored on a full Four-Actions grid had documented Differentiation-Index uplift readings 2.6× higher than raise-only redesigns. Second, redesigns that had targeted noncustomer tiers in addition to existing customers had preserved EBITDA-margin uplift above 400 basis points in 74% of cases against 22% for existing-customer-only redesigns. Third, tipping-point scale-ups anchored on four-to-six identified internal champions had sustained adoption above 60% of target scope at the eighteen-month mark in 76% of cases against 28% for all-plant big-bang rollouts.

Empirically, the post-2018 business-model-innovation literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, price-architecture shifts from per-unit to outcome-and-subscription pricing had, across a 2015–2023 sample of 24 commodity exits, sustained Price-Pressure-Index reductions of 18–28 points in 74% of cases against 22% for unchanged pricing architectures. Second, integrated governance-and-credibility dashboards in tipping-point rollouts had sustained scale-up discipline above 70 in 72% of cases against 28% for informal rollouts.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the December 2025 session, approved the USD 36M envelope and the June 28 review calendar. The Head of Industry Analysis had, on March 4, delivered the Five-Forces pack. The Chief Commercial Officer had, on March 11, delivered the noncustomer diagnostic. The Chief Financial Officer had, on March 18, delivered the per-project pricing model. The Chief Operating Officer had, on March 25, delivered the plant-level tipping-point diagnostic.

### What Salazar-Rengifo knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. A Full-Force / Full-Grid / Three-Tier / Per-Project / Four-Champion envelope projected Composite 74. A Rivalry-Only / Raise-Only / Existing-Customer / Per-Ton / Big-Bang envelope projected Composite 20. A Hybrid envelope projected Composite 46.

### What he did not know

Whether the per-project subscription architecture would clear a favourable accounting-treatment on revenue-recognition inside the envelope window; whether the small-contractor tier would absorb the three-year contract horizon at contract volumes that cleared the unit-economics hurdle; whether the four identified internal champions would convert plant-manager peers at the six-plant scale window; and whether the two regional peers would accelerate their own subscription-model launches in response.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Industry Analysis**: *"Full-force diagnosis. The substitute threat is the force we've underweighted for two years."*

**The Chief Commercial Officer**: *"Three-tier noncustomer map. Prefab factories and small contractors are 24% of demand we aren't even serving."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"Per-project pricing. Per-ton is how the red ocean stays red."*

**The Chief Executive Officer**: *"Blue ocean is what happens when you stop arguing about the red one. Pay the per-ton give-back."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Salazar-Rengifo had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Full-Force / Full-Grid / Three-Tier / Per-Project / Four-Champion:** the recommended envelope. Differentiation Index 54. Price-Pressure Index 52. EBITDA margin 10.4%. Composite 74. Recommended.
- **Option B — Rivalry-Only / Raise-Only / Existing-Customer / Per-Ton / Big-Bang:** the legacy envelope. Composite 20. Rejected — red-ocean entrenchment probability above 76%.
- **Option C — Hybrid / Phased / Phased / Phased / Phased:** the partial envelope. Composite 46. Rejected — value-curve redesign fragments under partial gating.

Salazar-Rengifo's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 4 — the subscription-and-outcome pricing — because it was the round in which the cement business escaped the per-ton price frame that every red-ocean dynamic depended on and entered an outcome-contract architecture that the peer set could not replicate inside the window.
