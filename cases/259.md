## Abstract

The Head of Corporate Venture at the case-universe São Paulo-headquartered industrial-technology conglomerate had been asked, in the March 2026 Group Strategy Committee mandate, to deliver a Q2 2026 board investment thesis on XR and immersive-technology across a USD 180M three-year investment envelope. The Group had accumulated approximately USD 38M in XR-adjacent pilot investments across 2022–2025 that had produced mixed outcomes — a successful industrial-maintenance AR pilot that had cut average repair times by 14%, a successful surgical-training VR distribution partnership, a VR shopping experiment that had never reached 10,000 MAU, and a metaverse-offices pilot that had been quietly decommissioned after six months of near-zero engagement. The March 2026 mandate asked the Head of Corporate Venture to evaluate eight named XR use cases — surgical-training VR, industrial-maintenance AR, VR shopping, virtual real-estate tours, VR fitness, AR eyewear try-on, metaverse offices and AR navigation — on a five-axis scorecard (technology maturity, business-model viability, user adoption, ROI potential, competitive moat), to commit to a three-year investment thesis, and to explicitly call the hype cases the thesis would not fund. The case opens at 7:18 a.m. on Monday, April 20, 2026, at the Group's São Paulo head office as **Solene Fernandes-Ogunyemi**, a 44-year-old Head of Corporate Venture, prepares the eight-case commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Case-1 surgical-training VR reading (technology maturity 9, business-model viability 8, user adoption 8 in teaching hospitals, ROI 9 measurable in reduced error rates, competitive moat 7 on specialized content) and the Case-2 industrial-maintenance AR reading (tech 8, model 9, adoption 7, ROI 9, moat 6) and articulate why these two use cases anchor the Real side of the distribution and why the ROI axis is the most discriminating of the five.
2. Analyse the Case-3 VR shopping reading (tech 6, model 4, adoption 3, ROI 3, moat 2) and articulate why a use case can pass the technology axis and fail the business-model and adoption axes simultaneously, and why "nobody wants to put a headset on to buy socks" is a business-model diagnostic rather than a technology diagnostic.
3. Diagnose the Case-4 virtual real-estate tours reading (tech 9, model 7, adoption 8, ROI 7, moat 5) and articulate why a use case can be Real but not defensible — why the combination of high adoption and low moat produces a shared-prosperity use case in which no single player captures durable value.
4. Respond to the Cases 5–8 pattern (VR fitness Real with a Peloton-shaped market, AR eyewear try-on Real with a 15% conversion lift, metaverse offices Hype because nobody comes to the virtual water cooler, AR navigation Real because Google Maps already proved the market) and articulate why the hype-vs-value split typically runs 60–75% Real in mature XR categories and 20–35% Real in emerging categories.
5. Design the three-year 2026–2028 investment thesis that funds the six Real use cases, stages the envelope against technology-maturity and adoption milestones, and explicitly deprioritises the two Hype use cases — articulating why hype builds demos and value builds businesses, and why the corporate-venture function's durable contribution is the discipline of calling the hype cases out loud.

## The Protagonist

Fernandes-Ogunyemi, 44, had been Head of Corporate Venture since 2023, had spent fifteen years across a top-tier global VC's emerging-tech practice, a Series C AR/VR startup's strategy function, and the Group's corporate-venture arm. In 2020–2022 she had led the Group's first XR portfolio review which had produced the industrial-maintenance AR investment that subsequently delivered the 14%-repair-time reduction. In 2024 she co-authored with her Principal Investor *Real or Hype*, a practitioner framework that became the template for the Group's 2026 thesis. The Group Strategy Committee had assigned her the eight-case commitment on three considerations: her operational command of the 2021 portfolio review; her discipline of calling hype cases explicitly rather than softly declining them; and her willingness to stake corporate-venture credibility on a three-year thesis with named exclusions.

## The Decision

Fernandes-Ogunyemi had to commit, by the 4:30 p.m. Group Strategy Committee pre-brief, the eight-case scoring and the three-year 2026–2028 investment thesis.

1. **Case 1 — Surgical-training VR.** Tech 9, model 8, adoption 8, ROI 9, moat 7. Real. Fund the follow-on ticket on the 2024 investment; stage against a teaching-hospital-adoption milestone at Q4 2026.
2. **Case 2 — Industrial-maintenance AR.** Tech 8, model 9, adoption 7, ROI 9, moat 6. Real. Fund the Series B follow-on; stage against a field-deployment milestone at Q3 2026.
3. **Case 3 — VR shopping.** Tech 6, model 4, adoption 3, ROI 3, moat 2. Hype. Explicitly deprioritise on the board thesis. No follow-on capital.
4. **Case 4 — Virtual real-estate tours.** Tech 9, model 7, adoption 8, ROI 7, moat 5. Real but not defensible. Fund a bounded Series A ticket; cap the envelope because the moat axis forecloses the durable-value claim.
5. **Case 5 — VR fitness.** Tech 8, model 7, adoption 6, ROI 7, moat 6. Real with a Peloton-shaped market. Fund a Series A ticket on one targeted operator.
6. **Case 6 — AR eyewear try-on.** Tech 9, model 7, adoption 7, ROI 8 with a 15%-conversion-lift documented, moat 5. Real. Fund the Series B follow-on.
7. **Case 7 — Metaverse offices.** Tech 6, model 3, adoption 2, ROI 3, moat 2. Hype. Explicitly deprioritise on the board thesis. No follow-on capital.
8. **Case 8 — AR navigation.** Tech 9, model 8, adoption 9 because Google Maps already proved the market, ROI 7, moat 4. Real. Fund a bounded Series A on one differentiated operator.

On Fernandes-Ogunyemi's working thesis, the three-year envelope split approximately USD 105M across the six Real use cases (surgical-training VR, industrial AR, virtual real-estate tours, VR fitness, AR eyewear try-on, AR navigation), reserved USD 45M for follow-on capacity, and explicitly retired the VR-shopping and metaverse-offices sleeves. Hype-vs-value-separation accuracy on a held-out validation set of historical XR investments landed at 84%.

## Sectoral Background

The case-universe global XR/immersive-technology market had, across 2020–2025, deployed approximately USD 48B in cumulative corporate-venture and institutional-VC capital, with a documented 2022 bubble peak, a 2023 compression of 38% in public-market XR valuations, and a 2024–2025 stabilisation that separated the durable categories (enterprise AR, training VR) from the recurring hype categories (VR shopping, metaverse offices). The 2024–2025 cycle produced three named milestones: the Q2 2024 Meta Reality Labs revenue disclosure that compressed market expectations for consumer metaverse; the Q4 2024 Apple Vision Pro launch that re-opened the consumer XR debate; and the Q3 2025 industrial-AR IPO that anchored the enterprise-AR category at a 12x forward-revenue multiple. The April 2026 context compounded three pressures: a Group board ROI review of the 2022–2025 XR portfolio, a competitor move into surgical-training VR that compressed the window on Case 1, and an emerging-technology conference in São Paulo in Q3 2026 where the Group had been asked to present its 2026 thesis.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2015–2025 emerging-technology literature produced three findings the thesis had to respect. First, emerging-technology hype cycles produced an average of 60–75% hype cases in early market phases and 20–35% hype cases in mature market phases, with the inflection typically 4–6 years from category launch. Second, enterprise-anchored XR use cases (training, maintenance, navigation) produced documented ROI readings in the 7–9 range on practitioner surveys, while consumer-discretionary XR use cases (shopping, offices, social) produced readings in the 2–4 range. Third, moat scores below 5 on enterprise-XR use cases were associated with shared-prosperity outcomes where no single player captured durable value — VR fitness, AR navigation and AR eyewear try-on all fell in this range.

The post-2022 corporate-venture literature added two findings. Theses with explicitly-deprioritised hype cases produced board-adoption rates approximately 36 percentage points higher than theses with softly-declined hype cases. And staged investment envelopes against named technology-maturity and adoption milestones produced documented return multiples 1.4x higher than unstaged envelopes on comparable 2018–2023 emerging-technology portfolios.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The March 2026 Group Strategy Committee mandate asked for the eight-case commitment by Q2. The Q1 2026 portfolio review, the scorecard drafting, and the held-out-validation accuracy reading had all landed by April 13. The Committee Chair had asked Fernandes-Ogunyemi to bring the commitment to the April 20 pre-brief.

### What Fernandes-Ogunyemi knew

Enterprise-anchored XR use cases land in the Real distribution; consumer-discretionary XR use cases land in the Hype distribution unless the adoption axis clears a threshold that VR shopping and metaverse offices have not; moat scores below 5 produce shared-prosperity outcomes; and explicitly-deprioritised hype cases produce higher board-adoption rates than softly-declined hype cases.

### What she did not know

Whether the Apple Vision Pro follow-on product in 2026–2027 would re-open the consumer-XR debate in a way that warranted re-scoring Case 3 or Case 7; whether the competitor move into surgical-training VR would compress the window on Case 1 in a way that required an accelerated follow-on ticket; whether the Q3 2026 São Paulo conference presentation would drive inbound pipeline on the Real cases; and whether the Group board would accept explicit exclusions on VR shopping and metaverse offices or push for a "watch but do not fund" posture.

### What others were saying

**The Principal Investor**: *"Six Real. Two Hype. Call them out. The thesis lands cleaner if the exclusions are explicit."*

**The Emerging-Tech Analyst**: *"AR navigation's moat is four. Cap the ticket. The category is Real but not defensible for long."*

**The Chief Strategy Officer**: *"The Group board will accept explicit exclusions if the accuracy reading on the held-out validation is above eighty. Eighty-four is defensible."*

**The Group CFO**: *"The three-year envelope at USD one-eighty million lines up with the board's capital allocation plan. Do not exceed it on the first commitment."*

## Options Under Consideration

Fernandes-Ogunyemi had narrowed the space to three thesis postures.

- **Option A — Fund six Real / explicitly deprioritise two Hype:** the recommended thesis. Held-out accuracy 84%; explicitly-deprioritised cases raise board-adoption; staged envelope against milestones. Recommended.
- **Option B — Fund six Real / softly decline two Hype with "watch but do not fund":** rejected — the soft-decline clause compresses board-adoption rates in documented cases and leaves the hype sleeves in an ambiguous state that absorbs analyst attention disproportionate to their expected value.
- **Option C — Fund all eight at small tickets:** rejected — commits capital to VR shopping and metaverse offices on Hype-side readings that the five-axis scorecard explicitly rejects.

Her private view was that Option A was the right thesis and that the case most correlated with the Group board's read was Case 7 — metaverse offices — because it was the case on which the Group's 2022 pilot had already produced negative evidence, and the explicit deprioritisation was the clearest signal the thesis could send on the "hype builds demos, value builds businesses" read.
