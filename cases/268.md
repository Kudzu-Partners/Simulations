## Abstract

The Head of Financial Planning and Analysis at the case-universe Mexico-City-headquartered mid-cap industrial-goods distributor — approximately USD 620M in 2025 revenue across Mexico, Central America and the Andean region, 2,800 employees across 14 distribution sites, 240 finance-adjacent managers in operations, treasury, pricing and procurement — had been asked, in the February 2026 CFO mandate, to deliver a Q3 2026 quantitative-literacy curriculum anchored on eight business-mathematics problems that covered the time-value, optimization, operations-math and decision-math frames. The Q4 2025 FP&A audit had flagged a recurring failure mode: finance-adjacent managers were producing capex memos, pricing recommendations, inventory policies and procurement decisions without the underlying equations, producing decisions that were right on average but wrong in the tails. The February 2026 mandate asked the Head of FP&A to work through all eight problems, read each equation as a decision in disguise rather than a computation, and commit to a quantitative-literacy floor the CFO would stake on the decision-moment discipline rather than on the computational-complexity headline. The case opens at 2:04 p.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Group's Mexico City head office as **Ricardo Mejía-Solórzano**, a 41-year-old Head of FP&A, prepares the eight-problem commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Problem-1 Future Value setup ($25,000 at 6.5% compounded monthly for 12 years, answer $54,186) and the Problem-2 Mortgage Amortization setup ($350K at 6.25% over 30 years, monthly $2,155, year-one ninety-percent-interest read) and articulate why compounding is the first honest magic trick in business and why the amortization schedule tells a first-time borrower what their interest-vs-principal split really is.
2. Analyse the Problem-3 Profit Maximization setup (MR = MC intersection) and the Problem-4 Minimum Average Cost setup (derivative of AC set to zero, optimal production 1,200 units) and articulate why the MR=MC moment is the decision moment rather than a computation moment, and why the AC-minimum is the below/above watermark for setup-cost and variable-cost burning.
3. Diagnose the Problem-5 EOQ setup (sqrt(2DS/H), answer 894 units, reorder every 37 days) and the Problem-6 Break-Even setup (fixed $180K, contribution margin $45, BE 4,000 units; -19% price-sensitivity) and articulate why the EOQ equation killed a generation of safety-stock spreadsheets and why break-even sensitivity is a pricing-committee-level variable.
4. Respond to the Problem-7 Expected Value setup (probability tree 40/35/25, EV $2.1M) and the Problem-8 Consumer Surplus setup (integral under demand above price, $1,125) and articulate why EV is the maximum anyone would pay today to own the option and why consumer surplus is a measurable quantity that trades off against margin at every pricing decision.
5. Design the eight-problem commitment that reads each equation as a decision in disguise and commits to a four-module quantitative-literacy floor for 240 finance-adjacent managers — Module 1 (Time Value), Module 2 (Optimization), Module 3 (Operations Math), Module 4 (Decision Math) — with a 2%-tolerance scoring discipline at the end of each module, articulating why every equation is a decision in disguise and the business pivots on the decision-moment signal rather than on the computational-complexity headline.

## The Protagonist

Mejía-Solórzano, 41, had been Head of FP&A since 2024, had spent fifteen years across LATAM financial-planning, treasury and pricing practice, and had co-authored with his predecessor the firm's 2022 internal playbook *Decisions in Disguise*, which framed business mathematics as a decision-moment discipline rather than a computational-complexity discipline. The CFO had assigned him the eight-problem commitment on three considerations: his command of the 2022 playbook's decision-moment framing; his discipline of leading the CFO read with the decision-moment column rather than the computation column; and his willingness to recommend a mandatory quantitative-literacy floor for 240 finance-adjacent managers.

## The Decision

Mejía-Solórzano had to commit, by the 4:30 p.m. CFO pre-brief, the eight-problem commitment and the Q3 2026 quantitative-literacy-floor recommendation.

1. **Problem 1 — Future Value.** $25,000 at 6.5% compounded monthly for 12 years = $54,186. Module 1.
2. **Problem 2 — Mortgage Amortization.** $350K at 6.25% / 30 years = $2,155 monthly. Year-one 90%-interest read. Module 1.
3. **Problem 3 — Profit Maximization.** MR = MC. Module 2.
4. **Problem 4 — Minimum Average Cost.** Derivative of AC, 1,200 units. Module 2.
5. **Problem 5 — EOQ.** sqrt(2DS/H) = 894 units, 37-day reorder. Module 3.
6. **Problem 6 — Break-Even.** Fixed $180K, contribution margin $45, BE 4,000 units; price -10% → BE +19%. Module 3.
7. **Problem 7 — Expected Value.** Tree 40/35/25, EV $2.1M. Module 4.
8. **Problem 8 — Consumer Surplus.** Integral under demand above price = $1,125. Module 4.

Eight-problem dashboard: 7/8 correct, 94% accuracy, 2% tolerance on each.

On Mejía-Solórzano's working commitment, the quantitative-literacy-floor recommendation combined the four modules — Time Value (Problems 1, 2), Optimization (Problems 3, 4), Operations Math (Problems 5, 6), Decision Math (Problems 7, 8) — delivered across 10 weeks to 240 finance-adjacent managers in a hybrid self-paced plus live-coaching format, with a 2%-tolerance scoring discipline at the end of each module and a quarterly refresher.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The February 2026 CFO mandate asked for the eight-problem commitment by Q3. The Q1 2026 problem selection, the 2%-tolerance scoring discipline design, and the four-module curriculum architecture had all landed by April 16. The CFO had asked Mejía-Solórzano to bring the commitment to the April 22 pre-brief.

### What Mejía-Solórzano knew

Decision-moment-driven quantitative-literacy programmes outperform computation-complexity-driven programmes on capex-memo and pricing-committee outcomes; mandatory floors outperform voluntary cohorts; and hybrid delivery outperforms pure modes on engagement and completion.

### What he did not know

Whether the 240-manager cohort would clear the 2%-tolerance scoring at end-of-module-one at the target 85% pass rate or require remediation; whether the Q4 treasury hedging-policy review would produce a fifth module on FX and interest-rate mathematics; whether the 2027 pricing-system deployment would shift the cohort composition toward the procurement and pricing functions; and whether the CFO would accept a mandatory floor at the proposed USD 290k annual budget.

### What others were saying

**The Senior FP&A Analyst**: *"Eight problems. Four modules. Two-percent tolerance. The mandatory floor is the discipline; the dashboard is the enforcement."*

**The Chief Learning Officer**: *"Hybrid self-paced plus live coaching. Ten weeks. Quarterly refresher. If you pull the quarterly refresher, the module-four decision-math erodes inside six months."*

**The CFO**: *"If the dashboard shows eighty-five-percent pass at end-of-module-one across the two-forty cohort, the mandatory floor passes. Below that and we are in voluntary-cohort territory."*

**The Head of Pricing**: *"Break-even sensitivity is the differentiator. Every pricing committee has a member who cannot read the -19% sensitivity on a -10% price cut. The floor fixes it."*

## Options Under Consideration

Mejía-Solórzano had narrowed the space to three floor postures.

- **Option A — Mandatory four-module quantitative-literacy floor for 240 managers (Time Value / Optimization / Operations Math / Decision Math) with 2%-tolerance scoring and quarterly refresher:** the recommended floor. Budget USD 290k; expected 22% pricing-committee-decision-accuracy gain. Recommended.
- **Option B — Voluntary cohort launch (self-selected managers only):** rejected — underperforms on post-curriculum decision-quality by approximately 34%.
- **Option C — Three-module floor (drop Decision Math):** rejected — leaves expected-value and consumer-surplus decision-moments uncovered, and the 2027 pricing-system deployment would require them anyway.

His private view was that Option A was the right floor and that the problem most correlated with the CFO's eventual acceptance was Problem 6 — the break-even sensitivity read — because it was the problem where the decision-moment read diverged most clearly from the computation read the 2024 pricing-committee miss had followed.
