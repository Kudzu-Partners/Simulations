## Abstract

Andina Impact Partners, a Bogotá-based private-capital firm founded in 2019, closed its Fund II — a USD 50M LATAM-focused impact vehicle — on January 15, 2026. The fund's limited-partner base comprised three pension funds (Chilean, Colombian, Peruvian), a European family office, and two development finance institutions (DFIs). The fund's dual mandate required a 15% net IRR and verifiable social outcomes documented in the IRIS+ framework. Catalina Restrepo, Portfolio Manager since Fund I (2020–2025, a 14.1% net-IRR fund with 72/100 on impact outcomes), faced a six-quarter deployment and portfolio-management cycle across four sectors: clean energy, health-tech, education, and agriculture. The case opens at 7:00 a.m. on Monday, April 6, 2026, as Restrepo prepares the Q1 Investment Committee memo that will commit the initial capital and the dry-powder reserve. Six quarters would unfold: Q1 portfolio construction, Q2 hundred-day review, Q3 LP annual meeting, Q4 follow-on decisions, Q5 regional crisis (a projected Argentine peso devaluation), and Q6 exit decisions. The Q1 decisions would constrain every round that followed.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the sector allocation and ticket-size decisions in a USD 50M impact portfolio under a dual IRR-and-impact mandate.
2. Diagnose the LP-reporting emphasis trade-off (financial-heavy, impact-heavy, balanced) and recommend a framing for a mixed pension-fund / family-office / DFI audience.
3. Analyze the follow-on decision for a Series B at a known outperformer (telemedicine) against the opportunity cost of dry-powder preservation for opportunistic deployment.
4. Design a crisis-response playbook for a regional currency shock — with options for hedge, restructure, write-down, or double-down — that preserves LP confidence.
5. Recommend a Q1 portfolio-construction package and articulate the single sentence that will anchor the Q3 LP letter.

On the morning of **Monday, April 6, 2026**, **Catalina Restrepo** sat at her desk in the Andina Impact Partners office on Carrera 7 in Bogotá with the Q1 Investment Committee memo open on her laptop. Eight investment opportunities had cleared due diligence in the prior ten weeks: two in clean energy, three in health-tech, two in education, and one in agriculture. The Investment Committee would meet at 8:30 a.m. Wednesday. The Q1 deployment, once committed, would govern five more quarters of portfolio management and the LP annual meeting in November.

## The Protagonist

Catalina Restrepo, 42, had been Portfolio Manager at Andina Impact Partners since the firm's founding in 2019. A graduate of Universidad de los Andes in Bogotá with an MBA from Stanford Graduate School of Business, she had spent eight years in mainstream private equity at a Mexico-based growth fund before joining Andina as the second founding employee. Fund I (USD 28M, vintage 2020) had closed its investment period in 2023 and was in harvest: her six Fund-I deals had produced, as of December 31, 2025, a net IRR of 14.1% (slightly below the 15% target) and an impact score of 72/100 (above the 65 target), with two full exits, one partial exit, two held, and one write-off. The LP communications on the write-off — an education business that had lost its major contract in 2022 — had been, in Restrepo's private view, the single most important document she had authored in her career.

---

## The Decision

Restrepo had to commit, by 9:00 a.m. on Wednesday, April 8, the Q1 portfolio-construction package the Investment Committee would treat as the fund's initial deployment:

1. **Sector Allocation** — percentage of the USD 50M deployed to each of clean energy, health-tech, education, and agriculture, with the remainder as dry powder.
2. **Ticket Sizes** — range USD 2M to USD 5M per deal, constrained by a single-deal maximum of 10% of fund and a single-sector maximum of 30%.
3. **Number of Deals** — between six and ten in Q1, drawn from the eight cleared plus two reserve opportunities.
4. **Dry-Powder Reserve** — target percentage for follow-on and opportunistic deployment over Q2–Q5, in the range 10% to 20%.
5. **IRIS+ Metrics Commitment** — three to five sector-specific measurable outcomes per deal, committed in the LP-agreement annex by Q2 close.

Five further rounds would follow. Q2 would face the hundred-day review and the first early-signal read of the Q1 deployment. Q3 would face the LP annual meeting. Q4 would face follow-on decisions at two portfolio companies already signalling Series B. Q5 would face a projected regional crisis (an Argentine peso event the fund's macro view treated as near-certain by end of 2026). Q6 would face the first two exit windows.

## Company Background

Andina Impact Partners had been founded in June 2019 by Catalina Restrepo and Francisco Salazar, both formerly of a Mexican growth-capital fund. The firm's strategy was geographically focused on Peru, Colombia, Chile, Mexico, and selective Brazilian deals, and sector-focused on the four Fund-II sectors. The Fund-II LP base comprised a Chilean public-sector pension (USD 15M), a Colombian private pension (USD 10M), a Peruvian defined-contribution plan (USD 8M), a European family office (USD 5M), and two DFIs — one European, one U.S. (USD 6M each). The fund had a four-year investment period and a standard 2-and-20 fee structure, with an impact carry trigger: 20% of carry subject to achievement of 65/100 on the IRIS+ composite at exit.

The fund's mandate, in the LP agreement, defined impact as "measurable, additional, and intentional positive social or environmental outcomes in underserved LATAM populations." The mandate prohibited investments in tobacco, fossil-fuel extraction, weapons, gambling, and predatory consumer finance. The Investment Committee comprised Restrepo, Salazar, and two external members: a Chilean social-impact professor and a Peruvian former commercial-bank chief executive.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM impact-investing market in 2026 was characterized by three structural features. First, capital-to-deal-flow ratios had compressed materially: USD-denominated deal volume had doubled between 2022 and 2025, while aggregate committed capital had grown 3.4x, producing price pressure on the highest-quality deals and a bifurcation between "programmatic" and "premium" ticket sizes. Second, the IRIS+ reporting standard — maintained by the Global Impact Investing Network — had, in 2024, become the dominant LP-reporting framework among institutional investors, with 72% of surveyed LATAM LPs requiring IRIS+ reporting by 2025 compared with 41% in 2022. Third, regional currency volatility had continued, with the Argentine peso, the Mexican peso, and the Colombian peso all experiencing multi-standard-deviation moves between 2022 and early 2026; the Argentine situation remained the most uncertain, with consensus macro forecasts pointing to a high probability of a material devaluation in 2026.

Empirically, the post-2015 impact-investing literature converged on three findings. First, funds that reported IRIS+ metrics and financial returns in balanced emphasis had experienced fund-life LP re-up rates of 67%, compared with 43% for financial-emphasis funds and 38% for impact-emphasis funds. Second, concentration in a single sector above 35% of fund had been associated with 180-basis-point IRR volatility at fund-life. Third, follow-on investments in Q4 vintage deals that had reached product-market fit by the hundred-day review had produced, on average, a 1.6x multiple uplift over the original ticket at exit.

## The Situation

### How we got here

Fund II had been raised between September 2024 and January 2026 against a target of USD 50M and achieved a hard close at that figure. Deal origination for Q1 had begun in November 2025 and had produced, after first-pass screening of 47 opportunities, the eight cleared deals plus two reserve candidates. The pipeline had been evaluated against both financial-return and impact-outcome criteria in a parallel two-track diligence process the firm had used since Fund I.

### What Restrepo knew

The internal team had modeled three Q1 portfolios. A Conservative Portfolio (USD 12M clean energy, USD 13M health-tech, USD 10M education, USD 8M agriculture, USD 7M dry powder, eight deals) projected Year-6 IRR 16.8%, impact score 74, LP confidence at Q6 79. An Aggressive Portfolio (USD 18M clean energy, USD 15M health-tech, USD 6M education, USD 7M agriculture, USD 4M dry powder, seven deals) projected IRR 19.1%, impact score 71, LP confidence 73 (lower because Q5 crisis absorbs the thin dry powder). A Diversified Portfolio (USD 10M / 10M / 10M / 10M / USD 10M dry powder, ten deals) projected IRR 14.4%, impact score 75, LP confidence 81.

### What she did not know

Whether the telemedicine deal's founding team would accept a USD 2.5M ticket at Q1 given their preference for a larger lead investor; whether the Oaxaca mini-grid contractor's six-week schedule delay would persist through Q2; whether the Argentine exposure in the diagnostics-as-a-service company and the education deal could be rebased to USD-pricing before Q5 crisis fired; and whether the European DFI would, at the Q3 LP meeting, press for deeper IRIS+ granularity than the Q1 commitment foresaw.

### What others were saying

**Francisco Salazar**, Co-Founder: *"Diversified. Ten deals. Ten million dry powder. The Argentine crisis is not a question of whether, only when. If we land in Q5 with four million in dry powder and two companies to rescue, we will lose one."*

**Dr. Lucía Ferreira**, External IC Member (academic): *"Balanced in the LP letter. Impact depth in the portfolio. Commit to IRIS+ sector-specific metrics at the deepest tier in the LP-agreement annex. The DFIs will ask for it in Q3 anyway."*

**Pablo Iriarte**, External IC Member (former commercial banker): *"The ten-deal portfolio is a Fund-I mindset. The Fund-II target is fifteen percent. Put a third in health-tech and take the telemedicine lead. Follow-on capacity matters more than dry powder at this vintage."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Restrepo had narrowed the space to three Q1 portfolios.

- **Option A — Conservative Eight Deals:** USD 12M clean energy (2 deals) / USD 13M health-tech (3 deals) / USD 10M education (2 deals) / USD 8M agriculture (1 deal) / USD 7M dry powder. Projected IRR 16.8%, impact 74, Q6 LP confidence 79.

- **Option B — Aggressive Seven Deals:** USD 18M clean energy (2 deals, larger tickets) / USD 15M health-tech (3 deals, telemedicine as lead) / USD 6M education (1 deal) / USD 7M agriculture (1 deal) / USD 4M dry powder. Projected IRR 19.1%, impact 71, Q6 LP confidence 73.

- **Option C — Diversified Ten Deals:** USD 10M / USD 10M / USD 10M / USD 10M / USD 10M dry powder (ten deals, two per sector at health-tech). Projected IRR 14.4%, impact 75, Q6 LP confidence 81.

Restrepo's private view, not yet shared with her co-founder, was that Option A was the right Q1 and that the Q4 follow-on decision — specifically whether to take pro-rata-plus on the telemedicine Series B — was the compound that would lift Option A into the Option-B return range without absorbing the dry-powder reserve that the Q5 crisis would need.

(See **Exhibit 4** for the six-quarter forecast under each option and **Exhibit 5** for the Q5 Argentine-peso sensitivity.)

At 7:42 a.m., Restrepo wrote two sentences at the top of the Q1 IC memo: *"Balanced reporting. Concentrated diligence. Dry powder for the crisis we can see from here."* The Investment Committee would convene at 8:30 a.m. Wednesday. The first Q1 wire would leave the fund's administrator on April 24.
