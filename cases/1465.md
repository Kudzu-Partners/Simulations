# Quillaven Capital Advisors: Defending the Signal Book

**Case Code:** FIN-2026-1465 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1465 — *The Behavioural Finance Lab — Pricing the Anomalies* · **v1.0 · September 2026**

---

At 14:30 on Wednesday, April 29, 2026, Mateo Aizpurua-Vidal, head of quantitative research at Quillaven Capital Advisors, watched Sofia Quiroga-Iturbe put a ten-bar chart on the screen of the firm's San Joaquín conference room. It sorted 85 Latin American mid-cap companies by how far their last quarterly earnings had beaten or missed analysts' consensus. The biggest beats had kept rising for sixty trading days after the announcement; the biggest misses had kept falling. Buying the top group and selling the bottom one had earned 7.3% over those sixty days, with a t-statistic of 2.4.

Quillaven's Investment Committee convened at 16:00. Aizpurua-Vidal had ninety minutes to turn that chart, and two other return patterns in the firm's own data, into a proposal the committee would fund. Carlos Rodríguez-Palmieri, the chief investment officer, had named the size three weeks earlier: USD 240 million.

## The Protagonist

Aizpurua-Vidal, 45, held a doctorate in finance and had spent eight years building systematic equity strategies at two investment banks in New York and São Paulo before joining Quillaven in 2018 to formalise its quantitative desk. The desk had four people and had never put a systematic strategy into production. Everything Quillaven sold was fundamental and discretionary, and several of its portfolio managers regarded the quant team as junior. Aizpurua-Vidal's own position, repeated to his team that week, was that the patterns in Quiroga-Iturbe's data were real and also fragile: crowded and expensive to trade in Latin America.

## The Decision

Aizpurua-Vidal had to decide what the committee would see: how much of the measured return Quillaven could actually keep, how to combine three signals into one book, how each signal should be run, and how much money to request.

All three signals came from Quiroga-Iturbe's hand-cleaned panel of 85 Costarian and Andean companies over 36 months (see Exhibit 2). The **earnings-surprise signal** bought the biggest beats and sold the biggest misses after each quarterly announcement, holding for sixty trading days. The **cheapness signal** bought shares priced low relative to book value and sold those priced high, rebalanced monthly. The **trend signal** bought the previous year's best performers and sold the worst, skipping the most recent month, also rebalanced monthly.

The committee would hear three voices. Rodríguez-Palmieri, a fundamental investor by training, distrusted patterns found in small samples: 36 months and 85 names, he had said, was a small place to find three of them. Andrea Mendívil-Castaño, head of risk, asked a different question: whether each return belonged to Quillaven or was exposure that could be bought more cheaply through products already on the market. Quiroga-Iturbe, who owned the data, would have to answer for every number in it.

## The Company and its Setting

Quillaven, founded in 2009 and headquartered in San Joaquín, Costaria, managed USD 4.2 billion, mostly for regional pension funds (see Exhibit 1). Its USD 1.6 billion flagship, the Quillaven LATAM Equity Fund, had trailed its regional benchmark by 320 basis points over eighteen months, the firm's worst stretch since 2015, and investor review meetings had followed. Rodríguez-Palmieri's diagnosis was that the managers picked stocks well but had carried the wrong style exposures at the wrong points in the cycle. He wanted a systematic overlay on top of the fundamental process.

Trading in the region was expensive. Pablo Mancera-Dueñas, head of trading, had supplied a six-month log of 14,000 executed trades: the median round trip in Latin American mid-caps cost 72 basis points, against 18 in U.S. mid-caps. Short selling was harder still. Of the companies in the bottom earnings group, 42% had no shares available to borrow at any price.

## The Situation

The team had split the ninety minutes into three working blocks: the earnings signal and its costs; the combined book; and, once Rodríguez-Palmieri joined, the implementation and the size of the request.

Quiroga-Iturbe had already measured each signal's after-cost return per unit of risk (see Exhibit 2) and how the three moved together. The cheapness and trend signals had tended to move in opposite directions.

If nothing persuasive reached the committee, the overlay would not be built, and the flagship would go into its next investor reviews with the process that had produced the eighteen-month shortfall.

## What Aizpurua-Vidal Controlled

In the first block Aizpurua-Vidal fixed three assumptions. For annual trading drag he could adopt 450 basis points, because a patient systematic book would trade more cheaply than the discretionary orders in Mancera-Dueñas's log; 650 basis points, because that log was the only cost evidence Quillaven had measured itself; or 1,000 basis points, because spreads and borrow recalls widened sharply in a political shock. For the short side he could present the full short leg, because the committee should see the signal as researched and borrow shifted from quarter to quarter; a constrained short covering about 60% of the bottom group, weighted toward borrowable names, because it kept a short leg while trading only what could be borrowed; or a long-only book, because it removed borrow fees and recalls altogether. On the sample, he could argue that 36 months was sufficient, because the patterns had been documented in other markets for decades; acknowledge the small sample and defend the result on its consistency, because stating a limit first denied a sceptic the chance to discover it; or decline to commit capital until five years of local data existed, because a failed launch after a bad eighteen months would cost more than a delay.

In the second block he set each signal's percentage share of the book, the three adding to 100%. Three positions had formed. Mancera-Dueñas favoured roughly equal thirds, because the gaps between the after-cost figures were smaller than the error in estimating any of them from 36 months, though an even split gave the weakest signal as much capital as the strongest. Mendívil-Castaño favoured tilting toward the earnings and trend signals, which had the highest after-cost figures, though that ranking rested on the same 36 months. Quiroga-Iturbe favoured a heavier cheapness weight, because its tendency to move against the trend signal made it the book's main diversifier, though it had the lowest after-cost figure of the three. Aizpurua-Vidal also decided whether to size the legs on measured volatilities and correlations, so that the book's risk reflected how the signals moved together, or on standalone figures, because three-year correlations were too unstable to build on.

In the third block he recommended, for each signal separately, whether Quillaven ran it in-house as an active long-short book or bought the exposure passively through an exchange-traded fund or swap on a published regional index built on the same idea. In-house kept control of the signal's definition and captured the version the research had measured; passive cost less in fees, staff and borrow, and was simpler to monitor. He chose the request — USD 60 million, 120 million, 180 million or 240 million — and a position on a review gate: accept an initial allocation revisited after six months against triggers negotiated now, because it let the committee approve with limited downside and gave Quillaven a defined path to the full amount; or argue for committed sizing, because six months covered only two earnings seasons and a scheduled re-vote could close a sound strategy on noise.

The committee's reception was tracked as an approval score, starting at zero and rising as each block's reasoning persuaded Rodríguez-Palmieri, Mendívil-Castaño and Quiroga-Iturbe. Aizpurua-Vidal also watched the earnings signal's annualised return after his cost, borrow and market-impact assumptions, in basis points; the three-signal book's expected return per unit of volatility, its Sharpe ratio; and the allocation, in USD millions.

## Closing

At 14:32 Quiroga-Iturbe uncapped a marker. Mancera-Dueñas's log lay open on the table beside the borrow survey. Aizpurua-Vidal wrote three numbers on the whiteboard — 450, 650 and 1,000 — and the room waited for him to choose one.
