## Abstract

NUBA Foods, a Mexican packaged-snack brand with 2025 revenue of USD 180M and a distribution footprint across Mexico, Colombia, and Peru, faced an emerging social-media crisis on the afternoon of Saturday, April 18, 2026. At 3:42 p.m., a customer posted a photograph on X (formerly Twitter) of what appeared to be a small piece of metallic wire inside a sealed package of NUBA Maíz Tostado. By 5:07 p.m., the post had 140 retweets, 412 likes, and a handful of replies from two mid-tier food-and-nutrition accounts. Verónica Elizondo, Chief Communications Officer since 2023, had — under the company's crisis protocol — two hours to initiate Phase 1 (Detection and Assessment). Five phases would unfold over the next three weeks: Detection, First Response, Escalation Management, Peak Crisis, and Recovery and Rebuilding. The case opens at 4:02 p.m. Saturday as Elizondo convenes the crisis team by video conference with a USD 200,000 crisis budget, a Net Sentiment score of +12, a Brand Trust index of 64, and a Stakeholder Score of 58. The Phase-1 calibration of four dials — response speed, transparency, tone, and stakeholder focus — would compound across the four subsequent phases.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the four Phase-1 crisis dials (response speed, transparency, tone, stakeholder focus) and their cross-effects under a Situational Crisis Communication Theory (SCCT) frame.
2. Diagnose the signals that a social-media post has crossed the threshold from background noise to managed-crisis status, and recommend a monitoring cadence.
3. Analyze the trade-off between voluntary recall and regulator-led recall in a contamination event, both financially and in stakeholder-trust terms.
4. Design a stakeholder sequencing plan across five phases that puts consumer and employee audiences ahead of media and legal without overexposing the company.
5. Recommend a complete Phase-1 dial setting and identify the single Phase-2 event most likely to force a re-tuning.

On the afternoon of **Saturday, April 18, 2026**, **Verónica Elizondo** sat at the kitchen table in her apartment in Condesa and read, for the third time, the screenshot that the social-listening vendor had sent to her mobile at 3:52 p.m.: a photograph of what the vendor had identified, with 84% confidence, as a piece of galvanized wire approximately 18 millimeters long inside a sealed package of NUBA Maíz Tostado. The post had been up for ten minutes. It had 62 retweets. By 4:01 p.m. it had 140.

---

## The Protagonist

Verónica Elizondo, 47, had been Chief Communications Officer of NUBA Foods since February 2023. A graduate of the Universidad Iberoamericana in Mexico City with a master's degree in communications from Northwestern University, she had spent twenty-one years in corporate communications across three companies: eight years at a Mexican beverages company (junior roles through senior manager), seven years at a European consumer-goods company (spokesperson and Latin American regional communications lead), and six years at a global insurance company's Mexican subsidiary (Director of Communications, Crisis Preparedness lead). The NUBA board had recruited her on the strength of a 2021 crisis she had managed at the insurance company: a ransomware incident that had been communicated to 180,000 policyholders across a weekend and had produced a Net Promoter Score that finished fifteen points higher than it had started.

---

## The Decision

Elizondo had to decide, by 6:00 p.m. Saturday, the four-dial Phase-1 calibration the crisis protocol would treat as her initial posture:

1. **Response Speed** — on a slider from Slow/Thorough (complete investigation before any public statement) to Immediate (acknowledge and engage within the first two hours).
2. **Transparency** — from Minimal (legal-cleared one-line holding statement) to Full Disclosure (specific product details, batch numbers, operational steps being taken).
3. **Tone** — from Legalistic (formal, liability-aware) to Empathetic (consumer-first, acknowledging concern).
4. **Stakeholder Focus** — from Media/Legal-first (press room, counsel) to Consumer/Employee-first (direct-to-consumer hotline, all-staff internal communication ahead of the external cycle).

The four dials were, in the protocol's logic, correlated: response speed below six hours required at least partial transparency; empathetic tone was incoherent with media-first stakeholder focus. But the combinations remained a choice, and four-dial calibrations that the literature had consistently identified as the strongest Phase-1 postures were not the default at most food manufacturers.

---

## Company Background

NUBA Foods had been founded in 1998 in Guadalajara by two former employees of a multinational snack-foods company and had grown from a single-SKU corn-based product to a fifty-SKU portfolio across corn, rice, and fried-vegetable snack categories. Fiscal 2025 revenue was USD 180M (approximately MXN 3.4B), with a gross margin of 38% and an operating margin of 9.2%. The company operated two manufacturing plants — one in Jalisco and one in Guanajuato — and sold through both modern trade (62% of revenue) and traditional trade (38%). Core portfolio brands were NUBA Maíz Tostado (32% of revenue), NUBA Vegetal (21%), NUBA Arroz (18%), and a line of premium imported snacks distributed under the NUBA Selecto brand (29%).

The product in the photograph was a 45-gram sealed bag of NUBA Maíz Tostado from the Jalisco plant, batch code JAL-M-0412-26 (produced April 12, 2026 — six days before the post). NUBA had a documented HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points) program, a 2024 third-party food-safety audit that had rated the Jalisco plant 94 out of 100, and a metal-detection protocol at the end of the production line that had, in NUBA's own records, rejected 214 units in the relevant shift without a documented false-negative signal. The crisis protocol had been updated in July 2025 and tested in a tabletop exercise in November 2025.

## Industry and Market Context

The Latin American packaged-snack industry in 2026 had faced five notable social-media crises in the prior thirty-six months, four involving contamination allegations and one involving nutritional mislabeling. The aggregate pattern from those five cases was consistent. First, crises handled with Phase-1 calibrations biased toward Immediate/Full-Disclosure/Empathetic/Consumer-first recovered, on average, 74% of pre-crisis brand trust within twelve weeks. Crises handled with Slow/Minimal/Legalistic/Media-first calibrations recovered 31%. Second, the single decision with the largest observed effect on twelve-week outcomes was the recall decision: voluntary recalls initiated before regulator-led recalls had been associated with 42% smaller net-sentiment troughs. Third, influencer engagement in Phase 2 had compounded effects in both directions; brands that had met with the relevant high-reach account, on the record, within 72 hours had recovered materially better than those that had issued generic statements.

Empirically, the Situational Crisis Communication Theory literature converged on a small number of robust findings. Clusters — victim, accidental, preventable — produced different optimal responses. A sealed-package foreign-object event, before a root-cause investigation, sat in the accidental cluster and supported an apology-plus-investigation posture. A prevention-failure finding in the investigation would move the cluster to preventable and, with it, the appropriate Phase-3 posture.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The NUBA social-listening vendor had flagged the post at 3:48 p.m. — six minutes after it was posted — on a pattern match against the product name and a negative-sentiment trigger. Elizondo had been notified at 3:52 p.m. The Chief Executive Officer had been notified at 3:55 p.m. by the CCO's mobile. The Director of Quality had been notified at 3:57 p.m. The head of the Jalisco plant had been reached at 3:59 p.m. and had begun the batch-isolation protocol at 4:06 p.m. The General Counsel was in São Paulo on vacation and had been reached at 4:14 p.m.

### What Elizondo knew

The internal batch-isolation had been initiated within twenty-four minutes of the post. The plant had retained the pack-line metal-detection logs for JAL-M-0412-26 and had preliminary confirmation that the metal-detector had not rejected any unit during the relevant shift window. The product was sealed and the photograph was consistent with a genuine event. Three additional posts had appeared on Instagram between 3:54 p.m. and 4:00 p.m. from accounts in other cities, at least one of which referenced a different batch code (JAL-M-0410-26). The social-listening vendor had flagged two further unverified posts pending sentiment confirmation.

### What she did not know

Whether the contamination was confined to the two named batches or reflected a multi-batch or multi-SKU pattern; whether the metallic object's source was the line, the raw material, the packaging, or a post-production tamper; whether the COFEPRIS (Mexican food-safety regulator) social-listening team had already seen the posts and would open an inquiry without waiting for a voluntary report; and whether Carla Comida, the 2.1M-follower nutrition influencer whose name had already appeared twice in the vendor's alerting, would post before the end of the weekend.

### What others were saying

**Mauricio Benavides**, Chief Executive Officer, had reached her at 4:09 p.m.: *"Get to Full Disclosure and Empathetic. Consumer first. Employees in parallel. Legal and media third. Run every decision through you and me — do not defer to external counsel on tone."*

**Laura Villarreal**, Director of Quality: *"We have not confirmed a line failure. We have not ruled one out. We can say, on the record in the next two hours, that we are isolating batches JAL-M-0412 and JAL-M-0410 and halting distribution of both until investigation closes."*

**Iván Corominas**, External Crisis Counsel (reached at 4:22 p.m.): *"Every sentence you put out before you know the root cause is a sentence you will read back in a class-action filing. Minimal statement. No apology. No batch numbers."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Elizondo had narrowed the Phase-1 calibration to three postures.

- **Option A — Full Disclosure, Empathetic, Consumer-First (the CEO's posture):** Response Speed Immediate (within 90 minutes). Transparency Full (product name, batch codes, investigation step, recall posture). Tone Empathetic. Stakeholder Focus Consumer and Employee. Direct-to-consumer hotline stood up by 6:00 p.m. Internal all-staff message by 6:30 p.m. External statement via NUBA channels and a media pool at 6:45 p.m. Estimated Phase-1 spend USD 15,000.

- **Option B — Partial Disclosure, Professional, Balanced (the counsel-informed middle):** Response Speed Moderate (within three hours). Transparency Partial (acknowledgment of the post, confirmation of investigation, no batch codes until Monday). Tone Professional. Stakeholder Focus Media and Consumer in parallel. Internal message Sunday morning. Estimated Phase-1 spend USD 8,000.

- **Option C — Minimal, Legalistic, Media-First (the external counsel's posture):** Response Speed Slow (first statement Monday morning). Transparency Minimal (one-line holding statement, "taking all concerns seriously"). Tone Legalistic. Stakeholder Focus Media and Legal first. Estimated Phase-1 spend USD 3,000.

Elizondo's private view, not yet shared with external counsel, was that Option A was the right posture and that the external-counsel recommendation reflected the 2015 playbook rather than the 2026 environment. The harder question was the Phase-4 voluntary-recall decision that lay three phases ahead — which Option A did not prejudge but which it made more credible when the decision arrived.
