## Abstract

The Chief Data Officer at the case-universe Santiago-headquartered mid-cap industrial-and-services holdings had been asked, in the March 2026 Group CEO mandate, to deliver an eight-problem business-statistics framing guide for the Q2 2026 board as part of a conglomerate-wide "intuition-to-number" audit. The Group had identified eight independent 2025–2026 decisions taken across portfolio companies where executive intuition had diverged from the statistical reading by more than 20 percentage points — an A/B test where marketing had reported a lift that the p-value did not support, a churn-model deployment where the base-rate fallacy had inflated the perceived precision, a factory capability study where the Cp reading had been misinterpreted as a yield target, a demand-forecast confidence interval that had been quoted as a single point, a customer-satisfaction survey with a margin of error that had been omitted from the press release, a medical-test reading where the positive-predictive-value had been confused with the sensitivity, a portfolio allocation where the minimum-variance weights had been rounded to convenience, and a clinical trial where the power calculation had been produced only after the sample had been set. The March 2026 mandate asked the Chief Data Officer to work through all eight problems as the board warm-up, identify the common framing failures, and commit to a one-page framing guide the Group would adopt across the portfolio. The case opens at 7:44 a.m. on Monday, April 20, 2026, at the Group's Santiago head office as **Ingrid Helgadóttir-Valenzuela**, a 52-year-old Chief Data Officer, prepares the eight-problem commitment.

2. Analyse the Bayesian-updating setup on customer churn (20% base rate, 85% sensitivity, 10% false-positive rate, posterior probability-of-churn-given-flagged 68%; posterior falls to 31% when the base rate drops to 5%) and articulate why base-rate sensitivity is the operational vulnerability of every ML-scoring workflow, and why the posterior number changes with the base rate even when the model does not.
3. Diagnose the process-capability setup (target 50, spec ±1.5, sigma 0.8, Cp = (USL−LSL)/(6σ) = 0.625, defect rate 6.1%) and articulate why Cp below 1.0 means the process is wider than the specification, why the fix lives at the process level rather than the specification level, and why the defect-rate number is the customer-facing translation of the Cp number.
4. Respond to the four inference-planning problems — demand-forecast confidence interval with 246-months-required sample for ±10 precision; survey sample size 897 for 3% margin vs. 200 for 6.4% margin; positive-predictive-value 8.8% at 1% prevalence even with a 95-sensitivity-90-specificity test; clinical-trial n = 686 per group for 80% power — and articulate why precision, margin, PPV and power are all governed by the same square-root-of-n mechanics, and why "precision is expensive" is the operational punchline that every sample-size question reduces to.
5. Design the eight-problem framing guide that pairs each point answer with the decision the answer serves — articulating why the Chief Data Officer's durable contribution is getting the question right before the method, and why in business statistics the method is secondary to the framing.

## The Decision

Helgadóttir-Valenzuela had to commit, by the 5:00 p.m. Group CEO pre-brief, the eight-problem framing-guide recommendation.

1. **Problem 1 — A/B test.** 32/1000 control vs. 41/1000 treatment. One-sided p-value 14.2%. Not significant at the 5% level. The eye sees a lift; the statistics do not. Framing sentence: the question was "does this move a KPI enough to matter" and the answer is "we cannot distinguish the lift from noise at this sample."
2. **Problem 2 — Bayes on churn.** 20% base rate, 85% sensitivity, 10% false-positive. Posterior = (0.85·0.20)/(0.85·0.20 + 0.10·0.80) = 0.68. At 5% base rate, posterior falls to 0.31. Base-rate sensitivity is the operational vulnerability.
3. **Problem 3 — Process capability.** Target 50, spec ±1.5, sigma 0.8, Cp = 3/(6·0.8) = 0.625. Defect rate ≈ 6.1%. Cp < 1.0 means the process is wider than the spec. Fix lives at the process, not the spec.
4. **Problem 4 — Demand-forecast CI.** 36 months, mean 500, SD 80. Upper 95% CI ≈ 526. For precision of ±10 units, required n ≈ 246 months. Precision is expensive.
5. **Problem 5 — Survey sample size.** Expected 70% satisfaction, margin 3%, required n = 897. At n = 200, margin = 6.4%. Square-root mechanics.
6. **Problem 6 — Medical-test PPV.** 1% prevalence, 95% sensitivity, 90% specificity. PPV = (0.95·0.01)/(0.95·0.01 + 0.10·0.99) = 0.088. Rare conditions plus good tests still produce mostly false positives.
7. **Problem 7 — Portfolio min-variance.** Minimum-variance allocation 12.9% in the risky asset; SD 7.7%. Rounding the weight to 15% for operational convenience raises variance by approximately 4%.
8. **Problem 8 — Clinical-trial power.** n = 686 per group for 80% power at a 0.3 effect size. Raising power to 90% requires an additional 34% of sample. Power is a purchase.

On Helgadóttir-Valenzuela's working guide, each point answer was paired with one framing sentence, one caution about when the answer changes (base rate for Problem 2, process drift for Problem 3, effect size for Problem 8), and one cross-reference to the Group's data-governance handbook. The board headline was that the toolkit was stable across all eight problems, and the operational leverage lived in the question-framing discipline.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The March 2026 Group CEO mandate asked for the eight-problem commitment by Q2. The eight audits, the toolkit review, and the framing-guide drafting had all landed by April 13. The Group CEO had asked Helgadóttir-Valenzuela to bring the commitment to the April 20 pre-brief.

### What Helgadóttir-Valenzuela knew

The statistical toolkit was stable across all eight problems; the operational failure mode was always the framing, not the method; base-rate sensitivity was the Bayesian vulnerability; process-capability readings below 1.0 required a process-level fix rather than a spec-level fix; and precision, margin, PPV and power all reduced to the same square-root-of-n mechanics.

### What she did not know

Whether the Group board would absorb the framing-guide recommendation cleanly or push for a method-compendium appendix; whether the eight problems were representative of the portfolio or biased toward the services subsidiaries; whether the base-rate caution on Problem 2 would translate into a re-scoring of the deployed churn model; and whether the clinical-trial power calculation for the Group's health-portfolio subsidiary would trigger a protocol amendment on an in-flight trial.

### What others were saying

**The Principal Statistician**: *"The toolkit does not change. The framing does. Do not bury the framing in the method appendix."*

## Options Under Consideration

Helgadóttir-Valenzuela had narrowed the space to three guide formats.

- **Option A — One-page framing guide with eight problems, eight framing sentences, three cautions:** the recommended guide. Board-adoptable; reproducible across the portfolio; pairs point answer with decision sentence. Recommended.
- **Option B — Twenty-page methods compendium with full derivations:** rejected — board-adoption rate on compendium-style documents below 40% in documented Group-level rollouts.
- **Option C — Five-problem summary with the three most-cited problems (A/B, Bayes, power):** rejected — the audit finding specifically flagged all eight problems as portfolio-wide; a five-problem summary leaves three operational gaps unaddressed.

Her private view was that Option A was the right format and that the problem most correlated with the audit-adjusted NPV-revision magnitude was Problem 2 — Bayesian churn — because the base-rate sensitivity was the vulnerability that had not been priced into the deployed model.
