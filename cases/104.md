## Abstract

The three-station serial processor line was, in the case universe, a simplified quantitative analog of a mid-complexity assembly-and-finishing line at a LATAM durable-goods plant that processed a single product family through three successive stations, each with a nominal service rate of 10 units per minute and a design throughput of 10 units per minute under perfect conditions. Across Q4 2025, the actual throughput had drifted to 6.8 units per minute against a design target of 9.4, against an unchanged labor and material envelope, and with a WIP buildup that had compounded over six consecutive weeks. On Monday, February 2, 2026, the Industrial Engineering Manager, a 39-year-old former lean consultant who had joined the plant as IE Manager in September 2024, had, after exhausting the operator-behavior, machine-maintenance and raw-material-variability hypotheses, opened the variability-lab commitment that would diagnose where the capacity had disappeared, and would recommend a station-level variability, inter-station buffer-capacity, and CONWIP-cap design that would restore throughput inside an envelope of USD 0 additional capital expenditure on new machinery, against a plant target of 8.5 units per minute at a cycle time below 18 minutes and a WIP cap at 24 units. The case opens at 6:14 a.m. on Monday, February 9, 2026, at the plant on the industrial corridor, as the IE Manager prepares the four-experiment lab protocol that will anchor the March 1 design commitment to the Plant Director.

## The Decision

The IE Manager had to commit, by the March 1 design commitment, the four-experiment lab protocol and the corresponding design recommendation:

1. **Experiment 1 — Benchmark Run** — whether to commit a 1,000-minute simulated run with all three stations at low variability, buffers at 0, CONWIP off, to establish the theoretical design throughput of 9.4 units per minute at an efficiency of 94% and a Little's-Law consistency check (WIP = Throughput × Cycle Time).
2. **Experiment 2 — Station-2 Variability Shift** — whether to commit a second 1,000-minute run with Station 2 shifted to high variability (Cs² = 1.0 from Cs² = 0.1), all other parameters held, to isolate the variability-propagation effect on throughput and document the blocked-Station-1 and starved-Station-3 pattern.
3. **Experiment 3 — Buffer Addition** — whether to commit a third 1,000-minute run with 5-slot buffers added between Station 1–2 and Station 2–3, to diagnose the throughput recovery at the cost of a WIP triple and the cycle-time inflation associated with the buffer build.
4. **Experiment 4 — CONWIP Cap** — whether to commit a fourth 1,000-minute run with a CONWIP cap of 12 units across the three stations, buffers held at 5 slots, Station-2 variability held at high, to diagnose the CONWIP-design commitment that preserves throughput above 8.5 units per minute while halving WIP and cycle time against the buffer-only design.

The four experiments would compound into a single design commitment. On the IE Manager's working model, the benchmark run delivered throughput 9.4 u/min, efficiency 94%, WIP 28 units, cycle time 3.0 minutes. Station-2 variability shift delivered throughput 6.8 u/min (a 28% drop), Station-1 blocked 34% of the time, Station-3 starved 31% of the time, efficiency 68%. Buffer-5 design delivered throughput 8.2 u/min, WIP 72 units, cycle time 8.8 minutes. CONWIP-12 design delivered throughput 8.0 u/min, WIP 12 units, cycle time 1.5 minutes — a near-parity throughput against buffer-only at a fraction of the WIP-and-cycle-time cost.

## Process Background

The three-station line had been commissioned in 2021 as part of a USD 3.1 million capacity expansion that had doubled the plant's throughput of the relevant durable-goods family. Each station had a nominal service rate of 10 units per minute under low-variability operating conditions, a five-year design life, and a maintenance schedule that had held mean service rates inside a 2% envelope through Q3 2025. The line ran two eight-hour shifts per day with a 40-minute changeover and a Saturday maintenance window.

The Q4 2025 plant-ops review had identified three specific observations the variability-lab envelope would investigate. First, the Q3 2025 throughput drift had, across a six-week window, produced a 28% drop in throughput without a corresponding drop in machine availability — a pattern that ruled out the mean-service-rate hypothesis. Second, the Q4 2025 operator-behavior audit had, across a 14-day observation window, documented no systematic deviation from standard work — a pattern that ruled out the operator-behavior hypothesis. Third, the Q4 2025 raw-material-variability check had, across a 30-batch sample, documented incoming quality variation inside the 2% envelope — a pattern that ruled out the raw-material hypothesis. Only the station-level service-time-variability hypothesis remained untested.

## Industry and Market Context

The factory-physics literature on three-station serial systems in 2026 was anchored by three structural findings the lab envelope sat inside. First, the Hopp-Spearman factory-physics evidence base had, across a 2000–2024 sample of serial-line case studies, documented a throughput drop of 20–35% per unit of Cs² increase at a mid-station under zero-buffer conditions — a pattern that anchored Experiment 2. Second, the buffer-design evidence base had, across a 2005–2024 sample of lean-manufacturing redesigns, placed the throughput recovery at 60–85% of the design throughput with 5-slot inter-station buffers against the zero-buffer baseline, at a WIP cost of 2.5x–3.0x — a pattern that anchored Experiment 3. Third, the CONWIP-design evidence base had, across a 2008–2024 sample of thirteen serial-line CONWIP implementations, documented a throughput-to-WIP efficiency of 2.0x–2.5x CONWIP-versus-buffer-only design, at a cycle-time compression of 4x–6x — a pattern that anchored Experiment 4.

Empirically, the post-1990 operations-research literature had two robust findings the lab envelope had to respect. First, serial-line throughput under a single variable-station shift was governed by the interaction of blocking and starvation at upstream and downstream stations — not by the mean service rate at the variable station. Second, CONWIP-controlled lines had, across the relevant commercial and academic sample, preserved throughput within 3–5% of the buffer-only design at 30–40% of the WIP and 20–30% of the cycle time — a design that made the CONWIP commitment the dominant envelope at any plant target with cycle-time constraints.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Plant Director had, in the December 2025 operating review, approved a March 1 design-commitment deadline, a throughput floor of 8.5 units per minute, a cycle-time ceiling of 18 minutes, and a WIP cap of 24 units. The Head of Maintenance had, on January 15, confirmed that Station 2's service-time variability had drifted from a Cs² of 0.1 to 1.0 across Q3 2025 on a wear-pattern on the station's second axis that would not be economically repairable inside the 2026 capex envelope. The Head of Scheduling had, on January 28, argued that the current zero-buffer design was the root cause of the throughput drift. The Lean Program Director had, on January 30, argued for a CONWIP design over a buffer-only design.

### What the IE Manager knew

The team had run three preliminary simulation runs that matched the known throughput and WIP observations within the expected variance envelope. The team had modelled three design envelopes. A Buffer-5-only Envelope projected throughput 8.2 u/min, WIP 72, cycle time 8.8 minutes — above the plant throughput target but above the WIP cap. A CONWIP-12-plus-Buffer-5 Envelope projected throughput 8.0 u/min, WIP 12, cycle time 1.5 minutes — a design consistent with all three plant targets. A Do-Nothing Envelope projected throughput 6.8 u/min — below the plant throughput target.

### What she did not know

Whether the CONWIP cap of 12 was the optimal cap or whether a 10 or 14 cap would dominate on the throughput-WIP-cycle-time joint target; whether the Station-2 variability could be compressed below Cs² = 1.0 inside the 2026 capex envelope; whether the three-station line would shift to a different product family inside the simulation envelope; and whether the Plant Director would accept a four-experiment lab protocol or push for a single experiment.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Maintenance**: *"Station 2 variability is locked. The wear pattern cannot be economically reversed."*

**The Head of Scheduling**: *"Buffer-5 between stations. That is the highest-throughput design inside the current machine envelope."*

**The Lean Program Director**: *"CONWIP cap on top of the buffer. WIP has to come down. Cycle time has to come down. Throughput holds."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

The IE Manager had narrowed the space to three designs.

- **Option A — CONWIP-12-plus-Buffer-5:** CONWIP cap at 12 units, inter-station buffers at 5 slots, Station-2 variability held at Cs² = 1.0. Throughput 8.0 u/min. WIP 12. Cycle time 1.5 minutes. Inside all three plant targets. Recommended.

- **Option B — Buffer-5-only:** Inter-station buffers at 5 slots, no CONWIP cap. Throughput 8.2 u/min. WIP 72. Cycle time 8.8 minutes. Above WIP cap of 24. Rejected.

- **Option C — Do-Nothing:** Zero buffers, no CONWIP cap, Station-2 variability held. Throughput 6.8 u/min. Below throughput floor. Rejected.

The IE Manager's private view was that Option A was the right design and that the single experiment most correlated with the March 1 design commitment was Experiment 4 — the CONWIP cap of 12 that, on its own, delivered a near-parity throughput against the buffer-only design at 17% of the WIP and 17% of the cycle time. Little's Law held across every experiment, as it had to: WIP = Throughput × Cycle Time was the identity that connected the three plant targets.
