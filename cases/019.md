## Abstract

Mercantia S.A., a USD 1.8 billion mid-cap Latin American consumer-goods distribution group, reported Q1 2026 EBITDA 22% below plan, against a revenue miss of only 4%, and against a competitive environment that showed no single obvious shock. The incoming Chief Analytics Officer, Silvia Kawasaki, had been given ninety minutes and access to a five-module BI stack — Sales & Revenue, Product Mix & Margin, Operations & Logistics, Customer & Segment, Macro & Competitive — and an investigation budget of four "deep-dive" queries in addition to standard dashboard navigation, to identify the root causes of the margin collapse before the Thursday-morning board meeting. The group CEO had asked for "three causes ranked by magnitude, a hypothesis on each, and one recommended action." The case places Kawasaki at her desk on the Wednesday evening before the board meeting with the five dashboard modules loading on her second monitor and a stopwatch on her phone.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Apply hypothesis-driven, MECE investigation to a multi-dimensional margin collapse.
2. Evaluate the information value of different BI modules given a fixed investigation budget.
3. Design an interrogation sequence that maximizes root-cause-identification probability per minute.
4. Diagnose the interaction between mix, price, volume, and cost-driver effects in an EBITDA bridge.
5. Recommend three ranked causes, a hypothesis per cause, and one next action.

On the evening of **Wednesday, April 22, 2026**, **Silvia Kawasaki** sat in the Chief Analytics Officer's office on the eleventh floor of Mercantia's Bogotá tower, the sky outside slate-colored, and clicked, for the first time, on the CAO's dashboard-stack link. Five modules loaded. A small banner across the top read: *Budget — 4 deep-dive queries remaining.* Her phone on the desk showed 19:02. The board meeting was at 09:00 the next morning.

---

## The Protagonist

Silvia Kawasaki, 42, had joined Mercantia as Chief Analytics Officer in February 2026 — six weeks before Q1 close — from a role as Head of Data Science at a São Paulo fintech. She held an undergraduate degree in industrial engineering from the Instituto Tecnológico de Buenos Aires and a master's in statistics from Stanford. Before the fintech she had been, for five years, a consultant in the analytics practice of a large strategy firm. She had been recruited by Mercantia's CEO on the strength of a specific prior: she had, at a large Chilean retailer, identified the root cause of a similar 2021 margin collapse within ten days by combining mix-shift analysis with supplier-rebate reconciliation.

Her contract carried a two-year performance gate tied to "measured analytical impact on operating results," a phrase her contract defined loosely and her bonus defined precisely.

---

## The Decision

By **08:30 on Thursday, April 23, 2026**, Kawasaki had to deliver to the CEO a three-slide memo:

1. Three ranked root causes of the Q1 EBITDA miss, each with a point estimate of its contribution.
2. A falsifiable hypothesis per cause, with an explicit data signal that would confirm or reject it.
3. One recommended next action — operational, commercial, or analytical — that the CEO could table at the Friday executive committee.

She had, to reach that memo, ninety minutes on the dashboards and four deep-dive queries. A deep-dive query consumed analyst-hours and could, at best, be returned in twelve hours; four queries could be dispatched that evening and would return by 07:00 Thursday.

---

## Company Background

Mercantia had been founded in 1974 in Bogotá as a regional consumer-goods distributor and had, over five decades, expanded into a multi-country platform spanning Colombia (56% of 2025 revenue), Peru (21%), Ecuador (14%), and Panama (9%). 2025 revenue was USD 1.8 billion with an EBITDA margin of 9.2%; Q4 2025 EBITDA margin was 9.6%. The Q1 2026 EBITDA margin stood at 7.2% — a compression of 240 basis points in one quarter against a revenue base that had declined 4%.

The product mix spanned three broad categories: food and beverage (44% of revenue), home and personal care (36%), pharmaceutical OTC and health (20%). The customer mix spanned modern trade (large supermarket chains, 38%), traditional trade (small independent stores, 31%), horeca (hotels, restaurants, cafés, 18%), and direct online (13%).

Mercantia's supply chain comprised four regional distribution centers (Bogotá, Lima, Quito, Panamá), a fleet of 280 trucks, and a workforce of 2,100 direct employees. A cost-transformation program had been running since Q3 2024 and was tracking to plan through Q4 2025.

## Industry and Market Context

The Latin American consumer-goods distribution sector had, in Q1 2026, sat inside three material dynamics: (i) FX volatility in the Colombian peso (which had moved from COP 4,050/USD in October 2025 to COP 4,220/USD in March 2026) had selectively stressed importers and distributors with USD-denominated inventory; (ii) a new Colombian e-invoicing compliance regime, effective January 1 2026, had required an IT upgrade that several distributors had deferred; (iii) modern-trade consolidation in Colombia — with the two largest supermarket chains having merged in late 2025 — had increased buying power on the modern-trade side.

Quarterly competitive data placed peer EBITDA margins within 50 basis points of their 2025 levels: no comparable competitor had reported a 240-basis-point compression. The Mercantia miss, in other words, was not the industry.

## The Situation

### What Kawasaki knew at 19:02

The five dashboard modules available on her stack were:

- **Sales & Revenue**: revenue by country, category, customer segment, day-of-week, sku, channel.
- **Product Mix & Margin**: gross margin by category, sku, brand; rebate receivables; price-point ladder.
- **Operations & Logistics**: DC-level throughput, truck utilization, delivered-on-time rate, shrink, returns.
- **Customer & Segment**: customer-level revenue, gross-margin contribution, payment terms, DSO.
- **Macro & Competitive**: FX, category growth, peer benchmarks, trade-promotion intensity.

Her four deep-dive queries would return: (a) custom joins beyond the dashboard's pre-modeled cubes; (b) sub-sku-level granularity; (c) event-time analysis (e.g., "revenue per hour since launch of new e-invoicing system"); (d) any analytical question she could pose that an analyst could execute in 12 hours.

### What she did not know at 19:02

Which of six hypotheses she had pre-written on a yellow legal pad was correct. In order: *(H1) FX stress on imported SKUs. (H2) Mix shift to lower-margin modern trade post-consolidation. (H3) Rebate timing (receivables slipped). (H4) E-invoicing implementation disruption. (H5) Cost-transformation program stalled. (H6) Hidden category-level shrink.*

### What the CEO had said

**Rodrigo Santamaría**, CEO, had told her at 17:18 that afternoon: *"I do not want you to tell me there are thirteen little causes. There may be thirteen. I want three ranked, and I want you to be willing to be wrong about the number-three for the sake of being right about the number-one."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Three investigation sequences were on the table:

- **Option A — Bridge-First (classical).** Start with the Q1 EBITDA bridge in Sales & Revenue and Product Mix & Margin. Quantify the price / volume / mix / cost decomposition before any hypothesis. Expected minutes: 35. Deep-dive queries consumed: 1 (sub-SKU margin).

- **Option B — Hypothesis-First (MECE).** Rank the six hypotheses by *a priori* likelihood × magnitude. Interrogate the top two in parallel across Customer & Segment and Operations & Logistics. Expected minutes: 45. Deep-dive queries consumed: 2.

- **Option C — Breadth-First (scan).** Spend the first twenty minutes scanning all five modules for anomalies. Then deep-dive. Expected minutes: 55. Deep-dive queries consumed: 3.

Kawasaki suspected that the correct sequence was a hybrid: a ten-minute bridge scan (Option A's opening), followed by the two highest-likelihood hypotheses from her legal pad (Option B's core), followed by one confirmatory deep-dive query. She had, however, not yet tested the hypothesis.

(See **Exhibit 3** for the Q1 EBITDA bridge vs. plan at category level and **Exhibit 4** for the BI-module interdependency map.)

---

Kawasaki picked up the phone, called her on-call analyst in Medellín, and said one sentence: *"Give me sub-SKU margin by category and country for Q1 versus Q4, de-duplicated for rebate timing, by 07:00."* That was one query. She hung up, looked at the clock — 19:09 — and opened the Product Mix & Margin dashboard.
