## Abstract

Altiplano Energía was, in the case universe, a La Paz-headquartered LatAm diversified energy group operating across Bolivia, Peru, Chile and Argentina with USD 2.8B in 2025 revenue, a portfolio spanning upstream hydrocarbons (USD 1.4B revenue share), gas midstream (USD 0.8B), renewables — utility-scale solar and wind (USD 0.4B) — and a USD 0.2B hydrogen-and-pilots portfolio, a 2025 Resilience Index of 52 on a 2023-rebased baseline of 50 (composite captures scenario-coverage, portfolio-option depth and time-to-rebalance), a 2025 Stranded-Asset-Risk Index of 46 against a LatAm-energy peer-benchmark of 34 (higher means more risk), a 2025 Strategic-Optionality Index of 38 against a peer-benchmark of 58, and a 2024 CapEx commitment pipeline of USD 1.2B across a 2026–2029 horizon. The Q4 2025 board had approved a USD 42M five-round strategic-foresight programme — the ForesightLab programme — and had positioned it as the company's response to three overlapping pressures: a 2024–2025 LatAm carbon-policy trajectory that had diverged across the four countries by more than ten points on the 2030 intensity-target axis, a 2025 stranded-asset diagnostic that had flagged 18% of the CapEx pipeline on the solar leg, and a 2025 competitor diagnostic that had surfaced two regional peers advancing hydrogen and CBAM-adjacent positions. The Q1 2026 board had reaffirmed the USD 42M envelope and a Chief-Strategy-Officer-led five-round commitment cycle against a Resilience floor above 68, a Stranded-Asset-Risk ceiling below 32, a Strategic-Optionality floor above 60, and a Composite ForesightLab Score above 68. The case opens at 6:44 a.m. on Monday, April 20, 2026, at the Altiplano Energía La Paz head office as **Javier Solís-Paredes**, a 51-year-old Chief Strategy Officer, prepares the Round-1 scenario-axis-selection commitment. Sixty-nine days separate him from the June 28 board strategic-review session.

5. Design the Round-5 weak-signal-anchored embedding commitment that converts ForesightLab from a strategy exercise into a permanent signal-tracking-and-rebalancing capability anchored on CBAM-class weak-signal monitors and a quarterly scenario-refresh cadence.

## The Protagonist

Javier Solís-Paredes, 51, had assumed the Chief-Strategy-Officer role on February 1, 2024, after seventeen years across strategy, corporate development and M&A leadership at two pan-LatAm utilities and an Andean mining group. He had, in 2020–2023, built a scenario-anchored portfolio-rebalancing discipline inside a Chilean utility that had, across a 2019–2023 carbon-policy cycle, preserved portfolio IRR inside a 250-basis-point band against peers that had suffered 400–900-basis-point compression. The Altiplano Energía Chief Executive Officer had recruited him in December 2023 specifically on two capabilities: his documented discipline of anchoring CapEx pipelines to scenario architectures rather than to single-point forecasts, and his willingness to price real options against board-level expansion bias. His 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the Resilience floor and 25% to the Stranded-Asset-Risk ceiling.

## The Decision

Solís-Paredes had to commit, by the May 11 board pre-review, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–5.

1. **Round 1 — Scenario-axis selection.** Three archetypes: carbon-policy × demand-elasticity (two uncertainty axes, four named quadrants, Composite 76), single-axis-price (price-of-oil as sole axis — Composite 28, structural-blindness), or three-axis (add technology-cost axis — Composite 54, decision fragmentation). Working envelope: carbon-policy × demand-elasticity.
2. **Round 2 — Portfolio-stress-test allocation.** Three archetypes: four-bucket (40-30-20-10 allocation across Fast, Slow, Chaos, Status Quo, Composite 76), majority-base-case (70% Status Quo, 30% distributed — Composite 32), or barbell (45 Fast / 45 Slow / 10 else — Composite 50). Working envelope: four-bucket.
3. **Round 3 — OPEC-shock rebalancing.** Three vectors: scenario-preserving (absorb the OPEC+ shock as a stress observation, preserve four-bucket architecture, reprice solar-leg stranded exposure, Composite 76), stranded-asset denial (double the hydrocarbons allocation — Composite 28, Chaos-path repricing), or phased (rebalance on two countries only — Composite 54). Working envelope: scenario-preserving.
4. **Round 4 — Real-options pricing.** Three vectors: deferral-and-abandonment (USD 12M one-year hydrogen-pilot deferral plus USD 6M abandonment option on stranded-solar leg, Composite 78), expansion-only (only expansion options priced — Composite 30), or phased (deferral only, no abandonment — Composite 54). Working envelope: deferral-and-abandonment.
5. **Round 5 — Weak-signal-anchored embedding.** Three vectors: integrated-architecture (standing scenario-and-signals committee, CBAM and policy-divergence weak-signal monitors, quarterly scenario-refresh cadence, CapEx-gate integration, Composite 78), one-off-exercise (treat ForesightLab as a single refresh — Composite 32), or phased (integrated on two signal classes only — Composite 54). Working envelope: integrated-architecture.

On Solís-Paredes's working model, a Two-Axis / Four-Bucket / Preserving / Real-Options / Integrated envelope delivered Resilience 70, Stranded-Asset Risk 28, Strategic Optionality 62, Composite 74.

## Company Background

Altiplano Energía had been founded in La Paz in 1978 and had, across forty-eight years, built a USD 2.8B-revenue diversified energy portfolio across the Andean four-country footprint. The 2022–2024 portfolio-pivot programme had allocated USD 1.1B into renewables and hydrogen pilots but had executed the allocation on a single-axis price-of-oil scenario model that the November 2025 internal review had flagged as structurally insufficient for the post-2023 carbon-policy divergence observed across the footprint. The Q4 2025 board had approved the USD 42M ForesightLab envelope explicitly as a scenario-architecture rebuild rather than a marginal refresh.

Three Q1 2026 events were named in the March 2026 strategic-review dossier. First, the January 2026 Chilean carbon-policy consultation had published a 2030 intensity-target band 12 points tighter than the Bolivian policy trajectory — the single largest cross-country divergence in the 2023–2026 window. Second, the February 2026 OPEC+ session had announced a production cut that had spiked Brent by 18% across a 14-day window and had repriced the stranded-asset probability on the Andean-solar leg from 12% to 26%. Third, the March 2026 EU CBAM consultation had opened a scope-expansion dossier that, on the Altiplano product mix, implied a 2029 cost-of-carbon exposure on 38% of hydrocarbons-linked exports into the EU market.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2023–2025 scenario-planning literature across diversified energy portfolios had, across 26 documented cycles, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, portfolios anchored on two-axis scenario architectures with four named quadrants had documented Stranded-Asset-Risk readings 2.1× lower than portfolios anchored on single-axis architectures. Second, four-bucket allocations had preserved Strategic-Optionality above 58 in 74% of cases against 22% for majority-base-case allocations. Third, portfolios that had priced deferral-and-abandonment options at a 2–4% of CapEx envelope had avoided stranded-asset write-downs in 76% of cases against 28% for expansion-only portfolios.

Empirically, the post-2023 strategic-foresight literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, weak-signal-monitoring architectures anchored on three-to-five signal classes with a quarterly-refresh cadence had, across a 2019–2024 sample of 22 programmes, preserved Resilience readings above 64 in 82% of cases against 28% for one-off exercises. Second, scenario architectures that had been integrated into CapEx-gate discipline had sustained IRR inside a 200-basis-point band across shock cycles in 78% of cases against 22% for scenario exercises that had remained disconnected from the gate.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the December 2025 strategy session, approved the USD 42M envelope and the June 28 review calendar. The Head of Corporate Development had, on March 3, delivered the scenario-architecture diagnostic. The Chief Financial Officer had, on March 10, delivered the stranded-asset repricing pack. The Chief Operating Officer had, on March 17, delivered the portfolio-option depth diagnostic. The Chief Legal Officer had, on March 24, delivered the CBAM regulatory dossier.

### What Solís-Paredes knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. A Two-Axis / Four-Bucket / Preserving / Real-Options / Integrated envelope projected Composite 74. A Single-Axis / Base-Case / Denial / Expansion / One-Off envelope projected Composite 24. A Hybrid envelope projected Composite 48.

### What he did not know

Whether the Bolivian energy ministry would generalise the Chilean intensity-target band within the envelope window; whether the OPEC+ shock was the opening observation of a Chaos-path repricing or a mean-reverting episode; whether the CBAM scope-expansion would reach the hydrocarbons chapter inside the envelope horizon; and whether the integrated scenario-and-signals architecture would clear the November 2026 plan refresh on the new CapEx-gate cadence.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Corporate Development**: *"Two axes. Carbon-policy and demand-elasticity. Any third axis fragments the decision."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"Real options on the hydrogen pilot. Twelve million buys you the right to be wrong."*

**The Chief Operating Officer**: *"Preserve the architecture through the OPEC shock. Doubling hydrocarbons is how you sign up for the Chaos path."*

**The Chief Executive Officer**: *"You don't predict the future. You build a portfolio the future can't kill. Pay the base-case give-back."*

## Options Under Consideration

Solís-Paredes had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Two-Axis / Four-Bucket / Preserving / Real-Options / Integrated:** the recommended envelope. Resilience 70. Stranded-Asset Risk 28. Strategic Optionality 62. Composite 74. Recommended.
- **Option B — Single-Axis / Base-Case / Denial / Expansion / One-Off:** the legacy envelope. Composite 24. Rejected — stranded-asset and optionality probability above 70%.
- **Option C — Hybrid / Phased / Phased / Phased / Phased:** the partial envelope. Composite 48. Rejected — scenario architecture fragments under partial gating.

Solís-Paredes's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 4 — the real-options pricing — because it was the round that converted the strategic-foresight exercise from a narrative into a priced inventory of deferral-and-abandonment rights that the CapEx-gate could actually exercise.
