## Abstract

The VP of Platform at PagaRápido, a case-universe Colombian mid-cap fintech payments processor with approximately 8.4 million monthly active users and USD 2.1 billion in 2025 transaction volume, had been asked, in the February 2026 CEO-Governance mandate, to deliver a four-round distributed-systems decomposition of the transaction platform ahead of the Q3 2026 Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) on-site audit. The baseline platform was a seven-year-old Spring-Boot monolith backed by a single-master PostgreSQL cluster, a nightly batch ETL into a Redshift warehouse, a custom in-process fraud-scoring module, and a SARLAFT pipeline that ran on a daily cron and produced compliance reports 24 hours after the underlying transactions. The Q4 2025 internal-audit reading recorded an operational RTO of approximately four hours and an RPO of approximately thirty minutes — both failing the SFC's documented operational-resilience expectations — and a SARLAFT reporting latency that was, in the Chief Compliance Officer's reading, "one audit cycle away from a formal enforcement action." The February 2026 mandate asked the VP of Platform to decompose the monolith across four rounds — CQRS, event streaming, lakehouse governance, streaming SARLAFT — and to commit to a distributed-systems posture that brought RTO, RPO and SARLAFT latency inside the SFC's resilience and AML-reporting expectations. The case opens at 6:48 a.m. on Tuesday, April 21, 2026, at PagaRápido's Bogotá head office as **Santiago Escobedo-Kowalski**, a 47-year-old VP of Platform, prepares the four-round commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Round-1 CQRS separation (command side against PostgreSQL for writes; query side against read replicas and materialized views; checkout latency drops 40% because the transactional path stops contending with the analytical path) and articulate why CQRS is the operational lever on the single-master-database contention problem and why the separation is the precondition for the rest of the decomposition.
2. Analyse the Round-2 Kafka event-streaming layer (transactions emit events; fraud-scoring, notifications and analytics services subscribe as loose-coupled consumers; the chain stops stopping when one service fails) and articulate why event streaming is the architectural answer to the "one failure propagating to all" pattern and why the loose-coupling payoff is the operational shape of resilience.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 lakehouse-governance step (bronze-silver-gold medallion, data contracts between teams, schema registry, partition skew on high-volume customers addressed by key-salting and rebalance) and articulate why the medallion pattern is the operational answer to the warehouse-plus-data-lake duplicate-of-truth problem, and why partition-skew addressing is the single most commonly-skipped step in lakehouse rollouts.
4. Respond to the Round-4 streaming SARLAFT pipeline (anomaly detection on the transaction stream, risk-tiered alerting, fully-auditable trail, blue-green deployment so SFC-mandated updates land without downtime) and articulate why moving AML compliance from a batch pipeline to a streaming pipeline changes the regulator's risk calculus on the institution and why the blue-green deployment clause is the operational lever that turns mandatory regulator-driven updates into a zero-downtime event.
5. Design the four-round commitment that brings RTO from four hours to forty-two minutes, RPO from thirty minutes to three, streaming event lag below 500ms, and the distributed-systems score to 86 — articulating why decomposing a monolith is not a rewrite but a negotiation with failure, and why resilience is the shape of the decomposition, not a feature on top of the monolith.

## The Protagonist

Escobedo-Kowalski, 47, had been VP of Platform since 2023, had spent nineteen years across three platform-heavy companies — a top-3 Colombian retail bank, a Kompralia competitor, and PagaRápido. In 2019–2022 he had led the retail bank's decomposition of the core-banking monolith into a CQRS-plus-streaming architecture that had reduced the bank's documented operational RTO from approximately six hours to approximately forty minutes and had cleared the Superintendencia's 2022 operational-resilience audit. In 2024 he co-authored with his Principal Platform Architect *Small Enough to Fail*, an internal white paper that framed platform resilience as a decomposition discipline. The PagaRápido CEO had assigned him the four-round commitment on three considerations: his operational command of the retail-bank decomposition; his discipline of sequencing the four rounds in the right order (CQRS first, streaming second, lakehouse third, SARLAFT fourth); and his willingness to commit to an RTO/RPO posture the SFC would accept in the on-site audit.

## The Decision

Escobedo-Kowalski had to commit, by the 5:30 p.m. CEO-Governance pre-brief, the four-round decomposition envelope and the SFC-audit-readiness posture.

1. **Round 1 — CQRS.** Write-side to PostgreSQL with idempotent command handlers; read-side to read replicas and materialized views refreshed on change. Checkout latency drops from approximately 220ms to approximately 130ms — a 40% improvement — because the transactional path stops contending with the analytical path for the same rows. The separation is the precondition for Rounds 2–4.
2. **Round 2 — Kafka event streaming.** Transactions emit events to a Kafka backbone. Fraud-scoring, notifications and analytics services consume as loose-coupled subscribers. One service failing stops stopping the whole chain. Event lag below 500ms is the operational target; the Staff SRE's load testing on the Q1 staging environment held the lag at approximately 180ms at 2x peak volume.
3. **Round 3 — Lakehouse governance.** Bronze-silver-gold medallion. Data contracts between teams. Schema registry. Partition skew on the top-100 highest-volume corporate customers addressed by key-salting and rebalance; without the salting the analytics job on the gold layer was running 84 minutes, with the salting it runs 11 minutes. Data contracts land on the December 2026 cutover for the full gold layer.
4. **Round 4 — Streaming SARLAFT AML pipeline.** Anomaly detection on the transaction stream with a Flink topology. Risk-tiered alerting (green/yellow/red). Fully-auditable trail in an append-only store. Blue-green deployment so SFC-mandated updates land without downtime. SARLAFT reporting latency drops from 24 hours to approximately 3 minutes — inside the SFC's emerging real-time-AML expectation.

On Escobedo-Kowalski's working posture, the four-round decomposition brought the RTO to approximately 42 minutes (from 4 hours), the RPO to approximately 3 minutes (from 30 minutes), the streaming-event lag below 500ms, and the distributed-systems audit score to 86. The SFC on-site audit in Q3 2026 was now defensible.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2015–2025 distributed-systems literature produced three findings the commitment had to respect. First, fintech processors that decomposed their core-transaction monolith into a CQRS-plus-streaming architecture recorded median RTO reductions of 76–83% against pre-decomposition baselines in documented cases. Second, lakehouse-plus-medallion architectures with explicit data-contract and schema-registry clauses reduced cross-team analytics-rework rates by 38–52% against pre-medallion baselines. Third, streaming SARLAFT pipelines reduced regulator-assessed AML-reporting-latency risk by approximately 68% against batch pipelines in documented LATAM cases.

The post-2020 operational-resilience literature added two findings. Blue-green deployment patterns on regulator-mandated updates reduced downtime-incident frequency during update cycles by approximately 84% against rolling-deployment baselines in documented banking cases. And event-streaming architectures with explicit loose-coupling contracts between consumers reduced cross-service failure-propagation rates by approximately 72% in documented processor-level audits.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The February 2026 mandate asked for the four-round decomposition by Q2. The Q4 2025 internal audit, the Q1 2026 architecture workshops, and the Q1 staging-environment load tests had all landed by April 14. The CEO had asked Escobedo-Kowalski to bring the commitment to the April 21 pre-brief.

### What Escobedo-Kowalski knew

CQRS is the precondition for the rest of the decomposition; Kafka event streaming is the operational answer to the "one failure propagating to all" pattern; the lakehouse-plus-medallion pattern is the operational answer to the duplicate-of-truth problem; and streaming SARLAFT is the operational answer to the SFC's updated AML-reporting expectation. The Q3 2026 SFC on-site audit was the hard deadline; the Series D raise was the soft deadline.

### What he did not know

Whether the SFC's emerging real-time-AML expectation would compress further from 15 minutes to 5 minutes before the Q3 audit; whether the partition-skew rebalance on the top-100 corporate customers would hold across the Black-Friday-equivalent Q4 volume event; whether the blue-green deployment on the SARLAFT pipeline would pass the SFC's update-audit clause on first pass; and whether the Series D lead investor's technical-due-diligence process would accept the four-round decomposition as Series-D-ready or push for a fifth round on a secondary-region disaster-recovery posture.

### What others were saying

**The Principal Platform Architect**: *"CQRS first. Streaming second. Lakehouse third. SARLAFT fourth. Any other order and the decomposition breaks."*

**The Staff SRE**: *"RTO forty-two minutes is on a clean failover. On a dirty failover it is sixty-eight. Report both."*

**The Chief Compliance Officer**: *"The streaming SARLAFT is the single most important round. The SFC's on-site team will start there."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"The Series D diligence will price the four-round decomposition at approximately 0.8x revenue. A fifth round on secondary-region DR would add another 0.3x. That is a board decision."*

## Options Under Consideration

Escobedo-Kowalski had narrowed the space to three decomposition envelopes.

- **Option A — Four-round decomposition (CQRS / Streaming / Lakehouse / SARLAFT) by Q3 2026:** the recommended envelope. Meets SFC audit; event lag below 500ms; RTO 42min; RPO 3min; SARLAFT latency 3min. Recommended.
- **Option B — Three-round decomposition (CQRS / Streaming / SARLAFT) with lakehouse deferred to 2027:** rejected — the lakehouse round is the precondition for the SARLAFT pipeline's fully-auditable trail; deferring it exposes the Round 4 to an audit-finding on the trail completeness.
- **Option C — Four-round decomposition plus a fifth round on secondary-region DR by Q4 2026:** rejected for the Q3 audit window — the fifth round is a Series-D-diligence conversation that belongs after the audit closes.

His private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the round most correlated with the Q3 audit outcome was Round 4 — the streaming SARLAFT pipeline — because it was the round that the SFC's on-site team would start on and because the blue-green deployment clause was the operational lever that would turn the rest of the audit into a sequence of defensible check-ins.
