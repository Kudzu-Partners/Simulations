## Abstract

The General Manager at the case-universe Santiago-headquartered Chilean coworking operator Nexo Coworking S.R.L. — approximately CLP 1.2bn in 2025 revenue, 14 employees, one 1,200-square-metre facility in the Providencia comuna, 60% occupancy at end of Year 1, a freelancer-and-remote-worker membership base with an embryonic corporate-membership category, a Business Model Canvas filled in during 2024 incorporation that remained the firm's de facto rulebook, and a market environment that included WeWork operations in Las Condes, Regus in Vitacura, and an emerging category of cafés installing fiber and bookable desks at sub-CLP-5,000 day rates — had been asked, in the April 2026 Board directive, to run the 75-minute three-period integrated-business primer sim and deliver a three-period commitment that would set the firm's pattern for the 2026–2027 cycle. The problem the sim was designed to reveal was that Year 1 had been a linear operation — fill the facility, retain the members, pay the rent — and Year 2 would be an integrated operation in which four functional levers interacted. Dropping the price chased occupancy but cascaded through Wi-Fi load, concierge time and coffee consumption into a gross-margin compression that only appeared one period later. Investing only in sales stalled member experience. Investing only in facilities stalled acquisition. The case opens at 2:12 p.m. on Thursday, April 23, 2026, at Nexo's Providencia office as **Francisca Aránguiz-Pino**, a 36-year-old General Manager, prepares the three-period commitment.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Read the pre-filled Osterwalder Business Model Canvas — Customer Segments (freelancers, remote workers), Value Propositions (reliable space, community, coffee), Channels (direct sales, member referral), Revenue Streams (monthly memberships), Cost Structure (rent, staff, coffee, internet) — as the firm's coherence rulebook, and articulate why the canvas is a constraint system rather than a design artefact, and why inconsistencies between the canvas and the Period-1 decisions are the Period-1 diagnostic signal.
2. Trace the Period-1 causal loop — drop price → occupancy rises to 78% → operating cost per member rises because coffee, concierge time and Wi-Fi load scale with heads → gross margin falls four points — and articulate why the decision cascades through the P&L into Period 2, why the first-order revenue lift masks the second-order margin compression, and why every functional decision carries its second-order consequence two quarters out.
3. Allocate the Period-2 budget across Porter's simplified value chain — facilities management, member experience, sales and marketing, administration — and articulate why investment concentrated in any one activity fails, why the value-chain-balance test is the Period-2 diagnostic rather than any single-activity optimisation, and why competitive advantage in coworking sits across the chain rather than in any single node.
4. Track the Kaplan-Norton Balanced Scorecard dashboard — financial (revenue, margin, EBITDA), customer (occupancy, NPS, churn), process (facilities quality, service consistency), learning (staff satisfaction, training, new offerings) — across the three periods and articulate why the Period-2 NPS drop predicts the Period-3 revenue drop, why leading indicators diverge from lagging indicators by one period, and why reading only the occupancy headline misses the leading-indicator signal.
5. Respond to the Period-3 competitive move — WeWork opens three blocks away with promotional pricing, a café installs fiber and bookable desks at half Nexo's price, corporate members want private rooms, freelancers want lower price, staff wants raises — and commit to the Mintzberg pattern test: whether the three-period decision sequence was a deliberate strategy or a series of reactions, articulating why a business is not four functions but four levers that cannot be pulled in isolation.

On the afternoon of **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, **Francisca Aránguiz-Pino** arrived at Nexo Coworking's Providencia office at 2:12 p.m., opened the NegocioCompleto workbook her community manager had finalised earlier in the day, and re-read the sentence she had pinned to her general-management-brief wall: *"Four levers. Zero of them pulled in isolation."*

---

## The Protagonist

Aránguiz-Pino, 36, had been General Manager since co-founding Nexo Coworking in 2024, had completed her undergraduate degree in Commercial Engineering at the Universidad Católica de Chile in 2012, had spent seven years at a Chilean retail holding in operations and commercial roles, and had opened Nexo with two co-founders after an eighteen-month build-out through 2023–2024. The Board had assigned her the April sim on three considerations: her command of the pre-filled Business Model Canvas as an operating document; her willingness to read the Period-1 margin compression as a second-order effect of the Period-1 price cut rather than as a cost-management failure; and her comfort committing to a strategic pattern rather than a reactive playbook in the face of the WeWork opening.

---

## The Decision

Aránguiz-Pino had to commit, by the 4:30 p.m. Board debrief, the three-period commitment.

1. **Period 1 — Pricing, facility, hiring, segment.** Do not drop the price to chase occupancy. Invest CLP 32m in a facilities upgrade (meeting-room-add and coffee-station upgrade) that raises the BMC value-proposition bar. Hire one community manager (CLP 1.8m monthly). Primary segment focus: remote workers from the tech sector. Secondary: freelance creatives.
2. **Period 2 — Value-chain balance.** Budget CLP 58m across the four value-chain activities: CLP 18m facilities management (meeting-room maintenance and Wi-Fi upgrade), CLP 16m member experience (event programme, partner discounts), CLP 14m sales and marketing (tech-sector outreach, referral programme), CLP 10m administration (financial-system upgrade, contract automation).
3. **Period 3 — Competitive response.** Differentiate on community and industry-cluster density rather than compete on price with WeWork or the café. Launch two private-room products for corporate members at CLP 480,000 per month. Keep freelancer membership at CLP 180,000 without cut. Staff: targeted CLP 2.2m retention raise for the community manager; performance-pool for the rest.

The case-universe Santiago coworking category had, across 2020–2025, grown from approximately CLP 28bn to CLP 68bn, concentrated on three operator tiers (international brands, local chains, independents), and absorbed pandemic-era remote-work demand that stabilised in 2023–2024. The 2025 Providencia-comuna coworking market registered an average occupancy of 66%, an average member-tenure of 14 months and an average net promoter score of +31, with significant variance across operators.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1985–2025 integrated-business-management literature produced three findings the primer sim had to respect. First, Osterwalder's Business Model Canvas, operationalised as a coherence rulebook rather than a one-off design document, produced documented Year-2 operating-margin readings approximately 25–40% higher than ad-hoc-management operations on comparable 2018–2024 small-operator cycles. Second, Porter's value-chain-balance discipline — investment distributed across all primary activities rather than concentrated in one — produced documented advantage-durability readings approximately 1.8× those of concentrated-investment strategies in people-and-facility-intensive businesses. Third, Kaplan-Norton's Balanced Scorecard with the leading-to-lagging-indicator translation taken seriously produced documented Period-N+1 revenue-prediction accuracy approximately 45% higher than occupancy-headline-only dashboards.

The post-2010 Mintzberg literature added two findings. The deliberate-vs-emergent strategy distinction, tested at the end of the three-period cycle, correctly identifies whether the decision sequence constituted a pattern or a series of reactions, with reactive patterns producing documented Year-3 margin readings 30–45% below deliberate patterns. And the competitor-response discipline — differentiate rather than match-and-escalate — was the single strongest predictor of small-operator survival in the 2022–2025 Chilean coworking cycle.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Year-1 cycle from January 2025 through December 2025 produced 60% occupancy, CLP 1.2bn revenue, CLP 230m gross margin and a CLP 44m net loss net of amortisation. The April 2026 Board directive asked for the three-period commitment. WeWork's Providencia announcement landed April 14. The café fiber-and-bookable-desks launch appeared on Instagram April 17.

### What Aránguiz-Pino knew

The BMC is the coherence rulebook; price cuts cascade into margin compression through coffee, concierge and Wi-Fi scaling; value-chain balance beats concentration; NPS drops precede revenue drops by one period; WeWork and the café can only be out-competed on community and cluster density, not on price or flash.

### What she did not know

Whether the tech-sector remote-worker primary-segment focus would absorb the Period-1 facility upgrade's costs by Period 2 or by Period 3; whether the private-room corporate-membership product would find its first three anchor clients in Period 3 or in Q4 2026; whether the WeWork promotional pricing was a three-month campaign or a twelve-month default; whether the community-manager retention raise would hold the manager through Period 3; and whether the Board would accept the hold-price-and-differentiate posture under the visible WeWork pressure.

### What others were saying

**The Board Chair**: *"The BMC was the Year-1 rulebook. The three periods are the Year-2 pattern. I want to see the pattern."*

**The CFO (part-time fractional)**: *"Do not drop price. The Period-1 margin compression from the occupancy chase is exactly what the scorecard is designed to flag."*

**The Community Manager**: *"If you launch the private-room corporate product in Period 3, I need training in Period 2. And a raise."*

**The Marketing Agency**: *"Tech-sector outreach produces anchor members in six to nine months. Start it in Period 1, not Period 2."*

**The Landlord**: *"The lease renegotiation opens Q4 2026. Your negotiating position is your Period-3 numbers."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Aránguiz-Pino had narrowed the space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — Hold-price, facilities-upgrade, value-chain-balanced budget, tech-sector focus, differentiate-on-community Period-3 response (Recommended):** BMC coherence, Porter value-chain balance, Balanced Scorecard leading-indicator discipline, Mintzberg deliberate pattern. Recommended.
- **Option B — Drop price 15% in Period 1 to chase occupancy, aggressive sales and marketing, match WeWork promotional pricing in Period 3:** rejected — the Period-1 margin compression cascades, the Period-3 price match destroys margin, and the Mintzberg pattern test reads as reactive.
- **Option C — Pivot entirely toward corporate membership and close the freelancer tier:** rejected — the BMC change is too large for the three-period window, the freelancer tier is the community-density anchor, and the corporate-product ramp is longer than the sim's horizon.

Her private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the period most correlated with the Year-2 survival was Period 2 — the value-chain balance — because Period 2 was the period where the Balanced Scorecard's leading indicators either improved or degraded, and Period 3's competitive response rested on Period 2's NPS trajectory.
