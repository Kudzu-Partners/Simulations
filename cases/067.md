## Abstract

Pacífico Group was, in the case universe, a Chilean-headquartered consumer-and-industrial conglomerate listed on the Santiago Stock Exchange, with 2025 group revenue of USD 3.42B, consumer-brands, food-processing, and industrial-distribution segments across Chile, Peru, Colombia, and Ecuador, and a nine-member Board of Directors with five independent directors, three family-representative directors, and the Chair. On Thursday, March 12, 2026, the Board Chair confirmed the Year-2026 Board calendar — five Board and committee sessions that would each carry one headline scenario: the Q1 annual CEO performance review, a Q2 contested acquisition of a USD 380M regional competitor, a Q3 whistleblower complaint alleging IFRS-15 revenue-recognition errors, a Q4 CEO long-term-incentive-plan maturation and AGM vote, and a Q4-late risk-committee proposal to enter Argentina via acquisition. The Q1 scenario opened first and required a posture on a CEO who had missed earnings in two consecutive quarters, whose CFO had privately flagged executive-retention concerns tied to the CEO's management style, and whose candidacy for a performance rating carrying a USD 6.4M LTIP-payout modifier would be voted by the Compensation Committee at Q4. Dr. Magdalena Ossa, Lead Independent Director and Chair of the Governance Committee since April 2024, faced five scenarios across the year. The case opens at 7:18 a.m. on Sunday, March 22, 2026, as Ossa prepares the Scenario-A executive session for the Tuesday Board meeting.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Scenario-A CEO-performance-review posture — executive session, 360-review commission, bilateral-shareholder-meeting discipline — and diagnose the lead-independent-director role in a divided Board.
2. Analyze the Scenario-B contested-acquisition decision — management-selected fairness opinion, CFO-signed integration-risk memorandum, deferred vote — and recommend a procedural-rigor framework.
3. Diagnose the Scenario-C IFRS-15 whistleblower complaint and recommend an audit-committee-without-management investigation protocol including external auditor and independent counsel engagement.
4. Design the Scenario-D Compensation-Committee negative-discretion framework that survives the AGM vote on the CEO LTIP maturation.
5. Recommend a complete Scenario-A package and identify the Scenario-E Argentina-entry phased-approval structure that gives the CEO a yes on governance's terms.

On the morning of **Sunday, March 22, 2026**, **Dr. Magdalena Ossa** sat at the dining-room table of her apartment in Las Condes and opened the Pacífico Group board pack at Section Six — *Annual CEO Performance Review*. The pack was 146 pages. The executive-summary memo ran four pages. An accompanying sealed envelope, delivered by the company secretary at 6:12 p.m. Friday, contained a three-page confidential note from the Chief Financial Officer flagging executive-retention risk. A separate email, time-stamped 9:04 a.m. Saturday, contained a request from the second-largest shareholder — a Chilean family-office holding 8.7% — for a bilateral meeting with the Lead Independent Director "before Tuesday."

---

## The Protagonist

Dr. Magdalena Ossa, 58, had been a Pacífico Group independent director since May 2022, Chair of the Governance Committee since April 2024, and Lead Independent Director since January 2025. A graduate of Universidad de Chile Law School with a doctorate in corporate law from the University of Oxford, she had, before her directorships, served as Partner at a Santiago-based corporate-law firm between 1998 and 2018 and Dean of the Universidad Católica de Chile Law School between 2018 and 2022. She held, at the opening of the case, two other LATAM-public-company directorships and chaired the audit committee at one of them. Her 2025 Lead-Independent-Director appointment letter had listed three priorities: Board-management interface discipline, governance-committee work-plan execution, and "active stewardship of the independent directors' role on matters the management cannot ask itself about."

---

## The Decision

Ossa had to commit, by 9:00 a.m. on Tuesday, March 24, the Scenario-A response package the Board would treat as the year's governance-posture anchor:

1. **CEO-Review Posture** — whether to convene an executive session of independent directors pre-Board, whether to commission an independent 360-review with retention-risk as an explicit scope item, and whether to take the bilateral shareholder meeting or decline pending Board-Chair consultation.
2. **CFO-Note Handling** — whether to formally incorporate the CFO's retention-risk note into the Board pack, treat it as confidential material to the audit committee, or manage it through the Lead-Independent-Director channel only.
3. **Scenario-Year Framing** — the single line the Lead Independent Director will use to frame the posture that carries through the remaining four scenarios.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across five scenarios. A Scenario-A package that convened an executive session, commissioned the 360-review with retention-risk scope, took the bilateral shareholder meeting with the Board Chair informed, and framed the year as "procedural rigor is not obstruction — it is the director's duty," produced, on the internal governance model, a Scenario-B contested-acquisition deferral that preserved Board independence, a Scenario-C whistleblower investigation that ran through audit committee and independent counsel, a Scenario-D negative-discretion LTIP resolution that survived the AGM vote, and a Scenario-E Argentina-entry phased-approval that gave the CEO a conditional yes.

## Company Background

Pacífico Group had been founded in 1972 as a Valparaíso-based consumer-distribution business by the Ossandón family and had, across fifty-four years, expanded into the three-segment conglomerate the case studied. 2025 group revenue was USD 3.42B, EBITDA USD 558M, net income USD 186M, and net-debt-to-EBITDA 1.8x. The three-segment split was 44% consumer-brands, 31% food-processing, 25% industrial-distribution. The nine-member Board comprised the Chair (Ossandón family, third generation), three other family-representative directors, five independents (including Ossa), and the CEO, Diego Castellanos, appointed in May 2022 after nine years as Chief Operating Officer.

The CEO's 2022–2025 track record showed 2022 and 2023 earnings in line with consensus, 2024 earnings beating consensus by 4.2%, and Q3 and Q4 2025 earnings missing consensus by 7.8% and 9.2% respectively — the first two consecutive misses under his tenure. The 2025 LTIP maturation schedule, signed in May 2022 and maturing at the Q1 2026 Board, set an EPS-CAGR target of 20% (max), 15% (target), and 10% (threshold); the 2022–2025 achieved EPS-CAGR was 14.5%, yielding a 72% maturation payout at USD 6.4M under the formulaic calculation. The peer group — six Chilean and Peruvian consumer-conglomerate comparators — had averaged 17% EPS CAGR over the same window, placing Pacífico in the bottom third of the peer set.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM corporate-governance landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Scenario-A decision sat inside. First, the 2021 Chilean Ley de Modernización de la Comisión para el Mercado Financiero had tightened the board-independence requirements on listed companies with dispersed ownership and had, through 2024–2025 administrative rulings, elevated the Lead-Independent-Director role on the Governance Committee's responsibilities on same-day shareholder bilaterals. Second, the ISS 2025 LATAM voting-guidelines update had introduced a formal negative-discretion expectation on LTIP maturation votes when currency or commodity tailwinds had demonstrably inflated the formulaic result; institutional investors had, in the Q4 2025 AGM cycle, voted against 38% of LATAM-conglomerate LTIP-maturation proposals that did not disclose negative-discretion reasoning. Third, the IFRS-15 revenue-recognition framework had, since its 2018 adoption, been the subject of fifty-seven publicly disclosed enforcement actions against LATAM public companies, with material-earnings-per-share impact findings in thirty-eight of the fifty-seven.

Empirically, the board-effectiveness research had two robust findings the Scenario-A package had to respect. First, independent directors who had, at a CEO-review inflection, commissioned a formal 360-review with explicit retention-risk scope produced, across a 2019–2024 sample of seventy-four LATAM public-company events, CEO-performance-trajectory improvement or orderly-transition outcomes in 81% of cases against 42% for boards that relied on the CEO's own self-assessment and the CFO's verbal flag. Second, audit-committee-without-management investigation protocols on IFRS-15 whistleblower complaints produced, across the same sample, regulator-no-action outcomes in 71% of cases against 28% for protocols that included management in the first-investigation loop.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Board had, at the September 2025 annual retreat, received a consultant-facilitated board self-assessment that had placed the Lead-Independent-Director role as the single most under-exercised governance lever. Ossa had been appointed LID in January 2025 and had, through 2025, resisted escalating any specific management issue to the full Board. The CFO's March 20 note was the first formal retention-risk flag. The shareholder bilateral request on March 21 was the first same-day bilateral request since her LID appointment. The Q3 and Q4 2025 earnings misses had been disclosed within the regular filing cycle.

### What Ossa knew

The internal governance team had modeled three Scenario-A packages. A Minimum-Intervention Package (rate the CEO at the formulaic level, decline the shareholder bilateral, treat the CFO note as confidential to the LID only) projected Scenario-E Argentina-entry under CEO-time-pressure approval, Scenario-D AGM-vote failure, composite 62/100. A Structural Package (executive session, 360-review with retention-risk scope, bilateral-with-Chair-informed, CFO-note to audit committee) projected Scenario-E phased-approval, Scenario-D survive-AGM, composite 84/100. A High-Intervention Package (executive session, 360-review, CEO-management-style-referral to the Governance Committee, shareholder bilateral plus full disclosure to the Board) projected composite 78/100 — a six-point drag against the Structural Package through relationship friction that the Scenario-C whistleblower response would pay for.

### What she did not know

Whether the CEO's Q1 2026 earnings would land at consensus (base case on the internal finance team's model) or miss a third consecutive time; whether the Scenario-B USD 380M contested acquisition that the CEO had signalled at the February off-site would come to a formal Board vote in the Q2 window (base case) or be deferred; whether the Scenario-C IFRS-15 whistleblower complaint that the internal audit working papers had quietly surfaced in late 2025 would formalize into an audit-committee-directed investigation in Q3; whether the Scenario-D peer-group-benchmarking would refresh in the Compensation Committee's Q4 working session in a way that pressured the negative-discretion decision up or down; and whether the Scenario-E Argentina reform-window the CEO had cited at the February off-site would actually close in ninety days or extend.

### What others were saying

**The Chair of the Audit Committee**: *"Commission the 360-review with explicit retention scope. If the CFO is right, the 360 is the only instrument that produces a defensible paper trail."*

**The Chair of the Board** (Ossandón family, third generation): *"Take the shareholder meeting. Keep me informed before and after. Do not commit the Board to any position the Board has not voted."*

**The Chief Financial Officer** (in the sealed note): *"Three members of the executive committee have initiated conversations with executive-search firms since October 2025. The CEO's management style is not, in itself, the issue. The CEO's management style in a missed-quarters-plus-acquisition-integration window is the issue."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Ossa had narrowed the space to three Scenario-A packages.

- **Option A — Minimum-Intervention:** Rate CEO at formulaic level, decline shareholder bilateral, CFO note stays LID-only. Composite 62/100. Lowest friction today. Highest friction in Scenarios D and E.

- **Option B — Structural Package:** Executive session, commissioned 360-review with retention-risk scope, bilateral-with-Chair-informed, CFO-note routed to audit committee. Composite 84/100. Aligns with the Chair of the Audit Committee's position and the Chair of the Board's signalled preference.

- **Option C — High-Intervention:** Executive session, 360-review, Governance-Committee referral of the CEO management-style question, shareholder bilateral plus full Board disclosure. Composite 78/100. Six-point composite drag through relationship friction.

Ossa's private view, not yet shared with the Chair of the Board, was that Option B was the right Scenario-A package and that the single subsequent decision most correlated with the composite outcome was the Scenario-D Compensation Committee's negative-discretion exercise — specifically, adjusting the formulaic USD 6.4M payout for the FX-tailwind-inflated EPS result and benchmarking against the peer-group seventeen-percent CAGR.
