## Abstract

Fondo Privado Cóndor was, in the case universe, a 14-year-old Santiago-de-Chile private fund founded in 2012 managing CLP 4.8bn AUM across twelve high-net-worth families under a private-investment-vehicle structure governed by an Investment Policy Statement (IPS) requiring 9.5 % target net return, minimum 10 % cash position, minimum 50 % CLP-denominated exposure, maximum 15 % single-position concentration and a quarterly drawdown ceiling of minus 15 %. The five-asset-class mandate covered Chilean equities, US equities, Chilean fixed income, Chilean REITs and cash. The 2026-Q1 Chilean market context combined a BCP-10-year at 5.8 %, a historical Chilean ERP at 5.2 %, a Round-1-calibrated equity correlation matrix and a macro-indicator set flagging probability-weighted stress-event risk at 34 % for the upcoming quarter. Three portfolio analysts competed for the Fund Manager seat opening in round three. On **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 7:14 a.m., **Sofía Valdés-Carrasco**, a 32-year-old analyst with 14 months at the fund, prepared the three-round commitment.

2. Derive expected returns using the CAPM formulation (Rf + β × ERP) with the 5.8 % Chilean risk-free rate and the 5.2 % Chilean equity risk premium, read the Security Market Line against eight Chilean-equity candidates (three above the line, two beneath, three straddling), and commit the weighting logic that reconciles DCF, relative-P/E and alpha signals when they agree and when they diverge.

Apply the Round 2 Behavioral Finance Engine responses — Kahneman-Tversky loss aversion on the 18 %-drop hold-or-sell decision, Bikhchandani 1992 herding on the TechCL four-fund-signal, Alpert-Raiffa overconfidence on the Round 1-winning-call concentration temptation, Tversky-Kahneman anchoring on the purchase-price reference — and articulate why the documented Bias-Report cost tabulation is the Fund-Manager-seat differentiator on the competing-analyst panel.

The Round-1 commitment deadline was 10:00 a.m.

## The Protagonist

Valdés-Carrasco, 32, had been Portfolio Analyst at Fondo Cóndor since February 2025, had completed an Ingeniería Comercial at the Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile in 2016 and a Master in Finance at the London School of Economics in 2020, had spent four years at a Santiago-based sell-side research shop covering Chilean mid-cap equities before joining Fondo Cóndor, and had earned her CFA charter in 2024. Her portfolio-management instincts had been shaped by three teachings: the 2019–2020 LSE exposure to Markowitz-Sharpe-Lintner-Kahneman-Tversky canonical literature; a 2023 sell-side call on a Chilean salmon-producer that had anchored to purchase price for five quarters and cost the firm a recommended-portfolio ranking; and a 2025 Behavioral-Finance-Engine practice round at Fondo Cóndor where she had logged loss-aversion cost of CLP 41m against a recommended cut-and-redeploy position she had held four weeks too long.

## The Decision

Valdés-Carrasco had to commit, by the 10:00 a.m. Round-1 deadline, the three-round package.

1. **Round 1 — Allocation.** Commit the five-asset-class allocation inside the IPS constraints (minimum 10 % cash, minimum 50 % CLP, maximum 15 % single-position, maximum quarterly drawdown minus 15 %). Execute DCF valuations on the three required Chilean-equity candidates (free cash flow projections given, WACC derived from CAPM at 5.8 % + β × 5.2 %, Gordon-Growth terminal). Reconcile DCF, relative-P/E and SML-alpha signals. Write the weighting logic.
2. **Round 2 — Behavioral-Finance-Engine responses.** Respond to the 18 %-drop-on-a-Round-1-holding hold-or-sell decision (rational: re-evaluate on current fundamentals; biased: hold because realising loss hurts). Respond to the four-fund-TechCL buying-signal herding feed. Respond to the Round-1-winning-call concentration temptation (overconfidence). Track the Bias-Report cost tabulation.
3. **Round 3 — Stress event and IPS defence.** Absorb the correlation-convergence event (Chilean equities minus 9 %, US minus 7 %, REITs minus 5 %). Defend the maximum-drawdown-minus-15 % constraint. Separate stock-picking skill from market-drawdown immunity in the performance-attribution narrative. Commit the Fund-Manager-seat recommendation.

Commitments: the Round-1 allocation and weighting logic; the Round-2 bias-by-bias responses; the Round-3 stress-event posture and attribution narrative; and a one-page Fund-Manager memo that frames the three-round commitment as a single Q1–Q3 arc.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1952–2025 portfolio-management literature produced three findings the simulation had to respect. First, the Markowitz 1952 MPT framework — diversification across uncorrelated assets reduces variance without sacrificing return — was robust across forty years of asset-allocation panels, with the caveat that correlation matrices converge toward one in documented stress events, collapsing the diversification-benefit calculation. Second, the Sharpe-Lintner-Mossin CAPM formulation, operationalised on Chilean-equity panels with the 5.8 %-Rf-5.2 %-ERP calibration, had produced alpha-readings with documented persistence at quarterly-horizon approximately 0.31 and at annual-horizon approximately 0.14 — alpha was real but noisy, and the Round 1 above-SML selection dominated long-run portfolio performance. Third, the DCF-plus-relative-P/E-plus-SML-alpha triangulation discipline, applied when the three signals agreed, produced a quarterly Sharpe ratio approximately 0.54 higher than any single-signal selection regime.

The 1974–2025 behavioral-finance literature added two findings. The Kahneman-Tversky 1979 prospect theory, operationalised as the loss-aversion-coefficient around 2.25× on realised-loss-pain versus realised-gain-reward, had produced documented hold-too-long pattern costs at approximately 4.1 % of portfolio-annual-return on Latin American private-fund panels. And the Bikhchandani-Hirshleifer-Welch 1992 informational-cascade-herding framework, combined with the Alpert-Raiffa 1982 overconfidence-calibration literature, had produced documented concentration-at-the-top and herd-at-the-signal patterns whose combined annual cost ran approximately 6.4 % of portfolio-annual-return on panel-match.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The January 2025 Valdés-Carrasco hire; the April 2025 CFA charter award; the Q3 2025 Fund-Manager-seat succession announcement; the February 2026 three-analyst practice-round sequence; the April 15 announcement that the Fund Manager seat would be decided on the Q3 performance-attribution and Bias Report; and the April 23 Round 1 commitment were the sequence.

### What Valdés-Carrasco knew

The IPS constraints; the five-asset-class mandate; the CAPM inputs; the correlation matrix as of 6:58 a.m.; the eight-Chilean-equity candidate set and their three-above-two-beneath-three-straddling SML position; the documented bias-cost panel; the two competing-analyst practice-round Bias-Report reads; and the Fund-Manager's appetite for a specific Q1–Q3 commitment rather than a running commentary.

### What she did not know

Whether the stress event probability-weighted 34 % would land in Q2 or Q3; whether the correlation-convergence would be the three-dimensional minus-9/minus-7/minus-5 scenario or a deeper minus-12/minus-10/minus-7 tail; whether the two competing analysts had logged loss-aversion or overconfidence as their dominant practice-round bias; whether the Fund-Manager would weight the Bias Report at the 40 % announced weighting or at a de-facto higher weighting if the stress event fired; and whether the twelve HNW families would accept the minus-15 % drawdown constraint as hard or would push for a minus-10 % softer ceiling mid-quarter.

### What others were saying

**The Fund Manager (outgoing, 58)**: *"La silla es del analista que cae en menos trampas. No del que tiene el mejor stock-pick. Eso lo hace cualquiera con un terminal."*

**The Chief Investment Officer (52)**: *"El IPS no es un documento. Es un pacto con doce familias. Si rompes el tope de drawdown, rompes el pacto."*

**The competing analyst A (29, two years at fund)**: *"Me la juego con el SML. Sobre la línea, compro. Bajo la línea, vendo. Lo demás es ruido."*

**The competing analyst B (34, six years at fund)**: *"Me la juego con el sesgo. Si no caigo en ninguno de los cinco, gano. Con el stock-pick igualado, la diferencia la hace eso."*

**The HNW-family-representative (spokesperson for the twelve)**: *"Nueve coma cinco neto. Menos quince máximo. Lo demás es suyo. Pero si rompen el tope una vez, perdemos una familia. Si lo rompen dos veces, perdemos tres."*

## Options Under Consideration

Valdés-Carrasco had narrowed the Round-1-through-Round-3 space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — IPS-respected, triangulated-stock-pick, bias-discipline (Recommended).** Round 1: 12 % cash, 52 % CLP fixed income, 18 % Chilean equities (three SML-above, two straddling, weighted by DCF-plus-relative-P/E), 10 % US equities, 8 % REITs. Round 2: re-evaluate on fundamentals (sell the 18 %-drop position), resist herding on TechCL, resist concentration temptation on winning call. Round 3: the cash-and-fixed-income position carries the stress event; IPS held; Bias Report cost under CLP 25m.
- **Option B — Max-equity allocation, chase alpha, minimal bias discipline.** Rejected — pushes equity to 48 % combined, minimal cash and fixed-income cushion; stress event breaks the minus-15 % constraint by approximately 4 pp; Bias Report cost estimated at CLP 140m+; the Fund-Manager-seat panel historically assigns zero probability to this posture winning.
- **Option C — Conservative, over-IPS cash, no stock-pick, de-facto 60 % fixed-income-plus-cash.** Defensible on drawdown-constraint grounds but surrenders the alpha opportunity documented on the three-above-SML candidates; under-allocates risk relative to the 9.5 % target-return mandate; likely delivers 6.2 % actualised return that fails the IPS return target and forfeits the Fund-Manager-seat on expected-value grounds.

Valdés-Carrasco's private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the single decision most correlated with the Q3 outcome was the Round 2 loss-aversion response on the 18 %-drop-on-a-Round-1-holding, because the documented behavioral-finance panel had shown that analysts who failed the hold-or-sell loss-aversion test accumulated approximately 42 % of their total Bias-Report cost on that single decision category, and because the competing-analyst A's stock-pick posture was vulnerable to the same bias at a higher probability given her practice-round track record.
