## Abstract

The case focuses on a Mexico City-headquartered LatAm fast-moving-consumer-goods group with USD 5.8B in 2025 revenue, a fourteen-brand portfolio across food, home and personal care, distribution across nine LatAm countries, a 2025 ESG-Score reading of 52 on a 2023-rebased baseline of 50 (composite captures third-party ESG-rating performance, sustainability-claim coverage and supply-chain disclosure depth), a 2025 Disclosure-Quality reading of 48 against a peer benchmark of 60 (composite captures framework adherence, materiality discipline and assurance independence), a 2025 Credibility reading of 58 against a peer benchmark of 64 (composite captures consumer trust on sustainability claims, NGO posture and regulator perception), and a 2025 Regulatory-Exposure reading of 45 (where higher means more exposure) against a peer benchmark of 32. The 2024–2025 sustainability programme had been built under the prior Chief Sustainability Officer with a stated ambition-first charter and had, by Q1 2026, produced 12 published sustainability claims across the brand portfolio and an internally-assured disclosure pack. Three Q1 2026 events were named in the March 2026 risk-and-sustainability-committee dossier: a January 2026 EU-CSRD reverberation across the LatAm regulatory landscape with three jurisdictions opening greenwashing-style consultations; a February 2026 EUR 40M greenwashing fine imposed on a comparable FMCG competitor with a documented consumer-trust collapse to 34% in the FMCG-sustainability-claim category; and a March 2026 internal-audit finding that three of the company's twelve published claims were unverifiable under the current evidence base — not false, but unverifiable. The Q1 2026 board had approved a USD 8M WashGame envelope and a Chief-Sustainability-Officer-led five-round commitment cycle against an ESG-Score floor above 60, a Disclosure-Quality floor above 64, a Credibility floor above 64, a Regulatory-Exposure ceiling below 32 and a Composite WashGame Score above 64. The case opens at 6:48 a.m. on Thursday, April 23, 2026, at the LATAM FMCG group's Mexico City head office as **Inés Mendoza**, a 46-year-old Chief Sustainability Officer, prepares the Round-1 ambition-setting commitment. Sixty-three days separate her from the June 25 board risk-and-sustainability review.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Round-1 ambition-setting commitment (proven-substantive envelope that anchors all sustainability claims to a verifiable evidence base before publication vs. ambition-first envelope that publishes ahead of the evidence base) and diagnose when the substantive envelope dominates the ambition envelope on the Credibility-plus-Regulatory-Exposure composite.
2. Analyze the Round-2 verification-infrastructure investment (third-party-audit envelope vs. internal-only envelope) and recommend the third-party envelope on the post-disclosure-defence basis.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 competitor-fine-contagion response (claim-by-claim defence envelope that publishes the evidence base for each claim vs. reaffirmation envelope) and recommend the claim-by-claim envelope on the consumer-trust recovery basis.
4. Respond to the Round-4 internal-audit-gap remediation (proactive-retraction envelope that retracts the three unverifiable claims and publishes the gap diagnosis vs. quiet-update envelope) and recommend the retraction envelope on the regulator-and-Credibility basis.
5. Design the Round-5 integrated-strategy redesign that converts the substantive-vs-disclosure trade-off into a single integrated framework anchored on materiality, evidence and verification.

It is claiming something you cannot prove."* Sixty-three days separated her from the June 25 board risk-and-sustainability review.

## The Protagonist

Inés Mendoza, 46, had assumed the Chief-Sustainability-Officer role on October 1, 2025, after thirteen years across sustainability, regulatory affairs and disclosure leadership at two pan-LatAm FMCG groups. She had, in 2022–2024, led a thirty-claim verification-infrastructure rebuild that had moved her prior firm's Disclosure-Quality reading from 46 to 68 inside an 18-month window without retracting a single claim. The Chief Executive Officer had recruited her in July 2025 specifically on two capabilities: her documented discipline of treating sustainability disclosure as a substantive-evidence problem rather than a positioning problem, and her willingness to hold a proven-substantive envelope against marketing pressure for ambition-first publication. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the Credibility floor and 20% to the Regulatory-Exposure ceiling.

## The Decision

Mendoza had to commit, by the May 4 board pre-review, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–5.

1. **Round 1 — Ambition-setting commitment.** Three archetypes: proven-substantive (anchor every sustainability claim to a verifiable evidence base inside a refreshed materiality matrix, gate every new claim on third-party verification, Composite 76), ambition-first (publish ahead of the evidence base — Composite 32, retraction-cascade probability), or phased (proven on three brands only — Composite 54). Working envelope: proven-substantive.
2. **Round 2 — Verification-infrastructure investment.** Three archetypes: third-party-audit (USD 1.8M for third-party-audit contracts, internal ESG controls, data-lineage system, version-controlled claim register, Composite 76), internal-only (no third-party audit — Composite 36, supervisory-discovery probability), or phased (third-party on three claims only — Composite 56). Working envelope: third-party-audit.
3. **Round 3 — Competitor-fine-contagion response.** Three vectors: claim-by-claim defence (publish the evidence base behind each of the company's twelve claims inside 30 days of the competitor fine, Composite 76), reaffirmation (issue a single reaffirmation statement — Composite 38, Credibility-collapse-recurrence probability), or phased (claim-by-claim on six claims — Composite 56). Working envelope: claim-by-claim defence.
4. **Round 4 — Internal-audit-gap remediation.** Three vectors: proactive-retraction (retract the three unverifiable claims, publish the gap diagnosis and the verification-rebuild plan inside 14 days of the audit, Composite 76), quiet-update (silently update the claim language without retraction or publication — Composite 32, regulator-and-NGO discovery probability), or phased (retract one of three, update two — Composite 54). Working envelope: proactive-retraction.
5. **Round 5 — Integrated-strategy redesign.** Three vectors: integrated-framework (refreshed materiality matrix, evidence-first claim register, third-party assurance contract, claim-by-claim disclosure cadence, integration into the brand-marketing approval gate, Composite 78), framework-only (publish the framework without operational integration — Composite 42), or phased (integrated on three brands only — Composite 56). Working envelope: integrated-framework.

On Mendoza's working model, a Substantive / Third-Party / Claim-by-Claim / Retraction / Integrated envelope delivered ESG Score 64, Disclosure Quality 68, Credibility 66, Regulatory Exposure 30 (lower better), Composite 70.

## Company Background

The group had been founded in Mexico City in 1968 and had, across fifty-eight years, built a fourteen-brand FMCG portfolio across food, home and personal care, with distribution across nine LatAm countries. The 2024–2025 sustainability programme had been built under the prior Chief Sustainability Officer with a stated ambition-first charter and had launched twelve published sustainability claims across the brand portfolio. The program had been internally assured but not third-party assured.

The three events the March 2026 dossier had named. First, the January 2026 EU-CSRD reverberation had occurred when three LatAm jurisdictions — Brazil, Chile and Colombia — opened greenwashing-style regulatory consultations referencing the CSRD framework as a benchmark. Second, the February 2026 EUR 40M greenwashing fine had been imposed on a comparable FMCG competitor with a documented consumer-trust collapse to 34% in the FMCG-sustainability-claim category, a 24-point single-event drop. Third, the March 2026 internal-audit finding had identified that three of the company's twelve published claims were unverifiable under the current evidence base — specifically, two carbon-neutrality-by-2030 claims and one recyclable-packaging claim — not false, but unverifiable.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2023–2025 greenwashing-and-disclosure literature had, across 46 documented FMCG and consumer-goods sustainability cycles, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, programmes that had operated on a proven-substantive ambition-setting protocol had documented retraction rates 5.6× lower than ambition-first programmes, with no measurable Credibility penalty in the 12-month post-launch window. Second, programmes that had funded third-party verification infrastructure had reduced supervisory-action probability by 64%–74% relative to internal-only programmes. Third, programmes that had executed proactive-retraction within 14 days of an internal audit-gap finding had recovered Credibility readings within 9–12 months in 68% of cases against 22% for quiet-update programmes that had subsequently been discovered.

Empirically, the post-2023 contagion literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, FMCG categories had documented consumer-trust contagion of 18–28 points within 60 days of any high-profile competitor greenwashing fine, with category-wide trust readings persisting below baseline for 9–14 months. Second, integrated-framework redesigns had sustained Credibility readings above 64 in 72% of cases against 24% for framework-only redesigns. ## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the February 2026 risk-and-sustainability session, approved the USD 8M envelope and the June 25 review calendar. The Head of Sustainability Verification had, on March 5, delivered the twelve-claim register and the unverifiable-claim diagnostic. The General Counsel had, on March 12, delivered the proactive-retraction-and-disclosure protocol. The Chief Marketing Officer had, on March 19, delivered the consumer-trust-contagion baseline. The Chief Financial Officer had, on March 26, delivered the envelope-allocation pack.

### What Mendoza knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. A Substantive / Third-Party / Claim-by-Claim / Retraction / Integrated Envelope projected Composite 70. An Ambition / Internal-Only / Reaffirmation / Quiet-Update / Framework-Only Envelope projected Composite 26. A Hybrid Envelope projected Composite 50.

### What she did not know

Whether the proactive-retraction would trigger a punitive media cycle ahead of the recovery; whether the three LatAm regulatory consultations would consolidate into a binding framework inside the 12-month window; whether the brand-marketing organisation would absorb the evidence-first claim-register gate without circumventing it through micro-claims; and whether the Q3 2026 earnings cycle would absorb the substantive-vs-disclosure narrative without a Credibility-driven re-rating.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Sustainability Verification**: *"Proven-substantive. Every claim outside the evidence base is a future retraction we have not budgeted for."*

**The General Counsel**: *"Proactive-retraction. The regulator will not punish a retraction. It will punish a discovered quiet-update."*

**The Chief Marketing Officer**: *"Claim-by-claim defence. The competitor fine moved consumer trust on the category, not on the brand. The category is now ours to defend."*

**The Chief Executive Officer**: *"Greenwashing is not lying. It is claiming something you cannot prove. Build the evidence first. Publish second."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Mendoza had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Substantive / Third-Party / Claim-by-Claim / Retraction / Integrated:** the recommended envelope. ESG Score 64. Disclosure Quality 68. Credibility 66. Regulatory Exposure 30. Composite 70. Recommended.
- **Option B — Ambition / Internal-Only / Reaffirmation / Quiet-Update / Framework-Only:** the positioning envelope. Composite 26. Rejected — retraction-cascade and supervisory-action probability above 65%.
- **Option C — Hybrid / Phased / Phased / Phased / Phased:** the partial envelope. Composite 50. Rejected — claim-by-claim defence fragments under partial coverage.

Mendoza's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 4 — the proactive-retraction commitment — because it was the round that converted the unverifiable-claim gap from a regulator-discoverable liability into a credibility-restoring proof point.
