## Abstract

Aseguradora Andina was, in the case universe, a LATAM insurance company headquartered in Bogotá with operations in Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, and Bolivia, 2025 gross written premium of USD 2.4B, and a portfolio weighted 58% to property-and-casualty, 28% to life, and 14% to health. Between 2023 and 2025 the company had deployed three production AI agents: ClaimBot (automated property-and-casualty claims adjudication on claims below USD 8,000, scaled to 72% of eligible claims by Q4 2025), UnderwriteAI (automated auto-insurance underwriting and pricing, 58% of new-policy volume), and HireAssist (resume screening and candidate ranking for operations and customer-service roles, 81% of screening volume). On Wednesday, February 4, 2026, Natalia Cisneros took office as the new Chief Operating Officer. Five rounds would unfold across a nine-month window, beginning with a governance-inventory decision at the February 18 ExCo meeting and ending with an expansion decision after the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) issued its regional-first agentic-AI governance framework. The case opens at 6:45 a.m. on Wednesday, February 11, 2026, as Cisneros prepares the Round-1 audit-sequencing package.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the five audit priorities (Fairness Audit, Continuous Monitoring, ClaimBot Recalibration, SFC Engagement, Reskilling) and sequence them against pre-incident rather than in-incident logic.
2. Analyze the ClaimBot scale-error event (890 complainant claims pointing to 12,400 likely underpaid claims at USD 34.7M exposure) and recommend a tiered-remediation posture.
3. Diagnose the human-in-the-loop architecture decision and design a claims-threshold model (human review above USD 15,000, auto below) that preserves cost efficiency without exposing the loss-ratio.
4. Design a proactive-compliance response to the SFC's agentic-AI governance framework that exceeds the minimum and positions Aseguradora Andina as a regulatory case-study.
5. Recommend a complete Round-1 package and identify the single Round-5 expansion decision most likely to preserve regulatory trust across the next permit cycle.

On the morning of **Wednesday, February 11, 2026**, **Natalia Cisneros** sat at the Chief Operating Officer's desk in the Aseguradora Andina tower in Bogotá and opened the February 10 scorecard the outgoing COO had handed off the previous Friday. Operational Cost Index 100. Service Quality 62. Workforce Health 71. Regulatory Compliance 68. Agent Portfolio ROI 0%. Three agents in production. Seven days remained until the Round-1 audit-sequencing decision was due to the ExCo.

## The Protagonist

Natalia Cisneros, 44, had joined Aseguradora Andina in January 2026 after nine years as Chief Operating Officer of a Chilean mid-market insurer, where she had, in 2021–2023, led a claims-operations transformation that had integrated the company's first automated adjudication system. A graduate of Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile with an executive MBA from IPADE in Mexico City, she had, in her prior role, built a claims-automation program from zero to 34% of eligible claim volume across thirty months without a material scale-error incident. She held, at appointment, no formal AI-governance certification but had, across 2023–2025, chaired her prior company's internal AI-ethics working group. The CEO's February 2 onboarding instruction had been, in a single sentence: "Manage the agents before they manage you."

---

## The Decision

Cisneros had to commit, by 9:00 a.m. on Wednesday, February 18, the Round-1 audit-sequencing package the ExCo would treat as the nine-month operating envelope:

1. **Audit Priority Sequence** — Five priorities to sequence in order: (A) Emergency ClaimBot Recalibration, (B) Continuous Monitoring Dashboard, (C) Employee Reskilling Program, (D) Proactive SFC Engagement, (E) Fairness Audit (UnderwriteAI + HireAssist).
2. **Budget Allocation** — Round-1 envelope of USD 2.8M across the five priorities.
3. **ExCo-Escalation Package** — accompanying memo, nine-month roadmap, and ExCo-accountability matrix.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across five rounds. A sequence of Fairness Audit first, Continuous Monitoring second, ClaimBot Recalibration third, SFC Engagement fourth, Reskilling fifth — with a USD 2.8M allocation of USD 620K, USD 540K, USD 480K, USD 420K, USD 740K respectively — produced, on the internal model, a Round-2 ClaimBot-scale-error response that stayed inside the thirty-day SFC self-disclosure window and a Round-5 SFC-status of "cooperative." A sequence of Emergency ClaimBot Recalibration first, with Fairness Audit deferred to Round 3, produced an in-crisis rather than pre-crisis audit posture and, on the model, a projected Round-5 SFC status of "under-investigation."

## Company Background

Aseguradora Andina had been founded in 1988 as a Colombian property-and-casualty insurer and had, across thirty-eight years, expanded across the four-country Andean region. 2025 gross written premium was USD 2.4B with a combined ratio of 96.4% and a return on equity of 13.8%. The company employed 3,420 people distributed as 1,240 in claims (property-and-casualty, life, health), 560 in underwriting, 480 in customer service, 380 in distribution and commercial, 380 in technology, and 380 in finance, actuarial, legal, and G&A. The three AI agents had been deployed under the prior COO's 2022–2024 transformation program, with post-deployment operational-performance improvement of 31% in claims-processing speed, 18% in underwriting throughput, and 42% in hiring-screening throughput.

The Round-2 ClaimBot scale-error — which the sim embedded as an event Cisneros would discover via an internal-audit finding on Day 42 of her tenure — would have been, by the time of Round-1 sequencing, partially signalled by 890 complainant claims in the fourth-quarter 2025 ombudsman file that the outgoing COO had flagged but not escalated. The internal-audit working-paper trail would, on the Round-2 reveal, find that the 890 represented the complainant tail of an estimated 12,400 underpaid claims across the four-year production history of ClaimBot, totalling approximately USD 34.7M of under-payment exposure.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM agentic-insurance landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Round-1 decision sat inside. First, the SFC — the Colombian financial-services supervisor — had signalled in late 2025 that it would issue an agentic-AI governance framework in 2026, becoming the regional first-mover; the framework was expected to specify continuous-monitoring, model-card-and-documentation, fairness-testing, human-in-the-loop thresholds, incident-disclosure, and customer-appeal-rights obligations. Second, the claims-automation-scale-error literature had converged on a finding: in twelve publicly observed incidents between 2021 and 2025, the single most consistent predictor of regulatory-cooperative-disclosure outcomes was the timing gap between internal-audit-finding-date and supervisor-disclosure-date; gaps of thirty days or less had produced cooperative classifications in nine of ten cases; gaps greater than sixty days had produced under-investigation classifications in eight of nine. Third, the algorithmic-fairness literature in insurance had converged on a finding: automated underwriting and hiring systems deployed for more than eighteen months without a documented fairness audit had a 62% probability of a fairness-complaint event in the following twenty-four-month window.

Empirically, the human-in-the-loop research had two robust findings. First, human-review threshold models — where automated adjudication applied below a specified dollar threshold and human review applied above — produced, in a 2022–2024 insurance-industry sample, 14% higher service-quality scores at a 4% operational-cost premium against fully automated portfolios. Second, claims-staff reskilling programs (three-to-six-month programs that moved junior adjudicators into adjudication-review-and-explainability roles) had, across a four-company sample, produced 8-point workforce-health score improvements at 40% the cost of equivalent headcount-and-replace strategies.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Board had, in the 2025 strategy off-site, committed to the agentic-enterprise operating-model trajectory and had, in the December 2025 search process, appointed Cisneros with a single written mandate: "Build the governance the agents need before the regulator or the market forces you to." The outgoing COO had handed off a 90-day prioritization memo on January 30, 2026 that had explicitly flagged the Q4 ombudsman file and the Fairness-Audit absence. The CEO had scheduled the Round-1 ExCo meeting for February 18 with a single-hour agenda slot for the audit-sequencing package.

### What Cisneros knew

The internal team had modeled three Round-1 packages. A Reactive Package (Emergency ClaimBot Recalibration first, ClaimBot Recalibration second, Reskilling third, Monitoring fourth, Fairness Audit fifth, SFC Engagement deferred) projected Round-5 SFC status "under-investigation," penalty exposure USD 8M–12M, Service Quality 72 at Round 5 (+10), Regulatory Compliance 78 (+10), Score 62/100. A Proactive Package (Fairness Audit first, Continuous Monitoring second, ClaimBot Recalibration third, Proactive SFC Engagement fourth, Employee Reskilling fifth) projected Round-5 SFC status "cooperative," penalty exposure USD 2M–4M (ClaimBot self-disclosure), Service Quality 84, Regulatory Compliance 92, Score 84/100. A Hybrid Package (Continuous Monitoring first, Fairness Audit second, ClaimBot Recalibration third, SFC Engagement fourth, Reskilling fifth) projected Round-5 SFC status "cooperative," Service Quality 81, Regulatory Compliance 89, Score 80/100.

### What she did not know

Whether the Q4 2025 ombudsman-complaint file — which the outgoing COO had flagged but not escalated — pointed to a 12,400-claim underpayment pattern (base case on the internal-audit working-paper trail) or to a 18,000-or-larger pattern; whether the SFC's agentic-AI framework — which the internal regulatory team expected to publish in the Round-4 window — would require a sixty-day compliance envelope or a ninety-day envelope; whether Banco Financial (a competitor insurer) would, in the Round-4 window, launch an instant-AI-pricing product that would compress the ExCo's appetite for a full human-in-the-loop rollout; and whether the Round-5 expansion question — Actuarial Agent, Platform Monetization, or Governance Consolidation — would be framed by the ExCo as an offensive or a defensive decision.

### What others were saying

**Francisco Uribe**, Chief Technology Officer: *"Fairness Audit first. UnderwriteAI and HireAssist are the agents with discrimination exposure the regulator will read against us at the next permit review. Emergency ClaimBot recalibration in Round 3. Not Round 1."*

**Beatriz Salgado**, Chief Risk Officer: *"Proactive SFC engagement is the single highest-leverage Round-1 priority. If we are in the SFC's working group on the framework, the framework is written against our governance architecture rather than somebody else's."*

**Esteban Villacís**, Head of People Operations: *"Reskilling fifth is fine on the numbers. Reskilling fifth is not fine on the workforce-health-score line. Junior claims adjudicators read the hiring-freeze signal before they read the reskill signal. Communicate the reskill in Round 1. Fund it in Round 1. Execute it in Round 3."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Cisneros had narrowed the space to three Round-1 packages.

- **Option A — Reactive Firefight:** ClaimBot first, Recalibration second, Reskilling third, Monitoring fourth, Fairness fifth. Round-5 SFC under-investigation. Penalty exposure USD 8M–12M. Service Quality 72. Score 62. Reacts to the scale error rather than pre-empting it.

- **Option B — Proactive Governance:** Fairness first, Monitoring second, ClaimBot third, SFC fourth, Reskilling fifth. Round-5 SFC cooperative. Penalty exposure USD 2M–4M. Service Quality 84. Score 84. Aligns with the Chief Technology Officer and Chief Risk Officer positions.

- **Option C — Hybrid Monitoring-First:** Monitoring first, Fairness second, ClaimBot third, SFC fourth, Reskilling fifth. Round-5 SFC cooperative. Service Quality 81. Score 80. Insures against the scale-error earlier but does not catch the fairness exposure before the Round-2 event.

Cisneros's private view, not yet shared with Francisco Uribe, was that Option B was the right Round-1 package and that the single subsequent decision most correlated with the Round-5 SFC-status outcome was the Round-2 tiered-remediation response to the ClaimBot scale error — specifically, the proactive-remediation-above-USD-5,000, reactive-below, and a thirty-day-or-less SFC self-disclosure inside the audit-finding-date-plus-thirty window.

(See **Exhibit 4** for the five-round projected outcomes under each option and **Exhibit 5** for the tiered-remediation and thirty-day SFC self-disclosure template.)
