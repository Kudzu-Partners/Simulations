## Abstract

Arvelia Dairy & Beverage Co. was, in the case universe, a mid-sized Arvelia dairy and beverage manufacturer with a fourteen-SKU portfolio across three product segments — value, mainstream, and premium — and three commercial channels — modern trade, traditional trade, and foodservice. On Monday, January 12, 2026, the company held 17.0% volume share against category leader Lactamar at 24.0% and a long tail of smaller regionals and private-label at the remaining 59.0%. 2025 revenue was ARV 182M with a gross margin of 33.8%, trade spend at 9.6% of revenue, and a premium portfolio contribution of 24% of revenue. Camila Bermúdez, appointed Brand Manager on November 24, 2025 after five years at the company in category-management roles, faced three annual decision rounds, a Year-2 Lactamar premium-price cut of 8% plus a Lactamar protein-beverage entry into modern trade, and a Year-3 strategic-growth window. The case opens at 7:14 a.m. on Tuesday, January 20, 2026, as Bermúdez prepares the Year-1 portfolio package for the January 29 Category Committee.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Year-1 portfolio-positioning choice between a value-segment-block against private-label encroachment and a premium-segment ladder against a category-leader price-lazy SKU, and diagnose when each dominates on a three-year composite.
2. Analyze the Year-2 competitive-shock scenario — Lactamar's 8% premium price cut plus a modern-trade protein-beverage entry — and recommend a non-price response that captures switchers without conceding the flagship.
3. Diagnose the Year-3 line-extension and channel-expansion trade-offs across a kids-protein variant, a traditional-trade e-commerce pilot, a QSR-chain foodservice bid, and a protein-production capacity upgrade.
4. Design a trade-promotion architecture that varies promo depth by channel and by segment, and that supports the Year-1 portfolio positioning without eroding gross margin.
5. Recommend a complete Year-1 portfolio package and identify the Year-2 move most correlated with the Year-3 composite outcome.

On the morning of **Tuesday, January 20, 2026**, **Camila Bermúdez** arrived at the Arvelia Dairy & Beverage headquarters in the industrial district of San Joaquín at 7:14 a.m. and opened the Year-1 portfolio workbook her category analyst had finalized at 10:47 p.m. Monday. Nine days separated her from the Category Committee. The category-dashboard on her wall read Arvelia 17.0%, Lactamar 24.0%, private-label 13.4%, regional tail 45.6%. Fourteen SKU cards on the portfolio-planning board carried pricing and trade-promotion signals from the December 2025 audit.

## The Protagonist

Camila Bermúdez, 37, had joined Arvelia in 2020 as Associate Category Manager on the mainstream-yogurt line and had progressed through Category Manager on premium drinking-yogurt (2022–2024) and Senior Category Manager on the beverage portfolio (2024–2025) before her November 2025 appointment as Brand Manager for the Arvelia portfolio. A graduate of the Pontificia Universidad de Arvelia with an MBA from IESE-São Paulo and an executive program on pricing at LBS, she had, in her 2023–2024 category-manager role, led the premium drinking-yogurt rescaling that had recovered 1.4 points of premium-segment share from Lactamar. The Chief Commercial Officer's November onboarding instruction had been: "Grow share where margin lives. The part of the portfolio that compounds is not the part Lactamar watches."

---

## The Decision

Bermúdez had to commit, by 9:00 a.m. on Thursday, January 29, the Year-1 portfolio package the Category Committee would treat as the three-year operating envelope:

1. **Pricing and Portfolio Architecture** — whether to hold list prices on mainstream yogurt, whether to move premium drinking-yogurt plus 2.5% against a price-lazy Lactamar premium SKU, and whether to cut value milk minus 1% to block a Lactamar value skew.
2. **Trade-Promotion Depth by Channel** — whether to run 8% promo depth on modern trade mainstream, 14% on traditional trade, and zero on premium; and whether to fund the trade spend from the mainstream contribution or from a neutral cross-SKU fund.
3. **Marketing Allocation, Production Mix, and Distribution Intensity** — whether to split marketing 60/40 brand/performance (the Category Manager's recommendation) or 45/55 (the Commercial Director's preference), whether to tilt the production plan to mainstream against a Year-1 premium demand forecast, and whether to intensify distribution in traditional-trade corridors at the expense of modern-trade shelf investment.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across three rounds. A Year-1 package that held mainstream prices, moved premium plus 2.5%, cut value minus 1% to block, ran 8/14/0 promo depth across modern-trade-mainstream / traditional-trade / premium, allocated marketing 60/40 brand-performance, tilted production to mainstream, and intensified distribution in traditional-trade corridors produced, on the internal model, a Year-1 share lift of 1.4 percentage points, a Year-2 composite that absorbed the Lactamar premium cut through a limited-edition protein variant, and a Year-3 composite of 86 out of a 100-point envelope. A Year-1 package that matched Lactamar's price architecture across the book and ran 14/18/4 promo depth produced a higher Year-1 share lift but opened a Year-2 margin exposure of 280 basis points and a Year-3 composite of 71.

## Company Background

Arvelia Dairy & Beverage Co. had been founded in 1986 as a regional dairy cooperative and had, across forty years, built the fourteen-SKU national portfolio the case studied. 2025 revenue was ARV 182M distributed as value (24%), mainstream (38%), and premium (38%). Channel mix read modern trade 44%, traditional trade 37%, foodservice 19%. The fourteen SKUs comprised four mainstream yogurts, three premium drinking yogurts, two value milk variants, two flavored-milk beverages, a single-serve yogurt drink, and two foodservice-only items. The premium drinking-yogurt SKU — launched in 2021 — had, since 2023, been the single highest-margin item at 43% gross margin, against a portfolio average of 33.8%. Trade spend at 9.6% of revenue was marginally above the FMCG dairy benchmark of 8.2–9.0% and reflected the 2024 traditional-trade shelf-share defense the company had launched against a Lactamar push.

The competitive set had three principal actors. Lactamar, the category leader at 24% share, competed on a portfolio-wide pricing program and a broad SKU-family footprint in both modern and traditional trade. A cluster of three private-label brands (retailer-owned) had, across 2024–2025, gained 2.4 points of value-segment share. A long tail of regional dairies competed on local relationships and occasional seasonal promotions.

## Industry and Market Context

The Arvelia dairy and beverage landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Year-1 decision sat inside. First, the category's modern-trade shelf economics had, since 2023, rewarded SKU-family rationalization — retailers were delisting SKUs below a 14-unit-per-store-per-week velocity threshold, a threshold three of the company's fourteen SKUs currently hovered near. Second, the national traditional-trade channel — roughly 48,000 mom-and-pop bodegas — was, in the 2025 category-intelligence survey, the single highest-contribution-margin channel for the full portfolio at a blended 41% vs 33% in modern trade, and had been the target of a Lactamar promotional push that had, in 2024, depressed Arvelia's traditional-trade velocity on value milk by 6%. Third, the foodservice channel was, in early 2026, entering a contract-renewal cycle with two multi-year opportunities on the table — a regional QSR chain and a hotel-group master agreement — and the Category Committee had, in December 2025, signalled that foodservice would be a Year-3 growth plank if a Year-1 foundation held.

Empirically, the post-2018 FMCG research had two robust findings the Year-1 package had to respect. First, non-price responses to a category-leader price cut — specifically, limited-edition SKU launches at premium-price tiers that captured segment-switchers — had, across a 2018–2024 sample of eighty-two category-shock events, produced three-year composite outcomes 14 points above price-matching responses, at a Year-1 marketing-investment cost of 180 basis points. Second, traditional-trade-focused promotional spend was, on a 2020–2024 sample of ninety-four FMCG portfolios, 2.2 times more gross-margin-accretive than equivalent-dollar modern-trade spend when targeted at mainstream-segment velocity.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Chief Commercial Officer had, in the November 2025 strategic review, committed to the Board a three-year portfolio-share target of 22% against the 17% baseline. The Category Committee had, on January 6, approved the target and the implied trade-spend envelope of ARV 17.5M across three years. The Category Manager had, on January 13, presented three draft Year-1 packages. The Chief Marketing Officer had, on January 17, flagged the Lactamar protein-beverage pipeline through a retail-trade intelligence source, placing a Year-2 Lactamar protein-launch probability at 78%.

### What Bermúdez knew

The team had modeled three Year-1 packages. A Price-Match Package (match Lactamar pricing across the book, 14/18/4 promo depth, 45/55 marketing, premium-heavy production, balanced distribution) projected Year-1 share lift 1.8pp, Year-2 margin drag 280 basis points, Year-3 composite 71. A Portfolio-Segmentation Package (hold mainstream, premium plus 2.5%, value minus 1%, 8/14/0 promo, 60/40 marketing, mainstream production tilt, traditional-trade distribution intensification) projected Year-1 share lift 1.4pp, Year-2 composite resilient through a limited protein variant, Year-3 composite 86. A Defensive Package (hold pricing across the board, 6/10/0 promo, 70/30 marketing, premium-conservative production, neutral distribution) projected Year-1 share lift 0.6pp, Year-2 composite resilient, Year-3 composite 74 — margin-stable but share-losing.

### What she did not know

Whether the Lactamar premium price cut would land at the intelligence-reported 8% or at a deeper 12% that a second-source retail contact had referenced; whether the Lactamar protein-beverage would launch in modern-trade only (base case) or extend to traditional-trade across the Year-2 window; whether the private-label value-milk push would extend from three retailer-private labels to five; and whether the regional QSR-chain foodservice RFP would close inside the Year-1 window or slip to the Year-2 window.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Commercial Officer**: *"Traditional-trade 14% promo on mainstream. That is the velocity lever. Do not spend modern-trade promo dollars into a Lactamar shelf-price war."*

**The Category Manager for Premium**: *"Premium plus 2.5%. Lactamar's premium SKU has not had a pricing move in fourteen months. They have abandoned the pricing power on that item."*

**The Head of Sales for Foodservice**: *"Lock two multi-year contracts in Year 1. The QSR chain will come; wait for it. The hotel-group master is the Year-1 anchor."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Bermúdez had narrowed the space to three Year-1 portfolio packages.

- **Option A — Price-Match:** Match Lactamar across the book, 14/18/4 promo, 45/55 marketing. Year-1 share lift 1.8pp, Year-2 margin drag 280bp, Year-3 composite 71. Highest Year-1 share lift. Structurally exposed to the Lactamar cycle.

- **Option B — Portfolio-Segmentation:** Hold mainstream, premium plus 2.5%, value minus 1%, 8/14/0 promo, 60/40 marketing, mainstream production tilt, traditional-trade distribution intensification. Year-1 share lift 1.4pp, Year-3 composite 86. Aligns with CCO and Category Manager for Premium.

- **Option C — Defensive:** Hold pricing across the board, 6/10/0 promo, 70/30 marketing. Year-1 share lift 0.6pp, Year-3 composite 74. Margin-stable, share-losing.

Bermúdez's private view, not yet shared with the CCO, was that Option B was the right Year-1 package and that the single subsequent decision most correlated with the Year-3 composite was the Year-2 response to the Lactamar premium cut and the protein launch — specifically, a limited-edition Arvelia protein variant launched at premium-tier pricing to cap the switcher gap without cannibalizing the flagship premium SKU, combined with two multi-year foodservice contracts locked with annual price escalators.
