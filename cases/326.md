## Abstract

Minera y Servicios del Pacífico S.A. (MSP) was, in the case universe, a twenty-nine-year-old Antofagasta-headquartered Chilean mid-cap mining-services and equipment firm founded in 1997 by two ex-Codelco mine-planning engineers and now serving six Chilean copper mines on long-term operations-and-maintenance and drilling-optimization contracts — CLP 312B (approximately USD 340M) of 2025 revenue of which 92% Chile-domestic and 8% from a Peruvian-copper-corridor exploratory sales presence established in 2022 as a CLP 4.8B minority position, 1,860 employees, a proprietary mine-planning-and-drilling-optimization software licensed by the six Chilean-mine clients and no other customers globally, a 2025 EBITDA margin of 16.8% and net debt / EBITDA of 1.4×, a 2024 CLP 28B five-year growth facility from a Chilean banking syndicate earmarked for Latin-American expansion, and a Board-approved 2025 strategic plan to diversify beyond the six-Chilean-copper-mine revenue concentration through a four-market internationalization over 2026–2029. On **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 7:38 a.m., **Ing. Francisca Del Campo-Urrutia**, 48, CEO of MSP, opened the Round-1 OLI worksheet her Head of Strategy had refreshed at 11:51 p.m. the previous evening and sat down to commit the four-round internationalization package for the 10:30 a.m. Board and Executive Committee session where the May 2026 entry-mode decisions for Peru and Brazil were scheduled for sign-off.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Apply Dunning's 1977 eclectic paradigm (OLI) to MSP's internationalization, complete the ownership-advantage, location-advantage and internalization-advantage worksheet for each of Peru, Brazil, Canada and Australia, and articulate why high-O plus high-L plus high-I implies FDI rather than licensing or export.
2. Apply Kostova's 1999 institutional-distance construct across regulatory, cognitive and normative dimensions to the four-market sequence, articulate why Chile-Australia presents the highest distance despite mining-sector affinity, and articulate why institutional distance modifies OLI such that location advantages identical on paper generate different entry-mode answers.
3. Apply Anderson and Gatignon's 1986 transaction-cost entry-mode framework to the five candidate modes (export, licensing, JV, WOS-greenfield, WOS-acquisition), resolve the tension between MSP's high-asset-specificity software (arguing for high-control modes) and Australia's high-institutional-uncertainty (arguing for local-partner modes), and commit the majority-controlled joint-venture architecture that preserves control while absorbing uncertainty.
4. Apply Luo and Tung's 2007 springboard theory to the Canada and Australia entries, articulate why emerging-market-multinational internationalization is often about strategic-asset acquisition rather than existing-advantage exploitation, and connect Porter's 1986 global-industry construct and Caves's 1996 MNE framework to Rio Pacific's three-distributor-partnership counter-move in Peru as a competitive-dynamics response to MSP's going-global posture.

Commit the Round-4 sequence-payoff capability-transfer architecture — Peru-and-Brazil build cross-border capability, Canada acquisition leverages it at a lower integration-risk premium, Canada-acquisition-technology makes MSP attractive as Australia-JV majority partner — and articulate why the *Entrada al Mundo* frame sim 326 occupies is distinct from Empresa Catalina LATAM Entry (sim 044), TechArena Disruption Response (sim 074) and ArtesanoMex MundoEspera (sim 302) — 326 emphasises the LATAM multilatina internationalization-as-capability-transfer synthesis across OLI / institutional distance / entry mode / springboard / sequence.

On the morning of **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 7:38 a.m., **Ing. Francisca Del Campo-Urrutia** arrived at MSP's Avenida Pedro Aguirre Cerda headquarters in central Antofagasta, greeted the two senior strategy analysts already at the whiteboard on which the four-market entry-mode matrix was drawn, and walked into the CEO office past the 1997-founding photograph of her predecessors Ignacio Del Campo (her father) and Manuel Urrutia at the first Codelco-Norte services contract. The Round-1 OLI worksheet her Head of Strategy had refreshed at 11:51 p.m. was open on her laptop. The April 14 Rio Pacific Limited press release — announcing three distributor partnerships in Peru — was pinned to the corkboard in red. The May 12 Board meeting agenda was on the desk, with the Peru-and-Brazil entry-mode decisions as the first item.

---

## The Protagonist

Del Campo-Urrutia, 48, had been CEO at MSP since January 2024, had completed an Ingeniería Civil de Minas at the Universidad de Chile in 2001 and an MBA at the University of Western Ontario's Ivey Business School in 2009, had spent nine years between 2001 and 2009 at Codelco's mine-planning division, had joined MSP in 2010 first as Head of Engineering and subsequently as Chief Operating Officer between 2018 and 2023 before the 2024 CEO appointment on her father's retirement from the Board Chair. Her internationalization instincts had been shaped by three formative exposures: the 2008 Ivey international-business elective taught on the Dunning / Kostova / Anderson-Gatignon / Luo-Tung canon; the 2022 Peruvian-copper-corridor exploratory sales presence she had led as COO and the CLP 4.8B minority position that had produced a learning-intensive 8%-of-revenue beachhead; and the 2024 IFC-sponsored multilatina-internationalization roundtable in São Paulo where three Chilean-mining-services peers had presented sequencing patterns that had converged on the capability-transfer architecture she was now committing MSP to.

---

## The Decision

Del Campo-Urrutia had to commit, by the 10:30 a.m. Board session, the four-round internationalization package.

1. **Round 1 — Dunning OLI.** Complete the OLI worksheet for each of the four markets. Confirm high-O on MSP's software and copper-ore expertise across all four. Commit FDI rather than licensing for Peru and Brazil.
2. **Round 2 — Kostova institutional distance + Anderson-Gatignon entry mode.** Read institutional distance. Peru — wholly-owned-greenfield subsidiary (Q4 2026). Brazil — wholly-owned-acquisition of a mid-sized São-Paulo-State mining-services firm (Q2 2027). Canada — acquisition of a British-Columbia specialist in advanced environmental-management for copper mining (2028).
3. **Round 3 — Luo-Tung springboard + Porter global dynamics.** Frame Canada and Australia as strategic-asset acquisition rather than existing-advantage exploitation. Commit the majority-controlled Australia JV (2029). Plan the response to Rio Pacific's Peru distributor counter-move with a Peruvian-market-presence acceleration.
4. **Round 4 — Sequence-payoff capability-transfer.** Commit the Peru-Brazil-Canada-Australia sequence. Use Peru and Brazil to build cross-border operations capability. Leverage the Canada technology in the Australia JV negotiation.

Commitments: the Round-1 OLI-backed FDI posture; the Round-2 institutional-distance-calibrated entry modes; the Round-3 springboard-and-competitive-dynamics response; the Round-4 capability-transfer sequence; and a one-page Board memo that frames the four-round commitment as a 2026-2029 multilatina internationalization arc.

## Sectoral Background

The 2010–2025 LATAM-multilatina mining-services internationalization panel had, in the case universe, registered approximately USD 14.8bn in aggregate cross-border revenue across the eight major Chilean, Peruvian, Brazilian and Mexican mining-services firms, concentrated in three operator tiers (listed super-regionals internationalising through acquisition; mid-cap multilatinas like MSP internationalising through sequenced entry; specialist exporters licensing technology). The 2015–2025 Chilean mining-services internationalization panel had documented that sequenced entry architectures in the category had resolved with documented success at approximately 36% of attempted programmes, with the failure modes distributed at approximately 58% on portfolio-of-market-entries patterns that fragmented resources across four independent fights, approximately 48% on mode-misfit patterns that entered Australia via wholly-owned-greenfield against the Kostova institutional-distance read, and approximately 42% on competitive-dynamics-blindness patterns that did not anticipate incumbent counter-moves.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1977–2025 international-business literature produced three findings the simulation had to respect. First, Dunning's 1977 eclectic paradigm combined with Rugman's 1981 internalization theory had produced documented entry-mode-outcome readings across approximately 2,600 multinational-entry panel cases averaging +28% superior-mode-choice rates when the OLI worksheet was completed for each market rather than inferred from a single-country anchor. Second, Kostova's 1999 institutional-distance construct, extended by Xu and Shenkar's 2002 review and by Chao and Kumar's 2010 subsidiary-survival panel, had produced documented institutional-distance-modification-of-OLI readings averaging 32–46% of entry-mode variance, confirming that location advantages identical on paper generate different entry-mode answers under different institutional distance. Third, Luo and Tung's 2007 springboard theory, supported by the 2010–2025 LATAM-multilatina panel, had produced documented strategic-asset-acquisition internationalization patterns accounting for approximately 58% of emerging-market-multinational developed-market entries, confirming that the internationalization is often a learning loop rather than an existing-advantage exploitation.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The 1997 MSP founding by Ignacio Del Campo and Manuel Urrutia; the 2005 first Codelco-Norte long-term contract; the 2010 Del Campo-Urrutia joining as Head of Engineering; the 2018 Chief Operating Officer appointment; the 2022 Peruvian-copper-corridor exploratory sales presence at CLP 4.8B minority position; the 2024 CEO appointment on Ignacio Del Campo's Board-Chair retirement; the 2025 Board-approved four-market internationalization plan; the April 14 2026 Rio Pacific Limited Peru-distributor press release; and the April 23 2026 Round-1 positioning session were the sequence.

### What Del Campo-Urrutia knew

The four-market OLI worksheet and her Head of Strategy's read; the Kostova distance rankings across the four markets; the five-mode entry-architecture; the Peru wholly-owned-greenfield and Brazil wholly-owned-acquisition candidates her deal team had identified in Q1 2026; the Canada environmental-management-specialist shortlist for 2028; the Australia-JV candidate partner — an Australian Mid West-based mining-services firm — that had reciprocated interest in Q4 2025; the CLP 28B 2024 growth facility and the Q3 2026 first-draw trigger; the Rio Pacific Peru counter-move and its implied competitive-dynamics frame; and the May 12 Board meeting agenda.

### What she did not know

Whether the Brazilian São-Paulo-State acquisition candidate would reopen negotiations on the BRL 180M price that had stalled at Q4 2025; whether the Canada British-Columbia candidate would accept a 51% MSP-majority structure or would insist on 35%; whether the Australia JV counterparty would sign the capability-transfer clause that made MSP the majority partner or would require a 50-50 structure; whether Rio Pacific's Peru counter-move would escalate to pricing competition in the Chilean home market; and whether the Board — including two family-member directors on the founder side — would accept the sequenced 2026-2029 horizon or would press for Australia-first on brand-visibility grounds.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Strategy (37, former Bain São Paulo consultant)**: *"Francisca, la secuencia importa más que el modo. Perú primero. Brasil después. Canadá tercero. Australia al final. Si invertimos el orden, vamos a pelear cuatro guerras independientes."*

**The CFO (52, former LarrainVial managing director)**: *"El facility está. La capacidad financiera está. El riesgo es de integración, no de liquidez. Y el riesgo de integración se baja con secuencia."*

**The Chair of the Board (71, MSP founder Ignacio Del Campo)**: *"Hija, la empresa la construimos en Antofagasta. La internacionalización no es traición. Es continuidad. Pero no apures Australia."*

**The Peru-beachhead country manager (45, former Buenaventura operations director)**: *"La respuesta a Rio Pacific es cerrar tres contratos locales este trimestre. No bajar precio. La respuesta es presencia."*

**The Australian JV counterparty (48, based in Perth)**: *"We can do 51-49 with MSP as majority if you bring the Canadian acquisition technology into the venture by 2028. Otherwise 50-50."*

**The 1,860 MSP employees (aggregate signal via the 2025 climate survey)**: *"El copper-Chileno sigue siendo el core. La expansión tiene que sumar. No tiene que diluir."*

## Options Under Consideration

Del Campo-Urrutia had narrowed the internationalization decision space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — Peru WOS-greenfield (Q4 2026) + Brazil WOS-acquisition (Q2 2027) + Canada acquisition (2028) + Australia MSP-majority JV (2029), sequenced for capability transfer (Recommended).** Respects the Dunning / Kostova / Anderson-Gatignon / Luo-Tung / Porter-Caves canon; builds cross-border capability in Peru and Brazil; leverages Canada environmental-management technology into the Australia JV; absorbs Australia institutional distance through the JV; responds to Rio Pacific in Peru with presence rather than price. Projected 2029 non-Chile revenue share at 32% against the current 8% and cumulative capability transfer sufficient to take the Australia JV to majority at renegotiation.
- **Option B — Four simultaneous entries + four wholly-owned-greenfield subsidiaries + no sequencing.** Rejected — reproduces the 58% portfolio-of-market-entries failure pattern documented in the 2015–2025 panel; violates the Kostova institutional-distance modification of OLI on Australia; surrenders the capability-transfer value-chain across the four markets.
- **Option C — License to Rio Pacific + harvest the Chilean copper base.** Defensible on cash-distribution grounds but surrenders the multilatina brand-building thesis, abandons the 2024 Board-approved strategic plan, and structurally locks MSP into a shrinking revenue concentration.

Del Campo-Urrutia's private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the single decision most correlated with the 2029 outcome was the Round-4 sequence commitment, because the 2,600-case Dunning-OLI-panel evidence had shown that capability-transfer sequences delivered +28% superior-mode-choice and +34% lower integration-risk-premium on downstream acquisitions, and because her 2024 São-Paulo multilatina roundtable had converged on exactly this sequencing pattern across three peer Chilean-mining-services firms. The *Entrada al Mundo* frame sits at the LATAM multilatina internationalization-as-capability-transfer tier in the 2026 Express catalogue, distinct from Empresa Catalina LATAM Entry (sim 044, SME LATAM-entry frame), TechArena Disruption Response (sim 074, tech-disruption frame) and ArtesanoMex MundoEspera (sim 302, SME-export frame) — 326 emphasises the OLI / institutional distance / entry mode / springboard / sequence synthesis inside a mid-cap Chilean mining-services multilatina.

La internacionalización es una secuencia de transferencias de capacidad. No una cartera de entradas de mercado."*
