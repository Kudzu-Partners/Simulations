## Abstract

Luminia Energía S.A., a Latin American vertically-integrated power utility with 14,500 MW of generation capacity (coal 3,800 MW, gas 2,400 MW, hydro 5,800 MW, solar 900 MW, wind 600 MW, battery storage 0 MW), entered 2026 with a carbon intensity of 312 gCO₂/kWh, a cash position of USD 470M, and a six-year board mandate to reach net-zero emissions by 2032. A newly-defined composite — the Trilemma Index — measured performance as the geometric mean of six pillar scores: Grid Security, Decarbonization, Affordability, Financial Health, Just Transition, and Regulatory Alignment. The geometric mean punished the weakest pillar: a single sub-40 score dragged the composite regardless of strength elsewhere. An El-Niño drought warning — 55% probability, placing the 5,800 MW of hydro at risk — was live at the start of Round 1. The capex budget was USD 1,500M across the five rounds. The case places Valentina Aguilar, the new Chief Strategy & Sustainability Officer, on the evening before the Round 1 capital plan is ratified.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate capital allocation across generation technologies under a geometric-mean scoring regime that punishes the weakest pillar.
2. Analyze the trade-offs among grid reliability, decarbonization pace, affordability, financial health, just-transition commitments, and regulatory alignment.
3. Diagnose the sensitivity of a utility's transition plan to hydrology, seismic, and technology-price scenarios.
4. Design a coal-retirement sequence that matches just-transition obligations without stranding assets.
5. Recommend a Round-1 package that preserves optionality for three known events (drought, seismic, technology breakthrough) in subsequent rounds.

On the evening of **February 18, 2026**, **Valentina Aguilar** stood at the window of the Strategy Office at Luminia Energía's Bogotá headquarters and looked, one more time, at the six-score dashboard her team had printed across an entire wall. The dashboard showed, from left to right: *Grid Security 72. Decarbonization 30. Affordability 68. Financial Health 62. Just Transition 40. Regulatory Alignment 55. Trilemma (geometric mean) 50.* Below the dashboard, taped at eye level, the single sentence her team had put there in red marker that morning: *"Decarbonization at 30 is the fuse."* The capital-plan vote was tomorrow morning at 9:00 a.m.

---

## The Protagonist

Valentina Aguilar, 46, had joined Luminia on January 5, 2026, from a regional development bank where she had spent nine years structuring energy-transition finance across the Andean region. Before that she had been a corporate-finance analyst at a global utility's Latin America desk, with a focus on hydro-asset stress-testing under El Niño scenarios. She had been recruited to Luminia on the strength of her hydrology modeling and her work on the public-sector side of just-transition programs. Her contract carried a six-year vesting gate tied to the Trilemma composite and to the 2032 net-zero milestone.

## The Decision

Aguilar had to recommend, by 9:00 a.m. on February 19, Round 1 settings on five capital-plan levers and a carbon-strategy posture:

1. **Coal Retirements (MW)** — how much of the 3,800 MW coal fleet to retire in Year 1.
2. **New Solar (MW)** — new solar capacity commissioned in Year 1.
3. **New Wind (MW)** — new wind capacity commissioned in Year 1.
4. **New Battery Storage (MW)** — new BESS capacity commissioned in Year 1.
5. **Tariff Adjustment (%)** — regulated-tariff change applied to residential customers.
6. **Carbon Strategy** — Buy-and-Comply, Bank Allowances, or Over-Comply-and-Sell.

Capex in Year 1 was capped at a rolling fraction of the five-year USD 1,500M budget. The El-Niño drought warning — 55% probability — was live. The six-score composite would be computed after Round 1 as the geometric mean, meaning a Decarbonization score that remained at 30 would cap the composite at approximately 49 regardless of improvements in the other five.

---

## Company Background

Luminia Energía S.A. had been founded in 1978 through the consolidation of three regional power utilities as a vertically-integrated generator, transmitter, and distributor serving 4.2 million customer connections. 2025 revenue was USD 3.1B with an EBITDA margin of 32%, a net debt / EBITDA of 3.6x, and a BBB− credit rating. The generation mix — 14,500 MW across five technologies plus zero storage — carried a 312 gCO₂/kWh intensity, above the regional median (245 gCO₂/kWh) and well above the peer-set leader (110 gCO₂/kWh).

Ownership was 38% government (a state-holding company), 24% a regional pension-fund consortium, 18% a European strategic minority, and the remainder float. The European minority's mandate, updated in December 2025, required a published net-zero-by-2032 plan with a Trilemma composite of 60 or above by end-2028. The pension-fund consortium held a separate board-level commitment to *just transition* provisions: any coal retirement had to be accompanied by a funded reskilling program for affected workers (currently 2,400 directly employed in the coal fleet plus 3,800 in the supplier base).

## Industry and Market Context

Latin American power-utility decarbonization had entered a bifurcated phase. First, solar and wind LCOE had fallen to below the operating cost of the regional coal fleet in 2024, making the economic case for retirement newly robust. Second, BESS (battery-energy-storage systems) prices were expected to drop a further 32–40% between 2026 and 2028 on the back of one public supply-chain breakthrough, giving optionality a real financial value. Third, El-Niño hydrological variability had increased measurably: five of the past eight years had shown below-median Andean hydrology and two of those had produced grid-scarcity events that penalized utilities without flexible back-up.

The Trilemma Index had been introduced as a regulator-preferred composite in 2024 and had been adopted by two of Luminia's three major shareholder groups. The geometric-mean structure — as distinct from a weighted sum — punished any single sub-pillar dropping below 40; a company at 72, 30, 68, 62, 40, 55 computed to 50 regardless of the four pillars above 60. The implication for allocation decisions was precise: raising the weakest pillar by five points lifted the composite more than raising any other pillar by ten.

## The Situation

### How we got here

Luminia's prior CEO, Hernán Caicedo (retired December 2025), had run a coal-heavy generation fleet through three policy cycles at high EBITDA margins and moderate capex. The 2023 board refresh had installed five new directors with explicit transition mandates. The European strategic minority had increased its stake from 12% to 18% in October 2025 on the condition of a published 2032 plan. The CEO who replaced Caicedo, Inés Valderrama, had hired Aguilar in December.

### What Aguilar knew

The internal planning team had modeled five capital packages across the six-pillar composite. A conservative package (800 MW coal retired, 250 MW solar, 50 MW wind, 100 MW BESS, tariff +3%, Buy-and-Comply) cost USD 1,180M in Year 1 and produced Year-1 scores of 70/44/66/61/46/58 — composite 56. An aggressive package (1,400 MW coal retired, 500 MW solar, 150 MW wind, 200 MW BESS, tariff +6%, Over-Comply-and-Sell) cost USD 1,480M in Year 1 and produced Year-1 scores of 61/58/58/52/54/65 — composite 58. A balanced package (1,000 MW coal retired, 350 MW solar, 100 MW wind, 150 MW BESS, tariff +4%, Bank Allowances) cost USD 1,310M in Year 1 and produced Year-1 scores of 68/52/64/58/50/62 — composite 59.

### What she did not know

Whether the El-Niño event would materialize at the 55% base case or at the 75% upper-case; whether a 6.0–6.5 magnitude seismic event in the Andean hydro region would recur in Year 3 (a standing 18% probability in the internal actuarial tables); whether the BESS supply-chain breakthrough would land in Year 2 (the technology roadmap's base case) or Year 3 (the pessimistic case).

### What others were saying

**Ricardo Moreno**, CFO, argued for the **Conservative Package**: *"Our debt-to-EBITDA is three point six. We cannot fund the aggressive package without a capital raise the European minority has not signalled support for. The composite will move slower, but the balance sheet will hold."*

**Carmen Echeverri**, Head of Operations, argued for the **Balanced Package with Drought Insurance**: *"Retire eight hundred megawatts of coal to hold Grid Security. Build two-fifty solar. Rush a hundred megawatts of BESS in Q4 against the El-Niño scenario. Tariff three percent. Bank the allowances. The base case is fine. The tail case survives."*

**Sofía Henao**, head of the European minority's representative on the board, argued for the **Aggressive Package with Sequencing Risk**: *"The 2032 plan has to be published. A conservative Year 1 is a Year-6 scramble. Retire fourteen hundred megawatts now. Build a real capital-raise plan alongside. Over-comply, sell credits, pay down the incremental debt with the proceeds."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Aguilar had narrowed the space to three concrete packages.

- **Option A — Conservative, Drought-Resilient (Moreno plan):** 800 MW retired; 250 MW solar; 50 MW wind; 100 MW BESS; tariff +3%; Buy-and-Comply. Year-1 cost USD 1,180M; Year-1 composite 56; Year-5 projected composite 64. Decarbonization pillar likely to stay below 45 through Year 3.

- **Option B — Balanced with Hydro-Hedge (Echeverri + Aguilar draft):** 1,000 MW retired; 350 MW solar; 100 MW wind; 150 MW BESS (100 front-loaded); tariff +4%; Bank Allowances. Year-1 cost USD 1,310M; Year-1 composite 59; Year-5 projected composite 69. Decarbonization pillar crosses 50 in Year 2 in base case.

- **Option C — Aggressive, Front-Loaded (Henao plan):** 1,400 MW retired; 500 MW solar; 150 MW wind; 200 MW BESS; tariff +6%; Over-Comply-and-Sell. Year-1 cost USD 1,480M; Year-1 composite 58 (lower than Option B because Grid Security drops); Year-5 projected composite 72 — conditional on BESS-price breakthrough and capital-raise execution.

Aguilar suspected Option B dominated in the median El-Niño run and the median seismic run; Option C dominated in the favorable-technology run and lost in the capital-raise-rejection run; Option A lost in the European-minority assessment in Year 2. At 8:31 p.m. she turned off the wall dashboard and walked to the CEO's office for the 8:45 pre-briefing.

The Trilemma was not a triangle. It was a geometric-mean vise. Whichever package Aguilar recommended, the weakest pillar at end-Year-1 would be the headline number.
