## Abstract

Iberian Textiles Group was, in the case universe, a Porto-Lisbon-Madrid vertically integrated textile manufacturer with EUR 1.9B in 2025 revenue across three business units (fashion basics, technical textiles and home textiles), eleven manufacturing sites across Iberia and Morocco, 4,200 Tier-1 and Tier-2 suppliers across 19 jurisdictions, a 2025 Compliance Score reading of 34 against an EU CSRD-subject-peer benchmark of 68 (composite captures ESRS scope coverage, audit-trail quality and stakeholder-engagement documentation), a 2025 Data-Quality reading of 29 against a peer benchmark of 62, a Stakeholder-Credibility reading of 42 against a peer benchmark of 64, and a first-wave CSRD reporting obligation under ESRS with a limited-assurance attestation due on the 2026 financial-year filing (March–April 2027). The 2023–2025 sustainability reporting model had been anchored on a GRI-style narrative report prepared by the sustainability team as a stand-alone PDF, with no financial-materiality quantification, no documented stakeholder-engagement protocol and no data infrastructure that could sustain an audit-trail at assurance-grade. The Q1 2026 board had approved a EUR 6.8M CSRD readiness envelope and a Chief-Financial-Officer-led five-round commitment cycle against a Compliance Score floor above 70, a Data Quality floor above 65, a Stakeholder Credibility floor above 60 and a composite DoubleMatrix Score above 68. The case opens at 6:05 a.m. on Monday, April 13, 2026, at the Iberian Textiles Group Porto headquarters as **Mariana Esteves**, a 48-year-old Chief Financial Officer, prepares the Round-1 scoping commitment. Three-hundred-thirty days separate her from the March 31, 2027 first-assured ESRS filing.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Round-1 CSRD scoping commitment (impact-first double-materiality envelope built from in-house value-chain diagnostic vs. competitor-matrix-copy envelope) and diagnose when the double-materiality envelope dominates the copy envelope on the limited-assurance-readiness basis.
2. Analyze the Round-2 stakeholder-engagement commitment (multi-stakeholder-panel envelope across workers, suppliers, communities, customers and investors vs. internal-only envelope) and recommend the panel envelope against the internal envelope on the ESRS-1 disclosure-defensibility basis.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 quantification commitment (financial-and-impact-quantified envelope with monetised impact-pathways vs. qualitative-heatmap envelope) and recommend the quantified envelope against the heatmap envelope on the investor-communication and capital-allocation basis.
4. Respond to the Round-4 data-infrastructure commitment (audit-grade data platform with GHG Scope 3, value-chain and workforce indicators vs. spreadsheet-consolidation envelope) and recommend the platform envelope on the assurance-cost and audit-finding basis.
5. Design the Round-5 assured-reporting posture that converts CSRD compliance from a reporting tax into a governance and capital-allocation asset.

## The Protagonist

Mariana Esteves, 48, had assumed the Chief-Financial-Officer role at Iberian Textiles Group on January 15, 2024, from a Tier-1 Iberian retail group where she had, across fourteen years, closed three capital-structure refinancings and had overseen the 2022 voluntary TCFD-aligned disclosure rebuild. Before that she had spent six years as an audit manager at a Big-Four practice covering consumer and retail clients. The Chief Executive Officer had recruited her in late 2023 specifically on two capabilities: her documented discipline of treating non-financial disclosure as a financial-reporting problem rather than a communications problem, and her willingness to commit the audit-grade data infrastructure capex ahead of the first regulatory cycle rather than during it. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 25% to the Compliance Score and 20% to the limited-assurance pass-through rate on the first-wave filing.

## The Decision

Esteves had to commit, by the April 30 audit-committee pre-review, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–5.

1. **Round 1 — Scoping.** Three archetypes: impact-first double-materiality (in-house value-chain diagnostic across fashion basics, technical textiles and home textiles, impact and financial materiality assessed in parallel, Compliance +16, Composite 76), competitor-copy (adopt the published matrix of a listed Iberian peer — Composite 34, assurance-trigger), or hybrid (double-materiality on fashion basics, copy on the other two units — Composite 54). Working envelope: impact-first.
2. **Round 2 — Stakeholder engagement.** Three archetypes: multi-stakeholder-panel (documented panel across workers, suppliers, communities, customers and investors, two engagement rounds with independent facilitation, Composite 76), internal-only (executive-committee workshop — Composite 36, disclosure-defensibility floor forfeited), or phased (panel on workers and suppliers only — Composite 54). Working envelope: multi-stakeholder-panel.
3. **Round 3 — Quantification.** Three vectors: quantified (monetised impact-pathway on climate, water and workforce, financial-materiality bridge to the management accounts, Composite 76), qualitative-heatmap (five-by-five likelihood-impact heatmap without monetisation — Composite 36, investor-credibility tax), or phased (quantified on climate only — Composite 56). Working envelope: quantified.
4. **Round 4 — Data infrastructure.** Three vectors: audit-grade-platform (Scope-1/2/3, value-chain and workforce indicators on an audit-grade platform with documented controls, Composite 78), spreadsheet-consolidation (finance-team spreadsheet consolidation — Composite 34, audit-finding probability 78%), or single-domain (platform for GHG only — Composite 56). Working envelope: audit-grade-platform.
5. **Round 5 — Assured-reporting posture.** Three vectors: reasonable-assurance-ready (limited-assurance Year 1, reasonable-assurance pathway pre-registered for Year 3, Composite 78), last-minute-limited (limited-assurance Year 1 with no forward pathway — Composite 38, reputational-tail risk), or phased (limited-assurance Year 1, reasonable-assurance deferred to Year 5 — Composite 58). Working envelope: reasonable-assurance-ready.

On Esteves' working model, an Impact-First / Multi-Stakeholder / Quantified / Audit-Grade / Reasonable-Assurance-Ready envelope delivered Composite 72. An opposite envelope delivered Composite 26 (assurance-trigger). A hybrid envelope delivered Composite 54.

## Company Background

Iberian Textiles Group had been founded in Porto in 1964 as a small fashion-basics manufacturer and had, across sixty-two years, grown into a three-business-unit vertically integrated textile group with eleven manufacturing sites and 4,200 Tier-1 and Tier-2 suppliers across nineteen jurisdictions. The 2023–2025 sustainability reporting model had been delivered as a stand-alone GRI-style narrative PDF, produced outside the financial-reporting calendar, with no audit-trail and no documented stakeholder-engagement protocol.

The November 2025 audit-committee review had surfaced three realities the CSRD envelope inherited: the 2025 first-time CSRD scoping exercise had identified 62 potentially material topics across the twelve ESRS, against a peer median of 22 assessed and 14 prioritised; the 2025 Scope-3 emissions estimate had carried an uncertainty band of ±38% and was not reconciled to the EUR 1.9B revenue base; and the 2025 audit-readiness diagnostic commissioned from the incumbent audit firm had identified 47 material controls gaps across data, governance and disclosure.

## Industry and Market Context

The European textile CSRD readiness landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2024–2025 Wave-1 CSRD filings across 1,100 listed European groups had surfaced a clear split: 36% of filings had attracted modified limited-assurance opinions, with the bulk of the modifications travelling through double-materiality scoping and Scope-3 data. Second, the 2023–2024 stakeholder-engagement literature had, across 84 EU corporate sustainability rebuilds, placed multi-stakeholder-panel envelopes at a disclosure-defensibility uplift of 16–24 points against internal-only envelopes. Third, the 2022–2024 audit-grade data-infrastructure literature had, across 56 CSRD readiness programmes, placed audit-grade platforms at an assurance-cost reduction of 28%–42% against spreadsheet-consolidation architectures.

Empirically, the post-2023 CSRD literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, programmes that had built their materiality matrix from an in-house value-chain diagnostic had sustained a Compliance Score above 65 in 84% of cases against 22% for programmes that had adopted a competitor matrix. Second, programmes that had pre-registered a reasonable-assurance pathway had reduced the 2028–2030 assurance-cost trajectory by 24%–32% against last-minute-limited postures.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the October 2025 audit-committee, approved the EUR 6.8M envelope and the March 31, 2027 filing calendar. The Sustainability Director had, on February 5, delivered the in-house value-chain diagnostic. The Head of Financial Reporting had, on February 19, delivered the materiality-to-management-accounts bridge. The Chief Legal Officer had, on March 4, delivered the ESRS-compliance matrix. The Chief Operations Officer had, on March 18, delivered the Scope-3 data-gap inventory.

### What Esteves knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. An Impact-First / Multi-Stakeholder / Quantified / Audit-Grade / Reasonable-Assurance-Ready Envelope projected Composite 72. A Competitor-Copy / Internal / Heatmap / Spreadsheet / Last-Minute Envelope projected Composite 26. A Hybrid Envelope projected Composite 54.

### What she did not know

Whether the 4,200-supplier Tier-2 data-gap would clear inside the twelve-month window; whether the independent facilitator of the multi-stakeholder panel would sustain the second engagement round through the 2026 European textile-labour debate; whether the audit-grade platform would integrate with the legacy manufacturing-execution system across the eleven sites inside the ten-month IT window; and whether the limited-assurance provider would accept the in-house monetised impact pathway without a material qualification.

### What others were saying

**The Sustainability Director**: *"Impact-first double materiality. A copied matrix is an invitation to a modified assurance opinion."*

**The Head of Financial Reporting**: *"Bridge the materiality topics to the management accounts. A sustainability report that does not reconcile is a governance problem, not a reporting problem."*

**The Chief Executive Officer**: *"Multi-stakeholder panel. If the board cannot look at the transcript, the disclosure is not defensible."*

**The Audit Partner**: *"Audit-grade platform. Every client that turned up with a spreadsheet received a modification."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Esteves had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Impact-First / Multi-Stakeholder / Quantified / Audit-Grade / Reasonable-Assurance-Ready:** the recommended envelope. Compliance Score 74. Data Quality 70. Stakeholder Credibility 68. Composite 72. Recommended.
- **Option B — Competitor-Copy / Internal / Heatmap / Spreadsheet / Last-Minute:** the status-quo envelope. Composite 26. Rejected — modified-assurance trigger.
- **Option C — Hybrid / Phased / Single-Domain / Phased / Phased:** the partial envelope. Composite 54. Rejected — stakeholder-credibility floor forfeited.

Esteves' private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 4 — the audit-grade data-infrastructure commitment — because it was the round that converted the other four rounds from narrative into assured evidence.
