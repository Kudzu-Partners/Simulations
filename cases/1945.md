# Vanta Play: The Breakthrough Pod

**Case Code:** ORG-2026-1945 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1945 — *Spark the Team* · **v1.0 · August 2026**

---

On Monday, 12 January 2026, Iona Brennan was handed a room, a budget line and a sentence. The room was an emptied meeting space on the second floor of Vanta Play's Ancoats building, with eight chairs in it. The budget line was GBP 2.4 million. The sentence was the whole of her brief: a category-defining home-audio product.

Thirty-four weeks later, on 4 September 2026, the doors of IFA Berlin would open and Vanta Play's stand would either carry the product its chief executive had promised the market or it would not. Twelve weeks from that Monday, at a stage-gate already in the diary, Brennan had to show the commercial sponsor a concept that was both new and useful, a team still willing to work on it, and a credible route to a working prototype. Short of that, the breakthrough brief would be withdrawn and the pod would spend the rest of the year preparing a fourth consecutive refresh.

## The Protagonist

Iona Brennan, 38, had been at Vanta Play for eleven years, the last four as principal design engineer on the home-audio line. She had shipped two of the company's three most recent products. She had never led a group larger than three people, had never held a budget, and had been chosen over two external candidates with innovation-lab experience.

## The Decision

Brennan had four commitments to record with the programme office in sequence, each closed before the next opened: who sat in the eight chairs; how she would lead the pod through the early weeks; how she would spend the weeks available before the engineering build had to begin; and what she would put in front of the sponsor at the week-12 gate.

## The Company and its Setting

Vanta Play Ltd, founded in 2014 and headquartered in Manchester, designed connected home-audio and smart-lighting products sold across Europe. It turned over GBP 78 million a year, employed 290 people, and earned a 9% operating margin in a category where one hit could double a year's profit and one miss could remove it.

Its last three launches had all shipped on their dates. All three were incremental refreshes of existing lines, and all three lost shelf share to competitors who had gone further. In the autumn of 2025 Cal Whitworth, chief executive since 2019, told the market that the next 18 months rested on a single breakthrough product, and named IFA Berlin as the place it would appear. Retailers had since allocated autumn shelf space against that promise. The buying cycle behind it was worth roughly GBP 22 million in first-year revenue, and a product that arrived late would not recover it.

## The Situation

The programme office opened four indices on Brennan's first day, each scored from 0 to 100 and reported every Friday to Neve Ashworth, the commercial director sponsoring the programme (see **Exhibit 1**). Idea Novelty — how new the concept was against what the category already sold — stood at 30. Idea Usefulness — how buildable it was and how much a buyer would pay for it — stood at 30. Team Motivation — how much of the pod's own drive was going into the work rather than into compliance with it — stood at 55. Prototype Readiness — how well the programme was tracking towards a working prototype by 4 September — stood at 40. On 12 January the first two indices described a brief with no concept behind it; the fourth described an engineering plan that had not begun.

Thirty-four weeks separated the pod's first day from the opening of IFA (see **Exhibit 2**). Engineering had costed the build at 18 uninterrupted weeks, which put the last date on which a concept could be locked and still built at week 16. Ashworth's estimate was that roughly ten of the early weeks were usable for front-end work before delivery risk turned severe, and she had put the stage-gate at week 12.

Two pieces of company history sat behind it. On the second of the three refreshes, weekly target reviews had run from January to launch; over that period two of Vanta Play's strongest designers went quiet, and one of them resigned in the spring. And of the fourteen names cleared for the pod, one carried an annotation in the file. Teo Vrancea, a senior industrial designer, had produced the two most-cited concepts in the company's internal review archive. He had also been the subject of two written requests from colleagues not to work with him again.

Nothing in the position improved on its own. Left alone, the brief stayed a sentence, the four indices stayed where they were, and the week-12 gate would arrive with no tested concept in the room. Ashworth had put in writing what she would do in that case: cancel the breakthrough brief and instruct the pod to prepare a refresh for the same date.

## What Brennan Controlled

Her first commitment was the eight seats. Vanta Play's shorthand divided the fourteen candidates into people who generated options and people who finished work. The pod could be six generators and two finishers, four of each, or two generators and six finishers. Separately, she had to decide whether Vrancea took one of the eight seats. And she had to say what she would do about the one capability the pod was short of, which engineering had identified as manufacturing-side finishing: spend GBP 300,000 to bring in an outside specialist immediately, stretch someone already in the pod into the gap at no extra cost, or leave the gap open and keep the money.

Her second commitment was how she would run the ideation weeks. The programme office recorded that as a single number between 0 and 100, where 0 meant a blank page and no constraints and 100 meant a locked brief with weekly targets, and Brennan had to name the number. Alongside it she had to say what the brief itself would contain: the sentence she had been given and nothing more; the user, the job the product had to do and the date, with the how left open; or the product, its features and its weekly targets specified before work began. And she had to set the reward: a GBP 10,000 cash bonus to whoever's concept was selected, recognition and autonomy and protected idea-time with credit shared across the pod, or nothing beyond salary.

Her third commitment was the calendar. She had to divide the ten front-end weeks between generating options, narrowing them down, and testing survivors with users, in whole weeks. She had to name the week the concept would be frozen and the build committed, which the programme calendar allowed anywhere from week 6 to week 16. And she had to say how many concepts went into testing: one, backed early; two or three held open until the testing separated them; or five or more carried to the end of the window.

Her fourth commitment was the gate. She had to choose what the pitch led on — the newness of the concept, its buildability and its value, or both together with the testing evidence behind them. She had to decide how much of the budget went into physical proof: nothing, and pitch on drawings and slides; GBP 400,000 for a rough functional mock-up; or GBP 800,000 for a working proof-of-concept and a costed build plan to IFA. She had to state on Ashworth's gate form, as a percentage, how firmly she was committing to deliver a prototype between week 12 and 4 September. And if Vrancea was one of the eight, she had to settle his position: he had told the pod he would not stay on a programme that carried a concept other than his own to the gate. She could give him the decision and keep him, accept his departure and lose the concept he had brought, or hold the concept choice to the pod's evidence and treat his position separately.

## Closing

On the Monday afternoon Brennan wrote the fourteen names on the whiteboard of the empty room and drew a line under the eighth. The gate was twelve weeks out. The doors in Berlin opened in thirty-four.
