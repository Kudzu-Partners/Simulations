## Abstract

BioTex Andina S.A.S. was, in the case universe, a vertically integrated Colombian Andean textile and apparel manufacturer — approximately USD 94m in 2025 revenue, 680 employees, two plants (a 12,400-square-metre Itagüí finishing plant and a 7,800-square-metre Envigado cut-make-trim facility), a cotton-sourcing partnership across six producer cooperatives in Antioquia and Tolima, a customer base composed of three large European apparel retailers (a Zara-style fast-fashion group representing 18% of revenue; a premium-casual German group at 11%; and a Scandinavian sustainability-oriented mid-tier group at 9%), plus domestic and regional customers. The firm had been acquired in late 2025 by a mid-market PE fund with an explicit ESG-transformation mandate: a positive MSCI-equivalent ESG rating, a 30% reduction in Scope-1-and-2-equivalent carbon footprint, sustained profitability, all within three years. The April 2026 three-round ESG exercise opened with the European fast-fashion retailer's Q2 Scope-3 supplier audit announcement (April 14) and closed with the Q4 investor-committee sustainability-report validation. The case opens at 4:14 p.m. on Thursday, April 23, 2026, at the firm's Medellín corporate office as **Mariana Escudero-Rueda**, a 44-year-old Chief Executive Officer, prepares the three-round commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Read the European CSRD double-materiality matrix — financial materiality on the x-axis (how much does the issue affect the business) and impact materiality on the y-axis (how much does the business affect people and planet) — select five priority issues from twelve candidates (cotton sourcing and water use, chemical discharge, factory safety, gender pay gap, supplier labour conditions, community investment, Scope 3 upstream, Scope 3 downstream, board independence, living wage, waste recyclability, circular-design packaging), and articulate why cosmetic-issue plans move nothing and structurally-material-issue plans move revenue-exposure and cost-exposure simultaneously.
2. Decompose BioTex's estimated 14,410-ton CO2-equivalent annual footprint across Scope 1 (direct emissions, 8.6% of total), Scope 2 (purchased electricity, 6.2%), Scope 3 upstream (cotton farming plus chemical manufacturing, 61.9%) and Scope 3 downstream (distribution and garment end-of-life, 23.3%), and articulate why planting trees at the factory moves the needle by less than one percentage point and why cotton-sourcing re-engineering is the single largest 30%-target lever.
3. Apply the greenwashing-detection discipline — flag "carbon neutral by 2030" without operational plan; flag "100% sustainable" without certification or metric; pass specific-measurable-independently-verified commitments — against three draft commitments the marketing team has pre-circulated, and articulate why specificity is the distinguishing feature between sustainability and greenwashing and why investor committees in 2026 read the distinction.
4. Design the Round 2 response to the European-retailer Scope-3 supplier audit in which 30% of BioTex's cooperative suppliers fail on labour or environmental criteria — drop them and lose 18% of revenue; support them to improve and absorb the cost; refuse the audit and lose the contract entirely — and articulate why this dilemma is structural to the LATAM manufacturer entering global value chains and why the decision reads differently under a three-year PE mandate than under an owner-operator horizon.
5. Commit the Round 3 investor-committee sustainability report against the three criteria — materiality (did BioTex address the right issues); credibility (specific, measurable, verified commitments with operational data); financial coherence (the business remained viable through the transition) — and articulate why this ESG-management frame is distinct from Verdant ESG Boardroom (sim 001), Vertice ESG Boardroom (sim 101), Capstone GLOBAL ESG (sim 084), Pacifica ESGCost (sim 166) and IberianTextiles DoubleMatrix (sim 162), and why the PE-owned LATAM-manufacturer ESG-transition frame 299 occupies is deliberately distinct.

On the afternoon of **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, **Mariana Escudero-Rueda** arrived at BioTex Andina's El Poblado corporate office in Medellín at 4:14 p.m., walked past the double-height reception space with the cotton-cooperative photography wall, opened the EmpresaQueMundoNecesita workbook her sustainability director had finalised Tuesday evening, and re-read the sentence the Scandinavian retailer's sustainability lead had spoken on the Q1 quarterly call: *"We don't ask you to be perfect. We ask you to be specific, measurable, and verified. The rest is greenwashing."*

---

## The Protagonist

Escudero-Rueda, 44, had been Chief Executive Officer since the PE fund's December 2025 acquisition, had completed an Ingeniería Industrial degree at EAFIT in Medellín in 2003 and an MBA at London Business School in 2010, had spent twelve years across two European apparel groups (four years at H&M Stockholm in sourcing roles and eight years at a Barcelona-based premium-casual group in supply-chain and sustainability leadership), and had returned to Medellín in 2022 to join BioTex Andina as Chief Operating Officer before her elevation to CEO at the PE close. Her sustainability instincts, her PE-chair had noted in the January 2026 onboarding memo, had been shaped by three teachings: the 2014 Rana Plaza post-collapse sourcing revolution she had watched from inside the European retailer; a two-year stretch inside the Barcelona group's 2019–2020 CSRD-preparatory program during which she had internalised the double-materiality discipline; and an eighteen-month doctoral-research exposure to the 2018 LBS climate-finance literature she had completed as an executive-fellow. The PE fund's investment committee had assigned her the April three-round exercise on three considerations: her command of the Scope-3 decomposition; her willingness to hold a supplier-audit posture against the European retailer's timeline; and her comfort presenting a credible sustainability report to the Q4 investor committee that refused both greenwashing and under-promising.

---

## The Decision

Escudero-Rueda had to commit, by the 6:00 p.m. PE-chair check-in call, the three-round ESG package.

1. **Round 1 — Double Materiality, Carbon Footprint, Greenwashing Discipline.** Twelve candidate issues; select five priorities against the double-materiality matrix. Canonical expectation: cotton-sourcing-water, Scope 3 upstream (cotton and chemicals), chemical discharge, supplier labour conditions, living wage. Decompose the 14,410-ton footprint and commit a 30%-by-three-years reduction plan that must include cotton-sourcing re-engineering. Apply the greenwashing test to three draft marketing commitments.
2. **Round 2 — European Retailer Scope-3 Supplier Audit.** Q2 audit of six cooperative suppliers; two (approximately 30% of supplier volume and 18% of revenue flow) fail on labour and environmental criteria. Three response options: drop the two failing cooperatives (lose six months of volume with the fast-fashion retailer); support them to improve through a cost-absorbing capability-uplift program; refuse the audit and lose the contract entirely.
3. **Round 3 — Investor-Committee Sustainability Report.** Q4 presentation to the PE fund's three-member investor committee on three criteria. Materiality: did the plan address the right issues. Credibility: specific, measurable, independently verified commitments with operational data. Financial coherence: did the business remain viable through the transition.

Commitments: the five priority issues; the 30%-carbon-reduction plan with cotton-sourcing as the largest lever; the greenwashing-disciplined commitment set; the Round 2 supplier-audit response; the Round 3 report package; and a one-page PE-chair memo that frames the three-round commitment as a single transition-arc rather than as three loosely-coupled exercises.

## Sectoral Background

The 2020–2025 LATAM vertically-integrated textile-and-apparel category had, in the case universe, compressed revenue by approximately 18% in real terms over the five-year window, concentrated in three operator tiers (large maquila exporters; vertically-integrated mid-cap manufacturers like BioTex Andina and two Peruvian peers; small specialty contractors). The Colombian Andean cotton-cooperative panel (approximately 140 cooperatives across Antioquia, Tolima, Cesar and Bolívar) had undergone a 2022–2025 re-composition driven by climate-shock losses (2023 and 2024 El Niño events) and by the post-2021 European retailer Scope-3 audit wave that had raised the regulatory-and-commercial floor on labour and environmental criteria. The 2025 LATAM-manufacturer ESG-rating panel registered an average MSCI-equivalent rating of BB on a AAA-to-CCC scale, an average Scope-3-upstream-share-of-total-emissions of 58%, and an average supplier-audit-pass-rate of 68%, with significant variance across manufacturers driven primarily by cotton-sourcing-partnership depth rather than by factory-level energy efficiency.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2002–2025 ESG literature produced three findings the simulation had to respect. First, the European CSRD double-materiality framework, operationalised as a five-issue priority matrix rather than as a comprehensive issue-list, produced documented plan-effectiveness readings approximately 2.2× those of breadth-first regimes on comparable mid-cap manufacturer panels. Second, the Greenhouse Gas Protocol's Scope 1-2-3 decomposition, applied to vertically-integrated textile manufacturers, consistently identified Scope-3-upstream (cotton and chemical inputs) as the dominant source of footprint — between 55% and 72% of total — and established that factory-level Scope-1-and-2 efficiency programs could not hit a 30%-total-reduction target without sourcing re-engineering. Third, the post-2019 greenwashing-detection literature, traceable to the EU Green Claims Directive consultation and subsequent enforcement actions, had raised the evidentiary bar on voluntary sustainability claims to specific-measurable-independently-verified commitments backed by operational data rather than by marketing language.

The post-2020 PE-ESG literature added two findings. The three-year transition horizon common to mid-market PE transformation theses produced documented sustainability-rating-improvement readings approximately 1.6× those of five-year transition horizons because the concentrated budget and attention compressed the transition; but this also increased the risk that PE-owned operators greenwashed to hit an artificial milestone. And the investor-committee three-criteria rubric (materiality, credibility, financial coherence) had become the de facto LATAM-PE-ESG-report standard in the 2023–2025 reporting cycle.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The December 2025 PE acquisition had set the three-year ESG mandate; the January 2026 investor committee had accepted a two-page initial ESG outline; the March 2026 Scandinavian retailer's sustainability-lead Q1 call had raised the greenwashing threshold explicitly; the April 14 European fast-fashion retailer's Q2 Scope-3 supplier-audit announcement had landed with a 90-day completion window; the April 23 commit was the PE-chair's first-full-cycle check-in. The PE-chair's April 20 email: *"El plan tiene que ser específico, medible y verificable. El compromiso tiene que sostenerse en los estados financieros. No fallemos ninguna de las dos."*

### What Escudero-Rueda knew

The twelve candidate materiality issues and the correct priority set; the carbon-footprint decomposition and the cotton-sourcing lever; the greenwashing-test standard; the two cooperative suppliers that would fail the Q2 audit and the approximate capability-uplift cost; the investor-committee three-criteria rubric; and the PE-chair's appetite for a specific plan rather than a narrative plan.

### What she did not know

Whether the two failing cooperatives could execute the capability-uplift program within the 90-day audit window or would require a three-to-six-month extension; whether the European fast-fashion retailer would accept a capability-uplift supported response in lieu of a supplier drop; whether the Scope-3-upstream reduction estimate (the cotton-sourcing lever) would hold as estimated or would compress in the presence of the 2026 El Niño probability; whether the Medellín factory-level Scope-1-and-2 efficiency projects could be delivered at budget and on time; and whether the Q4 investor committee would accept a report that showed EBITDA compression of 180–320 basis points during the transition as financially coherent or as a red flag.

### What others were saying

**The PE Fund Chair**: *"Específico, medible, verificable. Financieramente coherente. No cedo en ninguna."*

**The Sustainability Director (internal, recently hired from a European apparel group)**: *"El algodón es el lever del 30%. No hay plan sin re-ingeniería del sourcing."*

**The Head of Commercial**: *"El 18% de revenue del fast-fashion retailer es nuestra oxigenación. Perderlo por no aguantar el audit es inaceptable."*

**The European Retailer's Sustainability Lead (fast-fashion group)**: *"Acepto un plan de capability-uplift siempre que las dos cooperativas firmen un plan específico en 30 días y delivery en 90."*

**The Cotton Cooperative President (Antioquia, failing cooperative)**: *"Si BioTex nos apoya, podemos cumplir. Si nos sacan, se quiebran 62 familias."*

**The CFO**: *"La uplift cuesta USD 1.8m en seis meses. El drop cuesta USD 17m en revenue perdido. Numéricamente, el uplift es el camino."*

## Options Under Consideration

Escudero-Rueda had narrowed the ESG space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — Five priority issues with cotton-sourcing as the largest lever + greenwashing-disciplined commitment set + supplier-uplift Round-2 response + credible Round-3 investor report (Recommended).** Double-materiality respected; Scope-3 upstream addressed structurally; specific-measurable-verified commitments across the three rounds; supplier-uplift preserves the 18% revenue flow while meeting the retailer's audit standard; investor committee reads the plan as material, credible and financially coherent with a 180–220bp EBITDA compression during the transition.
- **Option B — Broad-issue plan covering all twelve issues at shallow depth + match the greenwashing language of the 2021–2023 LATAM peer panel + drop the two failing cooperatives + optimistic investor report.** Rejected — the breadth-first plan fails the materiality test; the greenwashing language fails the Q4 investor committee's credibility test; the supplier drop loses 18% of revenue on a PE mandate that cannot absorb the hit.
- **Option C — Five priority issues with factory-level Scope-1-and-2 as the primary lever + refuse the supplier audit + hold the investor report until Q2 2027.** Rejected — the factory-level lever cannot hit 30% (mathematically bounded at approximately 14.8%); refusing the audit loses the fast-fashion retailer entirely; holding the report to Q2 2027 misses the PE fund's committed three-year timeline.

Escudero-Rueda's private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the single decision most correlated with the three-year transition outcome was the Round 2 supplier-uplift response, because the European retailer's audit was the single forcing function that would compress the cotton-sourcing re-engineering timeline, cascade into the 30% Scope-3 reduction lever, and feed the Round 3 investor committee's credibility and financial-coherence readings simultaneously. The *Empresa que el Mundo Necesita* frame sits at the PE-owned LATAM-manufacturer ESG-transition tier in the 2026 Express catalogue, distinct from Verdant ESG Boardroom (sim 001, general ESG-boardroom frame), Vertice ESG Boardroom (sim 101, regulatory-focused ESG-boardroom), Capstone GLOBAL ESG (sim 084, global-policy capstone), Pacifica ESGCost (sim 166, ESG-as-cost-discipline for financial analysts) and IberianTextiles DoubleMatrix (sim 162, specifically European-textile DoubleMatrix frame). Sim 299 emphasises the three-round LATAM-manufacturer PE-mandate transition; the other sims emphasise their distinct scope, sector or role.
