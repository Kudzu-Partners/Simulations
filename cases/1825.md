# Greenfield Custom Programs: The Helvetia Academy Brief

**Case Code:** OPS-2026-1825 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1825 — *Tailored Fit* · **v1.0 · September 2026**

---

On the morning of 12 October 2026, Helvetia Industrial Group signed a brief committing Greenfield Custom Programs to develop 600 high-potential managers across five regions — Europe, North America, Latin America, the Middle East and Asia-Pacific — on a single leadership journey that had to feel, in the client's own words, locally built everywhere. Renata Voss, Greenfield's managing director, had twelve months to deliver the program and six months to get every region live, for a fixed price of US$4.5 million and against a 25 percent gross-margin floor the school's dean had just made non-negotiable.

The previous year, four similarly ambitious engagements had gone almost entirely bespoke. Clients loved them, scoring them 74 on the loyalty index the school called NPS, and the unit closed the year at a 9 percent margin. The marquee faculty Helvetia most wanted by name were already booked above 80 percent of safe capacity, and two had privately warned Voss they would step back if it climbed further. Before designing a single session, Voss had to decide what the brief actually required.

## The Protagonist

Renata Voss, 52, had led Greenfield Custom Programs since 2021, after seven years running its client-engagement practice. She joined the school in 2012 from a boutique talent-strategy consultancy and built her reputation on one discipline: never letting a client's enthusiasm write a check the faculty roster could not cash. Greenfield's business-development team had chased the Helvetia account for two years before it closed; what Greenfield would actually deliver was Voss's call alone. Her compensation, like every program director's, moved with the unit's gross margin and with client renewal, not with the size of the contract she had just signed.

## The Decision

The first decision was how to answer the brief. Voss could commit to five fully bespoke regional programs, promising a from-scratch design in every market to secure the relationship immediately. She could take the brief apart first — separating what was genuinely Helvetia-specific from content Greenfield already owned and testing which regional requests reflected real difference, not habit. Or she could offer all five regions Greenfield's strongest existing program with no new build, trading regional distinctiveness for cost and speed. Bound to it was a reading of the regional HR heads' demands: language, regulation and local case examples as the regions' only substantive differences, built onto a shared core; every regional request treated as equally valid, producing five separate designs; or all of it folded into one identical design worldwide. Separately, she set how rigorously the brief's economics were tested against the dean's 25 percent floor — from a light check to a full renegotiation of unfunded lines.

The second decision was how to classify the content. Every module had to fall into one of three categories that, however split, had to add up to the whole design: material reused as-is from Greenfield's library; material rebuilt around Helvetia's own cases and data without being built from scratch; or material built entirely new, at roughly US$90,000 a module (Exhibit 2). Layered onto it was a structural choice — one common journey with a thin local layer per region, a minimal core with a heavy custom build everywhere, or one global design with no regional layer at all — plus where any new material was concentrated: on the one or two modules carrying Helvetia's transformation strategy, spread thinly across every module in every region, or applied to the generic leadership fundamentals underneath the journey.

## The Company and its Setting

Greenfield Custom Programs was the bespoke executive-education arm of a business school in Ann Arbor, Michigan: 110 faculty, roughly US$52 million a year in revenue, most of it earned designing programs to a single client's specification rather than selling seats in an open classroom. Two facts governed nearly every design choice. First, the unit was measured against a 32 percent gross-margin target, and the dean had just told every program director that no new engagement could close below 25 percent that year — a floor, not an aspiration. Second, the faculty clients most wanted by name were already booked above 80 percent of teaching capacity, with attrition risk above 85 percent, and two had told Voss privately they would step back if it climbed further. Helvetia Industrial Group, a US$14 billion Swiss manufacturer, had chosen Greenfield to design and deliver one leadership journey for 600 high-potential managers across five regions, and wanted every region to feel built for it alone.

## The Situation

Helvetia's price was fixed at US$4.5 million for 600 managers — about US$7,500 a head, generous for an off-the-shelf program and tight for five separately built regional ones (Exhibit 1). The engagement opened against four measures at once. Client satisfaction started at 35 on the same NPS scale that had given last year's flagships a 74, against a target of 65. Gross margin started at 9 percent — precisely where last year's flagships had ended — against the dean's new 25 percent floor and the unit's usual 32 percent target. Marquee faculty utilization started at 80 percent, with 85 percent marking the point past which the bench counted as burned. On-time delivery, an index tracking whether all five regions would launch together, started at 50 out of 100, against a requirement that every region be live within six months. Helvetia's CHRO had framed this first year as the opening chapter of a three-year, US$13 million academy, with renewal following directly from how Year 1 scored on all four measures together. If Voss built what each region separately asked for, the previous year's arithmetic would repeat almost exactly. If she stripped the design down to protect margin, Helvetia would notice five regions had received the same course wearing five different covers.

## What Voss Controlled

Once the design was set, Voss had to turn it into a twelve-month rollout without breaking the faculty roster or the margin. She could launch one region first and carry what she learned into the other four; launch all five in the same month to move as fast as Helvetia's timeline allowed; or launch in two paired waves followed by a fifth. She had exactly 70 faculty-days to divide across three kinds of teaching capacity, which had to add up to the whole allotment: marquee professors Helvetia had asked for by name, at roughly US$24,000 a day; regional faculty who could deliver credibly inside their own market, at roughly US$9,500 a day; and a train-the-facilitator track that built local teaching capacity instead of flying it in. She also set how aggressively reuse savings from configured modules were built into the margin math, from a light assumption to an aggressive one.

Before presenting to Helvetia's CHRO, Voss had to lock a scorecard spanning client satisfaction, gross margin, faculty utilization and on-time delivery: targets across all four at once; targets that pushed satisfaction as far as it would go regardless of cost elsewhere; or targets that protected margin above everything, even if the client experience narrowed. She also had to decide how to frame the pitch for the academy itself — as proof Year 1 had cleared all four measures at once; as a promise of still more customization ahead to deepen Helvetia's attachment; or as a price discount to secure the three-year deal early. Partway through, the CHRO was expected to offer an additional US$1 million to make every region fully bespoke — money Voss could decline outright to protect a bench that could not absorb more work, accept in full and stretch the marquee faculty further, or accept only to deepen the configured layer rather than fund full bespoke work. Whatever she recommended, she had to decide how firmly to argue that a balanced design beat a win on any single number.

## Closing

By the end of that week, Voss had the signed brief, a blank module map with five regional columns and one empty row for the shared core, and a calendar with the first region's launch six months out. The dean's 25 percent floor was fixed. The marquee faculty's patience was not. Helvetia's CHRO expected a qualified brief and an early economic read before Voss designed a single session.
