## Abstract

Meridian Capital Partners was, in the case universe, a USD 1.4 billion long-short equity fund with a five-year net annualized return of 11.2%, a gross exposure range of 120%–160%, and a research team of eighteen analysts organised into four sector pods. On Monday, March 2, 2026, Andreas Vidal, a 34-year-old former investment-banking associate who had joined the fund in May 2023 as a senior analyst on the consumer-and-healthcare pod, had opened a four-round forensic audit across four investment candidates — Vantara Retail (a USD 4.2B regional retailer), CrestBridge Tech (a USD 7.8B regional enterprise-software platform), Harmon Consolidated (a USD 3.1B industrial-distribution business), and Novela Pharma (a USD 9.6B LATAM-and-Indian branded-generics pharmaceutical) — inside a 95-analyst-hour envelope against a nine-day fund-risk committee deadline. A potential catalyst event sat inside the envelope — Novela's announced Q2 2026 secondary offering at a 22.8x reported EV/EBITDA — a transaction that, if accounting manipulation were identified before the offering, would deliver a USD 60M avoided-loss on the fund's underweighted position and, under participation with conditions, a USD 80M–USD 120M swing on the adjusted EV/EBITDA of 48.3x. The case opens at 6:42 a.m. on Tuesday, March 3, 2026, at the fund's office in the capital, as Vidal prepares the Round-1 screening commitment that will anchor the full four-round envelope.

Cross-referencing finds the truth."* Nine days separated him from the March 12 risk-committee deadline.

## The Protagonist

Andreas Vidal, 34, had joined Meridian in May 2023 from a global investment bank's M&A advisory group, where he had, across six years, run diligence on forty-two LATAM and cross-border consumer-and-healthcare transactions with a combined enterprise value above USD 28 billion. Before that, he had been a credit analyst at a regional commercial bank for two years. The Head of Research had recruited him specifically on the strength of two capabilities: his experience running Beneish-M-Score and accrual-ratio screens inside an M&A-diligence window, and his reputation for refusing to convert a screening signal into a short-position recommendation without cross-referencing management-interview responses against the documentary evidence. His fund-level long-term incentive plan was tied 60% to the fund's five-year annualised return and 20% to the detection-accuracy and calibration score of his individual coverage.

---

## The Decision

Vidal had to commit, by the March 12 risk-committee deadline, the four-round envelope:

1. **Round 1 — Screening Approach (40 analyst hours)** — whether to commit Round 1 to a Beneish-M-Score screen across all four candidates against an accrual-ratio screen, a working-capital-trends screen, or a segment-reporting screen. The Beneish-M-Score integrated eight components — DSRI (days sales in receivables index), GMI (gross margin index), AQI (asset quality index), SGI (sales growth index), DEPI (depreciation index), SGAI (SGA expenses index), LVGI (leverage index), and TATA (total accruals to total assets) — and produced a single composite score; scores above 2.22 were the manipulation-territory threshold.
2. **Round 2 — Deep-Dive Focus (35 analyst hours)** — whether to commit Round 2 to a related-party-and-off-balance-sheet focus against a revenue-recognition focus, a inventory-and-cost-of-sales focus, or a segment-margin-diagnostics focus.
3. **Round 3 — Management-Interview Question (20 analyst hours)** — whether to commit Round 3 to a receivables-growth-vs-revenue-growth question against a gross-margin-sustainability question, a working-capital-policy question, or a segment-margin-attribution question.
4. **Round 4 — Novela Secondary-Offering Participation** — whether to commit Round 4 to a decline position, a participate-with-condition position (conditional on a receivables-reconciliation disclosure and a related-party-documentation annex), or a participate-fully position.

The four rounds would compound into a single investment recommendation. On Vidal's working model, a Beneish-M-Score screen plus related-party deep-dive plus receivables-growth interview plus decline envelope delivered a detection-accuracy score of 87, a calibration score of 21 (positive), and a fund-level avoided loss of USD 60M on Novela's secondary offering. A baseline envelope (accrual-ratio screen plus revenue-recognition deep-dive plus margin-sustainability interview plus participate-fully) delivered a detection-accuracy score of 48, a calibration score of -12 (negative), and a fund-level loss of USD 42M on Novela's secondary offering.

## Fund Background

Meridian had been founded in 2012 by three former equity-research analysts from a global long-only manager and had, across fourteen years, compounded a net annualised return of 11.2% against the regional MSCI benchmark's 8.4%. The fund's research process was anchored on a three-gate investment-committee mechanism — initial screening, fundamental-fit approval, and position-sizing — and a four-stage forensic-audit protocol that had been codified in the 2019 research-process review. The four-round envelope in the Novela-secondary-offering window sat inside the forensic-audit protocol's third application of the 2025 calendar year.

The Q1 2026 research-committee calendar had identified three specific catalysts the four-round envelope would evaluate. First, Novela Pharma's announced Q2 2026 secondary offering at a 22.8x reported EV/EBITDA placed the Round-4 decision inside a time-boxed window that required a March 12 risk-committee commitment. Second, the Q4 2025 sell-side consensus on Novela had placed a 58% buy rating against a 26% hold and a 16% sell, a distribution that made the fund's decline-plus-short-overlay position a differentiated view. Third, the January 2026 SEC comment-letter docket had, on a peer issuer in Novela's sector and in the same Indian-subsidiary structure, opened a revenue-recognition investigation that regional analysts placed at 60%–75% probability of spilling over into Novela's audit cycle inside the 2026 calendar.

## Industry and Market Context

The forensic-accounting and earnings-quality literature in 2026 was anchored by three structural findings the envelope sat inside. First, the 1999–2024 Beneish-M-Score evidence base had, across a large cross-country sample of manipulation cases, documented a detection rate of 76% at the 2.22 threshold against a 31% false-positive rate, a profile that made the M-Score a dominant screening tool against accrual-ratio, working-capital-trends, and segment-reporting screens across any 40-hour screening envelope. Second, the 2005–2024 related-party-and-off-balance-sheet evidence base had, across a commercial summary of 58 known manipulation cases, documented a related-party-channel share of 48% of cases — a pattern that anchored the Round-2 deep-dive focus. Third, the 2010–2024 management-interview evidence base had, across a sample of 34 disclosed forensic audits, placed the receivables-growth-vs-revenue-growth question at the highest evasive-response-rate among the four candidate questions, a pattern that calibrated the Round-3 question selection.

Empirically, the post-2000 forensic-investing literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, long-short funds that had sequenced Beneish-M-Score screens ahead of accrual-ratio screens had, across a 2015–2024 sample of twenty-two forensic long-short cases, closed a detection-accuracy score that was 2.4x higher than funds that had sequenced accrual-ratio first. Second, long-short funds that had committed to a decline-or-participate-with-condition position on a secondary offering at an adjusted-EV/EBITDA delta above 2.0x had, across the same sample, delivered an avoided-loss-to-participation-upside ratio above 4.5x.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Head of Research had, in the November 2025 analyst offsite, approved a four-round forensic-audit protocol with a 95-analyst-hour envelope, a Beneish-M-Score screen for Round 1, a deep-dive focus discretion for Round 2, a management-interview question discretion for Round 3, and a risk-committee-vote structure for Round 4. The Senior Risk Officer had, on February 18, argued that the Novela secondary-offering catalyst placed the envelope inside a time-boxed window. The Sector-Pod Lead (consumer and healthcare) had, on February 22, signed off on the four-candidate list conditional on the sector-pod coverage rationale. The Portfolio Manager had, on February 28, argued for a decline position on Novela conditional on the Round-2 evidence threshold.

### What Vidal knew

The team had modelled three four-round envelopes. A Beneish-plus-Related-Party-plus-Receivables-plus-Decline Envelope projected a detection accuracy of 87, a calibration of 21, and an avoided loss of USD 60M. A Baseline Envelope projected a detection accuracy of 48, a calibration of -12, and a loss of USD 42M. A Participate-With-Condition Envelope projected a detection accuracy of 87 and an upside capture of USD 80M–USD 120M conditional on the reconciliation-disclosure covenant holding through the offering close.

### What he did not know

Whether the Novela secondary offering would close inside the announced Q2 2026 window or be pulled on an audit-cycle issue; whether the Round-2 related-party deep-dive would clear the documentary-evidence threshold on the Indian-subsidiary round-tripping pattern; whether the Round-3 management-interview question would produce the evasive response at the modelled probability or a defensive response; and whether the risk committee would accept a decline-and-short-overlay position or push for a decline-only position.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Research**: *"Beneish Round 1. M-Score dominates any other screen at this time envelope."*

**The Senior Risk Officer**: *"The secondary offering is a time-boxed catalyst. The envelope closes March 12 or the fund takes no position."*

**The Sector-Pod Lead**: *"Novela deep-dive Round 2. The Indian-subsidiary structure is the right focus."*

**The Portfolio Manager**: *"Decline Novela, with a short overlay conditional on the Round-2 evidence threshold."*

## Options Under Consideration

Vidal had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Beneish-plus-Related-Party-plus-Receivables-plus-Decline:** Round 1 Beneish-M-Score across all four candidates (40 hrs); Round 2 related-party-and-off-balance-sheet deep-dive on Novela (35 hrs); Round 3 receivables-growth-vs-revenue-growth management-interview question with Novela's CFO (20 hrs); Round 4 decline position on Novela secondary offering with short overlay conditional on Round-2 evidence threshold. Detection accuracy 87. Calibration 21. Avoided loss USD 60M. Aligns with Head of Research, Senior Risk Officer, Portfolio Manager. Recommended.

- **Option B — Baseline:** Round 1 accrual-ratio screen; Round 2 revenue-recognition deep-dive; Round 3 margin-sustainability interview; Round 4 participate-fully. Detection accuracy 48. Calibration -12. Loss USD 42M. Rejected.

- **Option C — Participate-With-Condition:** Round 1 Beneish; Round 2 related-party deep-dive; Round 3 receivables-growth question; Round 4 participate-with-condition (reconciliation-disclosure covenant and related-party-documentation annex). Detection accuracy 87. Upside capture USD 80M–USD 120M conditional on covenant holding through close. Rejected on covenant-enforcement uncertainty.

Vidal's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the fund-level avoided loss was Round 2 — the related-party-and-off-balance-sheet deep-dive that cross-referenced the Round-1 Beneish screen against the Indian-subsidiary round-tripping pattern and that supplied the documentary evidence threshold the risk committee required for a decline-plus-short-overlay position.
