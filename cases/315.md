## Abstract

Cartón del Plata S.A. was, in the case universe, a 38-year-old Avellaneda-headquartered corrugated-packaging manufacturer founded in 1988 by the Lombardi family serving FMCG retail, industrial and e-commerce B2B clients across the Buenos Aires metropolitan area — 62,000 tonnes of corrugated output per year, approximately ARS 42bn 2025 revenue, 420 employees, a production technology-base dating from 1984 that relies on fluorocarbon-barrier coating for wet-strength boxes and solvent-based ink that collectively contaminate the fiber-recovery stream, a newly-enacted Argentine Extended-Producer-Responsibility law mandating 35% recovery by 2027, a Cencosud-and-DIA transition-plan request for 2025 Scope-3-reporting purposes, and a USD 8m VC joint-venture offer from Montevideo-based CircularLab Ventures conditional on a credible circular business model. On **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 8:16 a.m., **Ing. Sofía Lombardi-Giménez**, a 41-year-old second-generation family-member and incoming Sustainability and Innovation Director arriving on her one-hundred-twentieth day in role, opened the Ellen-MacArthur-Foundation butterfly-diagram mapping her circular-economy analyst had finalised at 11:38 p.m. the previous evening and sat down to commit the four-round package.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Apply the Ellen-MacArthur-Foundation 2012 butterfly-diagram framework to Cartón del Plata's material flows, map the cardboard-as-technical-cycle and the fluorocarbon-coating-and-solvent-ink as fiber-stream contaminants, and articulate why design decisions made in 1984 are what make the 2025 product uncircular.
2. Apply the EU-directive-2008 waste-hierarchy pyramid (Prevent / Reduce / Reuse / Recycle / Recover / Dispose) to the current 22%-landfill / 68%-fiber-recovery mix, quantify the prevention opportunity (23% of boxes oversized), the reduction opportunity (120→105 g/m² lightweighting), the reuse opportunity (B2B returnable industrial packaging with 4–6 cycles per functional unit), and articulate why higher-hierarchy interventions dominate recycling.
3. Apply Frosch-Gallopoulos 1989 and Chertow 2000 industrial-symbiosis frameworks to the four-stream Avellaneda-plant cluster (wastewater-to-biogas / offcuts-to-artisan-paper / steam-to-food-processor / spent-coatings-to-cement-kiln), quantify the ARS 4.5m revenue against the ARS 1.8m cost for ARS 6.3m net value creation, and articulate why Kalundborg-pattern symbiosis converts waste streams into feedstock streams.
4. Apply Walls's 2006 Extended-Producer-Responsibility framework to the three compliance options (PRO-membership at ARS 3.4m/year, own-take-back-center at ARS 12m capex with 24% IRR, product-redesign with eco-modulation-fee reduction and premium segment), apply ISO 14040 LCA to the standard-versus-circular-design comparison (−41% CO₂e, −39% water, −89% hazardous waste, +110% recyclability), and articulate why the quantified environmental impact is the commercial asset that opens the Cencosud Scope-3 procurement relationship.
5. Commit the Round 4 platform-business-model archetype selection (Product-as-a-Service / Performance-Economy / Resource-Recovery / Collaborative-Consumption), commit the Returnable-Industrial-Packaging product-as-a-service platform for the B2B segment, commit the USD 8m VC joint-venture sign-off, and articulate why the circular-economy frame sim 315 occupies is distinct from Lumena Electronics (sim 013), ReCicla (sim 178) and TerraVida (sim 152) — 315 emphasises the LATAM-corrugated-packaging-EPR-and-VC-joint-venture synthesis with the Returnable-Industrial-Packaging platform commitment.

On the morning of **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, at 8:16 a.m., **Ing. Sofía Lombardi-Giménez** arrived at the Cartón del Plata plant in Avellaneda on her one-hundred-twentieth day in the Sustainability-and-Innovation-Director role, greeted the two overnight-shift maintenance technicians, and walked into the Innovation office past the wall of six framed photographs (the 1988 Lombardi-family founding; the 1994 first corrugator line; the 2001 crisis-era restructuring; the 2010 FSC certification; the 2019 first-ISO-14001 audit; the 2025 Argentine-EPR-law publication). On the desk sat the 62-page butterfly-diagram mapping her circular-economy analyst Tomás Acuña had finalised at 11:38 p.m. The CircularLab Ventures term sheet was next to it, its 4:00 p.m. signing deadline on the same desk calendar. The 180-day EPR-transition-plan commitment window was on day 120 of 180.

---

## The Protagonist

Lombardi-Giménez, 41, had been Sustainability and Innovation Director at Cartón del Plata since December 24, 2025 — one hundred twenty days into role at the case opening — had completed an Ingeniería Industrial at the Universidad de Buenos Aires in 2007 and an MSc in Circular Economy at the Cranfield University in 2013, had spent six years at a European packaging-materials multinational in Bologna between 2014 and 2019, had led the circular-economy portfolio of a LATAM consumer-goods company between 2020 and 2024, and had returned to Avellaneda in 2025 as heir-apparent-on-sustainability of the Lombardi family firm. Her circular-economy instincts had been shaped by three formative exposures: the 2013 Cranfield Ellen-MacArthur-Foundation curriculum; the 2017 Bologna industrial-symbiosis project where a four-stream Kalundborg-pattern cluster had produced +6.3x the projected net value creation; and the 2023 LATAM-consumer-goods product-redesign where an eco-modulation-fee reduction had opened a premium Scope-3 procurement segment at 14% price premium.

---

## The Decision

Lombardi-Giménez had to commit, by the 4:00 p.m. CircularLab-Ventures-and-Lombardi-family-council meeting, the four-round package.

1. **Round 1 — EMF butterfly and waste hierarchy.** Accept the butterfly-diagram mapping. Commit the fluorocarbon-to-starch-based-barrier redesign and the solvent-to-water-based-ink migration. Commit the waste-hierarchy targets (prevention-sized boxes to 95%, lightweighting 120→105 g/m², B2B reuse platform).
2. **Round 2 — Industrial symbiosis and EPR.** Commit the four-stream Kalundborg-pattern cluster (wastewater-to-biogas, offcuts-to-artisan-paper, steam-to-food-processor, spent-coatings-to-cement-kiln). Commit the product-redesign EPR-compliance posture for eco-modulation-fee reduction and premium-segment access.
3. **Round 3 — LCA and Scope-3 commercial asset.** Commit the ISO 14040 LCA comparison. Package the −41%-CO₂e / −39%-water / −89%-hazardous-waste / +110%-recyclability reading as a Scope-3 commercial asset for Cencosud and DIA.
4. **Round 4 — Platform archetype and VC joint venture.** Commit the Returnable-Industrial-Packaging product-as-a-service platform for the B2B segment. Commit the USD 8m CircularLab Ventures joint-venture sign-off at 4:00 p.m. subject to the four-round commitment.

Commitments: the Round 1 EMF-and-waste-hierarchy redesign; the Round 2 symbiosis-and-EPR compliance commitment; the Round 3 LCA-and-Scope-3 commercial commitment; the Round 4 RIP-platform-and-VC joint-venture commitment; and a one-page board-and-VC memo that frames the four-round commitment as a single 180-day EPR-transition and 36-month RIP-platform arc.

## Sectoral Background

The 2010–2025 Latin American corrugated-packaging category had, in the case universe, registered approximately USD 14bn in aggregate annual revenue across the 340 CACIA-registered manufacturers, concentrated in three operator tiers (multinational manufacturers like Smurfit Westrock and International Paper; regional independents like Cartón del Plata and Papelera del Plata; smaller specialty converters). The 2024 Argentine EPR law had mandated 35% recovery by 2027, 52% by 2030 and 70% by 2035 with eco-modulation-fee adjustments tied to recyclability, reusability and recycled-content thresholds. Panel-match LATAM EPR-transition studies had documented that PRO-membership delivered compliance without differentiation, own-take-back produced 24% IRR with brand differentiation, and product-redesign produced the highest premium-segment access at 12–18% price premium.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1989–2025 circular-economy literature produced three findings the simulation had to respect. First, the Ellen-MacArthur-Foundation 2012 butterfly-diagram framework, combined with the EU-directive-2008 waste-hierarchy pyramid, had produced documented circular-transition readings across approximately 840 panel-match manufacturing cases where product-redesign-at-the-source interventions correlated at 0.78 with twelve-month eco-modulation-fee-reduction readings and at 0.62 with premium-segment-access readings. Second, Frosch-Gallopoulos 1989 and Chertow 2000 industrial-symbiosis frameworks, applied to the Kalundborg-pattern cluster, had documented net-value-creation-per-stream readings averaging 1.8–3.2× the projected baseline on panel-match manufacturing clusters in the 2010–2020 window. Third, Walls's 2006 EPR framework, combined with ISO 14040 LCA, had documented that the quantified environmental impact functions as a Scope-3-reporting commercial asset that opens procurement relationships otherwise unreachable under traditional-packaging-vendor positioning.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The 1988 Lombardi-family founding; the 1994 first corrugator line; the 2010 FSC certification; the 2019 first ISO-14001 audit; the 2024 Argentine EPR law publication with the 35%-by-2027 target; the Cencosud-and-DIA transition-plan request of February 2025; the December 2025 Lombardi-Giménez appointment; the CircularLab Ventures approach of March 2026; and the 180-day EPR-transition-plan commitment window that closes June 23 were the sequence.

### What Lombardi-Giménez knew

The butterfly-diagram mapping with the fluorocarbon-and-solvent-ink contaminant identification; the waste-hierarchy current mix and the prevention / reduction / reuse targets; the four-stream industrial-symbiosis feasibility; the three-option EPR compliance-fork economics; the ISO 14040 LCA readings; the four-archetype platform-business-model selection; the USD 8m CircularLab Ventures term sheet; and the Lombardi-family-council's appetite for a family-firm-compatible VC joint venture.

### What she did not know

Whether the CACIA industry-association auditor would accept the proposed redesign timeline as compliant; whether Cencosud-and-DIA would accept the LCA-packaged Scope-3 commercial asset within the 2025 transition-plan window; whether the family council would accept the VC joint-venture structure or would prefer the family-controlled own-take-back center; whether the Returnable-Industrial-Packaging platform would achieve 4–6 cycles per unit at scale or would regress to 2–3 cycles; whether the eco-modulation-fee reduction from the product redesign would compound into the premium-segment margin as projected; and whether the 180-day window would accommodate the starch-based-barrier supplier qualification.

### What others were saying

**The CEO and founder (father of the protagonist, 71)**: *"Sofía, lo que tu abuelo pensó en mil novecientos ochenta y ocho lo diste vuelta en ciento veinte días. Ahora muéstrame que la familia sigue adentro."*

**The CircularLab Ventures managing partner (44, Montevideo)**: *"Ocho millones, cuatro rondas, firma a las cuatro. No financiamos fabricación de cajas. Financiamos plataformas."*

**The Cencosud head of packaging procurement (37)**: *"Alcance tres. Si me das los números auditados, te pongo en el panel premium."*

**The CACIA industry-association auditor (52)**: *"Ciento ochenta días. El treinta y cinco por ciento de recuperación es de cumplimiento obligatorio. El modo es tuyo."*

**The Lombardi family-council senior (uncle, 68)**: *"La empresa es de la familia. El capital externo, bien venido. El control, nuestro."*

**The 62,000-tonne corrugated-packaging output (aggregate signal of the current fiber-and-coating configuration)**: *"Diseñado en el ochenta y cuatro. No circular en el veinticinco."*

## Options Under Consideration

Lombardi-Giménez had narrowed the circular-economy space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — EMF-redesign + waste-hierarchy targets + industrial-symbiosis cluster + product-redesign EPR + LCA-as-Scope-3-asset + RIP-product-as-a-service + USD 8m VC joint venture (Recommended).** Respects the EMF-butterfly framework; closes the fluorocarbon-and-solvent-ink contaminant gap; activates the four-stream Kalundborg cluster for ARS 6.3m net value creation; secures the EPR eco-modulation-fee reduction through redesign; packages the LCA reading as a Scope-3 commercial asset; commits the RIP product-as-a-service platform for the B2B segment; signs the USD 8m VC joint venture at 4:00 p.m. with Lombardi family-control retained.
- **Option B — PRO-membership + no-redesign + traditional-packaging + no-VC + no-RIP-platform.** Rejected — meets the EPR compliance threshold on paper; does not open the Scope-3 commercial asset; does not activate the symbiosis cluster; leaves the 1984-era product design in place; and accumulates the documented PRO-membership-undifferentiated pattern that produces zero premium-segment access.
- **Option C — Own-take-back-center + no-redesign + no-VC + limited-RIP-pilot.** Defensible on family-control grounds but commits ARS 12m of capex, does not reduce the eco-modulation fee, does not open the Cencosud Scope-3 premium segment, and surrenders the CircularLab Ventures funding window.

Lombardi-Giménez's private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the single decision most correlated with the thirty-six-month circular-transition outcome was the Round 1 product-redesign commitment (fluorocarbon-to-starch, solvent-to-water-based), because the 1989–2025 panel had shown that product-redesign-at-the-source interventions correlated at 0.78 with eco-modulation-fee reductions and at 0.62 with premium-segment-access readings, and because her 2023 LATAM-consumer-goods redesign had opened a premium Scope-3 procurement segment at 14% price premium under the same pattern. The *Residuo al Valor* frame sits at the LATAM-corrugated-packaging-EPR-and-VC-joint-venture tier in the 2026 Express catalogue, distinct from Lumena Electronics (sim 013, electronics-WEEE frame), ReCicla (sim 178, circular-platform-aggregator frame) and TerraVida (sim 152, greenforge-industrial-scale frame) — 315 emphasises the Returnable-Industrial-Packaging product-as-a-service platform for a family-firm B2B segment.
