## Abstract

TRADEEX Perú S.A.C. was a Lima-based processor of Amazonian botanical extracts for cosmetic and nutraceutical applications, founded in 2014, with 2025 revenue of USD 1.8M, an EBITDA margin of 11.2%, and a working-capital base of USD 384,000. On March 24, 2026, BioNordic AB, a Stockholm-based nutraceutical manufacturer, confirmed a purchase order of USD 2.3M — more than the whole of TRADEEX's 2025 revenue — for a single shipment of six botanical-extract SKUs. The order would be shipped within 90 days and paid, under a to-be-agreed payment instrument, within 90 days of shipment. Nicolás Arévalo, Chief Financial Officer and Export Lead at TRADEEX, had to negotiate the payment terms with BioNordic by April 8, book production capacity by April 15, ship by June 22, and ensure that TRADEEX did not insolvency-cascade between the production advance and the eventual payment. The case opens at 6:20 a.m. on Friday, April 3, 2026, as Arévalo prepares the Round-1 term sheet. Four rounds would unfold: payment-instrument and Incoterm selection, documentation, currency hedging, and shipment/collection.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Compare the four candidate payment instruments (D/P, D/A, unconfirmed LC, confirmed LC) on cost, risk, and buyer-relationship dimensions and recommend a fit for a first-time large order.
2. Evaluate the five Incoterm options (EXW, FCA, FOB, CFR, DDP) and match them to firm-level capability and buyer preference.
3. Diagnose the two most common LC-documentation failures — description mismatch and shipping-date margin insufficiency — and build a documentation-rigor protocol that avoids them.
4. Design a currency-hedging posture for a 90-day EUR receivable under limited working-capital constraints and compare forwards, options, and non-recourse factoring on expected cost and cash-flow timing.
5. Recommend a complete Round-1 term-sheet package and identify the single documentation pitfall most likely to require a Round-2 re-tune.

On the morning of **Friday, April 3, 2026**, **Nicolás Arévalo** sat in the narrow second-floor office of TRADEEX Perú's processing facility in Lurín and read, for the fourth time, the BioNordic purchase-order confirmation that had arrived at 5:47 p.m. Lima time the previous day. The order was real: a Swedish public-document lookup had confirmed BioNordic as a 28-year-old private nutraceutical manufacturer with EUR 62M in 2024 revenue and a long-standing relationship with Nordea, its issuing bank. The order was also three months of production capacity in a single shipment and 1.3x the whole of TRADEEX's 2025 revenue.

## The Protagonist

Nicolás Arévalo, 36, had been Chief Financial Officer and Export Lead at TRADEEX Perú since 2021. A graduate of Universidad del Pacífico in Lima with a master's degree in international finance from IE Business School in Madrid, he had spent eight years in corporate-banking trade-finance roles at two Peruvian banks — first as a junior trade-finance officer, then as a senior relationship manager for the export-SME segment at the second bank. He had, in the 2018–2019 cohort of his second bank, structured approximately 40 letters of credit for Peruvian exporters in the USD 0.5M–USD 5M range; he knew, from that cohort, that 11 of 40 had incurred LC discrepancies at presentation, four of which had produced material payment delays and one of which had produced a non-payment. He had joined TRADEEX to build the export function from the inside.

## The Decision

Arévalo had to commit, by 5:00 p.m. on Tuesday, April 7, the Round-1 term-sheet package that would anchor the BioNordic relationship and the four-round execution window:

1. **Payment Instrument** — Documentary Collection D/P (documents against payment), Documentary Collection D/A (documents against acceptance), Irrevocable Sight Letter of Credit Unconfirmed, or Irrevocable Sight Letter of Credit Confirmed.
2. **Incoterm** — EXW (Ex Works, buyer handles everything from TRADEEX's door), FCA (Free Carrier at Callao), FOB Callao (Free on Board at the ship's rail), CFR (Cost and Freight to Stockholm), or DDP (Delivered Duty Paid to the BioNordic warehouse).
3. **Currency Hedging** — Unhedged (carry the EUR-USD-PEN exposure for 90 days), Forward (lock the EUR-USD rate for 90 days), Option (pay a premium for a floor without giving up upside), or Factoring Non-Recourse (sell the receivable at a discount for immediate cash).

The three decisions would interact. A confirmed LC required the cost and time to get it issued through TRADEEX's local bank and confirmed through a second European bank, but produced the highest protection on buyer-and-bank risk. FOB Callao placed freight and European customs in BioNordic's hands and minimized the Incoterm-related cost, but required strong coordination with the buyer's forwarder. Forward hedging protected the margin against FX moves but consumed a small line of credit that the production advance already drew on. None of the combinations were free; several were cheap enough to be suspicious.

## Company Background

TRADEEX Perú S.A.C. had been founded in 2014 by Arévalo's older brother, Mauricio Arévalo (a biochemist) and a Swiss-Peruvian cosmetic-industry operator, Clara Keller. The company processed Amazonian botanical raw materials — primarily cat's claw (Uncaria tomentosa), maca, and a set of proprietary extracts — into standardized ingredients for cosmetic and nutraceutical customers. 2025 revenue was USD 1.8M across 42 customers in 9 countries; the largest single customer contributed 9% of revenue. The company employed 23 people at the Lurín processing facility and held organic and fair-trade certifications that had been the principal reason BioNordic had qualified the brand in the first place.

Working capital at April 3, 2026 was USD 384,000 — of which USD 212,000 was cash and USD 172,000 was receivables against orders in transit. TRADEEX had an existing revolving credit line of USD 150,000 at TIIE + 420 basis points, currently drawn at USD 90,000. Its primary local banking relationship was with a Peruvian mid-sized bank (disguised here as Banco Central de Crédito del Perú, or BCCP) that had issued three unconfirmed LCs for TRADEEX in 2022–2024, all of which had paid without discrepancy.

## Industry and Market Context

The global trade-finance landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Round-1 decision sat inside. First, LC usage at the SME export tier had stabilized around 14% of cross-border transactions in 2024–2025 (down from 38% in 2008), but remained the dominant instrument for first-time large orders across European-Latin-American corridors. Second, European buyer-bank creditworthiness had shown a mild deterioration pattern in the 2024–2025 period, with four Tier-2 European banks facing either ratings downgrades or public-scrutiny events; Nordea, BioNordic's bank, had remained rated Aa3/AA- through the period. Third, LC-discrepancy rates at first presentation across industry studies converged on 60%–75% of LCs showing at least one discrepancy, with description mismatches (35% of discrepancies) and shipping-date margin insufficiency (18%) the two most common.

Empirically, the trade-finance literature converged on three findings. First, confirmed LCs on first-time European buyers had produced payment-default rates of 0.4% across industry studies; unconfirmed LCs 1.2%; documentary collections 3.8%. Second, EUR/USD 90-day FX moves had exceeded 4% in 37% of quarterly observations between 2020 and 2025, creating a material margin threat for an 11% EBITDA-margin exporter on an unhedged receivable. Third, Incoterm misalignment between exporter and buyer — specifically, DDP on an exporter without European customs-clearance capability — had been associated with 42% of observed customs-delay incidents in the sample studied.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The BioNordic relationship had started in November 2025 at a trade-ingredients fair in Frankfurt that Clara Keller had attended. A technical-sample-validation process had run December through February. BioNordic had, in a February 28 meeting, signalled an intent to place an order "in the two-million-dollar range"; the March 24 confirmation had landed at USD 2.3M. The BioNordic buyer, Anders Lindqvist, had, in their March 28 video call, stated a preference for an unconfirmed LC with FOB Callao; Arévalo had agreed to return with a term sheet by April 8.

### What Arévalo knew

The internal team had modeled three Round-1 packages. A Low-Cost Package (Documentary Collection D/P, FOB Callao, Unhedged) projected total transaction cost USD 4,600, payment-default expected cost USD 87,000, FX expected cost USD 32,000, net expected margin USD 186,000. A Balanced Package (Unconfirmed LC, FOB Callao, Forward) projected total transaction cost USD 7,800, default expected cost USD 28,000, FX expected cost USD 2,300, net expected margin USD 221,000. A Premium Package (Confirmed LC, FOB Callao, Forward) projected total transaction cost USD 10,500, default expected cost USD 9,000, FX expected cost USD 2,300, net expected margin USD 238,000.

### What he did not know

Whether BCCP would issue an LC at TRADEEX's current line availability, or would require additional collateral against the face value of the instrument; whether Anders Lindqvist, whose stated preference was for an unconfirmed LC, would accept a confirmed LC without pushing a price concession; whether the six-SKU product-description alignment between BioNordic's LC draft and TRADEEX's commercial invoice would be clean on first pass or require the description-mismatch correction that the industry literature had found in 35% of cases; and whether the planned June 22 shipment date would hold under the production schedule or slip inside the LC expiry margin.

### What others were saying

**Mauricio Arévalo**, Chief Executive Officer and co-founder: *"Confirmed LC. Forward hedge. FOB Callao. The order pays for three months of production. If the payment is late the supplier to the supplier does not get paid. We are one discrepancy from a cascade."*

**Clara Keller**, Commercial Director and co-founder: *"Anders asked for unconfirmed. Do not push back on the term sheet in a way that signals we do not trust Nordea. Unconfirmed with a forward hedge. Keep the relationship clean."*

**BCCP Trade-Finance Desk**, Lima: *"Confirmed LC through Deutsche Bank as second bank — yes, we can structure it. The confirmation cost will be 140 basis points of face value. If you want unconfirmed, we can issue in ten days."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Arévalo had narrowed the space to three term-sheet packages.

- **Option A — Low-Cost (D/P, FOB Callao, Unhedged):** Total transaction cost USD 4,600. Net expected margin USD 186,000. Highest default and FX risk. Cannot be recommended for a first-time large order by industry consensus.

- **Option B — Balanced (Unconfirmed LC, FOB Callao, Forward):** Total transaction cost USD 7,800. Net expected margin USD 221,000. Aligns with buyer's stated preference. Retains residual issuing-bank risk.

- **Option C — Premium (Confirmed LC, FOB Callao, Forward):** Total transaction cost USD 10,500. Net expected margin USD 238,000. Minimizes bank risk at a USD 3,200 incremental cost over Option B. Requires convincing the buyer that confirmation is not a signal of distrust but a first-order-large-order hygiene step.

Arévalo's private view, not yet shared with his brother, was that Option C was the right package on the numbers and the margin-protection logic, but that Option B was the right package on the relationship logic if Arévalo could credibly sell Anders Lindqvist on a second-order "we can move to unconfirmed" upgrade pathway. The harder question was whether the documentation protocol for the LC — the description alignment, the shipping-date margin, the incoterms cross-reference — would hold through four rounds of execution on the tight 90-day clock.

At 7:14 a.m., Arévalo closed the BioNordic purchase-order PDF and opened a fresh term sheet. The April 7 deadline for the term-sheet return was ninety-six hours away. The first production batch for the order would — under the signed-by-April-15 schedule — enter the Lurín processing line on April 20. A payment arriving at the planned Day 87 of the LC presentation would land in TRADEEX's USD account on June 29 at the earliest and September 19 at the latest under the worst-case discrepancy-and-rework scenario.
