## Abstract

VELOX SYSTEMS was, in the case universe, a mid-sized European technology company with USD 680M in 2025 revenue, an in-house IP function that had accreted across 2016–2024 into an eighty-four-patent portfolio concentrated in three claim families (sensor-fusion middleware, low-latency edge-compute runtimes, and a small but high-citation-depth security-telemetry cluster), a trailing-twelve-month licensing run-rate of USD 320K at Q4 2025, and a Q4 2025 board-approved IP-transformation envelope of USD 6.2M across a thirty-six-month horizon. On Monday, January 12, 2026, Tomas Alarcón, a 49-year-old former patent litigator and fifteen-year in-house IP counsel, had opened the five-round IP Fortress commitment the board had asked him to land in service of a 2028 strategic-optionality window that would either route Velox into an M&A exit or into a stand-alone Series D growth round. The 2024–2025 IP-threat landscape had, across the cycle, delivered three NPE letters (two in the sensor-fusion claim family, one against the edge-compute runtime), one operating-company countersuit risk against a US-based competitor, and an inbound adjacent-vertical licensing approach from a non-competitor automotive-telemetry operator. The case opens at 6:06 a.m. on Tuesday, January 20, 2026, at the Velox office as Alarcón prepares the Round-1 threat-triage commitment.

## The Protagonist

Tomas Alarcón, 49, had joined Velox Systems in October 2019 from a European patent-litigation boutique where he had, across eleven years, litigated forty-two patent disputes for operating-company clients. Before that, he had spent six years as an in-house IP counsel at a US enterprise-software company. The Chief Executive Officer had recruited him specifically on the strength of two capabilities: his experience running multi-track IP strategy through a strategic-optionality window, and his reputation for refusing to litigate IP threats that could be resolved with a clean paid license and a settlement deed. His 2026 long-term incentive plan tied 40% to the Year-3 IP Value Score and 20% to the M&A-diligence readiness — a KPI the Chief Executive Officer had explicitly anchored on the 2028 strategic-optionality window.

## The Decision

Alarcón had to commit, by the February 2 board commitment, the Round-1 threat-triage response and the directional intent for Rounds 2 through 5.

1. **Round 1 — Threat Triage (Months 1–6)** — how to sequence a response to three NPE letters (NPE-1 on sensor-fusion middleware; NPE-2 on edge-compute runtime; NPE-3 on a broad low-latency claim family flagged by outside counsel as "bluff") and one operating-company countersuit risk. Options: file prior-art on NPE-1, negotiate a small paid license on NPE-2 (EUR 180K lump sum, bounded scope), and ignore NPE-3 pending ripeness — with a reserved counter-defence envelope on the operating-company countersuit risk.
2. **Round 2 — Strategic Positioning (Months 7–12)** — whether to accept an inbound adjacent-vertical licensing approach from a non-competitor automotive-telemetry operator at USD 240K per year, scoped narrowly to the adjacent vertical only, with no cross-license to the core.
3. **Round 3 — Portfolio Optimization (Months 13–18)** — how to prune the eighty-four-patent portfolio. Options: prune 22 patents for maintenance savings (USD 140K/year), sell 4 to a friendly defensive pool (USD 1.6M one-time), and hold the 24 crown-jewel claim families that anchored the moat.
4. **Round 4 — Ecosystem and Standards (Months 19–24)** — how to handle a GPL-3 dependency inside the edge-compute runtime (dual-license the module, re-implement, or comply with GPL-3) and whether to declare a narrow-scope set of SEPs on FRAND terms inside the relevant standards-development organisation (SDO).
5. **Round 5 — M&A Readiness (Months 25–36)** — how to present the portfolio to an acquirer-diligence process that was expected to open inside the Q3 2028 strategic-optionality window.

On Alarcón's working model, a Prior-Art-plus-Paid-License-plus-Ignore-plus-Adjacent-License-plus-Prune-plus-Dual-License-plus-Narrow-SEP envelope delivered IVS 79, EV-attributable USD 42M, LRR USD 680K/yr, Moat 74, Health 86 — a portfolio that cleared acquirer-diligence clean at Round 5. A Litigate-Everything-plus-Core-License-plus-Hold-All-plus-Re-implement-plus-Broad-SEP envelope delivered IVS 58, LRR USD 240K/yr, Moat 78 (short-term), Health 52 — a portfolio that maximised litigation spend but destroyed portfolio health.

## Company Background

Velox Systems had been founded in 2011 as a university spin-out focused on sensor-fusion middleware for industrial-telemetry applications. Across 2011–2019, the company had grown into a mid-sized technology operator with USD 680M in 2025 revenue, 1,420 employees, and three operating business lines: a middleware-licensing business, an edge-compute-runtime platform business, and a security-telemetry business that had been acquired in 2021 and had contributed the small but high-citation-depth security cluster to the portfolio.

The Q4 2025 IP-transformation dossier had identified three specific pressures the five-round envelope would inherit. First, the Q4 2025 NPE-letter landscape had delivered three letters against Velox across the 2024–2025 window, two of which had been scoped to the sensor-fusion claim family that anchored the middleware business — a pattern that framed the Round-1 triage commitment. Second, the Q1 2026 SDO-participation calendar had opened a narrow-scope SEP-declaration window in a low-latency-edge standards body — a calendar that framed the Round-4 FRAND commitment. Third, the 2028 strategic-optionality window had, in the CEO's December 2025 board pre-read, been anchored on either an M&A exit or a stand-alone Series D growth round — a dual-path framing that placed the Round-5 M&A-readiness commitment on the critical path of the full five-round trajectory.

## Industry and Market Context

The IP-strategy-and-monetization landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2019–2024 NPE-litigation evidence base had, across 182 operating-company NPE engagements, documented a Portfolio-Health differential of 12–18 points between prior-art-plus-paid-license-plus-ignore responses and litigate-everything responses — a pattern that anchored the Round-1 commitment. Second, the 2020–2024 adjacent-vertical-licensing literature had, across 46 inbound licensing approaches, placed scoped-narrow licensing at a Moat-preservation rate of 88% against 42% for cross-license commitments — a pattern that framed the Round-2 commitment. Third, the 2018–2024 SDO-SEP-declaration evidence base had, across 38 declarations, placed narrow-scope FRAND commitments at an M&A-diligence clean-pass rate of 84% against 38% for broad-scope commitments — a pattern that framed the Round-4 commitment.

Empirically, the post-2019 IP-strategy literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, portfolios that had pruned 22–28% of their claim families across a thirty-six-month window had, across a 2019–2023 sample of 54 mid-sized technology companies, delivered IP Value Score uplifts of 18–26 points against portfolios that had held all patents — a pattern that anchored the Round-3 commitment. Second, portfolios that had cleared M&A diligence clean at Round 5 had, across a 2020–2024 sample of 29 M&A closes, delivered EV-attributable-to-IP of USD 38–48M against USD 14–22M for portfolios that had entered diligence with open NPE exposures — a pattern that anchored the Round-5 commitment.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Chief Executive Officer had, in the December 2025 board pre-read, approved the five-round envelope, a Year-3 IVS target of 75, an LRR target of USD 600K/yr, and a Moat floor of 70. The Head of Patent Prosecution had, on January 6, circulated the Round-1 triage pre-read that recommended prior-art on NPE-1, a paid license on NPE-2 and ignore on NPE-3. The Head of Licensing had, on January 9, circulated the Round-2 adjacent-vertical approach and recommended a narrow-scope acceptance. The General Counsel had, on January 13, flagged the Q1 2026 SDO-participation calendar and argued for a narrow-scope SEP declaration. The Chief Financial Officer had, on January 16, endorsed the Round-3 prune-and-sell commitment on maintenance-cost grounds.

### What Alarcón knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. A Prior-Art-plus-Paid-License-plus-Ignore-plus-Adjacent-License-plus-Prune-plus-Dual-License-plus-Narrow-SEP Envelope projected IVS 79, EV-attributable USD 42M, LRR USD 680K/yr, Moat 74, Health 86. A Litigate-Everything-plus-Core-License-plus-Hold-All-plus-Re-implement-plus-Broad-SEP Envelope projected IVS 58, LRR USD 240K/yr, Health 52. A Prior-Art-plus-Paid-License-plus-Ignore-plus-Adjacent-License-plus-Prune-plus-Comply-plus-Narrow-SEP Envelope projected IVS 74, EV-attributable USD 36M, Health 80 — a defensible but less ambitious envelope.

### What he did not know

Whether the operating-company countersuit risk would crystallise inside Round 1 or slip to Round 2; whether the automotive-telemetry licensee would accept the narrow-scope license or push for a cross-license to the core; whether the friendly defensive pool would clear antitrust diligence on the Round-3 sale of four patents; and whether the Q2 2026 SDO-participation calendar would expand the SEP-declaration scope beyond the narrow-scope envelope.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Patent Prosecution**: *"Prior-art on NPE-1. Paid license on NPE-2. Ignore NPE-3. The ripeness test is unambiguous."*

**The Head of Licensing**: *"Narrow-scope adjacent license. Two-forty a year. Do not open the core."*

**The General Counsel**: *"Narrow-scope FRAND. Dual-license the GPL-3 module. Broad SEP is a 2028 diligence problem."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"Prune twenty-two. Sell four. The maintenance-cost savings fund the 2027 prosecution budget."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Alarcón had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Prior-Art / Paid-License / Ignore / Adjacent-License / Prune / Dual-License / Narrow-SEP:** Round-1 triage as above. Round-2 narrow-scope adjacent license at USD 240K/yr. Round-3 prune twenty-two, sell four, hold twenty-four crown jewels. Round-4 dual-license GPL-3 module and narrow-scope SEP declaration. Round-5 clean diligence pass. IVS 79. EV-attributable USD 42M. LRR USD 680K/yr. Moat 74. Health 86. Recommended.

- **Option B — Litigate-Everything / Core-License / Hold-All / Re-implement / Broad-SEP:** Maximalist litigation. IVS 58, Health 52. Rejected.

- **Option C — Prior-Art / Paid-License / Ignore / Adjacent-License / Prune / Comply (GPL-3) / Narrow-SEP:** IVS 74, Health 80. Rejected as the GPL-3 compliance path forfeits the dual-license optionality on the edge-compute runtime, which the 2028 M&A acquirer is expected to price.

Alarcón's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final EV-attributable was Round 1 — the triage commitment that preserved the Portfolio Health floor and that compounded through the Round-2 LRR and the Round-3 portfolio optimization commitments.
