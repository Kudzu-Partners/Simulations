## Abstract

TechArena Inc. was, in the case universe, a global B2B-and-consumer technology company with a 2025 revenue of USD 420M, R&D investment of 14% of revenue, a three-geography commercial footprint (Americas, EMEA, APAC), and a five-segment customer taxonomy (SMB, mid-market, enterprise, consumer, government). On Friday, January 9, 2026, the Board approved a three-year strategic commitment that the Chief Executive Officer would formalize in a Year-1 package. The technology landscape carried a 2025 late-stage venture-funding surge into AI-native platforms, with the Board's strategic-intelligence office placing an 81% probability that a well-funded AI-native mid-market entrant would enter the primary market at a 35–45% price discount inside the Year-2 window. Daniela Ferrer, appointed CEO on September 15, 2025 after seven years at the company as President of the Enterprise Business Unit, faced three annual rounds, a Year-2 disruption shock from a new entrant the strategic-intelligence office had internally code-named "NovaAI," and a Year-3 AI-rebuild-versus-overlay commitment. The case opens at 5:58 a.m. on Wednesday, January 14, 2026, as Ferrer prepares the Year-1 strategy package for the January 26 Board meeting.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Year-1 geographic-sequencing choice between a staged Americas-EMEA-APAC entry and a simultaneous all-at-once deployment, and diagnose when each dominates on a three-year revenue and Rule-of-40 composite.
2. Analyze the Year-1 R&D split between incremental and disruptive investment in the twelve months preceding a modelled AI-native disruption, and recommend a split consistent with the Year-2 response envelope.
3. Diagnose the Year-2 fighter-brand-versus-flagship-discount choice under a 40%-price-discount entrant, and design a channel response that preserves enterprise retention.
4. Design a Year-3 commitment between a full AI rebuild for Year-4 launch, a vertical-AI-tool acquisition, an EMEA doubling, and an APAC entry, and diagnose the sequence that converts a Year-2 defensive outcome into a Year-3 composite.
5. Recommend a complete Year-1 package and identify the Year-2 move most correlated with the Year-3 composite outcome.

On the morning of **Wednesday, January 14, 2026**, **Daniela Ferrer** sat in the corner office of the TechArena headquarters on Market Street in San Francisco at 5:58 a.m. and opened the Year-1 strategy workbook her Head of Corporate Strategy had finalized at 1:02 a.m. Twelve days separated her from the Board meeting. The revenue-by-segment dashboard on her wall read mid-market 34%, enterprise 42%, SMB 14%, consumer 6%, government 4%. The three-geography wall map carried Americas 68%, EMEA 24%, APAC 8%.

---

## The Protagonist

Daniela Ferrer, 49, had joined TechArena in 2018 as Senior Vice-President of Enterprise Sales and had been promoted to President of the Enterprise Business Unit in 2021. A graduate of Stanford University with an MBA from Harvard Business School, she had, in her Enterprise Business Unit role, grown the segment from USD 104M to USD 176M across 2021–2024 through a combination of direct-sales build-out, a partnership with the top three cloud-infrastructure players, and a focused vertical-industry push in healthcare and financial services. Her Board-mandate appointment letter, delivered September 15, 2025, had read: "Your predecessor ran a strong incremental company. The company the market will reward in 2028 is the one that shipped its AI answer in 2026, not the one that delayed the rebuild to be clean."

---

## The Decision

Ferrer had to commit, by 9:00 a.m. on Monday, January 26, the Year-1 strategy package the Board would treat as the three-year operating envelope:

1. **Product Portfolio and Pricing by Segment** — whether to run an SKU-ladder pricing on SMB to seed funnel, a bundled enterprise architecture, and a mid-market positioned between them, or to collapse the architecture to a simpler two-tier model.
2. **Geographic Sequencing** — whether to stage a geographic expansion (Americas first, EMEA in Q3 2026, APAC deferred to 2027), or to deploy simultaneously across all three geographies in Year-1.
3. **R&D Split and Channel Mix** — whether to split R&D 60/40 incremental-disruptive (the Head of Product's recommendation) or 75/25 (the CFO's preference), and whether to distribute direct-on-enterprise, channel-on-mid-market, and marketplace-on-SMB, or to unify on a single direct-sales motion.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across three rounds. A Year-1 package that laddered SMB pricing, bundled enterprise, staged geographic entry, split R&D 60/40, and ran a segment-tailored channel mix produced, on the internal model, a Year-1 revenue of USD 468M (+11%), a Year-1 Rule-of-40 at 38, a Year-2 fighter-brand response to NovaAI that contained the mid-market price gap, and a Year-3 composite of 83 out of a 100-point envelope. A Year-1 package that deployed simultaneously across geographies and ran a 75/25 R&D split produced a higher Year-1 revenue projection but opened a Year-2 disruption-response gap the strategic-intelligence office had modeled at 9–13 composite points.

## Company Background

TechArena had been founded in 2009 as a cloud-native B2B productivity platform and had, across seventeen years, built the three-geography five-segment footprint the case studied. 2025 revenue was USD 420M distributed as Americas (USD 285M, 68%), EMEA (USD 101M, 24%), APAC (USD 34M, 8%). The customer base comprised 3,820 mid-market accounts, 214 enterprise accounts, 38,400 SMB seat licenses, a consumer direct-to-user base of 180,000, and 28 government contracts (including three federal). The company's competitive position had rested, since 2020, on the enterprise-grade reliability of its core platform, a 96-point EMEA product-localization score that had outperformed the US-headquartered peer group, and an enterprise gross retention rate of 94% against a sector benchmark of 89%.

The Board's January 2026 strategy dossier had identified three specific pressures the Year-1 envelope would inherit. First, the 2024–2025 AI-native venture-funding cycle had produced five well-capitalized new entrants whose product ships were projected to land inside the Year-2 window. Second, the EMEA government-RFP pipeline had, through 2025, opened to TechArena's compliance posture and represented an estimated USD 64M opportunity across the Year-2 and Year-3 windows. Third, the APAC regulatory environment had tightened on cross-border data transfer, raising the expected compliance cost of an APAC launch by approximately 18% above the 2024 pre-regulation plan.

## Industry and Market Context

The global enterprise-technology landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Year-1 decision sat inside. First, the mid-market segment had, in the 2024–2025 venture-funding cycle, attracted a well-capitalized AI-native entrant cluster whose go-to-market had emphasized bottom-up viral growth, product-led pricing at 35–45% discounts to incumbents, and a 90-day-to-first-value claim. Second, the enterprise segment had, across the same window, shifted its procurement language from uptime-and-reliability to AI-roadmap-and-data-posture — the 2025 enterprise RFP sample had, in 71% of cases, required a bidder AI-architecture disclosure against 18% in 2023. Third, the APAC market, once a priority geography for global SaaS, had re-sorted since 2024 into three regulatory clusters — ASEAN plus Australia, Japan, and Greater China — each with different data-residency postures; the cost of a simultaneous APAC-wide launch had risen to a level that favored a deferred-but-AI-differentiated Year-3 entry.

Empirically, the post-2020 enterprise-software research had two robust findings the Year-1 package had to respect. First, companies that had shipped an AI-overlay inside a twelve-month window of a first enterprise-customer AI-roadmap RFP had, across a 2022–2024 sample of forty-two enterprise-SaaS events, retained renewal rates 9–13 percentage points above peers who had committed to a full AI-rebuild on a 24-month timeline. Second, fighter-brand responses to a new entrant at 30% discount had, across a 2018–2024 sample of twenty-one disruption events, contained flagship-brand cannibalization below 4% while absorbing 62% of the new entrant's addressable mid-market churn.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Board had, in the December 2025 strategy off-site, approved a three-year revenue target of USD 600M and a Rule-of-40 target of 45. The strategic-intelligence office had, on January 5, 2026, published a forty-page intelligence report on the five well-capitalized AI-native entrants, with NovaAI identified as the single most probable Year-2 mid-market threat. The CFO had, on January 8, circulated a Year-1 capital-budget pre-reading that proposed a USD 42M R&D increment to 16% of revenue with a 60/40 incremental-disruptive split. The Head of Product had, on January 12, pushed back with a 55/45 incremental-disruptive proposal and a deferred APAC launch.

### What Ferrer knew

The team had modeled three Year-1 envelopes. A Growth-First Envelope (simultaneous geographic deployment, 75/25 incremental-disruptive R&D, unified direct-sales motion) projected Year-1 revenue USD 492M, Year-1 Rule-of-40 41, Year-2 NovaAI-response composite 68, Year-3 composite 74. A Balanced Envelope (staged Americas-EMEA-APAC with APAC deferred, 60/40 R&D, segment-tailored channel mix) projected Year-1 revenue USD 468M, Year-1 Rule-of-40 38, Year-2 composite resilient through a fighter-brand launch and an enterprise AI overlay, Year-3 composite 83. A Defensive Envelope (Americas-only Year-1, 80/20 R&D, direct-sales-only) projected Year-1 revenue USD 442M, Year-1 Rule-of-40 35, Year-2 composite protected but Year-3 composite 69 — disciplined but growth-losing.

### What she did not know

Whether NovaAI's Year-2 entry would land at the intelligence-reported 40% price discount or at a deeper 50% that the second-source venture-intelligence had referenced; whether the enterprise AI-overlay response would clear the Year-2 renewal window (base case on the Head of Product's nine-month overlay feasibility assessment) or would slip to a twelve-month delivery that would miss 34% of the renewal cohort; whether the APAC data-residency tightening would stabilize in 2026 or continue to escalate through the Year-3 window; and whether the EMEA government-RFP pipeline would convert at the assumed 38% win rate or at a depressed 24% under a compliance re-audit.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Product**: *"Balanced Envelope. 60/40 R&D. The enterprise AI overlay in nine months is the right first answer to NovaAI. A full rebuild in Year-1 misses both the mid-market response window and the enterprise renewal cycle."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"Stage geography. The EMEA ramp is the revenue story. APAC in Year-1 is a capital burn against a regulatory mood."*

**The Chief Revenue Officer**: *"Segment-tailored channel mix. Direct on enterprise because the AI-roadmap conversation is a face-to-face conversation. Channel on mid-market. Marketplace on SMB. Do not unify on direct; unified direct collapses the SMB economics."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Ferrer had narrowed the space to three Year-1 envelopes.

- **Option A — Growth-First:** Simultaneous geographic deployment, 75/25 R&D, unified direct-sales. Year-1 revenue USD 492M, Year-3 composite 74. Highest Year-1 revenue. Under-equipped for Year-2 disruption.

- **Option B — Balanced:** Staged Americas-EMEA-APAC (APAC deferred to Year-2 at earliest), 60/40 R&D, segment-tailored channel mix. Year-1 revenue USD 468M, Year-3 composite 83. Aligns with Head of Product, CFO, and CRO.

- **Option C — Defensive:** Americas-only Year-1, 80/20 R&D, direct-sales-only. Year-1 revenue USD 442M, Year-3 composite 69. Capital-preserving. Growth-losing.

Ferrer's private view, not yet shared with the Board, was that Option B was the right Year-1 package and that the single subsequent decision most correlated with the Year-3 composite was the Year-2 response to NovaAI — specifically, a mid-market fighter brand launched at 30%-discount to halve NovaAI's price gap without cannibalizing the flagship, a 40/60 R&D flip to disruptive-incremental on the Year-2 decision cycle, an enterprise AI overlay shipped inside the nine-month window with a channel-partner AI-training kit, and APAC held for a Year-3 AI-differentiated re-entry.
