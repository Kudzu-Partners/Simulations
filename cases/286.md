## Abstract

The Chief Portfolio Officer at the case-universe Bogotá-headquartered Colombian-Chilean technology-holding firm — approximately USD 850m in 2025 revenue, 4,200 employees, a three-horizon portfolio with Horizon 1 enterprise software and services at 85% of revenue and slow decline, Horizon 2 cloud platform and data analytics burning cash but growing fast, and Horizon 3 AI agents, quantum services and sustainability consulting at proof-of-concept with high variance, an earnings miss in Q1 2026 that triggered a two-notch analyst downgrade, an activist-investor letter dated April 11 demanding the firm choose between 'optimize H1 aggressively' and 'spin off H3', and a competitor breakthrough announcement on April 19 in the H3 AI-agent space — had been asked, in the April 2026 CEO directive, to deliver by April 23 the four-year three-horizon allocation commitment. The exercise used the McKinsey three-horizon framework not as a presentation but as a binding capital-and-headcount allocation problem under external-event pressure. Each round was one fiscal year. Each round closed with an external event that tested whether the allocation held. The case opens at 3:00 p.m. on Thursday, April 23, 2026, at the firm's Bogotá headquarters as **Iván Restrepo-Cañas**, a 46-year-old Chief Portfolio Officer acting on behalf of the CEO, prepares the four-year commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Map the three-horizon portfolio — H1 enterprise software and services (85% of revenue, slow decline), H2 cloud platform and data analytics (growing, cash-burning), H3 AI agents/quantum services/sustainability consulting (proof of concept, high variance) — and articulate why the three horizons demand three different metrics, three different timelines and three different governance rhythms, and why applying H1 metrics to H3 kills the future while applying H3 tolerance to H1 misses guidance.
2. Move the allocation from the 70/20/10 starting mix toward the target mix under the binding capital and headcount envelope and articulate why every percent moved reshapes the four-year performance curve, why starving H2 is the single most common ambidexterity failure mode, and why moving too much to H3 triggers the earnings miss that brings the activist letter.
3. Navigate the Round-2 earnings miss and analyst downgrade and articulate why the pressure flows directly from the allocation decision in Round 1, why the H1 leader will use the earnings miss to argue for reinforcement, and why the H2 leader will be underheard even though H2 is the horizon most exposed to the under-allocation.
4. Respond to the Round-3 activist-investor letter demanding either 'optimize H1 aggressively' or 'spin off H3' and articulate why the activist letter deliberately ignores H2, why the H2 defence is the structurally hard argument, and why the spin-off-H3 option is the real-options-abandonment move disguised as a capital-discipline move.
5. Absorb the Round-4 competitor-H3-breakthrough announcement and commit to the stage-appropriate governance framework — H1 evaluated on operational excellence, H2 on unit economics and scale, H3 on learning milestones rather than revenue — articulating why strategy is where the money actually goes, why four years of allocation decide which company you are, and why the three-horizon portfolio is not a slide but a balance sheet.

On the afternoon of **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, **Iván Restrepo-Cañas** arrived at the firm's Bogotá headquarters at 3:00 p.m., opened the HorizonMap workbook his portfolio analyst had finalised at 11:45 p.m. the night before, and re-read the sentence he had pinned to his portfolio-brief wall: *"Strategy is where the money goes. Four years decides which company you are."*

## The Protagonist

Restrepo-Cañas, 46, had been Chief Portfolio Officer since 2024, had spent eleven years across the firm — six years as Head of M&A, three as Head of Corporate Development, and two inside the H2 cloud-platform business as Chief Operating Officer — and an earlier six-year stint at an IFC-backed LATAM growth fund where he had sat on four portfolio-company boards. In 2024 he authored the firm's internal playbook *Tres Horizontes, Tres Gobernanzas*, which framed portfolio allocation as a stage-appropriate-governance artefact rather than a capital-allocation artefact. The CEO had assigned him the lab on three considerations: his command of the three-horizon framework; his comfort with the activist playbook; and his willingness to commit to metrics that would be read as permissive by the H1 leadership.

---

## The Decision

Restrepo-Cañas had to commit, by the 5:00 p.m. Board pre-brief, the four-year allocation.

1. **Round 1 — Baseline allocation.** Move from 70/20/10 to 60/25/15 in Year 1. Capital envelope USD 310m fixed; headcount envelope 4,200 with a 6% reallocation budget.
2. **Round 2 — Earnings-miss response.** Hold the 60/25/15 allocation. Communicate variance drivers to analysts. Resist the reinforce-H1 argument.
3. **Round 3 — Activist letter response.** Reject both activist options as framed. Counter-propose the stage-appropriate governance framework. Defend H2 explicitly in the response letter.
4. **Round 4 — Competitor breakthrough response.** Accelerate H3 investment envelope for the winning adjacency; defund two other H3 bets; revise the H3 stage-gate on learning milestones; move to the 55/30/15 end-state mix.
5. **Governance framework.** H1 reviewed monthly on operational excellence. H2 reviewed quarterly on unit economics and scale. H3 reviewed bi-annually on learning milestones.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1990–2025 portfolio-allocation literature produced three findings the lab had to respect. First, three-horizon portfolios with stage-appropriate governance produced documented four-year value-creation readings approximately 35–55% higher than single-metric portfolios on comparable 2015–2024 mid-cap cycles. Second, portfolios that starved H2 were the single most common ambidexterity failure mode in the 2010–2024 record, with H2-starved firms underperforming the median by a documented 2.1× multiple over four years. Third, stage-gate governance with H3 evaluated on learning milestones rather than revenue produced documented breakthrough-conversion rates approximately 2.4× those of portfolios applying uniform revenue tests.

The post-2010 activist-investor literature added two findings. Activist letters deliberately ignore the messy middle horizon, and the structurally hard strategic argument — the H2 defence — is the argument CEOs most often lose in public. And real-options abandonment framed as capital discipline was the single most common four-year value-destruction pattern in the 2018–2024 record.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The 2024–2025 H2 investment cycle produced faster-than-expected revenue growth but slower-than-expected margin convergence. The Q1 2026 earnings miss flowed from the H2 margin trajectory. The April 11 activist letter was the immediate external response. The April 19 competitor H3 breakthrough compounded the pressure.

### What Restrepo-Cañas knew

H1 is predictable declining, H2 is moderate-variance growing, H3 is high-variance; the 70/20/10 mix is unsustainable; the activist letter ignores H2; the competitor breakthrough compresses the H3 timeline by three years; the H3 stage-gate on learning milestones is the right metric but reads as permissive to the H1 leadership.

### What he did not know

Whether the 60/25/15 allocation would hold against the Round-2 earnings-miss pressure or require retreat; whether the H2 scale-up would cross unit-economic break-even in Year 2 or Year 3; which of the three H3 bets would prove to be the right adjacency for the compressed timeline; whether the activist would escalate from letter to proxy contest within six months; and whether the Board would hold the stage-appropriate governance framework or default to uniform metrics under pressure.

### What others were saying

**The CEO**: *"The 70/20/10 mix was correct for the 2022 environment. It is not correct for 2026. The four-year commitment has to read as deliberate."*

**The CFO**: *"USD 310m is the binding capital envelope. Headcount reallocation is 6%. Neither envelope flexes on the Round-2 earnings miss."*

**The H1 Leader**: *"The earnings miss is the argument for more H1 investment. H1 pays today. H1 funded H2 and H3 for four years. The allocation should reflect that."*

**The H2 Leader**: *"Scale-up is eighteen months from unit-economic break-even. Starving me in Round 2 means the break-even slips to Year 4 and the whole thesis suffers."*

**The H3 Champion**: *"The competitor breakthrough compresses the window. Either the stage-gate moves forward or the H3 position becomes a follow rather than a lead."*

**The Activist Investor**: *"Optimize H1 or spin off H3. The messy middle is not a strategy."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Restrepo-Cañas had narrowed the space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — 55/30/15 end-state with stage-appropriate governance and explicit H2 defence (Recommended):** four-year glide path from 70/20/10, H1 operational excellence, H2 unit economics and scale, H3 learning milestones, activist-letter counter-proposal, competitor-breakthrough-triggered H3 consolidation. Recommended.
- **Option B — Accept activist framing; optimise H1 and spin off H3:** rejected — real-options abandonment disguised as capital discipline, literature's canonical four-year value destruction pattern.
- **Option C — Retain 70/20/10 and communicate long-horizon patience:** rejected — the mix is inconsistent with the H2 scale-up and H3 compressed timeline, and the earnings-miss context makes the patience narrative read as complacency.

His private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the round most correlated with four-year end-state performance was Round 3 — the activist-letter response — because Round 3 was the round where the H2 defence was made publicly or lost by default.
