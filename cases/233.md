## Abstract

Tessaline Quant Labs was, in the case universe, the Paris-headquartered Python-and-notebook research arm of the Tessaline quant family, operating as the shared labs function that supplied calibrated junior-analyst talent to the two downstream desks — Tessaline Quant Research (systematic research, USD 620M AUM; see sim 229) and Tessaline Quant Trading (systematic trading, USD 1.1B AUM). The unit ran a twelve-week junior rotation built around a six-task Python workbook that every incoming analyst had to close at a B grade or above before being assigned to a desk seat. The 2024–2025 cohorts had produced a rotation-completion rate of 78%, with the drop-outs concentrated on Task-4 (Black-Scholes calibration) and Task-5 (GARCH stationarity) — both of which required an intuition about ill-posedness and parameter-boundary behaviour that rote-library use could not supply. The Q2 2026 context had compounded three pressures: a fourteen-person junior cohort entering the twelve-week window; a reformed grading rubric that had tightened the pass mark on the calibration tasks; and a desk-partner request from Tessaline Quant Research that junior rotations on Task-6 (Monte-Carlo VaR) had to produce benchmarks at 99%-confidence-ten-day resolution rather than the previous 95%-confidence-one-day default. The case opens at 7:18 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Tessaline Quant Labs Paris notebook-room floor as **Camille Okafor-Duran**, a 38-year-old Head of Labs, prepares the Task-1 portfolio-Sharpe commitment.

## The Protagonist

Okafor-Duran, 38, had assumed the Head-of-Labs role at Tessaline Quant Labs on September 1, 2024, after thirteen years across systematic research, quant-development and rotation-programme leadership at a London hedge fund, a Zurich specialist and the Tessaline Paris research unit. She had, in 2020–2023, redesigned the junior-rotation workbook at a predecessor firm that had lifted the B-grade completion rate from 64% to 82% by moving the sequence from "libraries first, intuition second" to "intuition first, libraries second". She had, in 2022, delivered an internal rotation-programme review titled *Six Knobs, One Grade* that the predecessor firm had published verbatim as its rotation-curriculum public-facing document. The Tessaline management committee had recruited her on three capabilities: her documented discipline of treating the workbook as a calibration exercise rather than a library-use exercise; her willingness to grade Python literacy on parameter-intuition rather than code volume; and her command of the downstream-desk-feedback loop that ensured the rotation produced analysts the two Tessaline desks actually wanted to seat.

## The Decision

Okafor-Duran had to commit, by the 9:30 a.m. rotation-programme review, the six-task grading-and-rotation envelope.

1. **Task 1 — Portfolio Sharpe.** A single envelope: stock μ=12%, σ=20%; bond μ=4%, σ=5%; ρ=0.2; risk-free slider 0.5–8%; locate the Sharpe-maximising weight near 40% stock. Working envelope: accept as opener.
2. **Task 2 — Hidden Correlation.** A single envelope: lookback-window slider and smoothing-factor slider; true correlation hidden at 0.35; grade on lookback-vs-noise intuition. Working envelope: accept.
3. **Task 3 — MA-Crossover Backtest.** A single envelope: short MA 5–50, long MA 50–200, 10 bps per-trade slippage; locate the net-Sharpe peak. Working envelope: accept.
4. **Task 4 — Black-Scholes Calibration.** A single envelope: anchor strike, release IV slider, match market quote $10.45; grade on ill-posedness recognition. Working envelope: accept.
5. **Task 5 — GARCH(1,1).** A single envelope: α and β sliders, stationarity constraint α+β<1, long-run variance derived from both; target α=0.08, β=0.90. Working envelope: accept.
6. **Task 6 — Monte-Carlo VaR.** A single envelope: confidence slider, horizon slider; USD 1M book at drift 8%, vol 22%; square-root-of-time scaling, inverse-normal percentile; reproduce 99%-confidence ten-day Basel benchmark. Working envelope: accept.

On Okafor-Duran's working model, an Accept / Accept / Accept / Accept / Accept / Accept envelope delivered a B-or-above cohort-completion-rate target of 82% against the 2024–2025 78% baseline. A counterfactual "lower Task-4 and Task-5 pass bars" envelope would restore the historic completion rate but at the cost of downstream-desk-feedback signal quality.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2015–2025 Python-in-finance junior-rotation literature had, across 28 documented rotation-programme redesigns at systematic-research firms, produced three findings the labs had to respect. First, intuition-first rotations had, on twelve-week windows, lifted B-grade completion rates by 14–22 percentage points versus library-first rotations in 74% of documented cases. Second, calibration tasks (Black-Scholes implied-vol recovery, GARCH stationarity) had accounted for 58–68% of the variance in downstream-desk placement outcomes across the 2020–2025 cohorts, against 18–24% for backtest tasks. Third, regulatory-grade VaR reproduction (99%-confidence ten-day) had become the practitioner-default rotation exit ticket in 82% of surveyed European systematic-research firms.

Empirically, the 2020–2025 systematic-research hiring literature had two further findings. First, juniors who had completed Task-5-grade GARCH-stationarity fits had, on twelve-month trailing production metrics, out-performed library-use-only peers by 0.18–0.26 on Sharpe-per-unit-research-effort. Second, juniors who had not cleared the Task-4 Black-Scholes ill-posedness threshold on the workbook had, on the same trailing horizon, concentrated 68% of the downstream-desk model-validation re-work volume.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The 2024–2025 cohort had finished at a 78% B-grade completion rate, below the 82% target. Tessaline Quant Research had formally requested the 99%-confidence ten-day VaR benchmark for incoming analysts. The reformed grading rubric had tightened the Task-4 and Task-5 pass bars.

### What Okafor-Duran knew

The Sharpe frontier peaks near 40% stock; the lookback-and-smoothing trade-off peaks at a lookback that brackets the regime but does not span a regime change; the MA-crossover ridge is narrow and collapses under transaction costs; Black-Scholes calibration is ill-posed in two unknowns; GARCH stationarity requires α+β<1; the 99%-confidence ten-day VaR is the Basel-grade exit ticket.

### What she did not know

Whether the fourteen-person cohort would cluster above or below the 82% B-grade target; whether the Task-5 GARCH boundary would generate the same drop-out concentration as in 2024–2025; whether the 99%-confidence shift would require an additional rotation week; and whether the two downstream desks would continue to accept the six-task workbook as the standard rotation exit ticket.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Rotation Design**: *"Intuition first. Libraries second. A junior who can defend alpha and beta is worth five who can import arch."*

**The Head of Grading**: *"Task 4 separates the programmers from the quants. Task 5 separates the quants from the practitioners."*

**The Head of Desk-Feedback**: *"The two desks want 99-ten VaR. That is the exit ticket from now on."*

**The Head of Tessaline Quant Research**: *"Tessaline Labs is our talent funnel. The rotation output is our production hire rate."*

## Options Under Consideration

Okafor-Duran had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Accept / Accept / Accept / Accept / Accept / Accept:** the recommended envelope. B-grade completion-rate target 82%. Downstream-desk signal quality preserved. 99%-confidence ten-day VaR benchmark met. Recommended.
- **Option B — Lower Task-4 and Task-5 pass bars:** the completion-rate-optimised envelope. Rejected — desk-feedback signal quality degrades.
- **Option C — Reduce to four tasks (drop Task 2 and Task 3):** the simplified envelope. Rejected — systematic-research placement rate degrades on lookback-and-backtest intuition.

Her private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the task most correlated with the downstream desk-placement trajectory was Task 5 — the GARCH stationarity fit — because it was the task that decided whether Tessaline committed to a parameter-intuition-first rotation that fed the two downstream desks cleanly (Option A), relaxed into a completion-rate-maximising rubric that would over-promise and under-deliver (Option B) or retreated into a simplified four-task workbook that would fail the backtest-discipline signal (Option C).
