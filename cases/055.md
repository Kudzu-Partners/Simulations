## Abstract

Novatek Solutions was, in the case universe, a 500-person LATAM technology company headquartered in Ciudad de México with engineering hubs in Bogotá and Santiago and sales offices in São Paulo and Lima. 2025 revenue was USD 78M, with an EBITDA margin of 22.4%. The February 2026 diagnostic — commissioned by the incoming Chief Executive — showed women at VP-and-above at 14%, an unadjusted gender pay gap of 23%, a belonging score of 5.8 out of 10, under-represented-group turnover at 1.8× the company average, and an executive-alignment score on DEI of 5.4 out of 10. On Tuesday, March 10, 2026, Camila Arze, Chief People Officer (appointed six weeks earlier), presented to the Chief Executive the Quarter-1 DEI package: an eight-quarter program with a total budget of USD 2.4M, a target women-at-VP-plus of 22% by Quarter 8, a belonging-score floor of 7.0, an under-represented-turnover floor of 1.2×, and an executive-alignment target of 7.5. The case opens at 7:15 a.m. on Monday, March 9, 2026, as Arze prepares the Quarter-1 presentation. Eight quarters would unfold, including a pay-equity leak, a senior-woman exit, a mid-level-manager backlash, and an institutional-investor ESG-disclosure request.

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

3. Diagnose the ERG (Employee Resource Group) design choice — Social affinity, Talent development, or Business-impact — and recommend a model that survives the Quarter-5 backlash.
4. Design a governance architecture (CDO reporting line, executive-bonus tie, board accountability) that converts DEI from a values statement into a compensable metric.
5. Recommend a complete Quarter-1 package and identify the single Quarter-2 leak-response decision most likely to compound belonging-score gains rather than erode them.

On the morning of **Monday, March 9, 2026**, **Camila Arze** sat at the people-operations conference room on the fourth floor of the Novatek Solutions headquarters in Ciudad de México and read, for the third time, the Chief Executive's February 28 email: "Make it measurably better. I want the Board to see numbers at Q4 and a path at Q8." The Quarter-1 package was due to the Chief Executive on Tuesday, March 10, at 10:00 a.m. The investor ESG-disclosure request had landed in her inbox eight days earlier.

---

## The Protagonist

Camila Arze, 38, had been recruited to Novatek Solutions in late January 2026 from a senior people-operations role at a LATAM consumer-goods conglomerate, where she had led a 2022–2025 pay-equity program that had closed an unadjusted gender gap from 19% to 4.8% and had produced, through the same period, a doubling of women-at-VP-plus. An industrial-relations-and-labor-economics graduate of Universidad de los Andes with a master's from London School of Economics, she had, in the prior role, published one co-authored paper on LATAM pay-equity methodology and had, in her final-round interview with Novatek's board, presented the eight-quarter plan that would become the case's Quarter-1 decision. The Chief Executive — also new, six months in — had framed the mandate as a "measurable, not moral" program.

---

## The Decision

Arze had to commit, by 10:00 a.m. on Tuesday, March 10, the Quarter-1 DEI package:

1. **Pay-Equity Approach** — External Audit (USD 180K, third-party Big Four HR-analytics firm, 90 days to report), Internal Analysis (USD 40K, people-analytics team, 60 days), or Delay (no action in Q1; move to Q2 or Q3).
2. **Hiring Framework** — Structured Interviews (company-wide, behavioral), Skills Assessments (technical roles first), or Diverse Panels (mandated composition).
3. **ERG Design** — Social affinity groups (traditional, low governance), Talent development accelerators (career programs, sponsor pairings, quarterly reviews), or Business-impact (ERGs tied to product and market deliverables).
4. **Budget Allocation** — Five allocation sliders across Hiring, Pay Equity, Inclusion, ERGs, and Accountability totalling USD 600K in Quarter 1 of the USD 2.4M eight-quarter envelope.

The four sub-decisions would interact and would compound across eight quarters. External-audit plus Structured-Interviews plus Talent-development ERGs plus a 30/25/20/15/10 budget split (Hiring/Pay Equity/Inclusion/ERGs/Accountability) produced, on the internal model, a forecast belonging-score of 7.6 and women-at-VP-plus of 24% at Quarter 8 — above target on both dimensions.

## Company Background

Novatek Solutions had been founded in 2011 as a software-development services firm and had, between 2016 and 2024, pivoted to a B2B SaaS product portfolio across three verticals (logistics, retail, and financial services). 2025 revenue was USD 78M at 500 employees distributed as 218 engineering, 94 product and design, 72 sales, 48 customer success, 36 marketing, and 32 in G&A, finance, and people operations. Geographic distribution was 48% Mexico, 22% Colombia, 14% Chile, 10% Brazil, and 6% Peru. The board comprised the Chief Executive, the Chairman (who was also the largest shareholder), a financial-investor representative (Series-C-lead 2022 vintage), and three independent directors.

Headcount gender composition was 36% women overall, 28% women in engineering, 22% women at manager-plus, and 14% women at VP-plus. The 2025 exit interview file — which Arze had reviewed in her first two weeks — showed 22% of voluntary exits from the under-represented cohort citing "limited advancement opportunities" as a primary reason, against 8% from the majority cohort. The 2024 internal engagement survey had produced the 5.8 belonging-score baseline.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM DEI landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Quarter-1 decision sat inside. First, Colombia's September 2025 Pay-Equity Reporting Law (enforceable March 2026 for companies of 200+ employees) required the annual publication of unadjusted and adjusted pay gaps by gender, with non-compliance penalties of up to 4,000 monthly minimum wages. Brazil's 2024 Lei de Igualdade Salarial had, by March 2026, produced two years of published disclosure at the company level. Mexico had no equivalent federal law but had, in 2025, seen institutional investors formally request SASB and GRI DEI disclosures from 14 of the 24 top tech employers. Second, the DEI-backlash literature had converged on a clear finding: mid-level-manager backlash events (defined as a written dissent from 20+ managers) had, in the 2023–2025 cohort, fired in 27% of companies that framed DEI communications in zero-sum terms, against 6% that framed it in evidence-based terms. Third, the pay-equity-leak literature — across 41 publicly reported incidents between 2019 and 2025 — had shown that companies publishing adjusted and remediation figures within forty-eight hours preserved belonging-score on average; companies containing or denying the leaked document lost 1.8 belonging-score points on average inside one quarter.

Empirically, the ERG-design research had two robust findings. First, Talent-development ERGs — defined as sponsor-paired, quarterly-reviewed, career-outcome-focused — produced, in a five-company LATAM tech sample, 14% higher retention of under-represented talent and 9% higher promotion rates than Social-affinity ERGs. Second, executive-bonus ties to DEI metrics — when the tie was ten percent or above and was tied to time-bound representation and belonging targets — produced measurable representation movement in 76% of companies across an eighteen-month window; ties at five percent or below produced movement in 28%.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Chief Executive had been appointed in September 2025 and had, in her first ninety days, commissioned the DEI diagnostic. The diagnostic had landed on February 18, 2026, showing the 23% unadjusted pay gap, the 14% women-at-VP-plus, and the 1.8× under-represented-turnover. Arze had joined on February 2 and had, between February 2 and March 9, conducted forty-two one-on-one interviews across all six geographies and had drafted the eight-quarter plan.

### What Arze knew

The internal people-analytics team had modeled three Quarter-1 packages. A Conservative Package (Internal Analysis, Structured Interviews, Social ERGs, equal-split budget) projected Q8 women-at-VP-plus 18% (below target), belonging 6.7, adjusted pay gap 6.1%, DEI score 58. A Balanced Package (External Audit, Structured Interviews + Diverse Panels, Talent-development ERGs, 30/25/20/15/10 split) projected Q8 women-at-VP-plus 24%, belonging 7.6, adjusted pay gap 3.1%, DEI score 81. An Aggressive Package (External Audit, Skills Assessments + Structured Interviews, Business-impact ERGs, 25/25/25/15/10 split) projected Q8 women-at-VP-plus 26%, belonging 7.4, adjusted pay gap 2.4%, DEI score 83 — with a higher Q2–Q3 backlash probability (53% versus 31% for the Balanced Package).

### What she did not know

Whether the February 2026 investor ESG-disclosure request would convert to a formal GRI/SASB requirement at the April 14 board meeting; whether the 23% unadjusted pay-gap document — which circulated inside the people-operations team and two finance-team members — would leak to an internal anonymous channel in the Quarter-2 window (the HR-intelligence risk score was 62%); whether a senior woman on the leadership team — who Arze had identified as retention-at-risk from the first-week interviews — would exit to a competitor inside the Quarter-3 window; and whether the forty-plus mid-level-manager cohort the sim had embedded as a Quarter-5 backlash event would produce a coordinated written dissent or a series of one-on-one push-back conversations.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Executive**: *"External audit. Public number inside ninety days. I will stand behind it. I will not stand behind a number the market does not believe."*

**Javier Molina**, Chief Financial Officer: *"Fifteen percent of the VP-and-above bonus pool tied to time-bound representation and belonging targets. The ten percent the consultancy recommended is not enough to change behavior. The twenty percent our Chairman proposed is enough to trigger a resignation from two of the affected VPs in the first quarter."*

**Isabella Cano**, Head of People Analytics: *"Adjusted pay-gap baseline is eight-point-four percent. That is the number we will defend in the leak response. Not the twenty-three. Not the zero. Eight-point-four, with a forty-eight-month remediation plan and three-year target of three-point-one."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Arze had narrowed the space to three Quarter-1 packages.

- **Option A — Conservative Internal:** Internal Analysis, Structured Interviews, Social ERGs, equal-split budget. Q8 women-at-VP-plus 18% (under-target). Adjusted pay gap 6.1%. DEI score 58. Cheap, visible, slow; does not satisfy the Colombia-regulation floor.

- **Option B — Balanced External with Talent ERGs:** External Audit, Structured Interviews plus Diverse Panels, Talent-development ERGs, 30/25/20/15/10 split. Q8 women-at-VP-plus 24%. Adjusted pay gap 3.1%. DEI score 81. Leak-response-ready on 48-hour publishing protocol.

- **Option C — Aggressive Business-Impact:** External Audit, Skills Assessments plus Structured Interviews, Business-impact ERGs, 25/25/25/15/10 split. Q8 women-at-VP-plus 26%. Adjusted pay gap 2.4%. DEI score 83. Higher Q2–Q3 backlash probability.

Arze's private view, not yet shared with the Chief Executive, was that Option B was the right Quarter-1 package and that the single subsequent decision most correlated with the Q8 score was the Quarter-2 leak-response posture — specifically, publishing the adjusted pay-gap number and the remediation plan with dates within forty-eight hours of the leak rather than containing or re-framing the leak.
