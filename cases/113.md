## Abstract

MERIDIAN INDUSTRIALS was, in the case universe, a diversified industrial group with 11,200 employees across fourteen countries, a 2025 revenue of €2.9B, a legacy Oracle-HCM stack that had accreted across three acquisitions between 2018 and 2023, a Q4 2025 Time-to-Hire of 64 days against a European industrial-peer median of 42 days, a Q4 2025 HR-operating cost ratio of 1.8% of revenue against a peer median of 1.3%, and an Attrition-Accuracy metric (predictive precision at the twelve-month horizon) of 48% against a peer median of 62%. On Monday, January 26, 2026, Paula Reinoso, a 48-year-old former banking-sector CHRO who had joined Meridian Industrials in February 2024, had opened the three-round PeopleStack commitment the board had approved in December — an envelope that would, across 2026, pick one HCM platform, deploy three AI modules inside it under a human-in-loop envelope, and ride the adoption curve through a Glassdoor crisis the Q1 2026 HR-risk register had already flagged. The Q2 2026 EU-data-residency supervisory pass and the Q3 2026 German works-council consultation window framed the compliance floor. The case opens at 6:04 a.m. on Tuesday, February 3, 2026, at the Meridian Industrials HR centre-of-excellence as Reinoso prepares the Round-1 platform-selection commitment.

## The Protagonist

Paula Reinoso, 48, had joined Meridian Industrials in February 2024 from a Spanish banking group where she had, across eight years, run a CHRO mandate that had spanned a 2018 core-banking modernisation, a 2020 pandemic-workforce realignment and a 2022 AI-in-HR pilot that had been cited, across the 2023 European practitioner literature, as one of the five cleanest works-council-negotiated AI rollouts of the cycle. Before banking, she had spent nine years inside an industrial-conglomerate HR operations function. The Chief Executive Officer had recruited her specifically on the strength of two capabilities: her experience running HR-technology transformations across multi-country works-council landscapes, and her reputation for refusing to ship AI modules that could not be defended to a regulator. Her 2026 performance-bonus plan tied 35% to the PeopleStack Score at Year-1 close and 15% to the Compliance score — a KPI the Chief Executive Officer had explicitly protected inside the envelope.

## The Decision

Reinoso had to commit, by the February 20 executive-committee commitment, the Round-1 platform selection and the directional intent for Rounds 2 and 3.

1. **Round 1 — Platform Selection** — whether to commit to Nova HCM (the cheapest of the three, with a functional-fit score of 72 but a bias-audit depth flagged as "cursory" by the Q4 2025 external-vendor-diligence panel), Stratosphere People (functional-fit 84, strongest analytics layer, EU-data-residency evidence complete, TCO 10% above Nova), or ClearPath (functional-fit 78, best integration with the legacy Oracle stack, bias-audit depth rated "adequate", TCO 6% above Nova).
2. **Round 2 — AI Deployment** — how to deploy the three in-platform AI modules: Talent Acquisition (resume-ranking model), Performance Management (nudge engine) and Workforce Analytics (attrition-prediction model). The aggressive configuration (no human-in-loop on resume ranking, coarse-grained attrition prediction at cohort level) delivered Time-to-Hire minus 22% and Attrition Accuracy above 68%, but placed the bias flag at red on Talent Acquisition. The human-in-loop configuration on Talent Acquisition traded four points of Time-to-Hire speed (delivering minus 18%) for an amber bias flag — a trade-off the Q4 2025 HR-risk register had priced against a projected class-action exposure of €9M.
3. **Round 3 — Change and Adoption under Crisis** — how to respond to an anonymous Glassdoor post, landing inside the Round-3 window, alleging the AI was "scoring" employees: deny (Personal short-term plus four, Compliance minus eight), explain (Personal minus two, Compliance plus six, transparency-report commitment), or redesign (Personal minus five, Compliance plus nine, six-week module-redesign window).

On Reinoso's working model, a Stratosphere-plus-Human-in-Loop-plus-Explain envelope delivered PeopleStack Score 81, Time-to-Hire -18%, HR Cost -14%, Attrition Accuracy 72%, Compliance 94 — a commitment that preserved the regulator-facing trust story the CHRO had been recruited to defend. A Nova-plus-Aggressive-plus-Deny envelope delivered a headline Time-to-Hire of -24% but a Compliance score of 62 and a projected €9M class-action exposure. A ClearPath-plus-Human-in-Loop-plus-Redesign envelope delivered PeopleStack 74, Compliance 89 — a defensible but less ambitious envelope.

## Group Background

Meridian Industrials had been assembled across 2014–2023 through three industrial acquisitions into a diversified operating group with 2025 revenue of €2.9B, 11,200 employees across fourteen countries (Germany, Spain, France, Poland, Italy, Belgium, Mexico, Brazil, Colombia, Chile, Peru, the United States, Canada and South Africa), and an HR-operating cost of €52M in 2025. The legacy Oracle-HCM stack had accreted across the three acquisitions and had, at Q4 2025, eighteen integration points to non-Oracle satellites that the Q4 2025 IT-technical-debt review had flagged as a €6.8M annual cost line. Time-to-Hire had held at 64 days across 2023–2025 against a European industrial-peer median of 42 days.

The December 2025 board dossier had identified three specific pressures the three-round envelope would inherit. First, the Q4 2025 works-council pre-consultation had, across the German site, flagged the AI-in-HR rollout as a Q3 2026 consultation item — a calendar that placed the Round-2 human-in-loop commitment squarely inside the works-council field of view. Second, the Q1 2026 EU-AI-Act horizon-scan had classified resume-ranking models in employment as "high-risk" under Annex III — a classification that framed the Round-1 Stratosphere EU-data-residency preference. Third, the Q4 2025 HR-risk register had priced the class-action exposure at €9M and had flagged a Glassdoor-seeded crisis as a foreseeable Round-3 event — a pricing that framed the Round-3 explain commitment.

## Industry and Market Context

The HR-technology-transformation landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2020–2024 European-HCM rollout evidence base had, across thirty-seven industrial-group transformations, placed EU-data-residency-complete platforms at a Q3 post-go-live Compliance score of 92 against 74 for non-compliant platforms — a pattern that anchored the Round-1 Stratosphere preference. Second, the 2021–2025 AI-in-HR evidence base had, across twenty-two resume-ranking deployments, documented a class-action-exposure differential of €6–12M between human-in-loop and fully-automated configurations — a pattern that framed the Round-2 human-in-loop commitment. Third, the 2019–2024 change-and-adoption literature had, across eighteen HR-technology crises, placed explain-and-transparency responses at a ninety-day adoption-curve recovery of 78% against 38% for deny responses — a pattern that framed the Round-3 explain commitment.

Empirically, the post-2021 HR-technology literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, transformations that had deployed AI modules inside a human-in-loop envelope on Talent Acquisition had, across a 2022–2024 sample of 19 European industrial groups, preserved PeopleStack Scores above 78 through works-council consultations in 89% of cases against 34% for fully-automated deployments. Second, transformations that had chosen explain-and-transparency over deny on Glassdoor-seeded crises had, across the same sample, held Time-to-Hire uplift above 15% through the adoption-curve trough in 82% of cases — a pattern that anchored the Round-3 explain recommendation.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Chief Executive Officer had, in the December 2025 board committee, approved a three-round envelope, a PeopleStack Score target of 78, a Compliance floor of 90, a Time-to-Hire uplift target above 15%, and an Attrition-Accuracy target above 70%. The Director of HR Technology had, on January 12, circulated a Round-1 pre-read that recommended Stratosphere People on functional-fit and EU-data-residency. The Head of Talent Acquisition had, on January 18, argued for the aggressive AI configuration on Time-to-Hire grounds. The Chief Compliance Officer had, on January 22, flagged the EU-AI-Act Annex-III classification and argued for the human-in-loop commitment. The German Works Council chair had, on January 28, asked for written assurance that the Round-3 crisis-response playbook would not pre-empt the Q3 2026 consultation window.

### What Reinoso knew

The team had modelled three three-round envelopes. A Stratosphere-plus-Human-in-Loop-plus-Explain Envelope projected PeopleStack 81, Time-to-Hire -18%, Compliance 94. A Nova-plus-Aggressive-plus-Deny Envelope projected a headline Time-to-Hire of -24% but Compliance 62 and €9M class-action exposure. A ClearPath-plus-Human-in-Loop-plus-Redesign Envelope projected PeopleStack 74, Compliance 89.

### What she did not know

Whether the EU-AI-Act implementing-acts calendar would hold at Q2 2026 or slip to Q3 2026; whether the Glassdoor-seeded crisis would arrive inside the Round-3 window or slip to Q1 2027; whether the Stratosphere contractor would hit the Q2 2026 EU-data-residency certification deadline; and whether the German Works Council consultation would expand in scope to cover the Performance-Management nudge engine.

### What others were saying

**The Director of HR Technology**: *"Stratosphere. Functional-fit and EU residency. ClearPath looks cheap until you price the Annex-III compliance gap."*

**The Head of Talent Acquisition**: *"I can get you twenty-four points of Time-to-Hire with the aggressive configuration. Please let me try."*

**The Chief Compliance Officer**: *"Human-in-loop on Talent Acquisition. The AI Act reads the logs; the trade press reads the Glassdoor post."*

**The German Works Council chair**: *"Round 3 must leave Q3 2026 consultation intact. Redesign-by-press-release is not consultation."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Reinoso had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Stratosphere / Human-in-Loop / Explain:** Round 1 Stratosphere People. Round 2 human-in-loop on Talent Acquisition, coarse cohort-level attrition prediction, governed nudge engine. Round 3 explain-and-transparency response on the Glassdoor crisis with a published fairness-audit summary. PeopleStack 81. Time-to-Hire -18%. HR Cost -14%. Attrition Accuracy 72%. Compliance 94. Recommended.

- **Option B — Nova / Aggressive / Deny:** Nova HCM. Aggressive AI configuration. Deny on Glassdoor. Time-to-Hire -24% but Compliance 62, €9M class-action exposure. Rejected.

- **Option C — ClearPath / Human-in-Loop / Redesign:** ClearPath. Human-in-loop. Redesign on Glassdoor. PeopleStack 74, Compliance 89. Rejected as it trades defensibility for ambition the CEO did not ask for.

Reinoso's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final PeopleStack Score was Round 1 — the Stratosphere commitment that cleared the EU-data-residency gate and framed the Round-2 human-in-loop architecture the works council would ultimately endorse.
