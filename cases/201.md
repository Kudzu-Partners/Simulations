## Abstract

Corporación Vanguardia was, in the case universe, a LatAm industrial-retail conglomerate headquartered in Monterrey with USD 4.2B in 2025 consolidated revenue, 18,400 employees across a manufacturing arm (forty-one production lines across Mexico, Colombia and Peru), a retail arm (412 stores across the same three countries) and a logistics-services arm, a 2025 Coalition-Strength reading of 20% on a Q4 2025 transformation-readiness diagnostic (100 = full senior-leader sponsorship across every operating function), a 2025 Momentum reading of 25 on the same diagnostic (composite captures cadence, communication reach and visible-win frequency), a 2025 Trust reading of 30 against a 2024 baseline of 38, and a Q4 2025 board-approved USD 500M eighteen-month digital-transformation envelope. The board had positioned the envelope as the company's response to three overlapping pressures: a 2024–2025 ten-point gross-margin compression in the retail arm relative to two regional digital-native challengers, a 2025 Mexican plant-floor labour-relations diagnostic that had priced two named manufacturing sites as walkout-exposed inside an eighteen-month aggressive-rollout window, and a 2025 Group-IT readiness audit that had documented an eighteen-month critical path on the cross-arm CRM, MES and data-platform stack. The case opens at 6:24 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Corporación Vanguardia Monterrey head office as **Andrés Quiroga-Beltrán**, a 49-year-old Chief Transformation Officer, prepares the Round-1 vision-launch commitment. Five hundred and forty days separated him from the October 14, 2027 board-level transformation-close review.

It is a math problem."* Five hundred and forty days separated him from the October 14, 2027 board-level transformation-close review.

## The Protagonist

Quiroga-Beltrán, 49, had assumed the Chief-Transformation-Officer role at Corporación Vanguardia on January 8, 2026, after twenty-one years across operations, retail-network rebuild and post-merger-integration leadership at a São Paulo retail conglomerate, a Bogotá-based industrial group and a Mexico City management consultancy. He had, in 2020–2023, led the post-merger digital integration at the São Paulo conglomerate that had crossed a Coalition-Strength threshold of 68% inside eleven months and had landed an eight-country CRM rollout three weeks ahead of schedule. The Chief Executive Officer had recruited him in November 2025 specifically on two capabilities: his documented discipline of defaulting to coalition-first vision launches rather than top-down keynotes that had priced two peer transformations into Trust-collapse spirals, and his willingness to default to retail-pilot quick wins rather than plant-floor capital programmes that had exhausted Momentum reserves before the second wave. His 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the Coalition-Strength trajectory, 25% to the Momentum floor and 20% to the Trust floor.

## The Decision

Quiroga-Beltrán had to commit, by the May 6 transformation-steering-committee pre-brief, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–5.

1. **Round 1 — Vision-launch sequencing.** Two archetypes: coalition-first (Quiroga-Beltrán meets Miguel and Patricia individually in the two weeks before the all-hands keynote and books their public co-sign — Composite 78), or vision-first top-down (the CEO opens with a fifty-minute keynote and lets the coalition catch up — Composite 28, named-resistance probability above 60%). Working envelope: coalition-first.
2. **Round 2 — Stakeholder coalition build.** Three vectors: Patricia-and-Miguel pairing (book the Retail-CRM pilot first and let Manufacturing follow — Composite 76), Carlos-Manufacturing-first (book the plant-floor MES first and try to convert Carlos from –40 to neutral — Composite 36, USD 120M sunk before the first visible win), or balanced-three-way (split the envelope across all three — Composite 52, no anchor narrative). Working envelope: Patricia-and-Miguel.
3. **Round 3 — Quick-win anchoring.** Two vectors: USD 80M retail-CRM six-week pilot in Patricia's territory (Composite 78, Momentum +20, Coalition through the 40% threshold), or USD 80M plant-floor MES eighteen-week programme in Carlos's territory (Composite 38, Momentum +6 by the time the pilot ships). Working envelope: retail-CRM.
4. **Round 4 — Resistance containment after Plant-3 union walkout.** Three vectors: re-engage-and-redesign (Quiroga-Beltrán pulls Carlos in as co-sponsor of the Plant-3 redesign and absorbs a four-week schedule cost — Composite 76, Trust restoration), press-on (escalate to corporate and continue the rollout — Composite 22, Trust collapse to below 20), or pause-the-programme (Composite 44, Momentum collapse). Working envelope: re-engage-and-redesign.
5. **Round 5 — Three-country capability embedding.** Two vectors: full-stack rollout to Colombia and Peru with a country-CTO-led local-coalition cycle (Composite 78, Coalition above 72%), or Mexico-only ring-fence (Composite 48, eighteen-month transformation reverts to nine-month programme). Working envelope: full-stack three-country.

On Quiroga-Beltrán's working model, a Coalition-First / Patricia-and-Miguel / Retail-CRM / Re-Engage / Three-Country envelope delivered Coalition Strength 72%, Momentum 78, Trust 64, Composite 74.

## Company Background

Corporación Vanguardia had been founded in Monterrey in 1972 and had, across fifty-four years, built a USD 4.2B-revenue LatAm industrial-retail conglomerate organised into three operating arms: a manufacturing arm (USD 1.9B revenue, forty-one production lines across Mexico, Colombia and Peru) led by **Carlos Esquivel-Ramírez** as SVP Manufacturing; a retail arm (USD 1.6B revenue, 412 stores across the same three countries) led by **Patricia Salomón-Vásquez** as SVP Retail; and a logistics-services arm (USD 0.7B revenue) led by a corporate VP. The group's Group-IT function was led by **Miguel Tinajero-Esparza** as Head of IT. The 2024–2025 cycle had left the manufacturing arm at a defended-cost-leadership posture, the retail arm at a 920-bps gross-margin gap to two regional digital-native challengers, and the logistics arm at a Group-IT-dependent breakeven. The Q4 2025 board-approved USD 500M eighteen-month digital-transformation envelope — the PowerShift programme — had been positioned explicitly as a five-round rebuild of the cross-arm CRM, MES, data platform and store-and-plant capability stack.

Three Q1 2026 stakeholder readings were named in the March 2026 transformation-readiness dossier. First, Carlos Esquivel-Ramírez had logged a Q4 2025 readiness reading of –40 on the 2023-rebased composite (–100 = full opposition; +100 = full sponsorship), citing a 2018 manufacturing-modernisation programme that had under-delivered against committed plant-floor productivity gains. Second, Patricia Salomón-Vásquez had logged +30, citing the retail-arm CRM gap and a Q3 2025 store-network sales diagnostic. Third, Miguel Tinajero-Esparza had logged +50, citing the eighteen-month critical path on the data-platform rebuild.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2019–2025 LatAm digital-transformation literature had, across 38 documented multi-arm conglomerate programmes above USD 250M, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, coalition-first vision launches with Head-of-IT and one demand-side SVP co-signed before the all-hands keynote had crossed a Coalition-Strength threshold of 60% inside the first six months in 72% of cases, against 24% for vision-first top-down envelopes. Second, retail-CRM pilots above USD 50M with a six-to-eight-week shippable window had, in 68% of documented cycles, crossed the Momentum threshold of 60 inside the first nine months — plant-floor MES programmes below the eighteen-month shippable window had crossed the same threshold in 22% of cases. Third, mid-cycle plant-floor resistance events that had been absorbed through re-engage-and-redesign envelopes with the originally-resistant SVP elevated to co-sponsor had restored Trust to above the pre-event reading in 64% of documented cases, against 14% for press-on envelopes.

Empirically, the post-2022 change-management literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, three-country capability-embedding cycles led by country-CTOs with a documented local-coalition handover had sustained eighteen-month Coalition readings above 70% in 66% of documented cases. Second, transformations that had ended below a Coalition-Strength reading of 50% had reverted to single-country ring-fences within twenty-four months in 78% of documented cases.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the December 2025 session, approved the USD 500M envelope and the October 14, 2027 close review. The Head of Strategy had, on March 5, delivered the stakeholder-readiness diagnostic. The Head of Internal Communications had, on March 12, delivered the vision-launch sequencing options. The Group-IT Head Miguel Tinajero-Esparza had, on March 19, delivered the data-platform critical-path workbook. Patricia Salomón-Vásquez had, on March 26, delivered the retail-CRM pilot scoping.

### What Quiroga-Beltrán knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. A Coalition-First / Patricia-and-Miguel / Retail-CRM / Re-Engage / Three-Country envelope projected Composite 74. A Vision-First / Carlos-First / MES / Press-On / Mexico-Only envelope projected Composite 14. A Coalition-First / Balanced-Three-Way / Split-Quick-Win / Pause / Three-Country envelope projected Composite 46.

### What he did not know

Whether Carlos Esquivel-Ramírez would absorb a Plant-3 redesign sponsorship inside the four-week window the team had scoped; whether the Plant-3 union would hold a second walkout window in Q3 2026; whether the Patricia-led Retail-CRM pilot would clear the six-week shippable window with a measurable conversion lift inside Mexico; and whether the Colombia and Peru country leadership benches would produce a country-CTO inside the Q3 2026 selection window.

### What others were saying

**Patricia Salomón-Vásquez (SVP Retail)**: *"Open with retail. Six weeks. Show the store-network conversion lift. Then everyone else has a pattern to copy."*

**Miguel Tinajero-Esparza (Head of IT)**: *"Coalition first. If the keynote opens before Patricia and I have publicly co-signed, we lose the IT council in week three."*

**Carlos Esquivel-Ramírez (SVP Manufacturing)**: *"Don't open with a CRM pilot. Open with a plant. The factory is where the company is."*

**The Chief Executive Officer**: *"Change is not a memo. It is a math problem."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Quiroga-Beltrán had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Coalition-First / Patricia-and-Miguel / Retail-CRM / Re-Engage / Three-Country:** the recommended envelope. Coalition 72%. Momentum 78. Trust 64. Composite 74. Recommended.
- **Option B — Vision-First / Carlos-First / MES / Press-On / Mexico-Only:** the legacy envelope. Composite 14. Rejected — Trust-collapse probability above 70% and named-resistance assured.
- **Option C — Coalition-First / Balanced-Three-Way / Split-Quick-Win / Pause / Three-Country:** the cautious envelope. Composite 46. Rejected — no anchor quick win and Momentum below the 60 floor.

Quiroga-Beltrán's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 4 — the union-walkout containment — because it was the round that decided whether the Trust line continued downward into the eighteen-month cycle or whether Carlos became a sponsor inside the second wave.
