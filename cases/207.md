## Abstract

Meridian Logistics was, in the case universe, a European third-party-logistics operator headquartered in Rotterdam with EUR 265M in 2025 revenue, EUR 37.9M in 2025 EBITDA (14.3% margin), a 2025 asset base of EUR 182M across warehousing, cross-dock and last-mile nodes, a 2025 customer book of 212 named industrial-and-retail accounts, and a Q1 2026 strategic review that had framed the operator as a 2026–2028 mid-cap 3PL consolidation candidate. The Q1 2026 investment-banking-coverage team at a mid-market European sell-side desk had opened an eight-round valuation cycle on Meridian — the ModelOff modelling championship — against a Q2 2026 client-presentation deadline, a documented Q1 2026 management-guidance range of 8–10% Revenue CAGR and 14–15% EBITDA margin, and a named M&A-target (a EUR 620M-revenue German-based 3PL consolidation target with EUR 140M in guided synergies). The Q1 2026 desk charter had positioned the ModelOff cycle as the coverage team's response to three overlapping pressures: a 2024–2025 valuation-diagnostic cycle that had priced mid-market coverage-team recommendations at 68% in-market accuracy against a top-quartile benchmark of 82%, a Q4 2025 client-feedback scorecard that had flagged scenario-range width as the single largest coverage-quality gap, and a Q1 2026 M&A-pipeline that had priced a EUR 14M coverage-fee opportunity on the named Meridian–German-3PL synergy-target transaction. The case opens at 6:28 a.m. on Tuesday, April 21, 2026, at the sell-side desk's Rotterdam office as **Ingrid Solheim-Rønning**, a 27-year-old Junior Analyst on the Logistics Coverage team, prepares the Round-1 revenue-and-margin-guidance commitment.

## The Protagonist

Solheim-Rønning, 27, had assumed the Junior-Analyst-Logistics-Coverage seat at the sell-side desk on July 1, 2025, after an MSc in Corporate Finance at Stockholm School of Economics and two years at a Stockholm-based M&A-advisory firm. She had, across her first nine months on the desk, built three coverage-model rebuilds on industrial-distribution operators that had cleared the desk's named model-quality scorecard (above 80% on the quarterly peer-review) and had been named in two client-facing coverage pitch books. The Head of Logistics Coverage had pre-approved her to lead the ModelOff cycle in December 2025 specifically on two observations: her documented discipline of defaulting to management-guidance-middle revenue-and-margin assumptions rather than upper-band postures that had over-promised coverage cases by 18–26 points in peer junior-analyst cycles, and her willingness to default to Market-weight-and-YTM WACC constructions rather than Book-weight-and-Coupon shortcuts that had misread cost-of-capital by 80–140 bps in adjacent coverage rebuilds. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the Total Score on the ModelOff cycle, 25% to the quarterly model-quality scorecard and 20% to the named-deal-coverage-fee conversion.

## The Decision

Solheim-Rønning had to commit, by the 9:00 a.m. coverage-team stand-up, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–8.

1. **Round 1 — Revenue-CAGR and EBITDA-margin guidance.** Three archetypes: 9% CAGR and 14.5% margin calibrated-middle (Round Score 88), 10% CAGR and 15% margin aggressive (Round Score 62, model-defensibility exposure), or 8% CAGR and 14% margin conservative (Round Score 74, EV understatement). Working envelope: 9% and 14.5%.
2. **Round 2 — Opex-and-D&A lock.** Calibrated at D&A 4.2% of revenue, SG&A 8.8% of revenue.
3. **Round 3 — CapEx and working-capital discipline.** CapEx 5% of revenue, DSO 55 days, DPO 45 days (Round Score 72). Working-capital lock contributes –EUR 2M to EV base.
4. **Round 4 — Terminal-value calibration.** 2.5% perpetual-growth-rate anchor against long-run European-logistics-sector GDP-plus-50-bps. Working envelope: 2.5% anchor.
5. **Round 5 — WACC calibration.** Three archetypes: Market-weights-and-YTM envelope at 9.4% (Round Score 84), Book-weights-and-Coupon envelope at 7.8% (Round Score 38, cost-of-capital understatement), or Market-weights-and-Coupon envelope at 8.4% (Round Score 56, partial). Working envelope: Market-weights-and-YTM at 9.4%.
6. **Round 6 — Base-Downside-Upside scenario honesty.** Base EUR 612M, Downside EUR 418M, Upside EUR 790M (Round Score 82). Working envelope: scenario-honesty.
7. **Round 7 — Sensitivity-table and tornado analysis.** One-point WACC swing ±EUR 84M EV; one-point margin swing ±EUR 42M; one-point terminal-growth swing ±EUR 58M.
8. **Round 8 — M&A-target NPV commitment.** Two vectors: Pass — NPV negative at management case (Composite 78, recommendation-defensible), or Buy — NPV positive at upside case (Composite 28, upside-case-only). Working envelope: Pass.

On Solheim-Rønning's working model, a Calibrated-Guidance / Working-Capital-Discipline / Market-YTM-WACC / Scenario-Honesty / Pass-on-M&A envelope delivered EV EUR 612M, Equity/Share EUR 18.40, Total Score 84, Composite 78.

## Company Background

Meridian Logistics had been founded in Rotterdam in 2001 and had, across twenty-five years, built a EUR 265M-revenue European third-party-logistics operator organised into three operating lines: a warehousing-and-value-added-services line (EUR 140M revenue, 18 named warehouses), a cross-dock-and-transportation line (EUR 82M revenue), and a last-mile-and-e-commerce line (EUR 43M revenue, a 2024-acquired business). The 2023–2025 cycle had left the operator at a defended-cost-leadership posture in warehousing, an outsourced-cross-dock model in transportation, and a sub-scale last-mile line that the Q1 2026 coverage note had priced as either consolidation-target or divestment-candidate. The Q1 2026 management guidance had priced Revenue CAGR at 8–10% and EBITDA margin at 14–15% — the coverage team's ModelOff cycle was scoping a Q2 2026 client-presentation valuation against that range.

Three Q1 2026 coverage inputs were named in the April 2026 ModelOff workbook. First, the January 2026 earnings call had confirmed the 8–10% guidance and had priced a 50-bps margin-expansion case as the upside scenario. Second, the February 2026 German-3PL consolidation-target (a EUR 620M-revenue 3PL) had surfaced as a potential acquisition target at EUR 140M in guided synergies, EUR 18M in integration cost, and a 36-month synergy-ramp. Third, the March 2026 logistics-sector peer-multiple diagnostic had priced Meridian at 7.8× EV/EBITDA against a peer-median of 8.4×.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2019–2025 European-3PL valuation literature had, across 48 documented coverage-model cycles on EUR 150M–EUR 500M 3PL operators, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, calibrated-guidance envelopes inside the management range had cleared client-defensibility reviews in 78% of documented cases, against 46% for upper-band aggressive postures. Second, Market-weight-and-YTM WACC constructions had produced WACC readings 80–140 bps above Book-weight-and-Coupon shortcuts in 72% of documented coverage rebuilds, and had preceded upgrade-rating analyst reviews in 68% of cases. Third, Base-Downside-Upside scenario honesty with scenario ranges above 35% of the Base EV had cleared client-defensibility reviews in 74% of documented cases, against 38% for point-estimate envelopes.

Empirically, the post-2022 investment-banking coverage literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, M&A-target NPV-negative recommendations at management-case synergy levels had proved correct in 68% of documented transactions inside a 24-month window. Second, sensitivity-and-tornado analyses naming the three top EV-drivers (WACC, margin, terminal growth) had closed client-defensibility gaps in 74% of documented cases.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The coverage team had, in the January 2026 offsite, approved the ModelOff cycle and the Q2 2026 client-presentation window. The Head of Logistics Coverage had, on March 5, approved the guidance-middle calibration approach. The senior equity-research analyst had, on March 12, delivered the peer-multiple diagnostic. The M&A-coverage partner had, on March 19, delivered the German-3PL consolidation-target brief. The equity-capital-markets partner had, on March 26, delivered the WACC-calibration guardrails.

### What Solheim-Rønning knew

The team had modelled three eight-round envelopes. A Calibrated-Guidance / Working-Capital-Discipline / Market-YTM-WACC / Scenario-Honesty / Pass-on-M&A envelope projected Composite 78. An Aggressive-Guidance / Loose-Working-Capital / Book-Coupon-WACC / Point-Estimate / Buy-on-Upside envelope projected Composite 22. A Conservative-Guidance / Working-Capital-Discipline / Market-YTM-WACC / Scenario-Honesty / Pass-on-M&A envelope projected Composite 56.

### What she did not know

Whether the Q2 2026 earnings update would reaffirm the 8–10% revenue guidance; whether the German-3PL consolidation target would clear the Q2 2026 antitrust review; whether the working-capital discipline would hold if the last-mile line was divested inside the forecast horizon; and whether the Q2 2026 long-run-interest-rate regime would hold the 9.4% WACC anchor.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Logistics Coverage**: *"Calibrate at nine. Not ten. The model earns its credibility by not chasing upper-band guidance."*

**The M&A-Coverage Partner**: *"If NPV is negative at management case, the recommendation is pass. Write it cleanly. Explain the synergy haircut."*

**The Senior Equity-Research Analyst**: *"Market-weights and YTM. Book-weights and coupon are the shortcut that a junior analyst never takes."*

**The Equity-Capital-Markets Partner**: *"A model is not a prediction. It is a contract with your assumptions."*

## Options Under Consideration

Solheim-Rønning had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Calibrated-Guidance / Working-Capital-Discipline / Market-YTM-WACC / Scenario-Honesty / Pass-on-M&A:** the recommended envelope. EV EUR 612M. Equity/Share EUR 18.40. Total Score 84. Composite 78. Recommended.
- **Option B — Aggressive-Guidance / Loose-Working-Capital / Book-Coupon-WACC / Point-Estimate / Buy-on-Upside:** the shortcut envelope. Composite 22. Rejected — model-defensibility fails on client review.
- **Option C — Conservative-Guidance / Working-Capital-Discipline / Market-YTM-WACC / Scenario-Honesty / Pass-on-M&A:** the understated envelope. Composite 56. Rejected — EV below peer-multiple floor.

Solheim-Rønning's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 5 — the WACC calibration — because it was the round that decided whether EV anchored at EUR 612M (9.4% WACC) or drifted to EUR 780M+ (7.8% Book-Coupon shortcut) against a peer-multiple ceiling the coverage team could not defend.
