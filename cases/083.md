## Abstract

Grupo Latina CPG was, in the case universe, a LATAM consumer-goods manufacturer with a 2025 revenue of USD 468M, a debt-of USD 80M, an equity of USD 185M, a cash position of USD 28M, a 9.5% share of its Central home market, a brand equity score of 50, an innovation score of 40, and an installed production capacity of 120,000 tons per year. On Monday, March 2, 2026, the Board approved a twelve-quarter integrative strategic commitment inside which the Chief Executive Officer would commit, each quarter, seven operating levers — price index, marketing spend, R&D spend, sales-force headcount, capex, market-expansion decision, and dividend payout ratio — against three AI-driven rival firms in the Central market and across a sequenced entry into two additional markets (Andina and Austral). Rodrigo Villanueva, a 52-year-old former President of the Central Business Unit promoted to Chief Executive Officer on October 1, 2025, faced a Board mandate that the share targets of the operating committee had to be squared against the shareholder EPS and total-return expectations across three annual Board cycles. The case opens at 6:10 a.m. on Monday, March 9, 2026, at the Grupo Latina corporate headquarters in a Central-market capital city, as Villanueva prepares the Q1 2026 commitment package.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Year-1 price-index, marketing, R&D, and sales-force commitment against three AI-driven rivals and diagnose when a below-index entry buys share at a defensible margin cost.
2. Analyze the Year-2 Andina market-entry decision and the Year-3 Austral market-entry decision, and recommend the capex, marketing, and R&D allocation consistent with a three-market operator by Quarter 12.
3. Diagnose the Year-3 competitor price-war event on the Central flank, and design a defense that does not counter-price but lifts marketing and R&D to anchor the brand-equity-plus-innovation moat.
4. Design a twelve-quarter capital-structure and dividend envelope that paces debt, capex, and payout through market entries and market defense without breaching the Board's leverage ceiling.
5. Recommend a complete twelve-quarter envelope, identify the single quarter most correlated with the Year-3 share-price and EPS outcome, and write the shareholder-letter close that frames the three-market, seven-lever trajectory.

On the morning of **Monday, March 9, 2026**, **Rodrigo Villanueva** sat at the corner conference table of the Grupo Latina headquarters in a Central-market capital city at 6:10 a.m. and opened the Q1 2026 commitment package his Chief Strategy Officer had finalized at 2:17 a.m. Seven levers faced him on every quarterly decision — each interacting with the other six, each compounding across twelve quarters, each read by the Board against an AI-driven rival the competitive-intelligence office had modeled with enough fidelity that Villanueva treated the three rivals as counterparties rather than scenarios. Twelve days separated him from the March 21 Board commitment.

---

## The Protagonist

Rodrigo Villanueva, 52, had joined Grupo Latina in 2007 as Brand Director for the flagship coffee-and-beverage brand and had been promoted to President of the Central Business Unit in 2016. A graduate of the Universidad de Chile with an MBA from IESE, he had, in his CBU presidency, grown the Central book from USD 238M to USD 392M across 2016–2024 through a three-stage combination of a premiumization push in the flagship brand, a category-extension into snacking, and a gross-margin-by-SKU rationalization that had lifted the CBU gross margin from 29% to 34%. His October 2025 appointment letter from the Board had read: "Your predecessor ran a strong Central operator. The company the market will reward in 2028 is the one that carries three live markets and has monetized the innovation it paid for through four price wars."

---

## The Decision

Villanueva had to commit, by the March 21 Board meeting, the Q1 2026 commitment package and the Year-1 intent across the four quarters:

1. **Price Index, Marketing, and R&D** — whether to enter Q1 at a price index of 98 (1% below the sector average) to buy share, marketing at USD 18M per quarter, and R&D at USD 10M per quarter; whether to raise marketing to USD 22M in Q4 as the Andina entry drew closer.
2. **Sales-Force Size and Capex** — whether to hire 20 sales representatives in Q1 to carry the share lift through the year, and whether to commit USD 5M Q1 capex for incremental Central-market capacity and USD 15M Q4 capex for Andina-entry capacity.
3. **Market Expansion and Dividend** — whether to enter Andina in Q4 2026 at a USD 12M entry cost, hold Austral for Q10, and run a 15% dividend payout through Year 1 with a lift to 25% by Year 3.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across the twelve quarters. A Q1 package that anchored the below-index entry, the USD 18M marketing, the USD 10M R&D, the 20-rep sales-force hire, the USD 5M capex, a Q4 Andina entry, and a 15% dividend produced, on Villanueva's working model, a Q4 2026 revenue of USD 138M per quarter, a Central share of 10.8%, a brand equity of 58, an innovation score of 47, and a twelve-quarter composite of 79 out of a 100-point envelope — including a Year-3 Austral entry at a premium price point and a share-price close of USD 58.

---

## Company Background

Grupo Latina had been founded in 1971 as a single-brand coffee operator in the Central-market capital and had, across fifty-five years, built the coffee-beverage-snacking portfolio the case studied. The 2025 revenue of USD 468M was distributed as flagship coffee 54%, beverage extensions 22%, snacking 18%, and private-label co-packing 6%. The manufacturing footprint comprised three Central-market plants with a combined 120,000-ton annual capacity, a single R&D-and-innovation center, and a sales-and-distribution organization of 210 field representatives. The company's competitive position had rested, since 2015, on the flagship coffee's 54% share of the premium Central-market segment, a gross margin of 33%, and a 12% Return on Equity that had, in 2024–2025, compressed to 11% under AI-rival entry.

The Board's February 2026 strategy dossier had identified three specific pressures the twelve-quarter envelope would inherit. First, three AI-driven rival firms — modeled as Rival 1 (AI-native challenger), Rival 2 (traditional incumbent running an AI augmentation), and Rival 3 (regional multi-category aggressor) — had, in Q4 2025, been assessed at a combined share-taking capacity of 4.2 share points across the Year-2 window under a no-response scenario. Second, the Andina market — a neighboring three-country addressable market of USD 920M — had opened to a new-entrant regulatory window in Q2 2026 that would close by Q3 2027; the case's Year-2 Andina entry would land inside the window. Third, the Austral market — a two-country southern footprint of USD 480M — had, across 2024–2025, premiumized at a 6% annual rate, creating a Year-3 premium-entry opportunity.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM CPG landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the twelve-quarter envelope sat inside. First, the 2024–2025 AI-rival wave had, across a commercial-summary sample of forty-eight LATAM CPG cohorts, produced a share-taking capacity of 2.4–3.8 points per year under a no-response scenario — a benchmark the case's Year-2 Rival-2 price-war simulated at the upper end. Second, the LATAM multi-market premiumization trajectory had, across 2022–2025, carried premium-tier growth at 2.1-times the composite-category growth rate, with the Austral regional sample running 1.4 points ahead of the Central and Andina benchmarks; a finding that anchored the Year-3 Austral entry at a price index of 102 rather than the Central 100. Third, the Andina regulatory entry-window had, across 2015–2024, closed four times at an average commercial cost to late entrants of USD 28M in foregone Year-1 share equivalent — a benchmark that informed the Year-1 Q4 Andina commitment rather than a deferred Year-2 entry.

Empirically, the post-2018 multi-market CPG literature had two robust findings the twelve-quarter envelope had to respect. First, CEOs who had, in a competitor price-war event, held price and lifted marketing and R&D rather than counter-priced had, across a 2019–2024 sample of thirty-one LATAM CPG price-war events, preserved brand equity at a 72% rate against a 34% preservation rate for counter-price responders. Second, CEOs who had sequenced capex with market-entry commitments — capacity ahead by one quarter rather than lagging by one — had, across the same sample, absorbed Year-2 demand without stockouts at a 81% rate against a 48% rate for capacity-lagging sequences.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Board had, in the February 2026 strategy off-site, approved a three-year revenue target of USD 720M, a three-year EPS target of USD 3.00 annualized, and a three-year brand-equity-plus-innovation composite target of 138 (70 + 68). The Chief Strategy Officer had, on March 2, circulated a Year-1 pre-reading that proposed the below-index entry and the Q4 Andina commitment. The Chief Financial Officer had, on March 4, signed off on the 15%-dividend-and-USD-5M-Q1-capex envelope with a note that the USD 80M debt ceiling would hold only if the Q4 2026 Andina entry converted at the model's 6.8% share by Q6. The Chief Innovation Officer had, on March 7, pushed for a USD 12M Q1 R&D commitment rather than the USD 10M baseline — a position the CEO had held under review.

### What Villanueva knew

The team had modeled three twelve-quarter envelopes. A Share-First Envelope (Q1 price 98, Q1 marketing 18, Q1 R&D 10, Q4 Andina entry, Q10 Austral entry, Year-3 dividend 25%) projected a Q12 revenue of USD 218M, an EPS of USD 3.12, a brand equity of 71, an innovation of 68, a capacity of 180,000 tons, a debt of USD 85M, a share price of USD 58, and a composite of 79. A Price-Power Envelope (Q1 price 100, higher marketing and R&D, delayed Andina to Q5) projected a Q12 composite of 74 with a tighter Year-1 EPS. A Conservative Envelope (Q1 price 100, Andina deferred to Year-2, Austral off-table) projected a Q12 composite of 67 with a protected Year-1 EPS.

### What he did not know

Whether Rival 2's Q7–Q9 price cut would land at the modeled 5% depth or at a deeper 8% that the second-source intelligence had referenced; whether the Andina distribution partner's exclusivity commitment would hold through the Year-2 Rival-3 regional aggression or would renegotiate at a higher co-investment cost; whether the Austral regulatory environment would hold its premium-category tariff posture through the Year-3 entry window; and whether the Board's own composition — two of the seven directors rolling off at the September 2026 AGM — would remain supportive through the three-year arc.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Strategy Officer**: *"Share-First Envelope. Below-index entry, Q4 Andina, Q10 Austral. The three-market operator at Q12."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"Hold the USD 80M debt ceiling. Fund Andina Q4 capex from operating cash flow plus a modest bond issuance. No dividend lift until Year-3."*

**The Chief Innovation Officer**: *"R&D to USD 12M in Q1, not USD 10M. The innovation score will decide the Year-3 Austral premium."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Villanueva had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Share-First:** Q1 price 98, marketing 18, R&D 10, 20-rep hire, capex 5; Q4 Andina entry, Q10 Austral entry; dividend 15% Y1, 20% Y2, 25% Y3. Q12 composite 79. Aligns with CSO. Recommended.

- **Option B — Price-Power:** Q1 price 100, marketing 22, R&D 12, Andina deferred to Q5. Q12 composite 74. Higher Y1 margin, lower three-market trajectory.

- **Option C — Conservative:** Q1 price 100, Andina deferred to Year-2, Austral off-table, dividend flat 20%. Q12 composite 67. Balance-sheet-preserving. Growth-losing.

Villanueva's private view, not yet shared with the Board, was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single quarter most correlated with the Q12 composite was the Q7 price-hold response to Rival 2's price cut — specifically, the simultaneous marketing lift to USD 28M, the R&D lift to USD 16M, the +15 sales-force hire, and the dividend pause that would carry through the Year-2 stress quarter without breaching the debt ceiling.
