## Abstract

CONGLAT S.A. was, in the case universe, a LATAM industrial conglomerate headquartered in São Paulo with four operating segments — CIMENTA (Brazilian cement, 520,000 tCO₂e/yr), FERRACO (Brazilian steel, 610,000 tCO₂e/yr, 120,000 tonnes/yr of export to the European Union), MINANDINA (Colombian mining, 188,000 tCO₂e/yr), and MEXMANUFACTURAS (Mexican manufacturing, 83,000 tCO₂e/yr). Total 2024 Scope 1 and 2 emissions of 1,401,000 tCO₂e. The 2030 Science-Based-Targets-initiative target — validated in Q1 2026 — required a forty-two-percent absolute-emissions reduction against a 2020 baseline, implying a 2030 ceiling of 812,580 tCO₂e. Helena Cardoso, appointed Chief Sustainability Officer in January 2025 after fourteen years at CONGLAT in finance and operating roles, faced six annual decision rounds (2025 through 2030) inside a four-carbon-instrument envelope: the Brazilian Sistema Brasileiro de Comércio de Emissões (SBCE) entering its first compliance year in 2025 with free allocation declining through Phase 3; the Colombian carbon tax at USD 12/t; the EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), transitional through 2025 and financial beginning 2026; and the SECO Mexico carbon-pricing pilot. The case opens at 6:08 a.m. on Monday, April 6, 2026, as Cardoso prepares the 2025-close-and-2026-plan package for the April 21 Board Sustainability Committee.

5. Recommend a complete Year-1 package and identify the 2029 green-hydrogen-pilot-go decision most likely to meet the 2030 SBTi milestone with cushion.

## The Protagonist

Helena Cardoso, 48, had joined CONGLAT in 2011 as a corporate-treasury analyst and had, across fourteen years, rotated through roles in FERRACO's financial planning group (2013–2016), MINANDINA's operations (2016–2019), and the central-office Strategy team (2019–2024). She had, in 2022, co-led the SBTi-target-setting working group that had submitted the forty-two-percent-reduction commitment. A chemical-engineering graduate of the Universidade de São Paulo with an MBA from the MIT Sloan School, she had, in the MIT program, taken the energy-transition and carbon-markets specialization. The CEO's January 2025 onboarding instruction had been: "Get to eight-hundred-twelve in 2030 with abatement, not offsets."

## The Decision

Cardoso had to commit, by 9:00 a.m. on Tuesday, April 21, the Year-1 closing and Year-2 planning package the Board Sustainability Committee would treat as the six-year operating anchor:

1. **2025 Year-End Reconciliation** — ratification of the USD 26.4M abatement-budget execution across the two negative-cost measures (LED-BMS retrofits and Mexican solar PV), the SBCE-shortfall allowance purchase, and the Colombian carbon-tax payment.
2. **2026 Instrument Response** — the USD 32M 2026 abatement budget across the four instruments, with CBAM entering financial mode on 1 January 2026 and the clinker-substitution measure newly unlocked after SBTi validation.
3. **Board-Committee Escalation Package** — accompanying memo, six-year roadmap, and a DPI scorecard that feeds the quarterly Board pack.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across six years. A Year-1 package that executed the two negative-cost measures, purchased the SBCE-shortfall allowances at the prevailing USD 20/t price, paid the Colombian carbon tax, and framed 2026 as an Internal-Carbon-Price and clinker-substitution year produced, on the internal carbon-finance model, a 2028 REDD+-controversy-survivable portfolio quality score, a 2029 green-hydrogen-pilot go-decision inside the cushion, and a 2030 SBTi milestone landing at 794,000 tCO₂e — eighteen thousand tonnes inside the 812,580 ceiling.

## Company Background

CONGLAT had been founded in 1968 as a São Paulo-based cement business and had, across fifty-eight years, expanded into the four-segment industrial conglomerate the case studied. 2024 group revenue was USD 4.82B, EBITDA USD 720M, and net income USD 286M. The four-segment-emissions breakdown — CIMENTA 37%, FERRACO 44%, MINANDINA 13%, MEXMANUFACTURAS 6% — concentrated the decarbonization pathway on the two Brazilian industrial segments. The 2020 baseline had been 1,416,000 tCO₂e; the 2024 position at 1,401,000 tCO₂e represented a 1.1-percent reduction against baseline across four years — the "slow-start" profile that had triggered the January 2025 CSO-mandate-appointment and the USD 28M Year-1 abatement-budget allocation.

The SBTi validation process, which had been submitted in late 2024 and was completed in February 2026, had required CONGLAT to commit to a 2025–2029 annual-reduction trajectory with interim checkpoints in 2027 and 2029. The internal Climate Analytics team had modelled the pathway against the four instruments; the two-times-effect-per-dollar finding on clinker substitution (SBCE-plus-CBAM abatement) had been the single structural insight that had convinced the CEO to expand the 2026 budget from USD 24M to USD 32M.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM industrial-decarbonization landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Year-1 decision sat inside. First, the Brazilian SBCE had entered its first compliance year in 2025 with 80% free allocation against verified emissions, a trajectory to 0% through Phase 3 by second-half 2029, and a spot price range across 2025 of USD 9–22/t with a Q4 average of USD 20/t. Second, CBAM entered its financial-phase on 1 January 2026 with a covered-sector scope that included the FERRACO steel book; the at-point calculation — 120,000 tonnes of export at 1.82 tCO₂e/tonne embedded intensity and an EU-ETS-equivalent of USD 52/t — implied a USD 11.4M gross liability that the Brazilian-domestic-carbon-price-paid deduction (at the Q4 SBCE price of USD 20) reduced to USD 9.3M net. Third, the REDD+ credit-quality landscape had shifted through 2025: a Q3 investigative article on Amazonian-REDD+-additionality had triggered Verra-review actions against nine large-scale projects and had, in the Q4 offset-market benchmarking data, compressed REDD+ credit prices by 38%.

Empirically, the post-2015 carbon-markets research had two robust findings the Year-1 package had to respect. First, Internal Carbon Price calibration anchored to the forward-curve mid-point of the primary exposure instrument — rather than to the depressed spot of a thinner instrument — produced, across a 2018–2024 sample of forty-two Global-2000 industrial companies, Internal-Carbon-Price-consistency scores 420 basis points higher on the SBTi-progress-tracking benchmark. Second, offset-portfolio diversification away from single-method concentration (above 25%) had, in the same sample, reduced Credit Quality Score volatility by 44% through controversy events.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The SBTi target had been committed in 2022 under the previous CEO. The SBTi validation had been submitted in late 2024 under the current CEO. The CSO role had been created in January 2025. The USD 28M Year-1 abatement budget had been ratified in the February 2025 off-site. The 2025 execution had closed at USD 26.4M (the USD 1.6M slack reflected a deferred fleet-electrification decision that the SBTi-validation unlocked as a 2026 measure). The 2026 budget at USD 32M had been approved in the December 2025 budget cycle.

### What Cardoso knew

The internal Climate Analytics team had modeled three Year-1-closing-and-Year-2-plan packages. A Minimum-Action Package (purchase SBCE allowances to fill the shortfall, defer clinker-substitution to 2027, maintain offset portfolio at current 30% REDD+ weight) projected 2030 landing at 842,000 tCO₂e — thirty thousand tonnes above the SBTi ceiling and a probable missed-milestone public-disclosure event. A Structural Package (two negative-cost measures executed, SBCE-shortfall allowance purchase, 2026 clinker-substitution immediate execution, Internal-Carbon-Price at USD 50/t in 2027, REDD+ reallocation in 2028, green-hydrogen pilot in 2029) projected 2030 at 794,000 tCO₂e — inside the ceiling by eighteen thousand tonnes with DPI at 88. A Full-Stretch Package (all measures executed at peak economics, DAC pilot in 2030) projected 2030 at 782,000 tCO₂e with DPI at 90 and a USD 28M incremental spend against the Structural Package.

### What she did not know

Whether the SBCE price in 2026–2027 would track the base-case USD 20-to-USD 28 trajectory or spike into the USD 30s on a Phase-3-acceleration announcement; whether CBAM would, in its first financial year, enforce the at-point calculation on the FERRACO steel book as drafted or phase the enforcement through a compliance-grace window; whether the Verra review on Colombian Amazonian REDD+ projects would conclude with the additionality-defensible ruling (thirty-percent probability on the internal-offset-team's model) or with the portfolio-retirement ruling (fifty-five percent); and whether the 2029 green-hydrogen pilot at FERRACO would land at the internal cost-per-tonne modelled (USD 1,320/t) or the optimistic case (USD 920/t).

### What others were saying

**The Group Chief Financial Officer**: *"Structural Package. The clinker-substitution Year-2 move is the single highest-NPV project in the entire 2026 capex book. Double-effect per dollar. Do not defer."*

**The CIMENTA Chief Executive**: *"Clinker substitution and biomass co-firing — both — in 2026 and 2027. The SBCE-auction-failure window in 2027 is a buying opportunity, not a price signal."*

**The FERRACO Chief Executive**: *"The 2029 green-hydrogen pilot is priced at the Board ceiling. The strategic option on zero-emissions steel in the 2030s is not."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Cardoso had narrowed the space to three Year-1-closing-and-Year-2-plan packages.

- **Option A — Minimum-Action:** Allowance-heavy, defer clinker to 2027, maintain 30% REDD+ weight. 2030 at 842,000 tCO₂e. Fails SBTi milestone. Cheapest today.

- **Option B — Structural Package:** Two negative-cost measures executed, 2026 clinker-substitution immediate, ICP at USD 50/t in 2027, REDD+ reallocation in 2028, green-hydrogen pilot in 2029. 2030 at 794,000 tCO₂e. Aligns with Group CFO and CIMENTA CEO positions.

- **Option C — Full-Stretch:** All measures at peak economics plus DAC pilot. 2030 at 782,000 tCO₂e. USD 28M incremental spend against Option B.

Cardoso's private view, not yet shared with the Group CFO, was that Option B was the right Year-1-closing-and-Year-2-plan package and that the single subsequent decision most correlated with the 2030 SBTi milestone outcome was the 2028 REDD+ reallocation — specifically, replacing fifty percent of the REDD+ position with Brazilian biogas credits and Mexican cookstove credits before the Verra review concluded, and adding methane capture at the Colombian operations as the new direct-abatement measure.
