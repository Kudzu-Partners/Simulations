## Abstract

Brindlecote Components Ltd was, in the case universe, a Wolverhampton-headquartered Tier-1 automotive components supplier with GBP 240M 2025 revenue, 1,420 employees across two manufacturing plants and one distribution centre, 12 active OEM and Tier-1 customers including three German prestige accounts and two Korean EV accounts, an ISO 9001:2015 certificate first awarded in 2017 and re-issued in 2020 and 2023, and a surveillance audit scheduled for July 14, 2026 by the firm's external certification body. The 2024–2025 cycle had left the operator with a deteriorated QMS: a Q1 2026 internal pre-audit gap analysis commissioned by the COO had surfaced eleven nonconformities across clauses 4 to 10, including four Majors (empty risk register against clause-4 context; three-month-overdue calibration records against clause-7 monitoring-and-measuring-resources; irregular supplier-evaluation cadence against clause-8 externally-provided-processes; eighteen-month gap in management review against clause-9) and seven Minors (unevidenced policy communication under clause 5, absence of measurement frequency on quality objectives under clause 6, missing training records under clause 7, absence of effectiveness verification on corrective actions under clause 10). The case opens at 7:42 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Wolverhampton plant as **Sandra Whittaker-Olufemi**, a 47-year-old Quality Director and Management Representative, prepares the ten-week ISOnavigator commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the eleven nonconformities against the Major-versus-Minor classification heuristic in ISO 9001:2015 (a Major is a nonconformity that affects the system's ability to achieve intended results; a Minor is an isolated lapse that does not break the system) and articulate why an empty risk register is Major while an unevidenced policy communication is Minor.
2. Analyse the cross-clause dependencies in the standard (clause 4 context flows into clause 6 planning; clause 9 performance evaluation closes the loop back into clause 6; the corrective-action effectiveness-verification gap under clause 10 is the reason recurring defects keep recurring) and articulate why fixing one Major often unlocks two or three Minors.
3. Diagnose the calibration-records and supplier-evaluation findings as classic operations-discipline failures dressed as documentation gaps, and recommend the remediation envelope that fixes the discipline rather than the documentation.
4. Respond to the management-review gap (no review in 18 months at the highest level of the QMS), and design the management-review cycle that closes the loop and produces the documented evidence the external auditor will require.
5. Design the ten-week remediation plan that converts the four Majors and seven Minors into a 72% readiness composite, sequences the work so the Majors close before the surveillance audit and the Minors close before the next recertification audit in 2026, and produces the evidence pack the certification body's lead auditor can stand behind.

## The Protagonist

Whittaker-Olufemi, 47, had been the Quality Director and Management Representative at Brindlecote Components Ltd for nine years, had been promoted from Quality Manager in 2017 in the run-up to the original ISO 9001:2015 certification, and had subsequently led two recertification cycles (2020, 2023) and four interim surveillance audits. She had, in 2018–2021, led the IATF 16949 readiness work that had subsequently been suspended after a customer-account reshape and had retained the broader ISO 9001:2015 framework as the operator's primary QMS. She had, in 2024, co-authored with the Wolverhampton plant's Operations Manager an internal practitioner guide titled *Closing the Loop, Not the Binder* that had been adopted as the operator's reference rubric for nonconformity remediation. The Chief Operating Officer had assigned her to the ten-week ISOnavigator window on three considerations: her documented discipline of converting documentation-gap findings into operations-discipline remediation rather than into binder-thickening exercises; her command of the cross-clause dependencies that allowed a single Major closure to unlock two or three Minor closures; and her willingness to commit to a 72% readiness composite grounded in evidence rather than to an aspirational reading that would crumble under the lead auditor's walkthrough.

## The Decision

Whittaker-Olufemi had to commit, by the 5:00 p.m. Operations Steering Committee pre-brief, the seven-clause remediation envelope and the directional intent for the July 14 surveillance audit.

1. **Clause 4 — Context of the Organization.** Major: empty risk register; stakeholder map exists on paper only. Working envelope: populate risk register with named risks, owners and review cadence inside three weeks; refresh stakeholder map with documented engagement evidence inside two weeks.
2. **Clause 5 — Leadership.** Minor: quality policy exists but no documented evidence that management has communicated it. Working envelope: management cascade memorandum, three shop-floor town-halls, signed acknowledgement form on file inside one week.
3. **Clause 6 — Planning.** Minor: quality objectives tied to business plan but no measurement frequency. Working envelope: assign monthly / quarterly / annual cadence to each objective; update QMS objectives register inside two weeks.
4. **Clause 7 — Support.** Major: calibration records three months overdue. Major: training records incomplete on a proportion of operators. Working envelope: emergency calibration sweep across the Wolverhampton and Coventry plants inside three weeks; training-record reconciliation inside four weeks.
5. **Clause 8 — Operation.** Major: supplier evaluation conducted annually in theory, erratically in practice; one Tier-2 supplier has not been evaluated since 2023. Working envelope: full supplier-evaluation refresh on the top 30 suppliers inside six weeks.
6. **Clause 9 — Performance Evaluation.** Major: no management review in eighteen months. Working envelope: convene the missing management review on May 6 with the full standard agenda; subsequent reviews quarterly.
7. **Clause 10 — Improvement.** Minor: corrective actions raised but no effectiveness verification. Working envelope: introduce a 60-day effectiveness-verification step on every corrective action; back-fill the verification on open corrective actions inside three weeks.

On Whittaker-Olufemi's working model, the seven-clause envelope produced a 72% readiness composite at the start of the ten-week window, projected to 88% at the surveillance audit on July 14 conditional on closing the four Majors before the audit and on having the seven Minors in evidenced-remediation status. The lead auditor's recommendation on July 14 would be continued certification with no Majors outstanding and a Minor finding count consistent with the operator's recertification trajectory.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2015–2025 quality-management literature on UK Tier-1 automotive suppliers produced three findings the engagement had to respect. First, calibration-records lapses had been the single most-cited Major between 2018 and 2025, accounting for ~23% of all Major findings. Second, management-review-cadence gaps above twelve months were the second most-cited Major (~17%) and co-occurred with at least one further Major on the cross-clause dependency chain in 64% of cases. Third, corrective-action effectiveness-verification gaps accounted for the recurring-defect patterns the operators flagged in their own internal-audit reports in 71% of cases.

The post-2022 automotive-quality literature added two findings. OEM-customer audits scheduled within 60 days of an ISO 9001:2015 surveillance audit typically cross-reference the certification-body findings — a clean July 14 outcome would materially de-risk the August OEM-customer audit. And ten-week remediation windows on Major-finding counts of three to six cleared continued-certification recommendations in 74% of cases, while shorter windows or higher counts cleared only 41%.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Chief Operating Officer commissioned the internal pre-audit gap analysis in December 2025. The eleven-nonconformity register and the calibration-records audit confirming the three-month overdue status had landed in March. The Operations Steering Committee Chair had asked Whittaker-Olufemi to bring the ten-week remediation envelope to the April 22 pre-brief.

### What Whittaker-Olufemi knew

An empty risk register against clause 4 is a Major because the rest of the QMS flows from it; a three-month-overdue calibration-records lapse is a Major because the operating discipline has broken, not the documentation; an eighteen-month gap in management review is a Major because the loop is not closing; and the seven Minors collectively form the evidence pattern that an external auditor will read as a culture issue. A 72% readiness reading at the start of the window can land at 88% on July 14 if the work is sequenced correctly.

### What she did not know

Whether the May 6 management review would close cleanly or surface a fifth Major (the customer-quality complaint pattern on the Coventry lighting-component line could escalate); whether the Tier-2 supplier-evaluation refresh would surface additional supplier-quality issues; whether the OEM customer's August audit would absorb the surveillance audit findings cleanly or open a parallel inquiry; and whether the new Korean EV accounts would impose customer-specific quality requirements on top of the ISO 9001:2015 framework.

### What others were saying

**The Quality Manager**: *"Calibration sweep first. Three weeks. Then training records. Then suppliers. Major to Major to Major."*

**The Quality Engineer**: *"The risk register is empty because nobody has owned it. Populate it with named owners and a review cadence — the rest follows."*

**The Operations Manager**: *"The management review on May 6 is the unblocker. Without it, clause 9 stays Major and the audit goes nowhere."*

**The Chief Operating Officer**: *"ISO 9001 is not a binder. It is a habit. Earn the certificate by closing the loop."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Whittaker-Olufemi had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Ten-week remediation envelope / four Majors closed before audit / seven Minors in evidenced remediation / 72% to 88% readiness:** the recommended envelope. Operations-discipline first, documentation second. Recommended.
- **Option B — Documentation-thickening envelope:** the binder-only variant. Rejected — fixes paper but not discipline; the lead auditor's walkthrough on the shop floor would surface the same patterns.
- **Option C — Aggressive envelope (close all four Majors and seven Minors inside ten weeks):** the maximalist variant. Rejected — risks the supplier-evaluation refresh becoming superficial under time pressure, which would surface as a finding pattern in 2027.

Her private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the clause most correlated with the eventual certification recommendation was Clause 9 — Performance Evaluation — because the May 6 management review was the unblocker that closed the QMS loop and made the entire system inspectable as an operating habit rather than as a documentation exercise.
