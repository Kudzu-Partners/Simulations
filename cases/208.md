## Abstract

StreamFlow was, in the case universe, a European subscription-streaming platform headquartered in Amsterdam with 2,000,000 paid subscribers across fifteen European markets, EUR 320M in 2025 annual recurring revenue, a 2025 monthly-active-subscriber ratio of 71%, a Q1 2026 churn rate of 2.4% monthly, a 2025 product-experimentation cycle of 84 A/B tests across the year, and a 2025 post-mortem that had documented that only 42% of named-shipped experiments had produced sustained post-ship KPI lifts when re-measured at the 90-day horizon. The Q1 2026 product leadership had approved a four-round experimentation-discipline rebuild — the TestLab programme — and had positioned it as the platform's response to three overlapping pressures: a 2024–2025 documented drift toward sample-size-and-duration shortcuts (10k users and 1 week became the modal test in 2025), a 2025 multiple-comparison-correction audit that had shown 68% of named-winning tests had not applied Bonferroni or FDR corrections against five-or-more secondary metrics, and a 2025 novelty-effect audit that had shown that 34% of named-winning tests had been re-measured at +0 lift by the fourth week. The case opens at 6:42 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the StreamFlow Amsterdam head office as **Priya Venkatesan-Rao**, a 36-year-old Head of Experimentation, prepares the Round-1 sample-size-and-duration commitment.

## The Protagonist

Venkatesan-Rao, 36, had assumed the Head-of-Experimentation role at StreamFlow on October 1, 2024, after twelve years across product analytics, statistical-methods leadership and growth experimentation at a Berlin-based subscription-media group and a Dublin-based consumer-subscription platform. She had, in 2021–2024, led a 240-experiment annual cycle at the Berlin group that had sustained a 72% sustained-post-ship-lift rate at the 90-day horizon against a streaming-sector baseline of 42%. The Chief Product Officer had recruited her in August 2024 specifically on two capabilities: her documented discipline of defaulting to four-week-duration filter envelopes rather than first-week-read shortcuts that had priced two peer teams into false-positive-rich ship cycles, and her willingness to default to Bonferroni-corrected multiple-comparison envelopes rather than uncorrected postures that had consumed credibility capital in adjacent product teams. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 35% to the annual Correct-Decisions count, 25% to the post-ship-lift sustained-at-90-day rate and 20% to the Type-I-Error ceiling.

## The Decision

Venkatesan-Rao had to commit, by the 9:30 a.m. experimentation-cycle kickoff, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–4.

1. **Round 1 — Sample-size-and-duration discipline.** Three archetypes: 50k-users-and-4-weeks-with-Bonferroni-and-wait (Composite 78, Correct Decisions likely), 25k-users-and-2-weeks-with-FDR-and-weekly-peek (Composite 58, moderate), or 10k-users-and-1-week-without-correction-and-daily-peek (Composite 24, Type-I-Error elevated). Working envelope: 50k-and-4-weeks.
2. **Round 2 — Multiple-comparison-correction.** Three vectors: Bonferroni (Composite 76, conservative family-wise error rate), FDR (Composite 68, more power, moderate FWER), or no-correction (Composite 22, false-positive-rich). Working envelope: Bonferroni.
3. **Round 3 — Novelty-effect filtering.** Two vectors: four-week-duration filter (Composite 76, novelty-effect-filtered lift reading), or first-week-read (Composite 28, novelty-effect mistook as lift). Working envelope: four-week-filter.
4. **Round 4 — Correct-decisions commitment.** Two vectors: three-of-four-correct-decisions archetype (Composite 74) or four-of-four (Composite 58, one-false-negative risk from over-conservatism). Working envelope: three-of-four.

On Venkatesan-Rao's working model, a 50k-4-Weeks / Bonferroni / Four-Week-Filter / Three-of-Four envelope delivered Experiments 4, Correct Decisions 3, Composite 74.

## Company Background

StreamFlow had been founded in Amsterdam in 2016 and had, across ten years, built a two-million-subscriber subscription-streaming platform across fifteen European markets with EUR 320M in 2025 ARR, a 2025 customer-acquisition-cost of EUR 28, a 2025 LTV of EUR 184, and a 2025 experimentation-team of twenty-two product-analysts, four statistical-methods specialists and eight product managers running an 84-test annual cycle. The 2023–2025 cycle had left the platform at a defended mid-tier positioning against two top-quartile streaming platforms, and the Q4 2025 post-mortem had priced the experimentation-quality gap as the single largest contributor to the retention-KPI gap to the peer cohort.

Second, the Q1 2026 novelty-effect audit had priced 34% of 2025-winning tests at +0 lift by the fourth week. Third, the Q1 2026 experimentation-team diagnostic had surfaced the peek-strategy discipline as the single largest bias driver inside the Correct-Decisions distribution.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2019–2025 product-experimentation literature had, across 52 documented subscription-media experimentation programs above 500,000 subscribers, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, sample-size-and-duration disciplined envelopes (50k+ users, 4-week duration) had sustained a 72–84% Correct-Decisions rate in documented cycles, against 38–52% for 10k-1-week shortcut envelopes. Second, Bonferroni-corrected multiple-comparison envelopes against five-or-more secondary metrics had held the family-wise Type-I-Error rate below the nominal 5% in 88% of documented cases, against 42% for uncorrected envelopes that had surfaced 2–3 false positives per experiment. Third, novelty-effect filters executed at the four-week duration had sustained post-ship-lift-at-90-days above 68% in documented cases, against 28–36% for first-week-read shortcuts.

Empirically, the post-2022 evidence-based-management literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, daily-peek strategies without sequential-analysis corrections (e.g., mSPRT, Alpha-Spending) had inflated the effective Type-I-Error rate by 2.5–3.5× in documented cases. Second, experimentation teams that had ended a year with above 70% Correct-Decisions rate had priced product-velocity gains of 22–28% in the following year against teams below 50%.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The product-leadership council had, in the December 2025 session, approved the TestLab rebuild. The Head of Product Analytics had, on March 8, delivered the 2025 experimentation audit. The Head of Statistical Methods had, on March 15, delivered the multiple-comparison-correction diagnostic. The VP of Product had, on March 22, delivered the novelty-effect audit. The CFO had, on March 29, delivered the experimentation-throughput-vs-correct-decisions cost model.

### What Venkatesan-Rao knew

The team had modelled three four-round envelopes. A 50k-4-Weeks / Bonferroni / Four-Week-Filter / Three-of-Four envelope projected Composite 74. A 10k-1-Week / No-Correction / First-Week-Read / Zero-of-Four envelope projected Composite 12 with a 3.5× inflated Type-I-Error rate. A 50k-4-Weeks / FDR / Four-Week-Filter / Three-of-Four envelope projected Composite 68 — similar but with slightly higher family-wise-error exposure.

### What she did not know

Whether the Q2 2026 experimentation cadence would hold the four-week duration through two upcoming product launches; whether the engineering instrumentation stack would hold a 50k-user sample cell across all fifteen markets; whether the novelty-effect filter would also catch day-of-week seasonality in the sample; and whether the product-team incentive structure would absorb the documented 2.4× slower throughput of the disciplined envelope against the 2025 shortcut baseline.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Product Analytics**: *"Fifty thousand users. Four weeks. Bonferroni. Wait. The round takes longer. The decision is cleaner."*

**The Head of Statistical Methods**: *"Daily peek is the same as running the test four times. The corrected alpha is the one you earn, not the one you wish for."*

**The VP of Product**: *"Novelty effect. The first week is twice the fourth week. We shipped thirty-four percent of our 2025 winners on novelty, not lift."*

**The Chief Product Officer**: *"Rigor is not slow. Peeking is expensive."*

## Options Under Consideration

Venkatesan-Rao had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — 50k-4-Weeks / Bonferroni / Four-Week-Filter / Three-of-Four:** the recommended envelope. Experiments 4. Correct Decisions 3. Composite 74. Recommended.
- **Option B — 10k-1-Week / No-Correction / First-Week-Read / Zero-of-Four:** the shortcut envelope. Composite 12. Rejected — false-positive-rich.
- **Option C — 50k-4-Weeks / FDR / Four-Week-Filter / Three-of-Four:** the FDR envelope. Composite 68. Rejected — slightly above family-wise-error ceiling.

Venkatesan-Rao's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 3 — the novelty-effect filter — because it was the round that decided whether the 34% novelty-winning-ship pattern broke or whether Q2 2026 shipped a fresh cohort of decay-at-90-day experiments against the same 2024–2025 pattern.
