## Abstract

Andina Motriz S.A. was, in the case universe, a multinational industrial manufacturer of motor-control equipment for mining, oil-and-gas, and heavy-industrial customers across Latin America, headquartered in São Paulo, with 2025 group revenue of USD 1.42B, three manufacturing plants (Medellín, Buenos Aires, São Paulo), and a supply base whose single most critical input — the IGBT power-semiconductor module — came from a single Taiwanese supplier, KorTech Ltd., that represented 74% of unit shipments. On Monday, March 2, 2026, the US Bureau of Industry and Security added KorTech to the Entity List, subjecting any product with more than 25% US-origin technology content to a license requirement. Requalification of the European alternative, Infineon, was estimated at nine months and USD 2.1M. Mariana Duarte, appointed Head of Strategy and Geopolitical Risk on January 5, 2026, faced six quarterly rounds across 2026 and the first half of 2027, three live country fronts (Colombia, Argentina, Brazil), and a Board that had been told, at the February 18 strategy off-site, that earnings stability was the single non-negotiable. The case opens at 6:12 a.m. on Thursday, March 12, 2026, as Duarte prepares the Round-1 package for the March 23 Board Risk Committee.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the structural-versus-transactional choice in a US export-controls exposure — internal Export Controls Office versus outside counsel — and diagnose when each dominates.
2. Analyze the Colombian Decreto 847 local-content decision and design a compliance package that protects the Medellín plant's 1,200 jobs without ceding pricing power.
3. Diagnose the compound effect of a border closure and an Argentine FX-repatriation restriction on working capital and design a contingency-stock and intercompany-loan response.
4. Design a credible geopolitical-risk framework in ninety days that converts an ESG-divestment threat into a Board-Level Geopolitical Risk Committee with a country exposure cap.
5. Recommend a complete Round-1 package and identify the single Round-5 MERCOSUR-EU FTA move most likely to reset the manufacturing-network footprint.

Eleven days separated her from the Board Risk Committee meeting. The wire-diagram map on the wall ran Taiwan → Medellín → Buenos Aires → São Paulo. The Entity List designation on KorTech Ltd. had been published nine days earlier.

## The Protagonist

Mariana Duarte, 46, had joined Andina Motriz on January 5, 2026 after eleven years as Vice-President of Strategy at a Santiago-headquartered regional industrial group, where she had led, between 2022 and 2024, a cross-border manufacturing reconfiguration across four countries in response to the first post-pandemic tariff cycle. A graduate of Pontifícia Universidade Católica do Rio de Janeiro with a Master's in International Political Economy from Sciences Po and an executive program on export controls at Georgetown, she had, in her prior role, co-authored a Boardroom-Series note on political-risk leading indicators that the Andina Motriz Board had circulated in the November 2025 search dossier. The CEO's February written mandate had been: "Build the company that survives the next Entity List designation — not the one that plans for it."

---

## The Decision

Duarte had to commit, by 9:00 a.m. on Monday, March 23, the Round-1 geopolitical-risk package the Board Risk Committee would treat as the six-round operating envelope:

1. **Sourcing Strategy** — whether to qualify Infineon as primary IGBT alternative at EUR 2.1M and a nine-month requalification window, whether to maintain KorTech as secondary under license-exception provisions, and whether to dual-source the adjacent capacitor-bank subassembly in parallel.
2. **Organizational Response** — whether to establish an internal Export Controls Office (three FTE, USD 840K first-year cost) or retain Washington-DC outside counsel on a project basis.
3. **Supply-Chain Redesign** — whether to EAR-map the full product catalogue before any pricing move, or re-price the three exposed SKUs first.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across six rounds. A sourcing strategy that qualified Infineon plus dual-sourced the capacitor-bank, combined with an internal Export Controls Office and a full EAR product-catalogue map, produced, on the internal scenario model, a Round-4 ESG-divestment-ultimatum-resolution path with the European 18%-equity bloc retained and the proxy-advisor resolution withdrawn. A sourcing strategy that kept KorTech as primary under license exception, with outside-counsel support only, produced a Round-3 stock-out probability of 41% on the Medellín motor-controls line and a Round-4 ESG pathway with 11–14 percentage points of additional divestment risk.

## Company Background

Andina Motriz had been founded in 1994 as a Brazilian assembler of imported motor-control components and had, across thirty-two years, expanded into the vertically integrated industrial manufacturer the case studied. 2025 group revenue was USD 1.42B, EBITDA margin 14.2%, and total employment 4,640 distributed as 1,240 in Medellín (motor controllers for the mining and oil-and-gas customer sets), 1,080 in Buenos Aires (drive electronics for heavy industry), 1,680 in São Paulo (assembly, engineering, corporate), and 640 in distribution and service. The Medellín plant was, in the 2025 management P&L, the single highest-margin unit at 19.4% EBITDA. The supply-base concentration on KorTech was a legacy of the 2018 standardization decision that had chosen a single tier-1 supplier for IGBT modules to simplify certification across the three plants; seventy-four percent of unit shipments flowed through KorTech, twelve percent through a smaller Japanese alternative, and fourteen percent through an internal stock of legacy components the company had kept as buffer through the 2022 shipping cycle.

The BIS Entity List action on KorTech, published on March 3, 2026, cited alleged transshipment of dual-use semiconductor manufacturing equipment to a sanctioned end-user in the Russian Federation. KorTech had, in a public statement on March 5, denied the allegations and had announced a formal administrative appeal. Under the Entity List mechanics, any product with more than twenty-five percent US-origin technology content required a BIS license for sale to KorTech — a threshold that the IGBT module's downstream integration into Andina Motriz's motor-control systems crossed for thirty-eight percent of the 2025 product mix shipping into the US-sanctioned-adjacent end-user set.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM geopolitical-risk landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Round-1 decision sat inside. First, Colombia's new government had, in February 2026, published Decreto 847, requiring manufacturers above a defined employment threshold to source thirty-five percent of their inputs locally within twenty-four months or face a twelve-percent import tariff on the remaining foreign content; the Medellín plant, with seventy-one percent internationally sourced inputs, was squarely inside the obligation. Second, the Argentine central bank had, in December 2025, tightened the FX-repatriation framework, restricting dividend transfers above ARS 10B without BCRA authorization and imposing a five-point parallel-market discount on cross-currency intercompany balances. Third, the MERCOSUR-EU Free Trade Agreement, after eighteen years of intermittent negotiation, had been provisionally ratified in December 2025 and was expected to enter into force in July 2026, reducing Brazilian industrial-equipment tariffs to the EU from the seven-to-twelve-percent band to the zero-to-two-percent band.

Empirically, the political-risk research had two robust findings the Round-1 package had to respect. First, manufacturers that had established internal Export Controls Offices at the point of first BIS action had, across a 2018–2024 sample of thirty-one multinational events, faced second-cycle license-denial rates of 14% against 42% for companies relying on outside-counsel-only responses. Second, country-exposure caps at twenty-five percent had, across a 2020–2025 sample of forty-six industrial companies, reduced the composite-risk-score volatility by 38% with a measured 180-basis-point drag on revenue growth — a trade that the ESG-divestment literature rewarded and that the capital-markets literature quietly absorbed.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Board had, in the December 2025 off-site, approved the creation of the Head of Strategy and Geopolitical Risk position on the explicit instruction of the Chair of the Risk Committee, a retired career diplomat. Duarte had been appointed on January 5. Her first six weeks had been spent on a cross-country immersion — Medellín in week two, Buenos Aires in week three, São Paulo in weeks four through six. The Entity List designation on KorTech had been published on March 3, inside the first week of her strategy-planning process. The CEO had, on March 4, asked her to deliver the Round-1 package in nineteen days.

### What Duarte knew

The internal team had modeled three Round-1 packages. A Minimum-Disruption Package (KorTech maintained, outside-counsel only, re-price three SKUs first) projected Round-3 Medellín stock-out probability 41%, Round-4 ESG-divestment resolution 58%, composite Round-6 score 94/144. A Structural Package (Infineon qualified, Export Controls Office established, full EAR map) projected Round-3 stock-out probability 12%, Round-4 ESG resolution 88%, composite Round-6 score 132/144. A High-Optionality Package (Infineon plus a second Japanese alternative at incremental USD 1.3M, Export Controls Office, full EAR map, dual-sourced capacitor bank) projected Round-3 stock-out 8%, Round-4 ESG resolution 92%, composite Round-6 score 134/144 but a Round-1 cash cost of USD 4.2M against the USD 3.1M Round-1 envelope.

### What she did not know

Whether the Decreto 847 thirty-five-percent local-content target would be enforceable as drafted or, as the Colombian Ministry of Commerce had signalled off-record, enforced on a plant-by-plant negotiated timeline; whether the Colombia-Venezuela border closure that the internal political-risk team had placed at twenty-seven percent probability for the Round-3 window would land as a two-week disruption (base case) or a three-month disruption (downside); whether the European 18%-equity bloc, led by three Nordic pension funds, would accept a Board-Level Geopolitical Risk Committee and a twenty-five-percent country exposure cap as the ninety-day deliverable, or would escalate to a full divestment vote; and whether the MERCOSUR-EU FTA would enter into force on the expected July 2026 date or slip to Q4.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Supply Chain Officer**: *"Structural Package. Qualify Infineon. The license-exception path on KorTech is a six-month holding pattern that will end with the BIS designating a second Taiwanese supplier. Pay the USD 2.1M now."*

**The Head of the Medellín Plant**: *"Full Decreto 847 compliance. Twelve percent tariff is not absorbable at the current motor-control price point. The local supplier ecosystem is thin but buildable."*

**The Chair of the Risk Committee**: *"Country exposure cap at twenty-five percent. Board-Level Geopolitical Risk Committee with an external member. Do not accept an ESG-divestment resolution at the AGM."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Duarte had narrowed the space to three Round-1 packages.

- **Option A — Minimum-Disruption:** KorTech maintained under license exception, outside counsel only, re-price the three exposed SKUs first. Round-6 composite 94/144. Cheapest today. Structurally exposed to the next BIS cycle.

- **Option B — Structural Response:** Infineon qualified, internal Export Controls Office established, full EAR product-catalogue map. Round-6 composite 132/144. Aligns with the Chief Supply Chain Officer's position. USD 3.0M Round-1 cost.

- **Option C — High-Optionality:** Infineon plus second Japanese alternative, Export Controls Office, full EAR map, dual-sourced capacitor bank. Round-6 composite 134/144. USD 4.2M Round-1 cost above envelope. Incremental two-point composite gain against USD 1.1M of extra cash.

Duarte's private view, not yet shared with the CEO, was that Option B was the right Round-1 package and that the single subsequent decision most correlated with the Round-6 outcome was the Round-5 reset of the manufacturing-network footprint to position São Paulo as the MERCOSUR-EU FTA export base — specifically, the reversal of the pre-existing Argentina divestment study and the acceleration of São Paulo capacity by 18% through the FTA entry window.
