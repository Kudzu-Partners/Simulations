## Abstract

Solara Technologies was, in the case universe, a European advanced-electronics group headquartered in Munich with EUR 2.1B in 2025 revenue, 6,800 employees across Germany, Poland, Spain and the Czech Republic, three operating divisions (consumer audio, industrial sensors and specialty-magnet components) and a 2025 competitive-intelligence function that the July 2025 board review had documented as under-resourced against a three-competitor cohort. The Q4 2025 board had approved a EUR 800k four-round competitive-intelligence envelope — the IntelOps programme — and had positioned it as the group's response to three overlapping pressures: a Q2 2025 Korean-competitor product launch in the specialty-magnet-components category that had taken the group by surprise and had cost an estimated EUR 42M in lost 2025 revenue, a 2024–2025 market-share cycle that had documented the group's three competitors closing a five-point lead in the consumer-audio category, and a 2026 CES-Las-Vegas window (January 6–9, 2026) — now reframed for the Q1 2026 CES cycle and a forward-looking April-to-December 2026 intelligence horizon — that had priced a second Korean-competitor product launch in the same category. The case opens at 5:58 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Solara Munich head office as **Ewa Zielińska-Havel**, a 43-year-old Head of Strategic Intelligence, prepares the Round-1 channel-budget commitment. Two hundred and fifty-nine days separated her from the January 6, 2027 CES window.

## The Protagonist

Zielińska-Havel, 43, had assumed the Head-of-Strategic-Intelligence role at Solara Technologies on September 1, 2024, after seventeen years across market intelligence, OSINT analysis and war-gaming leadership at a Warsaw-based defence-electronics group and a Brussels-based competitive-intelligence consultancy. She had, in 2021–2024, led the Warsaw group's three-year signal-detection cycle that had pre-empted two named competitor launches inside the 2022–2023 defence-electronics cycle and had crossed a Confidence threshold of 78% on weak-signal fusion inside eleven months. The Chief Strategy Officer had recruited her in July 2024 specifically on two capabilities: her documented discipline of defaulting to pattern-weighted channel allocations rather than balanced-five-channel envelopes that had priced two peer intelligence units into surprise-launch exposures, and her willingness to default to war-gaming pre-empts rather than wait-and-see postures that had consumed market-share reserves in adjacent groups. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 35% to the Composite IntelOps Score, 25% to the Threat-Level-reduction trajectory and 20% to the Confidence floor.

## The Decision

Zielińska-Havel had to commit, by the May 7 strategy-committee pre-brief, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–4.

1. **Round 1 — Channel-budget rebalancing.** Two archetypes: Patents-40-Suppliers-30-Digital-20-Trade-Shows-10-Analysts-0 (Composite 78, signal-per-euro above the pattern-weighted threshold) or Balanced-Five-Channel 20-20-20-20-20 (Composite 42, coverage-as-signal fallacy). Working envelope: pattern-weighted.
2. **Round 2 — Weak-signal fusion.** Two vectors: rare-earth-volume-anomaly + magnet-geometry-patent-filing (Composite 78, Threat Level High → Medium, Confidence 78%) or isolated-signal reading (Composite 38, Threat Level misread). Working envelope: fusion.
3. **Round 3 — War-gaming pre-empt.** Three vectors: CES teaser-launch pre-announcement at Q4 2026 trade show (Composite 78, Threat Level Medium → Medium-Low, Korean-competitor surprise pre-empted), wait-and-see (Composite 44, Threat Level unchanged) or full-launch-match at Q1 2027 (Composite 58, capex exposure). Working envelope: teaser-launch pre-empt.
4. **Round 4 — Early-warning-scorecard embedding.** Two vectors: full-stack (quarterly board-level scorecard, named-signal library, war-game cadence — Composite 78) or ad-hoc (Composite 38, no compounding). Working envelope: full-stack.

On Zielińska-Havel's working model, a Pattern-Weighted / Fusion / Teaser-Launch-Pre-Empt / Full-Stack envelope delivered Total Score 78, Threat Level Medium-Low, Confidence 82%, Composite 76.

## Company Background

Solara Technologies had been founded in Munich in 1987 and had, across thirty-nine years, built a EUR 2.1B-revenue European advanced-electronics group organised into three operating divisions: a consumer-audio division (EUR 0.9B revenue, seventeen European markets), an industrial-sensors division (EUR 0.7B revenue, fifty-four B2B customers) and a specialty-magnet-components division (EUR 0.5B revenue, twelve named rare-earth-magnet customers including three automotive OEMs). The 2023–2025 cycle had left the consumer-audio division at a defended mid-tier positioning, the industrial-sensors division at a specification-based cost-leadership posture, and the specialty-magnet-components division at a rare-earth-supply-constrained growth curve. The Q2 2025 Korean-competitor product launch in the specialty-magnet-components category had been the named surprise event of the 2025 cycle — the Q4 2025 post-mortem had traced the surprise to a balanced-five-channel intelligence allocation that had not weighted patent filings against rare-earth-supplier-volume movements.

Three Q1 2026 events were named in the March 2026 strategy-committee dossier. First, the January 2026 CES-Las-Vegas cycle had surfaced two Korean-competitor provisional-patent filings referencing a new rare-earth-magnet geometry. Second, the February 2026 rare-earth-supplier-volume anomaly — supplier X had ordered twice the usual April-to-June volume — was consistent with a Q4 2026 product launch window. Third, the March 2026 Q1 OSINT scan had surfaced a Korean-competitor R&D-team expansion of twenty-two engineers in the magnet-geometry sub-discipline.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2019–2025 competitive-intelligence literature had, across 36 documented B2B-electronics intelligence cycles above EUR 400k in annual budget, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, pattern-weighted allocations that weighted patents above 30% and suppliers above 25% had pre-empted documented competitor launches in 72% of cases, against 28% for balanced-five-channel allocations. Second, weak-signal fusion that coupled two-or-more channel signals into a single named-threat reading had lifted Confidence by 26–38 points above isolated-signal readings in 68% of documented cases. Third, war-gaming pre-empts that had pre-announced a competing product geometry inside the six-month pre-launch window had reduced named-competitor first-quarter market share by 18–28 points in 64% of documented cases.

Empirically, the post-2022 strategic-intelligence literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, intelligence units that had not embedded a quarterly board-level early-warning scorecard had reverted to ad-hoc cycles within eighteen months in 72% of documented cases. Second, intelligence-unit staffing at a one-analyst-per-EUR-200k-revenue ratio had been the baseline for sustained signal-per-euro above the pattern-weighted threshold.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the December 2025 session, approved the EUR 800k envelope and the January 6, 2027 CES-window target. The Head of OSINT had, on March 8, delivered the digital-and-patent scanning plan. The Head of Supplier Intelligence had, on March 15, delivered the rare-earth-volume diagnostic. The Head of Analyst Relations had, on March 22, delivered the analyst-channel scoping (and documented the channel's low signal-per-euro). The Head of Trade-Show Intelligence had, on March 29, delivered the CES-Las-Vegas debrief.

### What Zielińska-Havel knew

The team had modelled three four-round envelopes. A Pattern-Weighted / Fusion / Teaser-Launch-Pre-Empt / Full-Stack envelope projected Composite 76. A Balanced-Five-Channel / Isolated-Signal / Wait-and-See / Ad-Hoc envelope projected Composite 22. A Pattern-Weighted / Fusion / Wait-and-See / Full-Stack envelope projected Composite 58.

### What she did not know

Whether the Korean-competitor launch window would land in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027; whether the rare-earth supplier X would accept a Solara counter-order that would compress the competitor's feedstock window; whether the EU patent office would publish the Korean-competitor provisional filing inside the Q3 2026 window; and whether the Q4 2026 CES teaser launch would hold inside the Solara R&D engineering schedule.

### What others were saying

**The Head of OSINT**: *"Patents forty. Digital twenty. The analyst channel is the last to know. Zero."*

**The Head of Supplier Intelligence**: *"Thirty on suppliers. The rare-earth-volume signal is the one that gives us the launch window."*

**The Head of Trade-Show Intelligence**: *"Ten on trade shows. We send two engineers, not four. And we send them to the Korean booth, not ours."*

**The Chief Strategy Officer**: *"Coverage is not signal."*

## Options Under Consideration

Zielińska-Havel had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Pattern-Weighted / Fusion / Teaser-Launch-Pre-Empt / Full-Stack:** the recommended envelope. Total Score 78. Threat Medium-Low. Confidence 82%. Composite 76. Recommended.
- **Option B — Balanced-Five-Channel / Isolated-Signal / Wait-and-See / Ad-Hoc:** the legacy envelope. Composite 22. Rejected — surprise-launch probability above 70%.
- **Option C — Pattern-Weighted / Fusion / Wait-and-See / Full-Stack:** the cautious envelope. Composite 58. Rejected — Threat Level reduction not captured.

Zielińska-Havel's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 3 — the war-gaming pre-empt — because it was the round that decided whether the Q4 2026 teaser-launch bought the market window or whether Solara absorbed a second Korean-competitor surprise inside the same category.
