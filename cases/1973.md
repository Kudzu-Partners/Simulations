# CBE-Adriatic: The Wish List

**Case Code:** MKT-2026-1973 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1973 — *Custom Program* · **v1.0 · August 2026**

---

On Tuesday 3 February 2026, Vesna Bergant read NovaBank Group's request for proposal twice and asked the centre's controller to cost it. Matic Perko brought the model up at ten past five: delivered exactly as the bank had drawn it, the twelve-month programme returned a contribution margin of 19 per cent. The board of the school that owned CBE-Adriatic had set a floor of 30.

The proposal was due on 24 February, three weeks out, and the three shortlisted schools would present to NovaBank's sponsor that same day. Bergant had until then to settle what CBE-Adriatic would offer, what it would charge, and which of her two most-requested professors would stand in front of the cohort.

Perko's second number was 34: the days of senior teaching time the design took from Boris Kralj and Irena Malovrh. The two professors had 40 left between them for the year, and three other live proposals were written against the same 40.

## The Protagonist

Vesna Bergant, 44, had directed the Centre for Business Excellence since 2022. She had joined nine years earlier from a Vienna advisory firm, running open-enrolment courses before taking over the custom portfolio and building the centre's practice of designing programmes rather than assembling them. She had never lost a bid she led personally. Her variable pay, like every programme manager's, moved with the centre's blended contribution margin and with the share of clients that came back.

## The Decision

Bergant had to decide what CBE-Adriatic would put in front of NovaBank — the shape of the programme, who would teach it, what sat around the teaching, and the price — then hold or revise it as the bank's commercial team and her calendar pushed back.

First she had to settle her reading of the brief. NovaBank's document described a specific solution: residential modules, coaching, a platform, consulting. It could be read as the specification to be delivered item by item; as a request for the school's name and its most senior professors on the bank's announcement; or as a description of an outcome — 90 managers capable of leading the bank's digital transformation — with the route left to whoever won the work. She had to name where the engagement bound first: on price, against two competing schools; on the probability of winning at all; or on the margin floor and the two professors' calendar together. She had to choose her posture in the first conversation: accept the brief as written and address the economics afterwards; cut the design to the cheapest credible version and let price carry the bid; or open the objective for discussion and reserve the right to propose a different shape. And she had to decide how much of the three weeks went to discovery, costing and calendar-mapping before anyone wrote a page.

## The Company and its Setting

CBE-Adriatic was the executive-education arm of a leading Central-European business school in Ljubljana, established in 2004. It delivered open short courses and bespoke in-company programmes across the Adriatic and DACH regions. In 2025 it ran 71 programmes for 38 corporate clients and generated €6.8 million of programme revenue at a blended contribution margin of 34 per cent (see **Exhibit 1**).

It drew on 45 faculty and practitioners — tenured professors, adjunct experts, external consultants. The centre sold customisation and the people in the room, not price, and its scarcest input was senior teaching time: a professor could give only so many custom days a year before degree teaching and research took the rest.

NovaBank Group was a regional bank, the anchor client the centre had chased for three years. Its request covered 90 managers over twelve months, with a multi-year extension on the table and a €540,000 budget. Two other schools were shortlisted.

## The Situation

The bundle NovaBank specified had four parts (see **Exhibit 2**), and Perko had costed each. Together they produced the 19 per cent — fifteen points below what the centre earned in 2025, eleven below the board's floor — on 34 senior teaching days against 40.

One commitment already sat in those weeks. Adriaport Group, a logistics operator that had bought a sustainability-strategy programme every year for six years, had pencilled its next €180,000 cohort into a window overlapping NovaBank's second and third modules. Adriaport also asked for Malovrh.

Four numbers would follow the engagement from the proposal onward. The first was the contribution margin: what was left of the price after the cost of delivering the programme. The second was how many of Kralj's and Malovrh's days it consumed against the 40. The third was how strongly NovaBank's sponsor, Andrej Vidmar, would recommend the centre to a peer, scored from −100 to +100 and unreadable until a design existed. The fourth was the share of the centre's clients that bought again, which stood at 68 per cent.

Bergant knew the shape of the next three weeks. NovaBank's commercial team ran a round of its own after the technical evaluation, and in her experience it arrived with a week left asking for more senior face time and the coaching included at the same €540,000.

If the proposal went out as drawn, CBE-Adriatic would sign twelve months at 19 per cent against a floor of 30, on 34 senior days the calendar had not been cleared for, with three other proposals and Adriaport claiming the same two professors.

## What Bergant Controlled

The programme's shape was hers. She could run the twelve months fully residential, every module off-site; largely online, with live sessions and self-paced material reaching managers at their desks; or blended, some modules residential and the rest digital. She could commit 34 of the two professors' days, the face time the brief specified; 20 days, with adjuncts and practitioners on the remaining modules; or 6 days, with adjuncts and practitioners carrying almost all the teaching. She could include all four add-ons at full depth — one-to-one coaching, a bespoke platform, embedded consulting; none of them, leaving the teaching days to carry the programme; or a narrower set — group coaching, a licensed platform, scoped roadmap consulting. She could price anywhere from €300,000 to €700,000, in €10,000 steps, against the indicated €540,000.

When the commercial round came she could concede, adding the senior days and the coaching at the same price; decline, holding the proposal and the price exactly as written; or trade, granting senior time against a multi-year term, a larger cohort or a public reference. If she traded, she had to name how much future business, from nothing to €600,000, the concession had to buy. Where the marquee name was not essential she could substitute adjuncts and practitioners nowhere, everywhere, or in selected modules. On Adriaport she could release the client, letting the sixth year go; promise both cohorts on the overlapping dates; or re-sequence, moving Adriaport's dates and staffing its non-critical modules with adjuncts.

For the three minutes with Vidmar she could lead on activity — 24 teaching days, 90 coaching hours, a platform, named professors; on price, under budget and under the competing schools; or on what the 90 managers would do differently and the business outcomes behind it. She could ask that both sides judge the programme on end-of-module satisfaction scores, on attendance and certificates issued, or on behaviour and business indicators measured against an agreed baseline. She could frame the work as a single contract, as an under-priced entry she expected renewals to repay, or as the first year of a renewable partnership. And she had to say, as a number, how firmly she held that a programme she designed would serve the bank better than the one it had written.

## Closing

At 17:40 Bergant wrote the four components of the wish list down the left of a sheet and drew three columns beside them: what the centre would deliver, who would deliver it, what it would cost. Perko's model was still open at 19 per cent. Kralj and Malovrh had 40 days between them and three other clients wanting some of the same ones. The proposal was due in twenty-one days, and Vidmar had allowed her three minutes.
