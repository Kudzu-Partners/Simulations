## Abstract

The VP of Logistics and Operations at the case-universe Lima-headquartered mid-cap CPG manufacturer-distributor, approximately USD 520M in 2025 revenue across the Andean region, had been asked, in the February 2026 CFO mandate, to deliver a Q3 2026 board network-redesign brief with a linear-programming audit of five operational scenarios — a production-mix decision on the Lima plant's second-shift allocation, a transportation decision on the three-plant-four-warehouse distribution network, a blending decision at the refinery-adjacent specialty-chemicals line, a workforce-scheduling decision at the two distribution centres in Arequipa and Trujillo, and a portfolio-allocation decision on the distribution-centre capital-budget ahead of the 2027–2028 capex cycle. The Q4 2025 operational audit had flagged a recurring failure mode across the planning function: operational teams were reading the optimal-solution column of the LP output and missing the shadow-price column, producing capacity-expansion recommendations that followed the demand-volume headline rather than the binding-constraint signal. The February 2026 mandate asked the VP of Logistics and Operations to work through all five scenarios, read each LP output through the shadow-price column, and commit to a network-redesign decision the Group board would stake on the dual-variable reading rather than the primal-volume reading. The case opens at 6:58 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Group's Lima head office as **Alejandro Quispe-Saenz**, a 49-year-old VP of Logistics and Operations, prepares the five-scenario commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Scenario-1 production-mix setup (two products, three resources — labor, machine time, material — optimal mix 40/60 units, objective 8,800; binding constraints labor and material with shadow prices 12$/hr and 8$/unit; slack on machine time) and articulate why the shadow-price column tells the operational team which resource to expand next, not the optimal-solution column.
2. Analyse the Scenario-2 transportation setup (three plants × four warehouses; minimum-shipping-cost objective at 12,400; plant-2-capacity shadow price 4$/unit as the capacity-expansion signal) and articulate why the LP output tells the capacity team that the right plant to expand is plant 2 — the plant with the highest shadow price — rather than the plant with the highest demand downstream, and why this is a routinely-missed operational read.
3. Diagnose the Scenario-3 blending setup (refinery-adjacent specialty-chemicals line; three crude streams, two products, quality constraints on octane and sulfur; shadow price on the sulfur constraint) and articulate why the regulator-set sulfur specification is effectively a price signal the LP output makes visible — a looser spec would be worth the shadow-price amount per unit of slack.
4. Respond to the Scenario-4 workforce-scheduling setup (seven-day shift pattern; minimum staffing per shift; shadow price on Tuesday staffing is zero because Tuesday has slack) and the Scenario-5 Markowitz portfolio setup (minimum variance subject to a return target; shadow price on the return constraint gives the variance cost per basis point) and articulate why zero-shadow-price constraints identify where to stop hiring and where expanding the constraint is operationally free.
5. Design the five-scenario commitment that reads each LP output through the shadow-price column and commits to a network-redesign decision that expands plant-2 capacity by 12% (Scenario 2 signal), closes under-utilised Tuesday staffing at two distribution centres (Scenario 4 signal) and reports the sulfur-constraint shadow price to the regulatory-affairs team (Scenario 3 signal) — articulating why the answer is the optimum and the insight is the shadow price, and why the business pivot lives in the dual.

## The Protagonist

Quispe-Saenz, 49, had been VP of Logistics and Operations since 2022, had spent twenty-two years across the Group's manufacturing, distribution and supply-chain functions, and an OR-advisory secondment with a regional logistics conglomerate in 2018–2019. In 2018–2021 he had led the Group's distribution-network redesign from a five-plant-eight-warehouse footprint to the current three-plant-four-warehouse footprint, which had reduced annual distribution cost by approximately 18% and had been driven primarily by the shadow-price reading on a 2019 LP audit. In 2024 he co-authored with his Head of Operations Research *Read the Dual*, an internal playbook that framed LP output as a shadow-price-first artifact. The Group CFO had assigned him the five-scenario commitment on three considerations: his operational command of the 2019 LP-driven redesign; his discipline of leading the board read with the shadow-price column rather than the optimal-solution column; and his willingness to recommend a capacity-expansion call that followed the dual signal rather than the primal-volume headline.

## The Decision

Quispe-Saenz had to commit, by the 4:30 p.m. Group CFO pre-brief, the five-scenario LP commitment and the Q3 2026 network-redesign recommendation.

1. **Scenario 1 — Production mix.** Two products, three resources. Optimal mix 40 and 60 units; objective 8,800. Binding: labor and material. Shadow prices: labor 12$/hr, material 8$/unit. Machine time has slack. The operational read: invest in labor capacity and material-sourcing flexibility; do not invest in additional machine capacity.
2. **Scenario 2 — Transportation.** Three plants, four warehouses. Minimum-shipping-cost objective at 12,400. Shadow price on plant-2 capacity is 4$/unit — the highest across the three plants. The operational read: expand plant 2, not the plant with the highest downstream demand. Expansion envelope of 12% on plant 2 returns NPV approximately USD 14M across the five-year planning horizon.
3. **Scenario 3 — Refinery blending.** Three crude streams, two products, quality constraints on octane and sulfur. Optimal blend. Shadow price on the sulfur constraint tells the regulatory-affairs team what a looser spec would be worth per unit of slack. The operational read: the regulator's sulfur cap is a price signal, and the planning function can quantify the cost of a tighter cap under any future regulatory scenario.
4. **Scenario 4 — Workforce scheduling.** Seven-day shift pattern. Minimum staffing per shift. Optimal schedule minimises labor cost while meeting every coverage constraint. Shadow price on Tuesday staffing is zero — Tuesday has slack. The operational read: no new hires for Tuesday; reallocate the Tuesday staffing budget to the peak-demand shifts at a per-shift NPV gain of approximately USD 180k across two distribution centres.
5. **Scenario 5 — Portfolio allocation.** Minimum-variance portfolio subject to a return target. Shadow price on the return constraint: each basis point of additional target return costs X in portfolio variance. The operational read: the capital-budget allocation can be framed as a variance-for-return trade, and the shadow price makes the trade visible to the CFO in basis points.

On Quispe-Saenz's working commitment, the network-redesign recommendation combined Scenario 2's plant-2 expansion (12%), Scenario 4's Tuesday staffing closure (two distribution centres) and Scenario 3's sulfur-constraint report to regulatory affairs — a three-lever redesign driven entirely by shadow-price readings rather than by primal-volume headlines. Combined five-year NPV of the three levers approximated USD 22M at the Group's cost of capital.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1960–2025 operations-research literature produced three findings the commitment had to respect. First, shadow-price-driven capacity-expansion decisions produced documented NPV outcomes 22–34% higher than primal-volume-driven decisions on comparable LP-anchored network-redesign cases. Second, workforce-scheduling LPs with explicit zero-shadow-price-constraint identification reduced over-hiring by an average 12–18% in documented distribution-centre operations. Third, blending LPs with sulfur-and-octane-style quality-constraint shadow-price reporting produced documented regulatory-affairs NPV readings in the 1–3% of annual revenue range in refinery-adjacent industries.

The post-2010 sensitivity-analysis literature added two findings. Reporting LP output as a shadow-price-first artifact rather than an optimal-solution-first artifact produced board-comprehension readings approximately 41 percentage points higher in a 2022 practitioner study of 94 capex-decision briefings. And combined multi-scenario LP commitments anchored on three-or-more shadow-price signals produced network-redesign NPV outcomes approximately 1.6x higher than single-scenario LP commitments on comparable 2015–2022 redesign cases.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The February 2026 CFO mandate asked for the five-scenario commitment by Q3. The Q1 2026 scenario drafting, the LP-audit on the 2024 capex withdrawal, and the three-lever NPV bridge had all landed by April 15. The Group CFO had asked Quispe-Saenz to bring the commitment to the April 22 pre-brief.

### What Quispe-Saenz knew

Shadow-price-driven capacity-expansion decisions dominate primal-volume-driven decisions on NPV; zero-shadow-price constraints identify where expanding the constraint is operationally free; sulfur-and-octane-style quality-constraint shadow prices give the regulatory-affairs team a price signal on any future regulatory scenario; and shadow-price-first LP-output reporting outperforms optimal-solution-first reporting on board-comprehension readings.

### What he did not know

Whether the Q4 2026 sulfur-cap regulatory consultation would land inside or outside the sensitivity range the Scenario-3 LP had priced; whether the plant-2 expansion envelope of 12% would fit the Q3 capex envelope cleanly or push capital timing into 2027; whether the Tuesday-staffing closure would be absorbed cleanly by the distribution-centre workforce or trigger a union-consultation escalation; and whether the Group board would accept a three-lever redesign framed on shadow-price signals or push for a single-lever headline.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Operations Research**: *"Read the dual. The primal gives the answer; the dual tells you what it is worth. The three-lever redesign follows the dual."*

**The Senior Supply-Chain Analyst**: *"Plant-2 expansion at twelve percent. Tuesday closure at two distribution centres. Sulfur report to regulatory affairs. Three levers."*

**The Group CFO**: *"The board will absorb a shadow-price-driven redesign if the NPV bridge is explicit. Walk them through it line by line."*

**The Head of Regulatory Affairs**: *"The sulfur shadow price gives us the position for the Q4 consultation. Do not release it until the consultation opens."*

## Options Under Consideration

Quispe-Saenz had narrowed the space to three redesign postures.

- **Option A — Three-lever redesign driven by shadow-price signals (plant-2 expansion / Tuesday closure / sulfur report):** the recommended redesign. Combined five-year NPV approximately USD 22M; board-comprehension reading strong; three-lever structure documented against the 2019 precedent. Recommended.
- **Option B — Single-lever redesign (plant-2 expansion only):** rejected — leaves the Tuesday-closure and sulfur-report NPV on the table and fails the "read the dual" discipline that the VP of Logistics and Operations committed to the 2024 audit.
- **Option C — Five-lever redesign covering all five scenarios:** rejected — Scenarios 1 and 5 are decision-support scenarios rather than network-redesign scenarios, and the five-lever framing overcommits the Q3 capex envelope.

His private view was that Option A was the right redesign and that the scenario most correlated with the Group board's eventual capex decision was Scenario 2 — the transportation shadow price on plant-2 capacity — because it was the scenario where the shadow-price read diverged most clearly from the primal-volume-headline read that the 2024 withdrawn capex recommendation had followed.
