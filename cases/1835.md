# Demir Bank A.Ş.: Launching Demir Asistan Without Spending the Brand

**Case Code:** MKT-2026-1835 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1835 — *Trust by Design* · **v1.0 · September 2026**

---

On the morning of 9 June 2026, Elif Aydın, Brand Manager at Demir Bank A.Ş., learned that the Chief Operating Officer had set a six-week deadline: a generative-AI assistant inside the mobile app, live before the Q3 earnings call, where management had already told the Board to expect an "AI efficiency story." The assistant, Demir Asistan, would triage requests, answer questions and resolve simple transactions using message history, transaction data and whatever customers typed into a chat box — complaints, life events, financial worries.

Aydın owned the rollout and the number it could damage. For a decade Demir Bank had published the highest Brand Trust Index in its peer set — 74 out of 100, against a sector median of 61 — built on the promise, printed on every branch window, that service at Demir was *"İnsan gibi"*: human-like. Her team's pre-test on the bank's customer panel had already produced a result she could not unsee: when customers learned they were sharing information with a program instead of a person, the Index fell sharply, and hardest among the customers who funded the balance sheet.

## The Protagonist

Aydın, 44, had joined Demir Bank in 2012 from an advertising agency and had run brand management since 2019, reporting to the Chief Marketing Officer but answering, in practice, to whoever cared most about the Trust Index that quarter. She had spent seven years defending a number most colleagues treated as a nice-to-have, with tracking data to show it moved deposits when it moved. The AI launch was not her idea; it was Finance's, costed against a savings target she had not been consulted on, handed to her with six weeks on the clock.

## The Decision

Aydın had four connected calls to make before the earnings call. First, settle with her team on the mechanism behind the trust drop, the segment most exposed, and the contact types carrying the highest trust cost. Second, configure the assistant's disclosure, confidentiality messaging, persona and data-use scope inside a fixed build budget. Third, set the rule for when the assistant handed a conversation to a person, and how visible that handoff was — a rule tested in public within the six weeks. Fourth, defend one set of numbers to the COO and an independent Board member, reconciling the savings promised with the trust the bank could not afford to lose.

## The Company and its Setting

Demir Bank A.Ş., founded in 1994 and headquartered in Istanbul's Levent district, served 4.2 million retail customers through 310 branches and a mobile app carrying 71% of customer interactions. The bank reported TRY 38.5 billion in net banking revenue and TRY 6.2 billion in net profit in 2025. Its call centre and chat handled 2.4 million service contacts a month at TRY 42 per contact, growing 14% a year while trained agents stayed scarce (see **Exhibit 1**). The assistant was Finance's answer to that cost curve — Aydın's problem to configure without spending the one asset it could not buy back.

## The Situation

The pre-test's numbers were specific. A design with no disclosure, no confidentiality messaging and full data access cut the Brand Trust Index nine points, from 74 to 65 — erasing most of the bank's margin over the sector median. Among the 38% of customers scoring high on data-privacy concern, who held 56% of deposits, the drop was roughly double. Finance had built its case on the assistant fully resolving 55% of contacts without a human, worth TRY 308 million a year; each ten containment points short was worth roughly TRY 56 million less (see **Exhibit 2**). The build was capped at TRY 4.0 million. KVKK, Turkey's data-protection law (Law No. 6698), required a lawful basis and explicit consent before sensitive data could be processed, and a journalist had already filed an information request; a launch that read as hidden would become the reporter's story during the earnings call itself. If Aydın changed nothing, the nine-point drop, the deeper cut among deposit-heavy customers, and that story were the default outcome already sitting in the data.

## What Aydın Controlled

In week one, the team committed to an explanation for the trust drop: that customers inferred their data now reached a far larger, less accountable audience and felt exploited by that; that people simply found talking to a program uncomfortable; that customers feared wrong answers, a matter of competence rather than trust; or that the discomfort was a novelty effect that would fade with use. Alongside it, they named the group most exposed — the privacy-concerned 38% holding 56% of deposits; younger, app-native customers; the customer base as one blended average; or low-balance customers, who complained most — and the contact types carrying the highest trust cost if handled first by the assistant: fraud, hardship and complaints; ordinary balance queries; or every type under one view. A dial recorded how candidly the team named its mechanism rather than reaching for a more comfortable fix.

Configuring the assistant meant four further choices against the TRY 4.0 million cap. Disclosure: a clear statement, before the conversation began, that the customer was chatting with Demir Asistan (TRY 0.4M); a line in the terms and conditions, present but easy to miss (TRY 0.1M); or an interface silent on whether the counterpart was a person or a program (TRY 0.0M). Confidentiality messaging: a stated guarantee, backed by data handling that kept it, that conversations were protected, not used to train models, and narrowly accessed (TRY 1.2M); no on-screen statement either way (TRY 0.0M); or a promise of complete privacy paired with data handling that still fed transcripts into model training (TRY 0.3M). Persona: a named, warm character with a competent, human-like tone (TRY 0.6M); a neutral, unnamed, functional interface (TRY 0.2M); or a persona with a human name and photograph that did not disclose it was a program (TRY 0.5M). Data-use scope: minimised and purpose-limited, with a KVKK opt-in before a sensitive disclosure (TRY 1.4M); standard history behind a generic consent checkbox (TRY 0.6M); or full history, transactions and free text, with no opt-in (TRY 0.3M).

In week three, the fallback rule decided when the assistant handed off: segmented, so fraud, hardship and complaints never started with the assistant while routine queries stayed contained; a single threshold for every contact type, so a fraud victim and a balance inquiry reached the same assistant; or assistant-first, escalating only on request. The human option could sit prominently on every screen, be tucked into a menu, or stay out of view. Mid-round, a screenshot of the assistant answering a distressed customer with a flat, scripted reply began circulating; Aydın had twenty-four hours to acknowledge it and move emotional conversations to people immediately (TRY 0.4M), patch the model quietly and say nothing (TRY 0.1M), or call it an isolated exception and continue unchanged (no cost). A dial, zero to eighty percent, set the share of contacts pushed to the assistant to resolve on its own.

For the week-four pitch, Aydın chose how to frame the numbers for the COO and Board member: a containment figure presented alongside a protected trust floor, assumptions stated; the full TRY 308 million in savings, less room given to the trust risk; or trust first, scaling the assistant back and missing the savings target. Her monitoring commitment could be a trust tracker, an exploitation reading and the opt-out rate, reviewed monthly with named owners; the quarterly Trust Index alone; or no formal tracker, just the app-store rating. For the journalist, she could brief them openly on the disclosure, consent and fallback design; say "no comment"; or frame the launch as a pure efficiency win and steer around data-use questions. A final dial, zero to one hundred percent, recorded how firmly she would commit, in writing, to a recovery plan if the Index slipped below the bank's 70-point floor.

## Closing

By the end of week one, Aydın had a mechanism memo, a segment name, and a rigour score her team had argued over for two days. Five weeks remained, a TRY 4.0 million budget still unspent, and a meeting on the calendar with the COO and a Board member who wanted the containment number and the Trust Index number in one sentence.
