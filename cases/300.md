## Abstract

Distribuidora Andina S.A.C. was, in the case universe, a 35-year-old family-owned Peruvian consumer-goods distributor serving approximately 8,400 bodegas across the four coastal-and-sierra coverage zones — approximately PEN 1.4bn in 2025 revenue, 280 total employees including a 180-person field sales rep force, one 18,500-square-metre warehouse on the Panamericana Sur near Lurín plus four regional cross-docks, a commission-paid rep structure that originated in 1991 and had never been materially re-negotiated, a customer-segmentation map that treated the approximately 11,200 addressable Peruvian bodegas as a four-tier segmentation (the top 42% by revenue served weekly; the bottom 24% underserved below the PEN 180 weekly minimum-order threshold), and a January 2026 announcement from RápidoB2B, a digital-native competitor backed by a Lima-based VC syndicate, that retailers could order direct from manufacturers through a mobile application at sub-Distribuidora-Andina pricing. The three-generation family board had, at its March 2026 extraordinary meeting, committed a PEN 4.2bn three-year transformation envelope and had assigned the CEO an eighteen-month forcing function: prove transformation works by Q3 2027 or face a forced-sale option the board had negotiated with the VC syndicate. The case opens at 4:42 p.m. on Thursday, April 23, 2026, at the firm's San Isidro corporate office as **Gonzalo Paredes-Villafuerte**, a 47-year-old Chief Executive Officer and fourth-generation family member, prepares the three-round commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Read Clayton Christensen's disruption trajectory as applied to RápidoB2B — starts below Distribuidora Andina's minimum-order threshold serving the bottom-24% underserved bodegas, offers simplicity and lower prices at initially inferior service quality, improves faster than incumbent expectations, climbs upmarket — and articulate why the incumbent's rational-looking move (focus on large customers, dismiss the threat) is exactly what the theory predicts and exactly what loses the eighteen-month game.
2. Apply the digital-maturity assessment across five dimensions (Customer Experience 2.8 / beginner; Operations 4.1 / developing; Strategy 3.2; Culture 3.8; Technology 3.4), articulate why every dimension starts behind the digital-native competitor, and articulate why the incumbent cannot fix all five in eighteen months and must choose which two-to-three to invest in against the finite budget.
3. Allocate the PEN 4.2bn budget across three portfolio buckets — quick-win efficiency projects (short payback, limited strategic impact); core customer-facing transformation (the mobile ordering app for bodega buyers); platform bets (the two-sided marketplace connecting manufacturers and retailers) — and articulate why loading any single bucket breaks downstream execution in the other two buckets.
4. Design the Round 2 sales-rep rebellion absorption: when 26 of the 180 reps formally protest the mobile ordering app because it threatens their commission model, either address the human problem through commission redesign and capability uplift, or launch the technology into a culture that will sabotage it. Apply Kurt Lewin's force-field analysis — the driving force is the digital-transformation strategy, the restraining force is the rep's family budget that depends on a commission model 35 years old — and articulate why Lewin's model predicts that the restraining force moves only when the rep sees a path to equivalent or greater total compensation inside the new structure.
5. Commit the Round 3 self-cannibalisation decision against RápidoB2B's upmarket expansion into the top-42% large-bodega segment, trace the two-sided-market platform economics (winner-take-most dynamics in each geographic cluster; near-zero marginal cost on the platform path; linear marginal cost on the traditional-distribution path), and articulate why this digital-transformation frame is distinct from ANDEX (sim 005, Colombian industrial-manufacturing digital roadmap), Banco Central Quisqueda (sim 053, central-bank digital-currency governor), Grupo Nativa GenAI (sim 058, generative-AI management frame), Hotel Mirador Digital (sim 077, hotel-specific digital), and Capstone COMPETE CPG (sim 083, CPG capstone) — and why the LATAM-PYME-in-Spanish primer-MBA frame 300 occupies is deliberately first-lab rather than executive or sectoral.

On the afternoon of **Thursday, April 23, 2026**, **Gonzalo Paredes-Villafuerte** arrived at Distribuidora Andina's San Isidro corporate office at 4:42 p.m., walked past the wall of four sepia photographs (grandfather in 1991 standing beside the first delivery truck; father in 2006 beside the first regional cross-dock; the 2015 family-picture in the Lurín warehouse opening; and the 2024 photograph of Paredes-Villafuerte standing alone beside the empty space reserved for the digital-transformation command-room), opened the GranSalto workbook his chief of staff had finalised at 10:12 p.m. the previous evening, and re-read the note his grandfather had hand-written on the back of the 1991 photograph and that had been framed on the CEO-office wall since 2010: *"El negocio no se pierde por el cambio. Se pierde por no cambiar."*

## The Protagonist

Paredes-Villafuerte, 47, had been Chief Executive Officer since 2019, had completed a Licenciatura en Economía at the Pontificia Universidad Católica del Perú in 2000 and an MBA at the Kellogg School of Management in 2007, had spent nine years at a Lima-based multi-national consumer-goods exporter in commercial and operations roles, and had joined Distribuidora Andina as COO in 2016 before his elevation to CEO at the formal family-succession conversation in 2019. His transformation instincts, he had told the family board at the March 2026 extraordinary meeting, had been shaped by three teachings: the 2008–2012 Kellogg exposure to Clayton Christensen's disruption literature and Geoffrey Moore's crossing-the-chasm work; an eighteen-month stretch inside the consumer-goods exporter's 2014 Argentine-market-entry transformation during which he had watched a well-funded go-to-market break on sales-force commission incentives; and a 2021 three-day RápidoB2B due-diligence exposure he had completed as an angel-investor evaluator before declining the syndicate on valuation grounds. The family board had assigned him the April three-round exercise on three considerations: his comfort treating the commission-paid sales force as the critical transformation constituency rather than as the enemy; his willingness to launch a platform that structurally cannibalised the traditional book; and his command of the Kotter-plus-Christensen-plus-Lewin-plus-platform-economics synthesis the exercise demanded.

## The Decision

Paredes-Villafuerte had to commit, by the 7:00 p.m. family-board check-in call, the three-round transformation package.

1. **Round 1 — Kotter Steps 1-3 plus Portfolio Allocation.** Create urgency: a one-page case-for-change memo the full executive committee can read at the May 5 off-site. Build the coalition: identify the three-to-five middle-manager champions whose reps' jobs will change (specifically, two regional-director seats already held by transformation-skeptical long-tenured managers and three assistant-regional-director seats held by 30-something Kellogg-style candidates). Form the strategic vision: a three-horizon vision document the board can validate. Portfolio allocation: PEN 420m quick-win efficiency projects (ERP stabilisation, basic digitisation of the current order-to-cash, route-optimisation AI); PEN 2.52bn core customer-facing transformation (mobile ordering app for bodegas, integrated CRM, data platform, customer-experience redesign); PEN 1.26bn platform bets (two-sided marketplace pilots in two regional clusters, manufacturer-onboarding prototype, digital-marketplace team).
2. **Round 2 — Kotter Steps 4-6: Enlist, Remove Barriers, Quick Wins.** Enlist the volunteer army: a 45-person transformation corps across the four coverage zones. Remove barriers: the commission-model redesign is the single largest structural barrier. Generate short-term wins: the ERP stabilisation (quick-win), the Costa Centro mobile-app pilot with 180 anchor bodegas, and a first manufacturer-onboarded marketplace pilot. Absorb the sales-rep rebellion: the 26-rep formal protest that lands in the inbox on Round 2 Day 14 must be addressed with a commission redesign that offers a path to equivalent-or-greater total compensation inside the new structure.
3. **Round 3 — Kotter Steps 7-8: Scale, Institutionalise, and the Self-Cannibalisation Dilemma.** Sustain acceleration: scale the mobile-app pilot to all four coverage zones. Institute change: bake the new commission structure and the platform-operating model into the firm's operating rhythms. Respond to RápidoB2B's upmarket move into the top-42% large-bodega segment: does the platform launched in Round 2 cannibalise the traditional book fast enough to beat RápidoB2B's expansion, or does Distribuidora Andina accept a smaller but sustainable traditional-plus-platform hybrid. The Christensen dilemma: disrupt yourself or be disrupted.

Commitments: the Round 1 portfolio; the Round 2 commission redesign and rebellion-absorption package; the Round 3 self-cannibalisation posture; and a one-page family-board memo that frames the three-round commitment as a single eighteen-month arc with the Q3 2027 prove-transformation-works milestone as the forcing function.

## Sectoral Background

The 2015–2025 Peruvian B2B consumer-goods-distribution category had, in the case universe, grown from approximately PEN 22bn to PEN 38bn in aggregate revenue, concentrated in three operator tiers (large traditional distributors like Distribuidora Andina and four regional peers; digital-native marketplaces like RápidoB2B and two smaller platforms; manufacturers' direct-distribution arms like Alicorp's and Gloria's). The bodega segment — approximately 11,200 active points-of-sale across the four coverage zones — had absorbed a documented 8–14% demand-side share-of-wallet shift toward digital-ordering platforms during 2022–2025, concentrated in the bottom-quartile bodega segment where the traditional-distributor minimum-order threshold of PEN 180 had left approximately 2,700 bodegas underserved. The 2024–2025 RápidoB2B-category-entrant revenue trajectory (from PEN 0 in late 2023 to an estimated PEN 340m by December 2025) matched the Christensen low-end disruption curve on the three-year trajectory.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1995–2025 digital-transformation literature produced three findings the simulation had to respect. First, Clayton Christensen's disruptive-innovation framework, operationalised as a low-end-then-climbing trajectory rather than as a new-product-launch event, produced documented incumbent-survival readings approximately 1.8× higher in firms that self-cannibalised than in firms that focused on large-customer defence on comparable disruption panels. Second, John Kotter's eight-step change model, applied as a sequenced rather than parallel execution discipline, produced documented transformation-completion rates approximately 2.4× those of unsequenced regimes and was particularly effective at the Step 4-5-6 (enlist-barrier-quick-win) middle phase where most transformations stalled. Third, the platform-economics tradition (Evans and Schmalensee, Parker and Van Alstyne) applied to B2B-distribution marketplaces had consistently shown near-zero-marginal-cost dynamics in two-sided markets with winner-take-most outcomes in geographic clusters, which mathematically precluded the incumbent's strategy of pricing-based-competition on a linear cost curve.

The post-2015 change-management literature added two findings. The Lewin force-field analysis, applied to commission-paid sales forces where the transformation threatened commission structure, produced documented rebellion-absorption rates approximately 3.6× higher under commission-redesign-with-equivalent-total-compensation-path programs than under capability-uplift-only programs. And the LATAM family-firm digital-transformation literature produced a consistent finding that the second-or-third-generation family CEO who had earned his succession through operational rather than transformational achievement faced a four-to-nine-point EBITDA-compression cost during the transformation window that the family board had to accept as the price of survival.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The January 2026 RápidoB2B announcement had landed at the firm; the February 2026 family-board request for a response had triggered the GranSalto exercise design; the March 2026 extraordinary board meeting had committed the PEN 4.2bn envelope and the Q3 2027 milestone; the April 23 commit was the first-round executive-committee package. The family board's March 20 letter: *"Tenemos 18 meses para probar que la transformación funciona. Si no funciona, el syndicate toma la empresa. Ese es el forcing function."*

### What Paredes-Villafuerte knew

The RápidoB2B trajectory and the Christensen framework; the five-dimensional digital-maturity baseline; the PEN 4.2bn envelope and the three-bucket portfolio allocation space; the sales-rep commission structure and the 26-rep formal-protest risk; the Kotter eight-step sequence; the two-sided-market platform-economics; and the family board's appetite for a specific eighteen-month plan rather than a narrative plan.

### What he did not know

Whether the 180-anchor-bodega Costa Centro mobile-app pilot would hit its 62% weekly-use-rate target in Q3 2026 or would slip to Q4; whether the commission redesign would hold the top-quartile sales reps through the eighteen-month window or would trigger a top-rep exodus to RápidoB2B or to a competing distributor; whether the manufacturer-onboarding prototype would secure the three anchor manufacturer commitments (Alicorp, Backus and one third candidate) within the ninety-day target window; whether RápidoB2B's upmarket expansion in Round 3 would come in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027; and whether the family board would accept the 6–8 percentage-point EBITDA-compression forecast during the transition as acceptable or would force a pullback at the Q1 2027 check-in.

### What others were saying

**The Chairman of the Board (father, third-generation)**: *"Tu abuelo construyó la empresa con una camioneta y un cuaderno. Tú vas a construirla con una app y un marketplace. Ambos son cuadernos. Uno funcionaba en 1991."*

**The Chief Commercial Officer (24 years with the firm)**: *"Los 180 reps son la razón por la que existe el negocio. Si rompemos el modelo de comisión, los perdemos. Si no lo rompemos, el app no se usa."*

**The Regional Director of Costa Centro (transformation-skeptical, 19 years in role)**: *"Los bodegueros no usan apps. Llevan toda la vida ordenando con el repartidor. No me muevan el modelo."*

**The Chief Digital Officer (externally hired in March 2026)**: *"La transformación no es tecnología. Es tecnología más coalición más secuencia más resistencia. Lo dice Kotter. Lo dice también cada transformación fallida que he visto."*

**The RápidoB2B Lima Country Manager (recently public remarks)**: *"Estamos sirviendo a los bodegueros que los distribuidores tradicionales no atienden. Cuando estemos listos, subiremos."*

**The VC Syndicate Chair (representing the forced-sale counterparty)**: *"Si a los 18 meses no hay tracción, ejercemos la opción. El plan tiene que ser creíble, no ambicioso."*

## Options Under Consideration

Paredes-Villafuerte had narrowed the transformation space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — Balanced portfolio (420m quick wins + 2.52bn core + 1.26bn platform) + Round-2 commission redesign with equivalent-total-compensation path + Round-3 self-cannibalisation through platform launch (Recommended).** Kotter sequence respected across the eighteen months; Christensen self-cannibalisation accepted; Lewin force-field addressed through commission redesign; platform economics embraced as the path to compete with RápidoB2B's near-zero-marginal-cost curve. The Q3 2027 milestone is achievable with approximately 6–8pp EBITDA compression during the transition.
- **Option B — Quick-win-heavy portfolio (PEN 2.1bn quick wins + PEN 1.68bn core + PEN 420m platform) + capability-uplift-only for sales reps + Round-3 focus-on-large-customer defence.** Rejected — the quick-win-heavy allocation treats the RápidoB2B threat as a marginal-efficiency problem rather than a structural disruption; the capability-uplift-only program fails the Lewin test; the large-customer-defence posture lands on exactly the rational-looking move Christensen's framework predicts will lose.
- **Option C — Platform-heavy portfolio (PEN 210m quick wins + PEN 1.26bn core + PEN 2.73bn platform) + immediate commission structure overhaul + Round-3 aggressive self-cannibalisation.** Defensible on Christensen grounds but loses the Kotter Step-4-5-6 quick-win-generation discipline; risks collapsing sales-rep coalition before the platform is operationally ready; compresses execution bandwidth across the organisation; and the Q1 2027 check-in read-outs would likely show insufficient quick-win progress for the VC syndicate.

Paredes-Villafuerte's private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the single decision most correlated with the eighteen-month survival outcome was the Round 2 commission redesign, because the sales-rep coalition was the single restraining force whose removal unlocked the mobile-app-adoption behaviour the Round 3 platform response depended on, and because the documented LATAM-family-firm panel had shown that transformations that failed the commission-redesign step almost always failed at the Round 2 check-in rather than at the Round 3. The *Gran Salto* frame sits at the LATAM-PYME-in-Spanish digital-transformation-first-lab tier in the 2026 Express catalogue, distinct from Manufacturera ANDEX (sim 005, Colombian industrial-manufacturing DAG-dependency roadmap with sixteen initiatives and an IoT plant-manager revolt), Banco Central Quisqueda Governor (sim 053, central-bank digital-currency and regulatory governor), Grupo Nativa GenAI Management (sim 058, generative-AI management frame for a services firm), Hotel Mirador Digital (sim 077, hotel-specific digital-transformation), and Capstone COMPETE CPG (sim 083, CPG capstone with digital elements). Sim 300 emphasises the Spanish-language primer for a LATAM PYME family distributor with the Christensen-plus-Kotter-plus-Lewin-plus-platform synthesis; the other sims emphasise their distinct scope, sector or role.
