## Abstract

Grupo Buen Gusto S.A. de C.V. was, in the case universe, a 47-year-old Mexico-City-headquartered diversified Mexican consumer-goods group founded in 1979 by Don Ignacio Aguilar-Buen-Gusto, listed on BMV under ticker BUENG* since 1998, with four business units — BG Foods, BG Beverages, BG Cuidado Personal and BG Nutrición — at 2025 consolidated revenue MXN 48B, 32,000 employees, 65% family control through Aguilar-Holding and 35% institutional free float. The Innovation Committee, constituted in 2022, held five seats and met monthly with a quarterly offsite. On **Monday, April 20, 2026**, at 7:18 a.m. Mexico-City local, in the eighth-floor innovation war-room at the Polanco corporate headquarters, the five-seat Committee opened the Q2 2026 strategy offsite with 23 active projects, a MXN 1.4B annual budget, and a three-horizon portfolio mix of 78/18/4.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Apply the Baghai-Coley-White 1999 three-horizon framework to a Mexican consumer-goods group's 23-project portfolio, compute the Weighted Average Horizon Score, and argue why a 78/18/4 mix constitutes core-heavy H3-starvation against the 70/20/10 ideal.
2. Apply the Cooper Stage-Gate methodology to kill 7 of 23 projects at their current gate on objective risk-adjusted-NPV criteria, navigate the sunk-cost political resistance of the VP of Foods defending two of his division's projects, and argue why "sending the wrong signal about innovation culture" is not a valid gate-pass criterion.
3. Apply the O'Reilly-Tushman 2004 ambidextrous-organization framework to the BGLabs launch decision, pick among structural-separate, structural-ringfenced and contextual 20%-time, and argue the autonomy-vs-reintegration trade-off.
4. Apply the Ries 2011 Lean Startup Build-Measure-Learn discipline to design a MXN 800K three-market MVP against a MXN 45M full-launch, name the riskiest assumption, design the minimum experiment, and commit the decision rule in advance.
5. Apply the Chesbrough 2003 open-innovation framework to design a two-channel external-ecosystem architecture across CVC, accelerator, university-R&D, innovation-challenges and acqui-hire with CONAHCYT tax-credit qualification, and articulate why *El Motor de Innovación* (sim 340) is an executive-corporate-innovation frame distinct from Nexum Innovation Pipeline (sim 020), Meridian Industrial InnoLab (sim 118), Zenith InnoParadox (sim 168) and EnerStar Andina Dilema Innovador (sim 318).

On the morning of **Monday, April 20, 2026**, at 7:18 a.m., **Ximena Aguilar-Mondragón** opened the eighth-floor innovation war-room at the Grupo Buen Gusto corporate headquarters on Avenida Presidente Masaryk 111, Polanco, Mexico City. The four-member committee — Castañeda-Leyva, Montiel-Ortega, Zepeda-Rivas, Fernando Aguilar-Mondragón — was seated around the table. The 23-project portfolio deck was projected on the south wall. The three-horizon mix diagnostic sat at 78/18/4. The 2025-budget-consumption-to-date report showed seven projects in a "zombie" status by objective risk-adjusted NPV criteria. Two of those zombies sat inside BG Foods.

---

## The Protagonist

Aguilar-Mondragón, 46, had graduated in 2001 from Universidad Iberoamericana in Comunicaciones, had completed an ITAM MBA in 2008, had worked 2008–2015 at Grupo Bimbo in new-product development and 2015–2022 at Google Mexico leading the Consumer Insights function for LATAM, and had joined Grupo Buen Gusto as CIO on February 14, 2022 under the mandate of her founding-uncle, Don Ignacio Aguilar-Buen-Gusto, 81. She held the Board's explicit license to "bring the group into the genuine third-horizon". Her cousin Fernando, 49, VP of Foods, held the opposite instinct: "primero el core, siempre". The 2026 offsite was the Committee's first attempt to resolve the tension on the table rather than in the hallway.

---

## The Decision

The Innovation Committee had to commit, by the end of the three-day Q2 2026 offsite (April 20–22), a four-round corporate-innovation package.

1. **Round 1 — Portfolio and zombies.** Three-horizon mix reset. 7-of-23 Stage-Gate zombie kill.
2. **Round 2 — BGLabs launch.** Ambidexterity architecture pick. First H3-concept pick.
3. **Round 3 — MVP.** Lean-Startup MXN 800K three-market design. Riskiest-assumption test.
4. **Round 4 — Open ecosystem.** Two-channel external-innovation architecture with CONAHCYT.

---

## Sectoral Background

The 2010–2025 Mexican diversified-consumer-goods category had, in the case universe, seen the BMV-listed set of seven integrated-family-plus-float groups grow innovation-pipeline spend from a median 1.8% of revenue in 2010 to a median 3.2% in 2025, with top-quartile operators reaching 5.1%. Against that, the median three-horizon mix across the set remained core-heavy at 76/19/5, with the 70/20/10 ideal rarely achieved outside one documented 2018–2022 Femsa pivot. The 1999 Baghai-Coley-White three-horizon canon, the 1990 Cooper Stage-Gate methodology, the 2004 O'Reilly-Tushman ambidexterity research, the 2011 Ries Lean Startup discipline and the 2003 Chesbrough open-innovation framework were the five anchors the simulation had to respect. CONAHCYT (Consejo Nacional de Humanidades, Ciencias y Tecnologías) offered tax credits at approximately 30% of qualified university-R&D and innovation-challenge spend for 2026 filers.

## The Situation

### How the Committee got here

The 1979 founding; the 1998 BMV IPO; the 2012 BG Nutrición launch; the 2018 three-horizon frame adoption (without the kill discipline); the 2022 Aguilar-Mondragón CIO appointment; the 2023 innovation-budget-doubling to MXN 1.4B; the 2024 first set of H3 proposals surfacing through BG Nutrición; the Q1 2026 close showing Brazil-imported-food CPG competition compressing the core; the April 20 offsite opening.

### What the Committee knew

The 23-project portfolio inventory; the Baghai-Coley-White WAHS calculation landing Buen Gusto at 78/18/4; the Cooper Stage-Gate zombie identification isolating 7 projects by NPV-adjusted-for-risk; the MXN 1.4B budget breakdown; the three ambidexterity options for BGLabs; the three H3 concepts; the MXN 800K MVP design against the MXN 45M full-launch; the Chesbrough five open-innovation modes; the CONAHCYT 30% credit for qualifying spend; and the Board's May 19 review calendar.

### What they did not know

Whether Fernando would accept a kill of the two BG Foods zombies under a concession on the ambidexterity architecture; whether the Mexican personalized-nutrition app-plus-meal-kits market could scale to justify the MXN 45M full-launch; whether the CONAHCYT 2026 qualifying-spend window would include the BGLabs first-year operational budget; whether the family-holding vote would back a structural-separate BGLabs or demand a structural-ringfenced inside-the-group pattern; and whether the Q3 and Q4 macro indicators would tolerate an uplift in the H3 budget line.

### What others were saying

**Don Ignacio Aguilar-Buen-Gusto (81, Chairman, founder)**: *"Ximena, tú haz el futuro. Yo te respaldo. Pero sin desarmar la casa."*

**Fernando Aguilar-Mondragón (49, VP Foods, cousin)**: *"No mates esos dos proyectos. Mandan el mensaje equivocado."*

**Luis Montiel-Ortega (58, VP Finance)**: *"MVP primero. Lanzamiento después. Disciplina de caja."*

**Andrea Zepeda-Rivas (44, VP Nutrición)**: *"La app de nutrición personalizada es la apuesta H3. No la diluyan."*

**Rafael Castañeda-Leyva (52, VP Strategy)**: *"Estructural separado. Si lo dejas adentro, los anticuerpos lo matan en dieciocho meses."*

**The BlackRock Mexico proxy team (via April 15 letter)**: *"Disclosure on any H3 concentration above 8% of annual capex."*

## Options Under Consideration

The Innovation Committee had narrowed the corporate-innovation decision space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — 70/20/10 target by FY2027 through Stage-Gate kill of 7-of-23 projects including both BG-Foods zombies, BGLabs launched as structural-separate P&L in Guadalajara with Aguilar-Mondragón as supervisory board chair, first H3 concept = personalized-nutrition app-plus-meal-kits-plus-AI piloted as a MXN 800K three-market MVP in Monterrey, Guadalajara and CDMX with the "customers will pay MXN 790/month for AI-personalised meal plans plus kit delivery" as the riskiest assumption, and a two-channel Chesbrough open-innovation architecture combining a MXN 120M CVC sleeve with a Tec-de-Monterrey-ITAM university-R&D partnership qualifying for CONAHCYT 30% tax credits (Recommended).** Respects the Baghai / Cooper / O'Reilly-Tushman / Ries / Chesbrough canon; commits the cultural kill that unlocks the H3 budget; protects BGLabs from corporate antibodies; qualifies the CONAHCYT credit.
- **Option B — Defer the zombie kill, structural-ringfence BGLabs inside BG Nutrición, pick the B2B-marketplace concept, skip the MVP in favor of a MXN 45M-full-launch, single-channel CVC.** Rejected — preserves Fernando's political capital but surrenders the Stage-Gate discipline, risks antibody capture of BGLabs, and forfeits the Ries Lean Startup learnings.
- **Option C — Freeze H3 investment entirely, reinforce the core with the FY2026 budget, revisit in FY2028.** Defensible under a macro-stress scenario but forfeits Femsa-type pivot optionality and contradicts the 2022 CIO-appointment mandate.

The Committee's majority view was that Option A was the right commitment, and that the single decision most correlated with the 24-month outcome was the structural-separate BGLabs architecture — because the 2020–2025 Latin-American CPG open-innovation track record documented that structural-ringfenced innovation units survive approximately 18 months before corporate antibodies capture them, and because the Guadalajara-P&L pattern replicated the one documented 2018–2022 Femsa pivot. The *El Motor de Innovación* frame sits at the executive-corporate-innovation tier in the 2026 Express catalogue, distinct from Nexum Innovation Pipeline (020), Meridian Industrial InnoLab (118), Zenith InnoParadox (168) and EnerStar Andina Dilema Innovador (318).
