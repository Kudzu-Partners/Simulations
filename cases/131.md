## Abstract

CASTELLAN BANK N.A. was, in the case universe, the New-York-chartered Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC)-supervised national-bank subsidiary of Castellan Financial Group, a mid-cap transatlantic banking group headquartered in London (the parent is the protagonist group of Case RSK-2026-124). The OCC's Q4 2025 supervisory letter had issued two Matters Requiring Attention (MRAs) against the BSA Officer's transaction-monitoring program: an MRA on monitor calibration (cash structuring, wire velocity, behaviour deviation and digital asset thresholds all defaulted at a risk-rating of three on a nine-point scale with no documented tuning) and an MRA on SAR inventory management (a 1,200-alert unreviewed backlog against an eighty-analyst-hour weekly capacity and a SAR-quality score of 2.3 out of 5 on a Q3 2025 internal sample). The Q1 2026 AML-remediation envelope had committed the BSA Officer to a four-round remediation cycle — Monitor Calibration, Backlog Allocation, SAR Quality Uplift, OCC Document Production — with a Risk-Coverage-Score target above 75, a Detection-Rate floor of 72%, a Backlog-Closure target above 70%, and a SAR-Quality floor of 3.8. The case opens at 6:06 a.m. on Monday, February 23, 2026, at the Castellan Bank New-York headquarters as **Dorian Althaus**, a 44-year-old BSA Officer, prepares the Round-1 monitor-calibration commitment. Forty-two days separate him from the April 6 OCC mid-cycle check.

## The Protagonist

Dorian Althaus, 44, had joined Castellan Bank N.A. as BSA Officer in November 2023 from a US top-ten money-centre bank where he had, across eleven years, run a transaction-monitoring program through three OCC examination cycles, two FinCEN consent-order remediations, and a 2021 Office-of-Foreign-Assets-Control look-back that had surfaced a sanctions-screening gap on a correspondent-banking corridor. Before that, he had spent six years as a senior financial-crime analyst at a US regional bank where he had built the five-typology behaviour-deviation rule-set that had anchored the bank's 2017 SAR-narrative quality uplift. The Chief Compliance Officer at Castellan Bank (Anselma Reguera — the protagonist of Case RSK-2026-124) had recruited him specifically on the strength of two capabilities: his experience running an MRA-closure cycle inside a supervisory window, and his reputation for refusing to ship a calibration tightening that had not cleared a documented tuning-rationale log. His 2026 variable compensation tied 40% to the MRA-closure outcome and 15% to the SAR-quality score.

## The Decision

Althaus had to commit, by the March 9 board-audit-committee pre-review, the Round-1 monitor-calibration envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–4.

1. **Round 1 — Monitor Calibration.** Four typology monitors — cash structuring, wire velocity, behaviour deviation, digital asset — each defaulted at risk-rating three on the nine-point scale. Three archetypes: blanket-tighten-all-to-seven (alert-volume spike from 72 to 210 per week, analyst-capacity exhausted, false-positive rate ~82%), differentiated-calibration (structuring at eight, wire velocity at six, behaviour deviation at seven, digital asset at five — alert volume 118, false-positive rate ~58%), or minimum-compliance-uplift (all monitors to five — alert volume 89, false-positive rate ~71%, risk-coverage score below MRA-closure threshold). Working envelope: differentiated calibration.
2. **Round 2 — Backlog Allocation under Senior-Analyst Resignation.** Mid-round event: the senior transaction-monitoring analyst resigns at week three with a two-week notice, cutting weekly capacity from 80 to 72 analyst-hours. Three vectors: 80%-backlog / 20%-new (backlog falls 1,200 → 640 → 320 but detection rate stalls at 58 because new typologies go un-reviewed), 60%-backlog / 40%-new (backlog closes to 480 at window-end, detection rate climbs to 71), or 40%-backlog / 60%-new (detection rate hits 76 but backlog closure forfeits the MRA-2 closure test). Working envelope: 60/40 split.
3. **Round 3 — SAR Quality Uplift.** Three levers: narrative-retraining (40 analyst-hours over two weeks, quality floor uplift 2.3 → 3.4), peer-review (12 additional hours per SAR on top-quintile-risk filings, quality uplift 2.3 → 3.8), or defensive-filing volume-up (quality drifts down to 2.1 because analysts file every flagged alert to insulate the bank from MRA exposure). Working envelope: narrative-retraining plus peer-review.
4. **Round 4 — OCC Document Production.** Three levers: proactive-weekly-production-binder (analyst capacity −6 hours per week, Composite-Score uplift of 8), OCC-only-on-request (Composite-Score drift −4, MRA-closure risk), or litigation-hold-managed batch production at mid-window (neutral). Working envelope: proactive-weekly-production-binder.

**URGENT Event (Round 2 mid-window).** On March 9, a FinCEN 314(a) list-match request arrives with a two-week clock covering forty-two subject names across six correspondent-bank corridors. Three vectors: dedicated-strike-team of three analysts for two weeks (EUR 0.1M incremental cost, Backlog-Closure held at 60%, Detection-Rate uplift of 3 points via corridor tuning), deprioritise-backlog-and-absorb (Backlog-Closure drops to 42%, MRA-2 closure at risk), or outsource-to-external-AML-advisory (EUR 0.3M cost, Backlog-Closure held at 58%, control-weakening risk on the 2026 OCC narrative). Working envelope: dedicated strike team.

On Althaus's working model, a Differentiated-Calibration / 60-40-Split / Narrative-plus-Peer-Review / Proactive-Weekly / Strike-Team envelope delivered Risk-Coverage Score 78, Detection Rate 76%, Backlog Closure 73%, SAR Quality 3.9, Composite 79 — the trajectory that closed both MRAs at the September 2026 supervisory sign-off. A Blanket-Tighten / 80-20-Split / Defensive-Filing / On-Request envelope delivered Risk-Coverage 62, Detection Rate 58%, SAR Quality 2.1, Composite 34 — the trajectory that, on the 2019–2024 OCC mid-cap-bank distribution, converted the two MRAs into a formal Memorandum of Understanding inside the 2026 supervisory cycle.

## Bank Background

Castellan Bank N.A. had been chartered in New York in 2004 as the US subsidiary of Castellan Financial Group with a domestic correspondent-banking, US-commercial-real-estate lending and US-trade-finance franchise that had, across 2019–2025, accumulated a USD 38B balance sheet, 2,100 employees across New York, Miami and San Francisco, and a supervisory file that had, through three OCC examination cycles, produced two MRAs in 2023 (correspondent-banking-corridor screening) and the two fresh MRAs at issue in the Q4 2025 supervisory letter. The 2023–2025 transaction-monitoring platform had run on a vendor TMS with forty-seven rules across five typology groups.

The Q4 2025 OCC supervisory letter had placed three pressures the four-round envelope would inherit. First, the January 2026 SAR-inventory sample at 2.3/5 had, on the OCC examination-in-charge's write-up, been flagged as a defensive-filing pattern that would convert into a Matter Requiring Immediate Attention if un-remediated inside the Q1 2026 window. Second, the 1,200-alert backlog had accumulated across 2023–2025 on a weekly burn-down shortfall of ~8% against intake. Third, the Q1 2026 correspondent-bank corridor-risk register had flagged six Singapore, Panama and Dubai corridors at enhanced-due-diligence-plus posture — the register against which the URGENT FinCEN 314(a) event would land.

## Industry and Market Context

The US bank anti-money-laundering landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2019–2024 OCC mid-cap-bank MRA evidence base had, across 87 documented BSA/AML MRA cycles, placed differentiated-calibration commitments at a twelve-month MRA-closure rate of 79% against 32% for blanket-tighten responses — a pattern that anchored the Round-1 commitment. Second, the 2020–2024 SAR-quality literature had, across 54 bank-sample remediations, priced narrative-retraining-plus-peer-review at a quality-floor uplift of 1.4–1.8 points against 0.3–0.5 points for volume-up defensive filing. Third, the 2018–2024 FinCEN 314(a) response evidence had, across 38 mid-cap-bank list-match cycles, placed dedicated-strike-team responses at a two-week-clock closure rate of 96% against 71% for absorbed responses — a pricing that framed the URGENT event envelope.

Empirically, the post-2022 US-banking-supervisory literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, programs that had published a documented tuning-rationale log across all four typology monitors inside the Round-1 calibration had, across a 2019–2024 sample of 44 mid-cap bank MRA cycles, preserved detection-rate above 72% in 81% of cases against 34% for programs without a documented log. Second, programs that had pre-committed a 60-40-backlog-new split under a mid-cycle capacity shock had, across the same sample, held SAR-quality above 3.8 in 76% of cases against 28% for programs that had shifted to 80-20-backlog-dominated allocation.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The OCC Examination-in-Charge had, on December 15, 2025, issued the supervisory letter carrying the two MRAs. The Chief Compliance Officer of the parent group had, on January 12, 2026, opened the quarterly remediation cadence. The Deputy BSA Officer had, on January 26, delivered the Round-1 differentiated-calibration pre-read. The Head of AML Technology had, on February 9, confirmed the tuning-rationale log architecture. The senior transaction-monitoring analyst had, on February 17, submitted a two-week resignation notice. The General Counsel had, on February 20, pre-committed the strike-team protocol on any inbound 314(a) request.

### What Althaus knew

The team had modelled four four-round envelopes. A Differentiated-Calibration / 60-40 / Narrative-plus-Peer-Review / Proactive-Weekly / Strike-Team Envelope projected Composite 79. A Blanket-Tighten / 80-20 / Defensive-Filing / On-Request Envelope projected Composite 34. A Minimum-Compliance-Uplift / 80-20 / Narrative-Retraining / On-Request Envelope projected Composite 52. A Differentiated / 80-20 / Peer-Review / Proactive-Weekly Envelope projected Composite 68 — the trajectory that closed the SAR-quality MRA but forfeited the detection-rate floor inside Round 2.

### What he did not know

Whether the senior analyst's replacement would close inside eight weeks; whether the FinCEN 314(a) list-match would expand on re-issue; whether the OCC examination-in-charge would, at the April 6 mid-cycle check, re-grade the SAR-quality sample inside the window; and whether the 2026 Q3 FCA thematic review at the London parent would couple transatlantic supervisory pressure onto the Q4 2026 US sign-off.

### What others were saying

**The Deputy BSA Officer**: *"Differentiated calibration. Structuring at eight. Document every threshold change."*

**The Head of AML Technology**: *"Proactive weekly OCC binder. Tuning-rationale log on every rule."*

**The General Counsel**: *"Strike team on any 314(a). Backlog pressure is the second-order concern."*

**The Chief Compliance Officer (group)**: *"Two MRAs must close. One MOU is one MRA too many."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Althaus had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Differentiated-Calibration / 60-40-Backlog-New / Narrative-plus-Peer-Review / Proactive-Weekly-Production / Strike-Team on 314(a):** the recommended envelope. Risk-Coverage 78, Detection 76%, Backlog Closure 73%, SAR Quality 3.9, Composite 79. Recommended.
- **Option B — Blanket-Tighten / 80-20 / Defensive-Filing / On-Request / Absorb-314(a):** the minimalist envelope. Composite 34. Rejected on MRA-closure grounds — trajectory converts to MOU.
- **Option C — Differentiated / 80-20 / Peer-Review / Proactive-Weekly / Strike-Team:** the backlog-heavy envelope. Composite 68. Rejected — forfeits Round-2 detection-rate floor.

Althaus's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 1 — the differentiated calibration that enabled the Round-2 60-40 split to hold detection above 72% under the analyst-capacity shock.
