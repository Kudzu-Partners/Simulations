## Abstract

Consultores Andes was, in the case universe, a mid-sized LATAM management consulting firm with a 2025 revenue of USD 18.2M, a cash position of USD 3.2M, a partnership of 5 equity partners, a management ranks of 15 managers and a 85-analyst staff, an active-client base of 12 engagements, a utilization rate of 72%, a gross margin of 58%, a client satisfaction of 78, and a staff-retention rate of 88% against a LATAM professional-services peer benchmark of 81%. On Monday, July 6, 2026, the Partnership approved a three-round strategic commitment inside which the Managing Partner would commit, each round, six operating levers — analyst hiring, salary-increase policy, training budget, RFP bids, price adjustment, and knowledge investment. Alejandra Pineda, a 48-year-old equity partner elected Managing Partner on April 1, 2026, faced a Round-1 IT-consolidation wave in financial services, a Round-2 competitor manager-poaching event, and a Round-3 macroeconomic softening that would defer client projects. The case opens at 6:15 a.m. on Monday, July 13, 2026, at the Consultores Andes Bogotá office, as Pineda prepares the Round-1 lever sheet.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Round-1 analyst-hiring, salary, training, RFP-bids, price-and-knowledge commitment under an IT-consolidation wave, and diagnose when an offense envelope on a new-sector playbook dominates on the three-round composite.
2. Analyze the Round-2 manager-poaching response and recommend the salary-signal, knowledge-investment, and RFP-pipeline posture consistent with preserving retention through a live competitive poaching event.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 macroeconomic-softening decision and recommend the price-lift, knowledge-investment, and modest-hiring sequence that converts a slowdown into a pricing-power proof-point.
4. Design a three-round firm-triangle trajectory that balances utilization, margin, and growth against the partnership's leverage ratios and inside a professional-services scorecard.
5. Recommend a complete three-round lever sequence, identify the single round most correlated with the Round-3 composite, and write the partnership-letter paragraph that frames the knowledge-as-leverage thesis.

On the morning of **Monday, July 13, 2026**, **Alejandra Pineda** arrived at the Consultores Andes Bogotá office on Carrera 7 at 6:15 a.m., opened the Round-1 lever sheet her Chief-Operating-Partner team had finalized at 10:30 p.m. the night before, and re-read the single line she had pinned to the sheet in her April acceptance note: "A consulting firm is utilization times price times knowledge. Two of three is a firm. All three is a practice. The three-round arc decides which we are." Ten days separated her from the July 23 Partnership commitment.

---

## The Protagonist

Alejandra Pineda, 48, had joined Consultores Andes in 2011 as a Senior Consultant on the financial-services practice and had been promoted to Partner in 2017. A graduate of the Universidad de los Andes in industrial engineering with an MBA from Wharton, she had, in her partnership tenure, led the firm's 2020 digital-transformation practice launch from zero to USD 4.2M in Year-3 revenue, sponsored the 2023 people-and-culture strategic review that had lifted staff retention from 78% to 88%, and had, in 2024, co-authored the firm's first formal knowledge-management framework that had anchored the Year-2 pricing-premium conversation. Her April 2026 election as Managing Partner had carried a three-year mandate inside which the partnership expected her to demonstrate whether the knowledge-as-leverage thesis — the thesis behind her 2024 framework — would hold through a live three-round commercial arc.

---

## The Decision

Pineda had to commit, by the July 23 Partnership commitment, the Round-1 lever sheet and the Round-2 and Round-3 intent:

1. **Hiring, Salary, and Training** — whether to hire 3 analysts in Round 1 at a +4% salary-policy lift and a USD 150,000 training line; whether to lift the Round-2 salary policy to +5% under the poaching event and hire 5 analysts; whether to moderate the Round-3 hire to 4 analysts and the salary lift to +3% under the softening.
2. **RFP Bids and Pricing** — whether to bid on 2 RFPs in Round 1 and lift price +4%, bid on 3 RFPs in Round 2 and moderate price lift to +2%, and bid on 2 RFPs in Round 3 at a bold +6% price lift underpinned by the knowledge-level trajectory.
3. **Knowledge Investment** — whether to commit USD 200K, USD 350K, and USD 400K across the three rounds, building the firm's knowledge-level score from 22 to 55.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across the three-round envelope. A Round-1 package that hired 3 analysts, lifted salary 4%, committed USD 150K training, bid on 2 RFPs, lifted price 4%, and committed USD 200K knowledge investment produced, on Pineda's working model, a Round-1 revenue of USD 5.8M, a utilization of 75%, a gross margin of 59%, a client satisfaction of 79, a retention of 88%, a knowledge-level of 28, and an RFP win of 1 of 2 at a 50% win rate. The three-round trajectory then carried the firm to a Round-3 revenue of USD 8.1M, a utilization of 81%, a gross margin of 62%, a knowledge-level of 55, a cumulative three-round revenue of USD 21M, and a composite of 79.

## Company Background

Consultores Andes had been founded in 2003 by three Pontificia Universidad Javeriana MBA graduates and had, across twenty-three years, built the three-office LATAM footprint the case studied — Bogotá (the headquarters and the largest office at 54 billable heads), Lima (28 billable heads), and Santiago (23 billable heads). The 2025 revenue of USD 18.2M was distributed as financial services 38%, consumer goods 22%, industrial 18%, public sector 12%, and healthcare 10%. The partnership comprised 5 equity partners (the three founders plus two elevated in 2017 and 2022) and 15 managers on a two-year partner-track cycle. The firm's competitive position had rested, since 2020, on the financial-services-practice 54% share of revenue concentration, a knowledge-level score of 22 that trailed the tier-one LATAM consulting benchmark of 48, and a four-point retention advantage against the LATAM professional-services peer-set.

The Partnership's June strategy dossier had identified three specific pressures the three-round envelope would inherit. First, the LATAM financial-services sector had, in Q1–Q2 2026, entered an IT-consolidation wave that the regional-regulator's Q2 bulletin had priced at USD 420M of addressable consulting demand across a twelve-month window — a wave the firm's financial-services practice was positioned to capture at a 24–31% win-rate on qualified bids. Second, a regional competitor had, in May 2026, re-launched its LATAM practice with a Bogotá-office opening and a stated intent to poach three of Consultores Andes's managers across the June–September window. Third, the LATAM macroeconomic outlook had, in the May 2026 regional bulletin, softened for H2 2026 at a projected 0.8-point downshift in client-capex commitments — a softening that would pressure the Round-3 RFP pipeline.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM management-consulting landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the three-round envelope sat inside. First, the 2024–2025 LATAM consulting growth cycle had, across a commercial-summary sample of twenty-six mid-market firms, produced a utilization-over-70% cohort margin of 58–62% against an utilization-below-68% cohort margin of 44–48% — a six-point margin spread that anchored the Round-1 utilization discipline. Second, the knowledge-management evidence base had, across a 2020–2024 sample of eighteen LATAM consulting firms, documented a pricing-power multiplier of 1.4 for firms that had lifted knowledge-level scores from the 20-range to the 50-range inside a four-round horizon — a multiplier that monetized the Round-3 +6% price lift. Third, the manager-poaching event pattern had, across a 2021–2025 sample of eleven LATAM consulting manager-poaching events, produced a salary-signal response of +5% within the event quarter that had preserved retention at an 85% rate against a 71% rate for firms that had held salary policy flat.

Empirically, the post-2018 professional-services literature had two robust findings the three-round envelope had to respect. First, firms that had sustained knowledge-investment-as-percent-of-revenue above 1.5% through a macroeconomic softening had, across a 2019–2024 sample of twenty-two firms, lifted pricing power by 3–6 points in the subsequent expansion against a 0.4-point lift for firms that had cut knowledge investment through the softening. Second, firms that had maintained a 2-to-1 analyst-to-manager leverage ratio through three-round horizons had, across the same sample, preserved utilization at a 76%-median against a 68% median for firms that had over-hired analysts into the softening window.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Partnership had, in the June 2026 off-site, approved a three-round composite target of 76, a cumulative three-round revenue target of USD 20M, a utilization floor of 74% across all rounds, a retention floor of 83%, and a knowledge-level-by-Round-3 target of 50. The Chief-Operating-Partner had, on July 1, circulated a Round-1 pre-reading that proposed the 3-analyst hire, the +4% salary policy, and the USD 200K knowledge line. The Financial-Services Practice Partner had, on July 4, pushed for a 4-analyst hire to carry the IT-consolidation-wave bidding capacity — a position the Managing Partner had held under review. The People-and-Culture Partner had, on July 6, signed off on the +4% salary policy with a Round-2 pre-commitment to +5% under the poaching scenario.

### What Pineda knew

The team had modeled three three-round envelopes. A Knowledge-Leverage Envelope (Round-1 3 analysts + 4% salary + USD 150K training + 2 RFPs + 4% price + USD 200K knowledge; Round-2 5 analysts + 5% salary + USD 200K training + 3 RFPs + 2% price + USD 350K knowledge; Round-3 4 analysts + 3% salary + USD 150K training + 2 RFPs + 6% price + USD 400K knowledge) projected a Round-3 composite of 79. A Growth-Heavy Envelope (Round-1 6 analysts + 5% salary + 4 RFPs + 3% price + USD 150K knowledge; Round-2 8 analysts + 6% salary + 4 RFPs + 0% price + USD 250K knowledge) projected a Round-3 composite of 68 on utilization compression. A Margin-First Envelope (Round-1 2 analysts + 3% salary + 2 RFPs + 6% price + USD 250K knowledge; Round-2 3 analysts + 4% salary + 2 RFPs + 4% price) projected a Round-3 composite of 72 on revenue compression.

### What she did not know

Whether the competitor's stated intent to poach three managers would execute at the stated Round-2 window or would slip to Round 3 and compound the softening-quarter retention risk; whether the IT-consolidation wave would hold through Round 3 or would compress at the modeled 18% depth under the macroeconomic softening; whether the price-sensitivity-above-7%-threshold (a firm-specific pattern from 2023–2024 RFP history) would hold through the Round-3 +6% lift or would reset under the macroeconomic environment; and whether the pilot knowledge-management initiative that the Chief Knowledge Officer had launched in Q1 2026 would, by the Round-1 close, deliver the top-twenty-diagnoses playbook at the projected cadence.

### What others were saying

**The Chief-Operating-Partner**: *"Knowledge-Leverage Envelope. 3 analysts round one, 5 round two, 4 round three. The salary moves signal intent. The knowledge investment is what the Round-3 price lift is paid with."*

**The Financial-Services Practice Partner**: *"Raise Round-1 hiring to 4 analysts. The IT wave is too big to bid on with 2 RFPs. The practice is the wave."*

**The People-and-Culture Partner**: *"Salary to +5% in Round 2. The poaching event needs an immediate signal. +3% in Round 3 protects retention without feeding the softening."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Pineda had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Knowledge-Leverage:** Round-1 3 analysts + 4% salary + 2 RFPs + 4% price + USD 200K knowledge; Round-2 5 analysts + 5% salary + 3 RFPs + 2% price + USD 350K knowledge; Round-3 4 analysts + 3% salary + 2 RFPs + 6% price + USD 400K knowledge. Round-3 composite 79. Aligns with COP, Practice, and P&C Partners. Recommended.

- **Option B — Growth-Heavy:** Aggressive hiring, 4 RFPs per round, zero-to-3% price lift. Round-3 composite 68. Utilization-compressing. Margin-eroding.

- **Option C — Margin-First:** Minimal hiring, 2 RFPs per round, aggressive pricing throughout. Round-3 composite 72. Revenue-compressing. Below partnership revenue target.

Pineda's private view, not yet shared with the partnership, was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the Round-3 composite was Round 3 — specifically, the discipline of maintaining the knowledge investment at USD 400K through the macroeconomic softening, the bold +6% price lift underpinned by the knowledge-level-55 trajectory, the moderate 4-analyst hire that preserved utilization, and the +3% salary lift that held retention without over-paying into the softening.
