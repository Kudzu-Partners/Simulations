## Abstract

Helicona Industrial S.A. was, in the case universe, a Bilbao-headquartered mid-sized industrial-equipment manufacturer with EUR 320M 2025 revenue, 1,140 employees across three plants in Spain, Slovakia and Mexico, a single-instance SAP ECC environment deployed in 2014 across the four primary modules SD / MM / PP / FICO, and a 2027 S/4HANA migration board-approved in November 2025 and now in assessment. The 2024–2025 cycle had left the operator fragile-but-working: the four modules functioned individually but the seams between them had been accumulating master-data drift, configuration mismatches and process-design gaps that surfaced as customer-facing failures more than once a week. The Q2 2026 context compounded three pressures: a Friday ship-window for a EUR 4.6M Korean OEM order; an internal commitment to lift plant OEE by nine percentage points before the S/4HANA assessment closed in Q3; and a backlog of approximately twenty-two open ERP support tickets triaged into eight cross-module integration scenarios for the week of April 21–25. The case opens at 7:46 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Helicona Bilbao plant ERP-support desk as **Idoia Etxebarria-Carrillo**, a 39-year-old ERP Process Lead, prepares the eight-scenario ERPsim commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate each of the eight integration failures and locate the seam between two modules where the root cause sits (FICO credit-limit blocking SD; PP BOM defect cascading into MM consumption and FICO costing; MM-PP cycle-count variance breaking PP planning; MM-PP MRP not refreshed on supplier lead-time change; SD-MM-FICO three-way match blocking payment; SD blocked delivery on FICO credit hold; PP-MM-FICO production-cost variance; MM-FICO vendor payment-terms mismatch from master-data drift).
2. Analyse the master-data-management discipline (the BOM defect, the phantom-inventory variance and the vendor payment-terms mismatch are all master-data failures dressed as process failures) and articulate why fixing master data once typically resolves three downstream symptoms.
3. Diagnose the MRP-refresh and three-way-match findings as configuration-and-process-design gaps respectively, and articulate the difference between a configuration fix (one-time, tactical) and a process-design fix (recurring, structural).
4. Respond to the eighth scenario (vendor master-data migration) and design the next-cycle-backlog placement that explicitly accepts a deferral rather than a sub-quality fix.
5. Design the eight-scenario ERPsim envelope that closes seven of eight failures inside the week, lifts OEE by approximately nine percentage points, lands at an Integration Score of 78, and pre-positions the master-data clean-up that the 2027 S/4HANA migration will require.

## The Protagonist

Etxebarria-Carrillo, 39, had been the ERP Process Lead at Helicona Industrial for six years, had been promoted from Senior Functional Consultant in 2020 after eight years across two SAP implementations at industrial operators in Spain and Portugal, and had run the cross-module support desk since the 2020 restructuring consolidated the function. In 2018–2020 she had led the integration recovery on a Vigo-based industrial operator that absorbed a 32% drop in customer-on-time-in-full from a botched SD-FICO credit-limit configuration. In 2024 she co-authored with the CIO an internal note titled *Eight Seams, Four Modules* that became the support desk's reference rubric. The Operations Director assigned her to the April 21–25 window on three considerations: her discipline of locating the seam between modules before fixing the symptom in any one module; her command of the master-data dimensions producing three-quarters of recurring integration failures; and her willingness to defer a sub-quality fix into the next-cycle backlog rather than ship a half-fix that surfaced as a regression.

## The Decision

Etxebarria-Carrillo had to commit, by the 5:30 p.m. ERP-support desk close-out on Wednesday, the eight-scenario sequencing and the directional intent for the Friday ship-window.

1. **Scenario 1 — Sales-order rejection.** Korean OEM order blocked in SD by FICO credit-limit check. Working envelope: raise the credit limit on the credit master (one-time configuration) or route the order with a one-time override (process workaround). The Korean customer is established and the limit has not been refreshed since 2024.
2. **Scenario 2 — BOM defect.** PP lists 2 bearings; product engineering confirms 4 bearings. FICO costs have been wrong for months. MM consumption has not reconciled. Working envelope: fix the BOM once.
3. **Scenario 3 — Phantom inventory.** System 200 units; warehouse 87. Negative variance has not been booked. Working envelope: emergency cycle count, write-down booked, master-data cleanup on the affected SKUs.
4. **Scenario 4 — Lead-time catastrophe.** Supplier moved from 4 weeks to 10 weeks; MRP did not refresh. Working envelope: refresh the supplier-master lead-time field, re-explode MRP, communicate the schedule slip to PP and SD.
5. **Scenario 5 — Invoice-price dispute.** PO at EUR 10, invoice at EUR 11. Three-way match blocks payment. Working envelope: chase the contract authority on the EUR 10 anchor, accept the EUR 11 invoice if the contract has been amended, escalate to procurement if not.
6. **Scenario 6 — Blocked delivery.** Delivery sitting on the dock under a credit hold. Working envelope: resolve the credit hold (cleared by Scenario 1's fix) and release the delivery.
7. **Scenario 7 — Production-cost variance.** Actual costs 12% above standard. Working envelope: variance analysis isolating materials versus scrap versus labour; corrective action on the largest contributor.
8. **Scenario 8 — Vendor payment-terms mismatch.** PO Net-30, invoice Net-60, system pays Net-45. Working envelope: vendor-master-data migration to clean up affected vendor records — too large to complete inside the week, place on the next-cycle backlog.

On Etxebarria-Carrillo's working model, the eight-scenario envelope closed scenarios 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7 inside the week, deferred scenario 8 into the next-cycle backlog, lifted plant OEE by approximately 9 percentage points (from 64% to 73%) and landed at an Integration Score of 78 against the firm's internal benchmark. The Friday ship-window for the EUR 4.6M Korean OEM order would clear inside the cleared credit-hold + cleared blocked-delivery sequence.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2015–2025 ERP-operations literature on European mid-sized SAP ECC operators produced three findings the engagement had to respect. First, master-data drift (BOM defects, vendor and customer-credit master staleness, supplier-lead-time gaps) accounted for roughly 64% of all cross-module integration failures and was the single highest-yield remediation domain. Second, the three-way-match block on procure-to-pay was the single most-cited support-desk trigger between 2020 and 2025, accounting for around 28% of open tickets at any time. Third, MRP-refresh failures on supplier-lead-time changes propagated into PP planning errors and SD on-time-in-full degradation inside three planning cycles in 71% of documented cases.

The post-2022 modernisation literature added two findings. Operators that cleaned master data systematically before an S/4HANA migration cut the migration timeline by an average of 18% and post-migration regression count by 41%. And OEE lifts in the 6–12 percentage-point band were substantially attributable to ERP-integration fixes rather than to shop-floor process changes in 64% of documented cases — the system had been masking operating capacity, not the operators.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Operations Director committed to the OEE-lift target in December 2025. The integration-failure register, master-data-quality scorecard and S/4HANA migration assessment had all landed by late March. The ERP Steering Committee Chair had asked Etxebarria-Carrillo to bring the eight-scenario sequencing to the April 21–25 window.

### What Etxebarria-Carrillo knew

Eight integration failures across four modules, seven amenable to in-week resolution, one (vendor master-data migration) requiring a structured backlog placement; the BOM defect, the phantom inventory and the vendor payment-terms mismatch are all master-data failures dressed as process failures; the MRP refresh on lead-time is a configuration discipline; the three-way-match block is a process-design issue; and the seam between two modules is where the root cause lives in every scenario. The Friday ship-window for the EUR 4.6M Korean OEM order would clear if scenarios 1 and 6 cleared.

### What she did not know

Whether the Korean OEM customer would absorb the corrected lead-time on the affected component lines without re-opening the contract; whether the production-cost-variance corrective action would land on materials, scrap or labour and what the implication would be for the Q3 cost forecast; whether the deferred Scenario 8 vendor master-data migration would absorb cleanly into the S/4HANA pre-migration cleanup or surface as an additional support-desk ticket; and whether the ERP Steering Committee would absorb the Integration Score of 78 as on-track or push for a higher reading.

### What others were saying

**The SD/MM Functional Lead**: *"Scenarios one and six are the same problem with two faces. Fix the credit hold once and both clear."*

**The PP/FICO Functional Lead**: *"Scenarios two, three and eight are master data. Three symptoms, one root cause family."*

**The Chief Information Officer**: *"Every master-data fix you ship before the S/4HANA migration is one less migration risk."*

**The Operations Director**: *"An ERP is only as strong as its weakest seam. Find the seam in each scenario before you write a line of configuration."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Etxebarria-Carrillo had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Seam-first triage / 7-of-8 in-week fixes / Scenario 8 to next-cycle backlog / +9% OEE / Integration Score 78:** the recommended envelope. Master-data discipline first, configuration second, process design third. Recommended.
- **Option B — Module-by-module triage / mixed in-week fixes / regressions inside two cycles:** the historical baseline. Rejected — fixes symptoms inside one module without locating the seam, surfaces as regression in the following week.
- **Option C — Aggressive envelope (all 8 in-week including the vendor master-data migration):** the maximalist variant. Rejected — vendor master-data migration cannot be done well inside the week and a half-fix would propagate into the procure-to-pay process for months.

Her private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the scenario most correlated with the Friday ship-window was Scenario 1 — sales-order rejection — because the credit-master refresh closed both Scenario 1 and Scenario 6 (blocked delivery on the same credit hold) and unblocked the Korean OEM order without requiring an exception process. The ERPsim frame is the *cross-module process-integration* seat in the 2026 Express operations catalogue, complementary to and distinct from sim 247 ISOnavigator (the QMS gap-analysis framework that certifies that processes are documented and operating) and sim 249 PrintOrMake (the make-or-print decision lab on a part-by-part basis) — the boundary the participant should leave with is that ERPsim runs the operating fabric inside the ISO-certified envelope and produces the data that PrintOrMake uses for its decisions.
