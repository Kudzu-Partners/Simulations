## Abstract

VANTARA GROUP was, in the case universe, a Madrid-headquartered diversified group with €1.3B in 2025 revenue, a global-mobility function that had, across 2023–2025, run an average of eleven concurrent expatriate assignments at a Year-1 retention rate of 78% against an international-HR-peer median of 86%, and a 2026 expansion plan that had committed to three simultaneous market launches inside a ninety-day window: a Singapore Asia-Pacific engineering hub, a Dubai Middle-East operations build, and a Nairobi frontier-markets commercial beachhead. The Q4 2025 group-executive-committee envelope had committed €1.8M across assignment-design, cross-cultural-adjustment support and repatriation for three senior expatriate seats — one per market — against a retention-rate target above 85%, an incidents ceiling of two across the three markets, a total-envelope ceiling of €1.8M, and a market-launch closure target of three out of three. On Monday, February 9, 2026, Ines Varona, a 42-year-old former international-HR director who had joined Vantara in 2022, had opened the three-round commitment that would, across 2026, design each expatriate package, hold each market through a Q2 2026 adjustment window, and close each repatriation inside a Q4 2026 career-design review. The case opens at 6:12 a.m. on Tuesday, February 17, 2026, at the Vantara HQ as Varona prepares the Round-1 assignment-design commitment.

## The Protagonist

Ines Varona, 42, had joined Vantara Group in June 2022 from a European consumer-goods multinational where she had, across seven years, run an international-HR-director mandate that had included thirty-two expatriate assignments across Asia-Pacific and sub-Saharan Africa. Before that, she had spent six years as a regional HR partner at a Tier-1 logistics operator. The Chief People Officer had recruited her specifically on the strength of two capabilities: her experience designing expatriate packages that survived the Round-2 adjustment window, and her reputation for refusing to approve mobility envelopes that under-funded family-fit or hardship differentials. Her 2026 performance plan tied 30% to the GlobalTalent Score at Year-1 close and 20% to the group-mobility-incidents ceiling — a KPI the Chief People Officer had explicitly protected inside the envelope.

## The Decision

Varona had to commit, by the March 3 group-executive-committee commitment, the Round-1 assignment-design envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2 and 3.

1. **Round 1 — Assignment Design** — how to allocate €1.8M across three senior expatriate seats and the four package dimensions (role fit, family fit, tax equalisation, hardship differential). The forecast optimal allocation seated the senior engineer with family in Singapore under a family-fit-heavy package (€620K), the mid-career ops lead solo in Dubai under a role-fit-heavy package with a six-month build commitment (€490K), and the early-career GM with a full settling-in package in Nairobi under a hardship-differential-heavy envelope (€540K). The alternate allocation under-funded Nairobi hardship by €80K and reduced the total envelope to €1.57M but lifted the Round-2 incident-count risk to 0.72 against 0.14 under the full hardship envelope.
2. **Round 2 — Cross-Cultural Adjustment** — how to respond to a Dubai spouse-employment-visa event forecast to land in week twenty-one, when the ops-lead's partner could not secure a work visa and the Dubai monthly pulse dropped from 58 to 34. Three vectors: wait out the visa process (Dubai pulse trend collapse, retention-risk red), escalate to regional embassy channels (two-week delay, ambiguous outcome), or approve a short-term HQ remote role at €12K incremental cost (Dubai pulse climb to 62, retention-risk green).
3. **Round 3 — Repatriation** — how to place the Nairobi GM, who had acquired frontier-market M&A capability that the Madrid HQ did not have a role to use, inside the Q4 2026 career-design review. Three vectors: redesign a new GM-for-LATAM-expansion role at HQ (Retention plus twelve, capital-allocation cost, cross-functional sponsor coordination), redeploy into an existing HQ role that under-used the new capability (Retention minus eight, attrition risk), or release with outplacement (Retention minus eighteen, incidents-count risk).

On Varona's working model, a Full-Hardship-Envelope-plus-Short-Term-Remote-plus-Redesign envelope delivered GlobalTalent Score 79, Retention 88%, Incidents 1, Total Cost €1.71M, Launches 3/3. A Cost-Compressed-Envelope-plus-Wait-plus-Redeploy envelope delivered GlobalTalent 58, Retention 71%, Incidents 3, Launches 2/3. A Full-Hardship-Envelope-plus-Short-Term-Remote-plus-Release envelope delivered GlobalTalent 65, Retention 74%, Incidents 2, Launches 3/3 — a trajectory that closed the three markets but forfeited the repatriation story the Chief People Officer had been recruited to protect.

## Group Background

Vantara Group had been assembled across 2010–2022 through five industrial-services acquisitions into a diversified operating group with €1.3B in 2025 revenue, 4,800 employees across eleven countries, and a global-mobility function that had run an average of eleven concurrent expatriate assignments across 2023–2025. Year-1 retention on expatriate seats had averaged 78% across the window, and the 2024 post-assignment review had placed repatriation-role-fit as the single weakest dimension in the mobility dashboard — a gap that framed the Round-3 Nairobi redesign commitment.

The December 2025 board dossier had identified three specific pressures the three-round envelope would inherit. First, the three-market expansion plan had committed closure dates of Q3 2026 (Singapore), Q4 2026 (Dubai) and Q1 2027 (Nairobi) — a calendar that placed each round's incident-count risk on the critical path of a specific market launch. Second, the Q4 2025 mobility-dashboard review had flagged the Dubai spouse-visa regime as a foreseeable Round-2 event on a 58% expected-landing basis — a flag that framed the Round-2 short-term-remote commitment. Third, the Q1 2026 group-executive-committee had explicitly priced the repatriation-incidents ceiling at two across the three seats — a ceiling that framed the Round-3 redesign commitment.

## Industry and Market Context

The global-mobility landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2019–2024 expatriate-retention evidence base had, across fifty-two European multinational mobility programs, documented a Year-1 retention differential of 14 percentage points between full-hardship-envelope and cost-compressed-envelope designs — a pattern that anchored the Round-1 commitment. Second, the 2020–2024 cross-cultural-adjustment evidence base had, across forty-one Dubai-and-Singapore assignment trails, placed short-term-remote-role commitments at a Round-2 pulse-recovery rate of 81% against 38% for wait-out responses — a pattern that framed the Round-2 commitment. Third, the 2018–2024 repatriation-career-design literature had, across thirty-six repatriations into European HQs, placed redesign commitments at a twelve-month retention rate of 87% against 58% for redeploy commitments and 29% for release commitments — a pattern that framed the Round-3 commitment.

Empirically, the post-2020 international-HR literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, mobility programs that had funded hardship differentials above the median peer benchmark on frontier-market seats had, across a 2020–2024 sample of 24 European multinationals, closed Round-3 retention rates above 85% in 79% of cases against 38% for programs that had under-funded hardship. Second, programs that had pre-committed a Round-2 short-term-remote-role protocol had, across the same sample, held the incidents-count at or below one in 83% of cases against 41% for programs without a pre-committed protocol — a pattern that anchored the Round-2 design commitment.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Chief People Officer had, in the December 2025 group-executive-committee, approved a €1.8M envelope, a retention target above 85%, an incidents ceiling of two across the three markets, and a market-launch closure target of three out of three. The Director of Global Mobility Operations had, on January 20, circulated the Round-1 package model that recommended the full-hardship envelope on the 2024 evidence. The Head of International Rewards had, on January 27, argued for a cost-compressed envelope that preserved 13% of budget for Q2 contingency. The General Counsel had, on February 2, flagged the Dubai spouse-visa regime and argued for a pre-committed short-term-remote-role protocol. The Head of LATAM Expansion had, on February 7, expressed written interest in a new GM role for a frontier-market-M&A mandate — an interest that framed the Round-3 redesign commitment.

### What Varona knew

The team had modelled three three-round envelopes. A Full-Hardship-plus-Short-Term-Remote-plus-Redesign Envelope projected GlobalTalent 79, Retention 88%, Incidents 1, Cost €1.71M, Launches 3/3. A Cost-Compressed-plus-Wait-plus-Redeploy Envelope projected GlobalTalent 58, Retention 71%, Incidents 3, Launches 2/3. A Full-Hardship-plus-Short-Term-Remote-plus-Release Envelope projected GlobalTalent 65, Retention 74%, Incidents 2, Launches 3/3.

### What she did not know

Whether the Dubai spouse-visa event would arrive at the forecast week twenty-one or slip to week twenty-eight; whether the Head of LATAM Expansion's interest in the new GM role would clear the capital-allocation review inside Q3 2026; whether the Nairobi GM's frontier-market-M&A capability would transfer cleanly into the LATAM context; and whether the incidents-count ceiling of two would hold through a Singapore schooling-enrolment edge case flagged in the Q1 2026 risk register.

### What others were saying

**The Director of Global Mobility Operations**: *"Full hardship in Nairobi. The 2024 evidence is unambiguous — compressing hardship is a Round-2 incident."*

**The Head of International Rewards**: *"I can carve out thirteen percent for Q2 contingency. The envelope stretches."*

**The General Counsel**: *"Pre-commit the short-term-remote-role protocol. Dubai spouse-visa is the most foreseeable Round-2 event in the register."*

**The Head of LATAM Expansion**: *"I want the Nairobi GM. Write me the redesign and I will sponsor the capital-allocation review."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Varona had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Full-Hardship / Short-Term-Remote / Redesign:** Round 1 full-hardship Nairobi envelope, family-fit Singapore, role-fit Dubai. Round 2 pre-committed short-term-remote-role on Dubai spouse-visa event. Round 3 redesign new GM-for-LATAM-expansion role. GlobalTalent 79. Retention 88%. Incidents 1. Cost €1.71M. Launches 3/3. Recommended.

- **Option B — Cost-Compressed / Wait / Redeploy:** Cost-compressed envelope with Nairobi hardship under-funded. Wait on Dubai. Redeploy Nairobi GM into an existing HQ role. GlobalTalent 58. Launches 2/3. Rejected.

- **Option C — Full-Hardship / Short-Term-Remote / Release:** Full envelope through Rounds 1–2; release Nairobi GM with outplacement. GlobalTalent 65. Retention 74%. Rejected.

Varona's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final GlobalTalent Score was Round 1 — the full-hardship Nairobi commitment that anchored the Round-2 incidents-count floor and compounded through the Round-3 redesign commitment.
