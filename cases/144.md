## Abstract

COOPVERDE was, in the case universe, a cooperative rural credit union operating across central Mexico, with 12,000 active members, USD 72M in total assets, USD 56M in active loan portfolio, USD 8M in cash, a non-performing-loan ratio of 12% against a Mexican-cooperative peer-benchmark of 6.8%, an efficiency ratio of 78% against a peer-benchmark of 62%, a 2025 social-impact index of 56 against the cooperative-charter-defined floor of 70, and a charter-defined social mission that obliged the entity to serve smallholder-farmer members whose underwriting profile imported a 14–18% NPL envelope against mid-sized-commercial counterparts at 3–5% NPL. The 2023–2025 CEO-operating model had been anchored on a mission-first credit-discipline that had allowed NPLs to drift and operational costs to compress margin — a pattern the Q4 2025 supervisory-commission memo had priced at an approximately USD 1.2M of capital-adequacy-erosion risk. The Q1 2026 board had approved a four-round Chief-Executive-Officer-led rebuild cycle against a Financial-Health Score above 72, a Social-Impact Score above 70, an NPL ceiling below 7%, and a Composite Cooperative Score above 72. The case opens at 6:22 a.m. on Thursday, April 9, 2026, at the CoopVerde headquarters in Toluca as **Elena Mondragón**, a 42-year-old newly-appointed Chief Executive Officer, prepares the Round-1 dual-scorecard commitment. Seventy-seven days separate her from the June 25 cooperative-assembly review.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Round-1 dual-scorecard commitment (Financial Health and Social Impact as two simultaneous performance dimensions) and diagnose when a dual-scorecard mode dominates a financial-only or social-only mode on the cooperative-charter trade-off.
2. Analyze the Round-2 loan-portfolio-mix commitment (60% smallholder / 40% mid-sized-commercial) and recommend the mixed-portfolio envelope against the all-smallholder or all-commercial envelopes on the NPL-versus-social-impact trade-off.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 operational-efficiency commitment (digital-loan-origination, three-branch consolidation, mobile-agent investment) and recommend the digital-plus-mobile-agent envelope against the branch-preserving or branch-closing-only envelopes on the Efficiency-Ratio and member-trust basis.
4. Respond to the Round-4 Banco-Nacional acquisition offer (2.3× book for a clean exit) and recommend the cooperative-preserving counter-offer (lower price, member board-seats, charter protection) against the accept-offer or walk-away envelopes on the Composite-Cooperative-Score basis.
5. Design a four-round cooperative-rebuild sequence that clears Financial-Health above 72, Social-Impact above 70, NPL below 7%, and Composite above 72 under a cooperative-charter constraint.

## The Protagonist

Elena Mondragón, 42, had assumed the Chief-Executive-Officer role at CoopVerde on February 16, 2026, from a Tier-1 Mexican financial-inclusion institution where she had, across ten years, led two loan-portfolio-mix rebuilds and one rural-cooperative turnaround, and had closed a 2022 dual-scorecard rebuild that delivered a Financial-Health uplift of 28 points and a Social-Impact uplift of 16 points without breaching the cooperative-charter. Before that she had spent six years as Head of Credit Risk at a Mexican mid-market commercial bank. The cooperative-board had recruited her specifically on two capabilities: her documented ability to read the two scorecards simultaneously, and her history of refusing to close a branch without a mobile-agent coverage plan in the same quarter. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the Financial-Health outcome and 30% to the Social-Impact floor.

## The Decision

Mondragón had to commit, by the April 30 cooperative-assembly pre-review, the Round-1 dual-scorecard envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–4.

1. **Round 1 — Dual-scorecard diagnosis.** Two scorecards × one cooperative. Three archetypes: dual-scorecard-discipline (Financial Health and Social Impact as two simultaneous performance dimensions with separate floors, Composite 76), financial-only (Financial Health as sole performance dimension — Composite 42, charter-breach risk), or social-only (Social Impact as sole performance dimension — Composite 48, solvency risk). Working envelope: dual-scorecard.
2. **Round 2 — Loan-portfolio mix.** Smallholder loans at an NPL envelope of 14–18% against mid-sized-commercial loans at 3–5% NPL. Three archetypes: mixed-60-40 (60% smallholder / 40% mid-sized-commercial — NPL projected to 9%, Social-Impact intact, Composite 74), all-smallholder (100% smallholder — NPL projected to 16%, solvency risk, Composite 48), or all-commercial (100% mid-sized commercial — NPL projected to 4%, charter-breach, Composite 44). Working envelope: mixed-60-40.
3. **Round 3 — Operational efficiency.** Eighteen branches × 78% efficiency ratio. Three vectors: digital-plus-mobile-agent (digital-loan-origination, three-branch consolidation, USD 1.6M mobile-agent investment covering consolidated zones, Efficiency to 62%, member-trust index recovered, Composite 76), branch-preserving (no consolidation — Composite 52, efficiency unmoved), or branch-closing-only (close three branches with no mobile-agent coverage — Composite 54, member-trust collapse). Working envelope: digital-plus-mobile-agent.
4. **Round 4 — Banco-Nacional acquisition response.** Banco Nacional has offered 2.3× book for a clean exit. Three vectors: cooperative-preserving-counter (structured as a cooperative-preserving acquisition, 1.7× book, member-board-seats, charter-protection, Composite 74), accept-offer (2.3× book, clean exit, member-governance dissolved — Composite 48 on the cooperative-continuity basis), or walk-away (reject offer, forfeit capital injection, NPL remediation self-funded — Composite 58). Working envelope: cooperative-preserving-counter.

On Mondragón's working model, a Dual-Scorecard / Mixed-60-40 / Digital-Plus-Mobile-Agent / Cooperative-Preserving-Counter envelope delivered Financial-Health +38%, Social-Impact +22%, NPL 9%, Composite 76. A Financial-Only / All-Commercial / Branch-Closing-Only / Accept-Offer envelope delivered Financial-Health +24%, Social-Impact −18%, NPL 5%, Composite 44. A Dual-Scorecard / Mixed / Branch-Preserving / Walk-Away envelope delivered Financial-Health +14%, Social-Impact +12%, NPL 11%, Composite 56.

## Company Background

CoopVerde had been founded in Toluca, State of Mexico, in 1973 as a fifty-two-member agricultural cooperative and had, across fifty-three years, grown into a rural credit union with 12,000 active members spread across the central-Mexican agricultural belt, USD 72M in total assets, USD 56M in active loan portfolio, USD 8M in cash, and eighteen branches. The 2023–2025 CEO-operating model had been anchored on a mission-first credit-discipline that had delivered strong Social-Impact membership engagement but had left NPLs drifting from a 2020 baseline of 7% to a 2025 exit of 12% and an efficiency ratio drifting from 68% to 78%.

The Q1 2026 cooperative-board dossier had identified three specific pressures the four-round envelope would inherit. First, the March 2026 Mexican cooperative-supervisory-commission letter had placed CoopVerde inside a capital-adequacy remediation track with a September 2026 submission deadline. Second, the Banco Nacional acquisition-approach of February 18, 2026, had imported a 120-day decision window under a letter-of-intent. Third, the 2025 member-survey had documented a sixteen-point drop in member-trust index across the four branches that had been evaluated for consolidation — a signal the CEO could not ignore while designing the efficiency round.

## Industry and Market Context

The Latin-American rural-credit-union landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2018–2024 dual-scorecard evidence base had, across 52 LATAM cooperative-credit-unions, documented a Composite-Cooperative-Score differential of 18–24 points between dual-scorecard envelopes and financial-only envelopes. Second, the 2020–2024 portfolio-mix literature had, across 38 rural cooperatives, placed mixed-60-40 portfolios at an NPL differential of 5–8 points against all-smallholder portfolios without forfeiting Social-Impact scores above 70. Third, the 2018–2024 cooperative-acquisition literature had, across 28 cooperative-to-commercial-bank deals, placed cooperative-preserving counter-offers at a member-trust twelve-month decline of 6 points against 34 points for clean-exit acquisitions.

Empirically, the post-2022 cooperative-management literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, cooperatives that had consolidated branches without mobile-agent coverage had, across a 2019–2024 sample of 41 LATAM cooperatives, registered a member-trust-index decline of 18–24 points within twelve months against 4–6 points for cooperatives that had paired consolidation with mobile-agent investment. Second, cooperatives that had responded to commercial-bank acquisition offers with cooperative-preserving counter-offers had, across the same sample, preserved charter-intact status in 74% of cases against 8% for cooperatives that had accepted clean-exit offers.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The cooperative-board had, in the January 2026 assembly, approved the rebuild mandate and the June 25 review calendar. The Chief-Financial-Officer had, on February 23, delivered the NPL and capital-adequacy diagnosis. The Chief-Mission-Officer had, on March 2, delivered the Social-Impact scorecard baseline. The Head of Operations had, on March 9, delivered the branch-consolidation-and-mobile-agent proposal. The Banco Nacional acquisition approach landed on February 18 with a 120-day LOI window. The cooperative-supervisory-commission letter landed on March 11.

### What Mondragón knew

The team had modelled three four-round envelopes. A Dual-Scorecard / Mixed-60-40 / Digital-Plus-Mobile-Agent / Cooperative-Preserving Envelope projected Composite 76. A Financial-Only / All-Commercial / Branch-Closing / Accept-Offer Envelope projected Composite 44. A Dual-Scorecard / Mixed / Branch-Preserving / Walk-Away Envelope projected Composite 56.

### What she did not know

Whether the Banco Nacional bid team would accept the cooperative-preserving structure or walk to a commercial-bank target; whether the mobile-agent coverage would preserve member-trust on the four consolidation-candidate branches or whether trust would still decline 8–12 points; whether the Q4 2026 rural-credit-cycle would stress the smallholder sub-portfolio above the 14% NPL envelope; and whether the cooperative-supervisory-commission would accept the 12–9% NPL trajectory within the remediation-track window.

### What others were saying

**The Chief-Financial-Officer**: *"Mixed sixty-forty. Enough commercial to protect capital. Enough smallholder to honor the charter."*

**The Chief-Mission-Officer**: *"Mobile agents. We cannot consolidate branches and leave the members without a face."*

**The Head of Governance**: *"Counter-offer. Not acceptance. The charter is the asset."*

**The Chair of the Cooperative-Board**: *"A cooperative that stops serving members is not a cooperative. A cooperative that goes bankrupt is not either."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Mondragón had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Dual-Scorecard / Mixed-60-40 / Digital-Plus-Mobile-Agent / Cooperative-Preserving-Counter:** the recommended envelope. Financial-Health +38%. Social-Impact +22%. NPL 9%. Composite 76. Recommended.
- **Option B — Financial-Only / All-Commercial / Branch-Closing-Only / Accept-Offer:** the exit envelope. Composite 44. Rejected — charter-breach and member-trust collapse.
- **Option C — Dual-Scorecard / Mixed / Branch-Preserving / Walk-Away:** the status-quo envelope. Composite 56. Rejected — forfeits efficiency and capital injection under supervisory-remediation clock.

Mondragón's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 4 — the cooperative-preserving counter-offer that would fund remediation without dissolving member-governance.
