## Abstract

Bistro Meridiano, a seventy-two-seat full-service bistro in the downtown of a Latin American capital, finished its first quarter under new ownership with a USD 95,000 revenue month, a 68% prime cost, and a net margin of negative four percent. Lucía Beltrán, the General Manager and minority owner, had four months — until the end of Q2 2026 — to bring prime cost to 60% and net margin to positive eight or face a covenant trigger on the partner loan that had financed the takeover. Five Month-1 levers — menu strategy, waste-control investment, beverage-program investment, table-turn target, and online-review-response policy — would compound monthly against a backdrop of seasonal demand, a recently raised delivery-app commission, and a planned visit from a local food blogger. The case opens on the morning of April 6, 2026, the first Monday of Month 1, as Beltrán prepares the operating plan she will discuss with the head chef at 10:00 a.m.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Apply menu-engineering classification (Stars, Plowhorses, Puzzles, Dogs) to a 28-item menu and recommend specific actions per category.
2. Diagnose the components of a 68% prime cost (food, beverage, labor) and identify the two highest-leverage interventions.
3. Evaluate the trade-off between table-turn acceleration and customer-experience risk in a full-service environment.
4. Analyze the channel economics of an off-premise (delivery-app) order versus a dine-in cover at the new commission rate.
5. Recommend a complete Month-1 lever package and identify the random event most likely to force a Month-2 re-tune.

On the morning of **Monday, April 6, 2026**, **Lucía Beltrán** stood behind the host stand at Bistro Meridiano at 8:14 a.m. with the March P&L printed on three sheets of paper and a black marker in her hand. The first sheet showed revenue: USD 95,200. The second sheet showed cost of goods sold and labor: USD 64,700, or 68% of revenue. The third sheet showed net income: negative USD 3,800. The head chef, Esteban Quiñones, would arrive at 10:00 a.m. for the weekly menu meeting. The covenant on the partner loan required a quarterly net-margin print above zero by the end of Q2.

## The Protagonist

Lucía Beltrán, 38, had taken the General Manager and 18% equity position at Bistro Meridiano on January 2, 2026, after eleven years in restaurant operations. She had begun as a server at a hotel restaurant in Mendoza, become a beverage manager at a Buenos Aires steakhouse, and then spent seven years as the operations director for a four-unit Mexico City bistro group. She had walked away from the four-unit role in October 2025 to take a single-unit operator position with equity. The Bistro Meridiano takeover had been financed by a USD 240,000 partner loan from a former colleague turned investor; the loan carried a Q2 covenant requiring a positive net-margin quarter and a Q4 covenant requiring 60% prime cost. The covenants had been stress-tested at the takeover assuming a Q1 cleanup quarter — which had now been delivered, slightly worse than plan.

---

## The Decision

Beltrán had to set, by the end of the day on Monday April 6, the Month-1 values on five operating levers:

1. **Menu Strategy** — Maintain, Reprice Stars, Reengineer Plowhorses, Cut Dogs, or Full Redesign.
2. **Waste-Control Investment** — USD 0 to USD 1,500 in Month 1, on prep sheets, yield tracking, and walk-in inventory cycling.
3. **Beverage-Program Investment** — USD 0 to USD 2,500 in Month 1, on a cocktail rotation, a wine-by-the-glass refresh, or a non-alcoholic program.
4. **Table-Turn Target** — 70 to 110 minutes per dinner seating.
5. **Online-Review-Response Policy** — none, batched weekly, empathetic with personal follow-up, or aggressive with comp offers.

The five levers would compound monthly. A Month-1 menu reengineering would land its full margin lift in Month 2, not Month 1; a waste-control investment paid back in Month 2 and Month 3; a beverage-program investment took a full month to land in average check. The covenant deadline was the end of Month 3, not the end of Month 4.

---

## Company Background

Bistro Meridiano had been founded in 2017 by a husband-and-wife team who had operated it as a hands-on owner-operator restaurant until December 2025, when the wife's health had forced a sale. The previous ownership had not raised prices since 2023, had not changed the menu since 2022, and had not measured prime cost monthly. The 2025 audited revenue was USD 1.08M; reported net margin was 1.4%; the brokered sale had priced the unit at USD 380,000 plus the equity position to the General Manager. The seventy-two seats sat in a corner unit on a one-way street in a residential-commercial mixed-use neighborhood with three direct competitors within four blocks: an Italian trattoria, a Peruvian small-plates concept, and a French steakhouse.

The menu Beltrán had inherited had twenty-eight items: eight starters, fourteen mains, and six desserts. A one-week classification exercise in February had categorized the menu by popularity and contribution margin: 9 Stars (popular, profitable), 7 Plowhorses (popular, unprofitable), 5 Puzzles (unpopular, profitable), 7 Dogs (unpopular, unprofitable). The two highest-volume items — a 380-gram rib-eye steak and a seafood paella for two — were both classified as Plowhorses. The walk-in inventory turn was 4.1 days against an industry benchmark of 2.5; the bar pour cost was 26% against a benchmark of 18%; the dinner table turn was 102 minutes against the floor's design capacity of 85.

## Industry and Market Context

The full-service independent restaurant segment in the city had, between 2022 and 2025, faced three structural pressures. First, food-input inflation of 18% cumulative versus a menu-price increase of 9% cumulative had compressed gross margin by approximately 280 basis points across the segment. Second, delivery-app commissions, after a 2021–2023 freeze, had been raised twice — from 18% to 22% in 2024, and from 22% to 28% in March 2026 — destroying the unit economics of off-premise orders for any restaurant that had built its model assuming the 22%. Third, online review platforms had grown to drive 38% of new-customer trial decisions in the under-45 segment, and the response cadence to reviews had become a measurable lever — restaurants that responded within 24 hours to negative reviews recovered an average 41% of those reviewers as future customers; restaurants that did not respond, or responded after 72 hours, recovered 8%.

Empirically, the menu-engineering literature converged on a small number of robust findings. Reengineering Plowhorses (portion adjustment, plate cost re-targeting, slight reprice) recovered an average 4–6 points of contribution margin on the affected items without measurable popularity loss. Cutting Dogs reduced inventory carrying cost and waste by 8–12% while affecting less than 5% of revenue. Cutting Stars under the assumption that they were "always there" produced the most damaging single decision; the literature found a 14–22% revenue loss in the affected dayparts.

## The Situation

### How we got here

Beltrán had spent Q1 in cleanup mode: rebuilding the recipe book, instituting a shift-by-shift waste log, retraining the kitchen on portioning, retraining the floor on table-turn targets, and recoding the POS to capture item-level contribution margin. She had not yet changed the menu, had not yet reset the beverage program, and had not yet implemented the table-turn target. The Q1 P&L reflected the inherited operating model, with cleanup overhead.

### What Beltrán knew

The five-lever model her finance partner had built — based on three years of POS data — projected three Month-1 packages. A Conservative Package (Reengineer Plowhorses, USD 800 waste, USD 1,200 beverage, 85-minute turn, empathetic responses) projected end-of-Month-1 prime cost of 64%, revenue USD 102K, margin +1%. An Aggressive Package (Cut Dogs and Reengineer Plowhorses, USD 1,200 waste, USD 2,000 beverage, 80-minute turn, aggressive responses) projected prime cost 61%, revenue USD 108K, margin +5%. A Defensive Package (Maintain, USD 400 waste, USD 600 beverage, 95-minute turn, batched responses) projected prime cost 67%, revenue USD 96K, margin -2%. The Conservative Package landed the Q2 covenant; the Aggressive Package landed the Q4 covenant.

### What she did not know

Whether the head chef, Esteban Quiñones, would accept the Plowhorses reengineering on the rib-eye and the paella without escalating to the senior partner; whether the Tuesday-Wednesday traffic — the two slowest days — would hold under an 85-minute turn target; whether the food blogger's planned visit (booked for May 9 under a pseudonym) would land in a Month-1 menu still in transition; and whether the delivery-app commission shift would compress off-premise volume so much that the dine-in book had to absorb the full Q2 revenue plan.

### What others were saying

**Esteban Quiñones**, Head Chef: *"I will reengineer the rib-eye. Twelve percent down on portion, seven percent up on price, plate redesign. The paella stays as is — it is the photograph that brings the table of four."*

**Carla Restrepo**, Floor Lead: *"Eighty-five minutes is doable Friday and Saturday. Not Tuesday. We try eighty-five Friday and Saturday, ninety-five the rest of the week. Otherwise we lose the regulars."*

**Andrés Pinto**, Senior Partner and lender: *"The covenant matters. Hit Q2 and we have eighteen months. Miss Q2 and we restructure."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Beltrán had narrowed the space to three Month-1 packages.

- **Option A — Conservative (Q2-covenant focused):** Reengineer Plowhorses, USD 800 waste, USD 1,200 beverage, 85-minute turn (Friday-Saturday) with 95-minute weekday, empathetic review responses. End-Month-1 prime cost 64%, margin +1%. Q2 covenant landed comfortably; Q4 60% target requires Month-3 acceleration.

- **Option B — Aggressive (Q4-covenant focused):** Cut three Dogs plus Reengineer Plowhorses, USD 1,200 waste, USD 2,000 beverage, 80-minute turn floor-wide, aggressive review responses with comp offers. End-Month-1 prime cost 61%, margin +5%. Both covenants landed; menu transition risk in the food-blogger window.

- **Option C — Beverage-First (margin-mix focused):** Maintain menu, USD 400 waste, USD 2,500 beverage, 90-minute turn, empathetic responses. End-Month-1 prime cost 65%, margin +2%. Q2 covenant landed; relies on beverage attach rate to hit Q4. Lowest kitchen disruption.

Beltrán's private view, not yet shared with the head chef, was that the right answer was Option A in Month 1 and Option B's Dogs cut in Month 4 — sequenced to land the food-blogger visit on a stabilized menu. The senior partner, she anticipated, would press for Option B.

At 9:53 a.m., Beltrán turned the marker over in her hand and wrote three words on the whiteboard above the host stand: *Plowhorses, Dogs, Stars.* Esteban Quiñones came in through the kitchen door at 10:01 a.m. The first table of the new Month 1 was at 12:30 p.m.
