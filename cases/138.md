## Abstract

VIRIATO INDUSTRIAL S.A. was, in the case universe, an Iberian diversified industrial operator with USD 412M in trailing-twelve-month revenue as of Q4 2025, three operating divisions (heavy-fabrication, industrial-components and specialty-chemicals), five manufacturing plants across Spain and Portugal, 2,840 employees, and a 2023–2025 trading trajectory that had, across eight consecutive quarters of declining EBITDA, driven the operator into a covenant-triggered restructuring process. The Q4 2025 twelve-month-forward outlook had priced USD 65M of senior-unsecured bond debt maturing on June 30, 2026, a monthly EBITDA burn of USD 2.1M, and a closing-cash balance of USD 8.2M. On Friday, April 10, 2026, the senior lending-bank syndicate had granted a 90-day standstill agreement against the May 15 interest payment and the June 30 maturity; the standstill had twelve days of elapsed time at case open, leaving seventy-eight days of protected runway. The 2026 board had appointed a Chief Restructuring Officer with a four-track mandate (Cash, Operations, Creditors, New Financing) against an eleven-week timeline, a target of Weeks-of-Liquidity above zero at maturity, an EBITDA flip above zero by week nine, a Creditor-Coverage above 95% across senior and subordinated claims, and a composite Turnaround Score above 70. The case opens at 5:36 a.m. on Tuesday, April 21, 2026, at the Viriato Madrid headquarters as **Estela Mondariz**, a 48-year-old Chief Restructuring Officer, prepares the Round-1 cash-track commitment.

## The Protagonist

Estela Mondariz, 48, had joined Viriato Industrial S.A. as Chief Restructuring Officer on April 4, 2026, from a European distressed-and-turnaround advisory firm where she had, across eleven years, led four Iberian-industrial turnaround mandates (two covenant-triggered, one pre-petition, one debtor-in-possession-financed post-petition) and had closed three of the four inside the contracted standstill window. Before that, she had spent six years as Chief Financial Officer of a Madrid-listed specialty-chemicals operator. The Chair of the Viriato Board had appointed her specifically on the strength of two capabilities: her experience running a four-track parallel turnaround inside a ninety-day standstill, and her reputation for refusing to accept a new-financing mandate that had not cleared a documented Weeks-of-Liquidity-at-maturity test. Her 2026 mandate tied 50% of the variable compensation to solvent case-outcome at June 30 and 20% to the closing-cash balance at Day-90 close.

## The Decision

Mondariz had to commit, by the April 27 board-restructuring-committee review, the Round-1 cash-track envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–4.

1. **Round 1 — Cash track.** Three archetypes: three-lever-cash-preservation (cut non-essential capex USD 4.2M; hiring freeze, saving USD 0.4M/month; 60-day payables stretch, releasing USD 3.8M of working capital — cash-burn drops from USD 2.1M/month to USD 0.8M/month, three extra weeks of runway), capex-only (cut capex USD 4.2M — cash-burn USD 1.4M/month), or payables-only (stretch payables — supply-chain integrity risk on critical vendors). Working envelope: three-lever cash-preservation.
2. **Round 2 — Operations track.** Three vectors: Andalusian-plant-closure (close the loss-making plant on week 5, lose 18% of revenue, save USD 3.4M/year in fixed cost, EBITDA flips positive by week 9), defer-closure (continue loss-making operation — EBITDA negative through week 11, creditor-coverage risk), or partial-closure-plus-reduced-shift (transitional path — EBITDA flip by week 14, outside window). Working envelope: Andalusian-plant-closure.
3. **Round 3 — URGENT creditor-track event.** Event: trade creditors accept a 22% haircut standstill; the pension fund refuses; the bond trustee files a liquidation motion at week 8. Three vectors: trade-creditor-haircut-plus-pension-fund-secured-claim (pension fund secured against specialty-chemicals division assets, bond-trustee motion deferred pending new-financing commitment — Creditor-Coverage 96%), defer-and-negotiate (pension-fund negotiation runs in parallel without secured-claim anchor, Creditor-Coverage 78%), or blanket-haircut-proposal (seek 22% across all creditors, pension-fund rejection risk, bond-trustee motion escalates — Creditor-Coverage 62%). Working envelope: trade-creditor-haircut-plus-pension-fund-secured-claim.
4. **Round 4 — New-financing track.** Three vectors: distressed-bridge-loan at 14% plus 15%-equity warrants on a covenant-lite structure (USD 70M bridge, clears bond maturity, 18-month-runway post-refinance), rights-issue (USD 30M new equity from existing shareholders, USD 40M shortfall on June 30 maturity — incomplete clearance), or extend-and-amend (negotiate 18-month maturity extension with existing bondholders — 64% Bondholder-Acceptance-Rate on 2020–2024 evidence, residual maturity-wall risk). Working envelope: distressed-bridge-loan.

On Mondariz's working model, a Three-Lever-Cash / Andalusian-Closure / Trade-Haircut-plus-Pension-Secured / Distressed-Bridge envelope delivered Weeks-of-Liquidity +3 at June-30 clearance, EBITDA positive from week 9, Creditor-Coverage 96%, Composite 78 — solvent case outcome. A Capex-Only / Defer-Closure / Blanket-Haircut / Rights-Issue Envelope delivered Weeks-of-Liquidity -4, EBITDA negative through week 11, Creditor-Coverage 62%, Composite 28 — insolvency-filing outcome. A Three-Lever-Cash / Andalusian-Closure / Blanket-Haircut / Distressed-Bridge Envelope delivered Weeks-of-Liquidity 0, Creditor-Coverage 78%, Composite 54 — clears the wall but triggers a bond-trustee residual claim inside Q3 2026.

## Company Background

Viriato Industrial S.A. had been founded in Cáceres in 1978 as a heavy-fabrication operator and had, across forty-eight years, grown into an Iberian diversified industrial operator with USD 412M in 2025 revenue, three operating divisions (heavy-fabrication, industrial-components, specialty-chemicals), five manufacturing plants across Spain and Portugal, 2,840 employees, and a 2019 USD 85M senior-unsecured bond issuance that carried a June 2026 final-maturity date. The 2022–2025 trading decline had been driven by three compounding pressures: a 2022 energy-cost spike that had compressed specialty-chemicals margin; a 2023 Iberian construction-cycle contraction that had compressed heavy-fabrication volumes; and a 2024–2025 automation-investment lag inside industrial-components that had driven a three-point market-share loss against Tier-1 European competitors.

The Q1 2026 board restructuring dossier had identified three specific pressures the eleven-week envelope would inherit. First, the Q4 2025 covenant-breach on net-leverage had triggered the senior-lending-bank syndicate's request for a 2026 restructuring plan, delivered in March 2026 and approved on April 10 as the 90-day standstill. Second, the Q1 2026 enhanced cash-flow diagnostic had priced the monthly EBITDA burn at USD 2.1M and the Andalusian-plant-specific contribution-margin drag at USD 0.6M/month. Third, the Q1 2026 creditor register had placed the pension-fund senior-claim at USD 18M, the bondholder senior-unsecured claim at USD 65M, and the trade-creditor claim at USD 32M — a register that framed the Round-3 creditor-track commitment.

## Industry and Market Context

The European distressed-and-restructuring landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2018–2024 Iberian-industrial restructuring evidence base had, across 48 covenant-triggered mandates, placed four-track-parallel-turnaround envelopes at a solvent-case-outcome rate of 79% against 34% for single-track-sequential envelopes inside a 90-day standstill. Second, the 2019–2024 bond-trustee-liquidation-motion literature had, across 34 European-industrial pre-maturity motions, placed trade-creditor-haircut-plus-secured-pension-claim envelopes at a motion-deferral rate of 82% against 38% for blanket-haircut proposals. Third, the 2020–2024 distressed-bridge-loan market had, across 52 European-industrial covenant-lite bridges, priced 14%-coupon plus 15%-equity-warrant structures at an issuance-acceptance rate of 68% against 28% for rights-issue alternatives inside a 90-day standstill.

Empirically, the post-2022 restructuring literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, operators that had cut non-essential capex inside Week 1 of a standstill and stretched payables by at least 45 days had, across a 2019–2024 sample of 42 European industrials, held Weeks-of-Liquidity above zero at standstill-close in 78% of cases against 24% for operators with capex-only preservation. Second, operators that had closed their single highest-loss-making plant inside Week 5 had, across the same sample, flipped EBITDA positive inside Week 10 in 81% of cases against 32% for operators with defer-closure positions.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Chair of the Board had, on April 4, appointed Mondariz as Chief Restructuring Officer. The senior-lending-bank syndicate had, on April 10, granted the 90-day standstill against the May 15 interest payment and June 30 maturity. The Controller had, on April 13, finalised the eleven-week cash-flow forecast. The Debt-Restructuring Counsel had, on April 16, delivered the creditor-register review and the pension-fund-secured-claim architecture. The Andalusian-plant General Manager had, on April 18, confirmed the plant-closure timeline at five weeks. The Chief Executive Officer had, on April 20, pre-committed to full cooperation with the Chief Restructuring Officer mandate.

### What Mondariz knew

The team had modelled three eleven-week envelopes. A Three-Lever-Cash / Andalusian-Closure / Trade-Haircut-plus-Pension-Secured / Distressed-Bridge Envelope projected Composite 78 with solvent-case outcome. A Capex-Only / Defer-Closure / Blanket-Haircut / Rights-Issue Envelope projected Composite 28 with insolvency-filing outcome. A Three-Lever-Cash / Andalusian-Closure / Blanket-Haircut / Distressed-Bridge Envelope projected Composite 54 with residual bond-trustee claim risk.

### What she did not know

Whether the Andalusian-plant closure would drive a contagion effect on the industrial-components division's order book through Week 11; whether the pension-fund secured-claim architecture would hold under the pension-fund trustee's fiduciary review; whether the distressed-bridge-loan fund would price the covenant-lite structure at 14% or escalate to 16% on due-diligence findings; and whether the Q3 2026 Iberian-industrial cycle would provide the post-refinance tailwind that the bridge-loan EBITDA-covenant assumed.

### What others were saying

**The Controller**: *"Three-lever cash. Capex, hiring, payables. Cash burn drops from 2.1 to 0.8. Three extra weeks of runway."*

**The Debt-Restructuring Counsel**: *"Trade-creditor haircut first. Pension-fund secured claim second. The bond-trustee motion is deferrable."*

**The Andalusian-Plant General Manager**: *"Five weeks to orderly closure. We can hit it."*

**The Chair of the Board**: *"Turnaround isn't strategy. It's a cash schedule with a deadline."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Mondariz had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Three-Lever-Cash / Andalusian-Closure / Trade-Haircut-plus-Pension-Secured / Distressed-Bridge:** the recommended envelope. Weeks-of-Liquidity +3. EBITDA flips Week 9. Creditor-Coverage 96%. Composite 78. Solvent case outcome. Recommended.
- **Option B — Capex-Only / Defer-Closure / Blanket-Haircut / Rights-Issue:** the minimalist envelope. Composite 28. Rejected — insolvency-filing trajectory.
- **Option C — Three-Lever-Cash / Andalusian-Closure / Blanket-Haircut / Distressed-Bridge:** the aggressive-creditor envelope. Composite 54. Rejected — clears the wall but triggers residual bond-trustee claim in Q3 2026.

Mondariz's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single track most correlated with the final Composite was the Round-3 creditor-track — the trade-creditor-haircut-plus-pension-fund-secured-claim commitment that, under the bond-trustee liquidation motion, unlocked the Round-4 distressed-bridge-loan commitment that cleared the June 30 maturity.
