## Abstract

Lumena Electronics, a Portuguese consumer-electronics manufacturer with EUR 2.1 billion in 2025 revenue and a product mix of smartphones (42%), audio (28%), and small home appliances (30%), entered 2026 under converging pressure: the EU's Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR) was moving to binding product-level requirements for 2027; two of its five largest institutional investors had signed a circularity-disclosure commitment; and its largest retail customer had begun to grade suppliers on a published circularity score. Lumena's linear business model (design → produce → sell → recycle) carried a circularity index of 0.18 (industry median 0.34, leader 0.62). A new Chief Operations & Sustainability Officer, Iara Menezes, had been given a four-quarter transformation budget of EUR 60M and the authority to rebalance across four investment levers: reverse logistics, refurbishment, product redesign (design-for-disassembly), and business-model innovation. Quarter 1 would allocate EUR 15M. The case places Menezes in the days before her first board-investment committee and in front of three internally coherent options advocated by three senior voices.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate capital-allocation decisions across four circularity levers with different risk, timing, and organizational-change profiles.
2. Analyze the interaction between regulatory thresholds, investor disclosure, and customer scorecards in a circular transition.
3. Design a multi-quarter capital sequence that builds optionality across refurbishment, product redesign, and product-as-a-service.
4. Diagnose the near-term margin impact of circularity investment and the conditions under which it becomes margin-accretive.
5. Recommend a Q1 plan and articulate the falsifiable assumptions and event-sensitivities that define its failure modes.

On the evening of **April 7, 2026**, **Iara Menezes** sat in the Chief Operations & Sustainability Officer's office on the ninth floor of Lumena's Lisboa headquarters and looked, for the fourth time, at the single-page summary the treasury team had prepared: *Q1 Circularity Budget — EUR 15M — Allocation To Be Decided.* The Board Investment Committee would convene at 08:00 the next morning. She had, on her desk, three printed proposals from three senior colleagues, and, next to them, a fourth sheet that was still blank.

---

## The Protagonist

Iara Menezes, 46, had been named Lumena's first Chief Operations & Sustainability Officer in January 2026. Trained as an industrial engineer at Instituto Superior Técnico and holder of an Executive MBA from IESE, she had spent ten years running manufacturing operations at an automotive Tier-1 in Porto, followed by four years leading sustainability transformation for a Scandinavian white-goods group. Her reputation at Lumena rested on two priors: she had led her prior employer through the first independently audited circular-product ranges in its category, and she had published a short paper on the economics of reverse logistics that the two sustainability-disclosure investors had cited in their pre-offer call with Lumena's Chair.

Her contract carried a four-year vesting gate tied to Lumena's circularity index and to the absolute margin trajectory of the refurbishment business unit she was being asked to stand up.

---

## The Decision

Menezes had to allocate, by 08:00 on April 8, a Quarter-1 budget of EUR 15M across the four investment levers of the circular transformation:

1. **Reverse logistics** — take-back infrastructure, collection, triage, sorting.
2. **Refurbishment** — re-manufacturing lines, quality assurance, warranty architecture.
3. **Product redesign** — design-for-disassembly, modular architectures, parts standardization.
4. **Business-model innovation** — product-as-a-service, leasing, subscription.

In addition she had to declare a strategic stance for the four-quarter transformation: *Defensive (compliance-led), Progressive (customer-led), Bold (investor-led),* or *Financial (margin-protective)*. The board would vote the combination as a single package. Lumena's CEO had told Menezes at 16:40 on April 7: *"Pick the direction in which we will look back from 2028 and say 'we are glad we chose it.' Do not pick what is safe."*

---

## Company Background

Lumena had been founded in 1989 in Braga as a contract manufacturer of portable radios and had, over thirty-six years, become a mid-cap consumer-electronics group listed on Euronext Lisbon. 2025 revenue was EUR 2.1 billion with an EBIT margin of 6.9% — substantially below Asian peers (9.5%) but representative of European mid-cap electronics. The group employed 7,800 people across three assembly plants in Portugal, Spain, and Poland and had a service-and-warranty network across twenty-two European markets.

Lumena's product architecture had been optimized for assembly cost and component consolidation. As a consequence, ninety-four percent of its smartphone units and eighty-seven percent of its small-appliance units were not economically disassemblable on return. The company's reverse-logistics flow comprised three contracted e-waste handlers and no internal re-manufacturing capacity. Refurbished product accounted for less than 1% of 2025 revenue. A pilot leasing program for professional audio products, launched in 2024, had reached a EUR 6M run-rate by end-2025.

Lumena's institutional-investor base had shifted materially in 2024–2025 toward ESG-integrated mandates. Two of the five largest institutional holders had, in February 2026, co-signed a circularity-disclosure framework requiring portfolio companies to publish a circularity index and a multi-year transformation plan by Q3 2026.

## Industry and Market Context

The consumer-electronics industry, globally, sat in 2026 at an inflection. The ESPR's product-level delegated acts, expected to be binding by end-2027 for smartphones, small appliances, and selected audio categories, would require: minimum repairability scores, minimum recycled-content shares, and a published digital product passport. Right-to-repair legislation had, by March 2026, become binding in eight European markets and voluntary in six others.

Industry benchmarks placed the category leader — a Scandinavian audio incumbent — at a circularity index of 0.62, two categories of margin-accretive refurbishment, and a product-as-a-service revenue stream of 11% of group revenue. The industry median sat at 0.34. The bottom quartile — where Lumena resided — sat at 0.22 and below.

Internal analytics estimated four dynamics: (i) each EUR 1M in reverse-logistics spend above EUR 10M returned 2.1 index points but was, below that threshold, effectively sunk cost; (ii) refurbishment lines became positive-contribution above a throughput of 60,000 units per quarter, with a payback of 2.3 years at mid-point pricing; (iii) product redesign carried a 24-month lag to first revenue impact but unlocked the other three levers; and (iv) product-as-a-service, properly capitalized, moved revenue recognition and materially changed unit economics but required an expanded working-capital line.

---

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board's decision to create the CO&SO role had been crystallized by the January 2026 investor circularity letter and by the March 2026 scorecard from Lumena's largest retail customer — a pan-European electronics chain — which had grouped suppliers into A, B, and C tiers. Lumena had graded C. The retail customer had announced that, from Q1 2027, only A- and B-tier suppliers would qualify for feature promotions and end-caps.

### What Menezes knew

The internal analytics team had modeled six-quarter trajectories under three allocations. Reverse-logistics and refurbishment spend moved the circularity index fastest in the near term; product redesign moved it slowest but unlocked the highest ceiling. Product-as-a-service moved disclosure-friendly metrics the fastest but required the largest organizational change (sales incentive redesign, credit-risk function, service operations).

### What she did not know

Whether the ESPR delegated acts would land at the regulatory minimum or above it. Whether the retail customer's grading would be symmetric (B moves to A becomes A) or asymmetric (B needs to clear a higher bar to advance). Whether the CFO's concern over the EUR 15M margin drag was correct at the full rate or overstated by half (the disagreement between finance and sustainability was live).

### What others were saying

**Henrique Sá**, CFO, argued for **Defensive / compliance-led**. *"Spend what regulation requires. Nothing more. Hold margin. We transform on the board's money, not on Q1 margin."*

**Marta Lopes**, Chief Commercial Officer, argued for **Progressive / customer-led**. *"The retail customer is the constraint. B-tier by Q4 2026 or we lose shelf. Fund reverse logistics and refurbishment. Product redesign waits."*

**André Valente**, Director of Innovation, argued for **Bold / investor-led**. *"The investor letter is a signal, not a constraint. Invest in product redesign and product-as-a-service. Those compound. Reverse logistics is a middle-of-the-pack play."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

- **Option A — Defensive / Compliance-Led (Sá plan):** Reverse logistics EUR 6M; refurbishment EUR 4M; redesign EUR 3M; PaaS EUR 2M. Index trajectory to 0.22 by end-Q4; margin drag EUR 5M. Low regulatory risk; low customer-grading movement; no investor signal.

- **Option B — Progressive / Customer-Led (Lopes plan):** Reverse logistics EUR 8M; refurbishment EUR 5M; redesign EUR 1M; PaaS EUR 1M. Index to 0.28 by end-Q4; refurbishment line commissioned in Q3; retail customer likely moves to B-tier by Q1 2027. Margin drag EUR 7M. Low long-run optionality.

- **Option C — Bold / Investor-Led (Valente plan):** Reverse logistics EUR 4M; refurbishment EUR 3M; redesign EUR 5M; PaaS EUR 3M. Index to 0.25 by end-Q4 but 0.38 by end-Q6. Investor disclosure strengthened. Margin drag EUR 8M. High Q2–Q3 execution risk on redesign.

Menezes's own instinct, still unspoken, was that Option C was right provided she could sequence it behind a small Q1 down-payment on refurbishment that would give the CCO enough to place on the retail-customer account manager's table on April 22.

Menezes picked up the fourth, still-blank sheet, drew four columns — RL, Refurb, Redesign, PaaS — and wrote, over the top, in pencil: *"Bold, sequenced."* She would sleep on it. The meeting was at 08:00.
