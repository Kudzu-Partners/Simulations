## Abstract

Puerto Atlántico was, in the case universe, a regional LATAM container port with 2025 annual throughput of 800,000 TEU, a dwell time of 11 hours, an average turnaround of 4 hours, revenue of USD 320M, operating cost of USD 220M, profit of USD 100M, market share of 12%, shipping-line satisfaction of 60, an eight-berth footprint with an average crane age of 17 years, and a Board-approved capital budget of USD 200M across three rounds. Its principal competitor, Puerto Nueva, ran an eight-hour dwell with nearly identical throughput. On Monday, January 12, 2026, the Board approved a three-round integrated commitment inside which the Chief Executive would commit efficiency in Round 1 (IT, customs, cranes, labor), capital in Round 2 (berth expansion, hinterland rail, specialization), and resilience in Round 3 (shipping-contract rebid, green infrastructure, seawall) — under a scenario that a Category-Three hurricane would hit during Round-3 peak season. Rodrigo Nieto, a 54-year-old former Chief Operating Officer promoted to Chief Executive on November 4, 2025, faced a Board mandate that the three-round trajectory had to lift throughput above 1.25M TEU, compress dwell below 8 hours, lift market share above 17%, hold profit above USD 180M, clear the hurricane resilience test, and close Round 3 on a composite above 80 — against the shipping-line rebid at the end of Round 3 that would lose 30% of book if the dwell gap with Puerto Nueva did not close. The case opens at 5:28 a.m. on Monday, January 19, 2026, at the Puerto Atlántico operations-center floor on the coastal industrial corridor, as Nieto prepares the Round-1 efficiency commitment.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Round-1 efficiency commitment (25% IT, 15% customs, USD 72M aggressive crane replacement across four cranes, productivity-bonus labor restructuring) and diagnose when a labor-plus-IT-plus-customs envelope dominates crane-only on the dwell-time compression.
2. Analyze the Round-2 capital commitments (USD 95M for two new berths, USD 45M hinterland rail to inland dry port, USD 22M reefer-plus-chemical specialization, transshipment to 15% of capacity) and recommend the debt-service trajectory consistent with a Round-3 shipping-contract rebid.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 resilience commitments (multi-year five-year indexed shipping contract with three majors locking 68% of bookable volume, USD 38M shore-power-plus-LNG-bunkering green infrastructure, USD 28M seawall-plus-generator resilience) and design the Category-Three-hurricane response that keeps the port open.
4. Design a three-round integrated sequence that pays for capital with the efficiency margin that Round 1 delivered and pays for resilience with the capital-utilization that Round 2 opened.
5. Recommend a complete three-round sequence, identify the round most correlated with the Round-3 composite under the hurricane scenario, and write the Board-and-shipping-line-facing paragraph the Round-1-to-Round-3 trajectory would anchor.

On the morning of **Monday, January 19, 2026**, **Rodrigo Nieto** arrived at the Puerto Atlántico operations-center floor on Avenida del Puerto at 5:28 a.m., opened the Round-1 efficiency sheet his Chief Operating Officer and Chief Technology Officer had finalized at 12:46 a.m., and re-read the sentence he had pinned above the operations-center dashboard in his November appointment address: "The cargo that moves faster through us is the cargo that stays with us when the lines rebid." Twelve days separated him from the January 31 Board commitment.

---

## The Protagonist

Rodrigo Nieto, 54, had joined Puerto Atlántico in 2005 as an operations manager for container terminal A, had been promoted to head of container operations in 2014, and had been named Chief Operating Officer in 2020. A graduate of the Universidad Marítima in naval architecture with an MBA from IE Madrid and a port-terminal-operations certification from the TOC Europe program, he had, in his Chief Operating Officer tenure, led the 2021 turnaround-time compression from 5.2 hours to 4 hours, designed the 2023 pre-clearance pilot that had proven the customs-plus-IT integration case, and had, in 2024, co-authored the Board memorandum that had framed the three-round envelope as efficiency-funded-capital-funded-resilience — the memorandum that earned him the November 2025 Chief Executive appointment.

---

## The Decision

Nieto had to commit, by the January 31 Board commitment, the Round-1 sheet and the Round-2 and Round-3 intent:

1. **Round-1 Efficiency** — whether to commit 25% of capital to IT (port community system replacing three legacy systems, booking integrated with customs pre-clearance, truck-appointment system), 15% to customs digitization (funded by the port rather than waited for from the government), USD 72M to aggressive crane replacement (four of the oldest cranes at USD 18M each), and a labor restructure to productivity bonus (not status quo).
2. **Round-2 Capital** — whether to commit USD 95M to two new berths (the most expensive line but the one that compounds if the Round-3 rebid wins), USD 45M to a hinterland-rail connection with the inland dry port (addressing the 14%-of-cargo-on-truck-lines constraint), USD 22M to reefer-and-chemical specialization (Puerto Nueva does not have the capability), and grow transshipment to 15% of capacity.
3. **Round-3 Resilience** — whether to sign a multi-year five-year indexed shipping contract with three majors (locking 68% of bookable volume), commit USD 38M to green infrastructure (shore power and LNG bunkering, addressing the IMO regulation European lines asked for), and commit USD 28M to seawall-raise-and-backup-crane-generators resilience (pre-empting the Category-Three hurricane scenario).

The three sub-decisions would compound. On Nieto's working model, Round 1 delivered dwell at 8.8 hours (from 11, a 30% compression), turnaround at 3.1 hours, throughput at 920K TEU (+15%), revenue at USD 365M, labor cost down 6%, satisfaction at 72, market share at 13.4%, and profit at USD 133M. Round 2 delivered throughput at 1.18M TEU, dwell at 7.6 hours (below Puerto Nueva's 8), revenue at USD 482M, rail moves at 22% of cargo (from 0), reefer share at 14% (from 4), transshipment at USD 38M, satisfaction at 81, market share at 16.2%, profit at USD 188M, and debt at USD 65M (serviceable). Round 3 delivered throughput at 1.32M TEU, dwell at 7.1 hours, revenue at USD 612M, profit at USD 228M, market share at 18.7% (+6.7 points), satisfaction at 86, green infrastructure score at 78, sustainability at 72, debt serviced, hurricane absorbed, and a composite of 84.

## Company Background

Puerto Atlántico had been established in 1968 as a break-bulk port on the regional coastal industrial corridor, had been containerized across the 1990–2005 period, and had, across the 2005–2025 period, built the eight-berth 800,000-TEU-throughput footprint the case studied. The 2025 revenue of USD 320M was distributed as container-handling fees 58%, storage-and-demurrage 14%, value-added-services 12%, specialized-cargo-handling 10%, and ancillary 6%. The port's competitive position had rested, since 2012, on a coastal hinterland that served three provincial capitals and an inland dry port 140 kilometers inland, and on a service-level agreement with the three principal LATAM shipping lines (East-Atlantic, Pacific-West, Trans-Pacifico) that carried 58% of throughput volume.

The Board's December 2025 strategy dossier had identified three specific pressures the three-round envelope would inherit. First, Puerto Nueva had, across the 2023–2025 period, compressed dwell from 10.2 hours to 8 hours on an incremental USD 120M investment, creating a structural dwell gap that had, in 2025, re-routed approximately 14% of containerized volume that had historically flowed through Puerto Atlántico. Second, the three principal shipping lines had, on December 4, 2025, notified Puerto Atlántico that the 2024 five-year contract would close at the end of Round 3 and would rebid on a dwell-plus-green-infrastructure-plus-resilience framework — a pattern that framed the Round-3 rebid-and-green-and-seawall commitments. Third, the regional meteorological authority had, in the January 2026 seasonal forecast, placed a 62% probability on a Category-Three-or-higher hurricane impact inside the 2026–2027 peak-season window — a pattern that framed the Round-3 hurricane scenario.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM regional container-port landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2018–2024 IT-plus-customs-plus-labor-restructure evidence base had, across a commercial-summary sample of 14 LATAM regional container ports that had committed the three-lever envelope in a single round, documented a dwell compression of 2–3 hours within 12 months at a labor-cost compression of 4–8% — a pattern that monetized the Round-1 commitment. Second, the hinterland-rail-plus-specialization evidence base had, across a 2020–2024 sample of 9 LATAM ports that had committed rail-plus-reefer-plus-chemical above USD 60M, produced a transshipment-and-specialized-cargo revenue lift of 20–32% within 24 months — a pattern that anchored the Round-2 commitment. Third, the shore-power-plus-LNG-bunkering evidence base had, across a 2021–2024 sample of 7 LATAM ports that had committed green-infrastructure above USD 30M ahead of the IMO 2030 mandate, produced a contract-rebid premium of 12–18% on lock-in rate against ports that had deferred — a trajectory that anchored the Round-3 commitment.

Empirically, the post-2019 port-operations literature had two robust findings the envelope had to respect. First, ports that had sequenced efficiency (Round 1) ahead of capital (Round 2) ahead of resilience (Round 3) had, across a 2019–2024 sample of 17 LATAM peers, closed the composite 1.8 times higher than ports that had sequenced capital-first. Second, ports that had committed seawall-raise-and-backup-generator resilience ahead of a Category-Three hurricane event had, across a 2018–2024 sample of 8 LATAM Caribbean-and-Atlantic ports, preserved 94% of peak-season volume against a 58% median for un-invested peers.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Board had, in the December 2025 off-site, approved a three-round composite target of 80, a Round-3 dwell floor of 8 hours, a market-share floor of 17%, a profit floor of USD 180M, and a hurricane-resilience clearance requirement. The Chief Operating Officer had, on January 5, circulated a Round-1 pre-reading that proposed the IT-plus-customs-plus-crane-plus-labor envelope. The Chief Technology Officer had, on January 8, signed off on the port-community-system replacement conditional on the Round-1 labor-productivity-bonus restructure. The Chief Financial Officer had, on January 12, argued for the Round-2 USD 95M berth expansion conditional on the Round-1 profit lift to USD 130M or above.

### What Nieto knew

The team had modeled three three-round envelopes. A Efficiency-Capital-Resilience Envelope (Round-1 IT-plus-customs-plus-cranes-plus-labor; Round-2 berths-plus-rail-plus-specialization; Round-3 rebid-plus-green-plus-seawall) projected a Round-3 composite of 84. A Capital-First Envelope (Round-1 USD 150M on berths before efficiency) projected a composite of 52 — dwell gap with Puerto Nueva not closed, rebid lost. A Resilience-Only Envelope (Round-1 USD 80M on seawall before efficiency) projected a composite of 48 — hurricane absorbed but dwell gap unaddressed.

### What he did not know

Whether Puerto Nueva would respond to the Round-1 dwell-compression with its own second-wave efficiency push; whether the three principal shipping lines would hold the five-year rebid framework or re-price after the Round-2 berth-expansion signal; whether the Category-Three hurricane would hit inside Round 3 or delay to Round 4; and whether the hinterland-rail connection would clear the inland-dry-port municipal-permitting framework inside Round 2.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Operating Officer**: *"Round-1 efficiency envelope. Labor-productivity-bonus is the single highest-return line. The union wants it; finance has feared it wrongly."*

**The Chief Technology Officer**: *"Round-1 IT-at-25%. Port community system replaces three legacies; pre-clearance cuts dispatch."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"Round-2 USD 95M berth expansion funded by Round-1 profit lift. Do not issue new debt above USD 75M."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Nieto had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Efficiency-Capital-Resilience:** Round-1 IT-customs-cranes-labor; Round-2 berths-rail-specialization; Round-3 rebid-green-seawall. Round-3 composite 84. Aligns with COO, CTO, CFO. Recommended.

- **Option B — Capital-First:** Round-1 USD 150M on berths. Round-3 composite 52. Dwell gap not closed, rebid lost. Rejected.

- **Option C — Resilience-Only:** Round-1 USD 80M on seawall. Round-3 composite 48. Hurricane absorbed but rebid lost on dwell. Rejected.

Nieto's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the Round-3 composite was Round 1 — the IT-plus-customs-plus-cranes-plus-labor commitment, which compressed dwell below Puerto Nueva's 8 hours ahead of the rebid and funded every capital-and-resilience commitment that followed.
