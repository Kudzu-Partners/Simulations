# Caldorra Industrial Services: The Xalteca Tender

**Case Code:** MKT-2026-1406 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1406 — *Sentinela Re-Pricing — Caldorra B2B Brand Premium Defence* · **v1.0 · October 2026**

**Time required: 80 minutes**

---

At 09:14 on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, a tender cover letter from Xalteca, a Mexican state-owned oil company, reached the inbox of Verónica Castelar-Drago, managing director of Caldorra Industrial Services (PIS). Xalteca was PIS's largest customer: sixteen sites running the Sentinela Suite process-safety and industrial-monitoring platform, worth US$38 million of annual recurring revenue (ARR), 14% of the business. The letter put the contract, which expired on March 31, 2027, out to competitive bids. For the first time, it also invited Vaudrey to bid its new Plantara platform against Sentinela.

Eighteen minutes later a calendar invitation arrived from Andrés Calderón, group chief financial officer of Caldorra: "Xalteca situation review — 15:00 today, my office. Roberto is aware. Bring the team." By three o'clock Castelar-Drago had to bring Calderón a position on three questions: what Sentinela was worth to Xalteca, how PIS's brand line-up should answer Vaudrey, and what PIS would offer Xalteca.

## The Protagonist

Castelar-Drago, 49, had spent twenty-three years in industrial software. She joined Caldorra in 2014 to turn a thirty-person internal-tools team into a commercial unit, and she set Sentinela's original price that year. Calderón approved it because the value evidence cleared his bar. For twelve years she had defended the premium that followed.

Two colleagues would work the problem with her in the war room on the 14th floor. Manuel Echeverría-Solís, head of sales, 51, had covered Xalteca since 2009, at PIS since 2015, and could name every buyer in its procurement chain. Iván Reigosa-Bernal, head of product, 43, had built the Suite's architecture with her. He owned its machine-learning predictive-maintenance modules, the features competitors had found hardest to copy.

## The Decision

The working group had until 15:00 to settle three things, in order: the value case Castelar-Drago would put in front of Calderón; the brand architecture PIS would use to answer Vaudrey across its whole base; and the Xalteca offer, with its contract term and the line the sales force would use when other customers heard about it. Calderón would then decide whether to sign off a rollout memo that Roberto Solana, Caldorra's group chief executive, would read the following Monday.

## The Company and its Setting

PIS was the business-software arm of Caldorra, a US$14 billion energy group controlled by the Solana family. It closed 2025 with US$272 million of ARR from 312 customer sites in 89 accounts: refineries, petrochemical plants, mines and pipelines, most of them in Latin America. Sentinela had led the Latin American market for twelve years at a list price about 22% above the next-best established platform.

On September 2, 2026, Vaudrey launched Plantara in Latin America, fully localised, with a thirty-person sales team that included three former PIS account managers. Plantara listed at about 40% below Sentinela Suite. Customers buying Vaudrey hardware could roll the software licence into the same financed payment plan, which brought the effective gap close to 50%.

## The Situation

The cover letter was signed by Xalteca's procurement director, Eduardo Manjarrez-Vela. It cited an August 2026 cost-compression directive from Xalteca's director-general and weighted price at no less than 35% of the evaluation, up from prior cycles. Echeverría-Solís knew the number behind the directive: Manjarrez-Vela had to show 15% cost compression on this contract. Bids were due in six weeks.

Xalteca's sixteen sites were not alike. Three flagship refining and petrochemical complexes used the advanced modules heavily. Five mid-complexity plants used some of them. Eight pipeline midstream nodes used basic monitoring only. The current contract was worth about US$167 million in net present value over five years, discounted at Caldorra's 9.4% cost of capital (see Exhibit 1).

Reigosa-Bernal's team had spent two months assembling the value evidence: what Sentinela was worth to Xalteca over Plantara, as distinct from what it cost, which the team called the economic value to the customer, or EVA. It had four drivers: measured operating value, with unplanned downtime down 2.3 points across the sixteen sites against 1.4 points in Vaudrey's published cases; compliance value; the integration Xalteca had already paid for; and the cost of switching. The total ranged from US$117 million to US$166 million in net present value, with a central estimate of US$141 million (see Exhibit 2). The downtime figure rested on telemetry; the fine-reduction element of compliance value, on four major incidents avoided in eight years.

Across PIS's whole base, a cheaper line touched customers unevenly: the 41% of accounts using four or more advanced modules carried 68% of ARR, and the quarter using one or none carried 8% (see Exhibit 1). Reigosa-Bernal had already scoped a feature-stripped Sentinela that could be priced at parity with Plantara: no machine-learning modules, bespoke regulatory layer or advanced connectors, removed at code level rather than switched off by a flag.

If nothing changed and PIS bid the Suite at list on all sixteen sites, Echeverría-Solís's read was simple. Manjarrez-Vela could not deliver his mandate with that bid, and the account, US$38 million of ARR, would go to Vaudrey on March 31, 2027. If PIS cut its price for Xalteca alone, he expected Xalteca's buyers to share the number with their counterparts at Tupiara in Brazil and Valdora in Colombia within weeks, whatever the confidentiality clause said.

## What Castelar-Drago Controlled

At 09:35 she settled the value case. She chose how to build it: the **four-driver decomposition** covering operating, compliance, integration and switching value; an **operating-anchored** build resting on measured downtime and throughput; or a **switching-anchored** build leading with the cost Xalteca would face to leave. She also chose which point of the range to present: the **conservative** lower bound, the **central** estimate or the **aggressive** upper bound.

At 10:03 the question moved to the whiteboard. She chose the **brand architecture**: a **branded house** that kept every offer under the single Sentinela name and held the line; a **sub-brand**, a feature-stripped fighter under the Sentinela name; an **endorsed brand**, a separate name carrying Sentinela's endorsement; or a **house of brands**, a fully independent identity. Reigosa-Bernal's shortlist for a sub-brand's **name** was *Sentinela Lite*, *Sentinela Pro* or *Sentinela Foundation*. She also chose how hard to **contain** downward migration. **Soft** left it to sales discretion. **Medium** added a sales-process gate requiring regional approval to quote the cheaper line. **Hard** added a contract gate barring Suite customers from moving outside renewal windows or splitting sites, plus a scripted answer.

For the 15:00 meeting she chose the **Xalteca response**: (A) defend the premium and accept losing Xalteca if it came to that; (B) match Vaudrey's price for Xalteca only, protected by confidentiality; (C) shift the mix, keeping the Suite on the more complex sites and the new line on the simpler ones; or (D) concede Xalteca and redeploy account capacity to Brazil. She chose the **contract term**: three years firm; five years, as three plus a two-year extension at PIS's option; or seven years firm. And she chose the **script** for when other customers called: **none prepared**; a **discount narrative** presenting the offer as Xalteca's best price; or a **different-product narrative** presenting the lower line as the right product for one site profile and the Suite for another.

Calderón would grade the three pieces of work, the value diagnostic, the architecture and the defence, each out of 100, and his approval, also out of 100, would move as each landed. He would watch three other numbers: forecast **cannibalisation**, the share of revenue lost as Suite customers moved down to a cheaper line; **channel-poisoning risk**, from 0 to 100, the chance that the Xalteca terms became every other buyer's opening position; and the **Xalteca ARR** PIS would keep. Every number started at zero. His verdict could run from escalating the memo to Solana, through sending it back for rework or signing it on Friday, to Solana reading it over breakfast.

## Closing

At 09:35 Castelar-Drago sat down in the war room, the evidence base already on the screen. "Verónica, before we decide what to do," Echeverría-Solís said, "we need to know what Sentinela is actually worth to Xalteca over Plantara. Not what it costs. What it is worth." The meeting on the 22nd floor was five and a half hours away.
