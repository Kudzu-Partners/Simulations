## Abstract

NOVACHEM VENTURES was, in the case universe, the corporate venture arm of a specialty-chemicals parent with USD 8.4B in 2025 revenue, a Q4 2025 fund size of USD 100M inside a five-year investment window, a 2022–2025 deployed-capital run-rate of USD 22M annually across 2022–2024 that had, on average, delivered an IRR of 18% and a MOIC of 1.9x against the corporate-venture-median IRR of 14% and MOIC of 1.6x, and an explicit dual mandate from the parent board that required the fund to clear a financial hurdle (IRR above 15%, MOIC above 2.0x) and a strategic hurdle (a curated portfolio that anchored the parent's 2028 circular-chemistry roadmap). On Monday, January 5, 2026, Claudia Orellana, a 44-year-old former corporate-development director who had joined NovaChem Ventures in August 2022 as a senior associate and had been promoted to Managing Director on December 1, 2025, had opened the three-round VentureArm commitment that would, across 2026, deploy USD 40M of first capital across four of ten targets, navigate three Round-2 crises, and position the portfolio for a Round-3 exit window. The case opens at 6:02 a.m. on Tuesday, January 13, 2026, at the NovaChem Ventures office as Orellana prepares the Round-1 thesis-setting commitment.

On the morning of **Tuesday, January 13, 2026**, **Claudia Orellana** arrived at the NovaChem Ventures office on the top floor of the parent's Madrid HQ at 6:02 a.m., opened the Round-1 thesis-setting sheet her Head of Deal Sourcing and Head of Portfolio Operations had finalised at 10:28 p.m. the night before, and re-read the sentence she had written at the top of the December 2025 promotion-transition notebook: *"A corporate venture desk is two scoreboards and one balance sheet."* Twenty-one days separated her from the February 3 Investment Committee commitment.

## The Protagonist

Claudia Orellana, 44, had joined NovaChem Ventures in August 2022 from the corporate-development function of the parent specialty-chemicals group where she had, across six years, led fourteen acquisitions and minority investments in specialty-additives and advanced-materials companies across Europe and North America. Before corporate development, she had spent four years as an analyst at a Tier-1 specialty-chemicals-focused private-equity firm and had run a post-MBA product-management rotation at a US coatings operator. The parent-company Chief Financial Officer had promoted her to Managing Director on December 1, 2025, specifically on the strength of two capabilities: her experience navigating dual-mandate deployments where financial discipline and strategic portfolio construction had to be balanced, and her reputation for refusing to fund deals whose strategic-rationale paragraph would not survive a two-year retrospective. Her 2026 long-term incentive plan tied 35% to the Year-3 IRR, 25% to the Strategic Score at Year-3, and 10% to a qualitative parent-board-review metric.

## The Decision

Orellana had to commit, by the February 3 Investment Committee commitment, the Round-1 thesis-setting envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2 and 3.

1. **Round 1 — Thesis-Setting and First Deployments** — how to select four of ten deep-tech chemistry targets for USD 40M of first capital. The ten-target universe included BioForm Materials, BioProcess Dynamics, CatalystAI, CircleLoop, CircularChem, DataChem Analytics, EcoBarrier Films, GreenSynth Biologics, NanoCoat Solutions and PolyGreen Synthesis. The dominant working envelope seated CatalystAI (USD 12M), CircularChem (USD 14M), NanoCoat Solutions (USD 9M) and BioProcess Dynamics (USD 5M follow-on) — a four-deal combination that delivered a Strategic Score plus-11 on the parent's circular-chemistry roadmap and projected a blended IRR of 22% at Round 3.
2. **Round 2 — Portfolio Management and Mid-Course Correction** — how to respond to three crises landing inside the Round-2 window. A co-founder dispute at CatalystAI (mediation recommended at a USD 3M bridge), a competitor CVC leading the next round of PolyGreen (pro-rata of USD 4M recommended to protect the signal), and a parent-budget pull of USD 20M from follow-on reserves (forced a re-pricing of the reserve envelope from USD 18M to USD 12M).
3. **Round 3 — Exit Positioning and Strategic Harvest** — how to sequence three exit-window events. A NanoCoat secondary offer at 3.2x (sell half to lock MOIC); a tech-disruption event on EcoBarrier (write down the stake); and a regulatory-tailwind on CircularChem (hold for a strategic acquirer over an eighteen-month horizon, against a parent-premium window modelled at 1.4–1.7x the public-market mark).

On Orellana's working model, a CatalystAI/CircularChem/NanoCoat/BioProcess-plus-Bridge-plus-Pro-Rata-plus-Reserve-Cut-plus-NanoCoat-Secondary-plus-EcoBarrier-Write-Down-plus-CircularChem-Hold envelope delivered IRR 22%, MOIC 2.4x, Financial Score 74, Strategic Score 81, Capital Deployed USD 88M, Portfolio Value at mark USD 212M. A BioForm/DataChem/EcoBarrier/GreenSynth-plus-Litigate-plus-No-Pro-Rata-plus-No-Reserve-Cut envelope delivered IRR 11%, MOIC 1.4x, Strategic Score 52. A CatalystAI-heavy concentration envelope (CatalystAI USD 22M) delivered IRR 26% if the deal cleared Round 3, but collapsed to IRR 8% under the co-founder-dispute tail-risk.

## Parent Background

NovaChem Ventures was the corporate venture arm of a publicly-listed specialty-chemicals parent with USD 8.4B in 2025 revenue, four operating divisions (coatings, performance additives, bio-based intermediates and advanced materials), and a 2028 circular-chemistry roadmap that had been adopted by the parent board in September 2024. The fund had, across 2022–2024, deployed USD 44M of first capital across eleven portfolio companies at an 18% blended IRR and 1.9x MOIC, a track record that had placed the fund in the top quartile of specialty-chemicals CVCs against a peer benchmarking study of 24 comparable programs.

The Q4 2025 parent-board dossier had identified three specific pressures the three-round envelope would inherit. First, the 2028 circular-chemistry roadmap had explicitly anchored three parent-strategic priorities on the portfolio: circular chemistry, catalysis AI and bio-based intermediates — priorities that framed the Round-1 target selection. Second, the Q3 2025 parent-capital-discipline review had signalled a Q2 2026 budget-pull event of USD 15–25M from follow-on reserves — a signal that framed the Round-2 reserve-cut response. Third, the Q1 2026 public-market signal had, on the NanoCoat-comparable basket, placed secondary-offer premiums at 2.8x–3.4x of the first-round mark — a signal that framed the Round-3 NanoCoat secondary commitment.

## Industry and Market Context

The corporate-venture-capital landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2018–2024 CVC dual-mandate evidence base had, across 58 specialty-chemicals and advanced-materials CVC programs, documented a Strategic-Score-uplift differential of 22–32 points between roadmap-aligned target selection and financial-only target selection — a pattern that anchored the Round-1 commitment. Second, the 2020–2024 CVC portfolio-management literature had, across 41 follow-on events under parent-budget pull, placed pro-rata-plus-reserve-cut responses at a MOIC-preservation rate of 84% against 42% for no-pro-rata responses — a pattern that framed the Round-2 commitment. Third, the 2019–2024 CVC exit-positioning evidence base had, across 34 strategic-acquirer exits, placed eighteen-month strategic-hold windows under regulatory-tailwind conditions at parent-premium windows of 1.3–1.8x — a pattern that framed the Round-3 CircularChem commitment.

Empirically, the post-2020 CVC literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, funds that had seated four-target Round-1 envelopes against the parent's strategic roadmap had, across a 2020–2024 sample of 27 CVC programs, closed Year-3 Strategic Scores above 80 in 78% of cases against 31% for funds that had seated financial-only envelopes. Second, funds that had executed NanoCoat-style secondary exits at 2.8x+ had, across the same sample, preserved capital for Year-3 follow-on reserves in 84% of cases against 38% for funds that had held through Series D — a pattern that anchored the Round-3 secondary commitment.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The parent-company Chief Financial Officer had, in the December 2025 Investment Committee pre-read, approved the three-round envelope, an IRR target above 20%, a MOIC target above 2.2x, and a Strategic Score floor of 75. The Head of Deal Sourcing had, on January 2, circulated the ten-target deal-flow pack and recommended the CatalystAI/CircularChem/NanoCoat/BioProcess envelope. The Head of Portfolio Operations had, on January 6, circulated the Round-2 crisis-response pre-read. The parent Chief Strategy Officer had, on January 9, argued for the CircularChem strategic-hold commitment. The parent CFO had, on January 12, confirmed the USD 20M Round-2 budget-pull signal.

### What Orellana knew

The team had modelled three three-round envelopes. A CatalystAI/CircularChem/NanoCoat/BioProcess-plus-Bridge-plus-Pro-Rata-plus-Reserve-Cut-plus-Secondary-plus-Write-Down-plus-Hold Envelope projected IRR 22%, MOIC 2.4x, Strategic 81, Capital Deployed USD 88M. A BioForm/DataChem/EcoBarrier/GreenSynth-plus-Litigate-plus-No-Pro-Rata Envelope projected IRR 11%, MOIC 1.4x, Strategic 52. A CatalystAI-heavy Concentration Envelope projected IRR 26% on clearance but collapsed to IRR 8% under the co-founder-dispute tail-risk.

### What she did not know

Whether the CatalystAI co-founder dispute would resolve inside the Round-2 window or slip to Round 3; whether the competitor CVC would accept Orellana's pro-rata in PolyGreen at the signalled terms; whether the parent-budget pull would land at the modelled USD 20M or expand to USD 25M; and whether the CircularChem regulatory-tailwind would hold through the Q3 2027 window or compress inside Q1 2027.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Deal Sourcing**: *"Four targets. CatalystAI, CircularChem, NanoCoat, BioProcess. The roadmap alignment is unambiguous."*

**The Head of Portfolio Operations**: *"Pro-rata PolyGreen. Bridge CatalystAI at three. Cut reserves from eighteen to twelve. The signal holds."*

**The parent Chief Strategy Officer**: *"Hold CircularChem for the strategic acquirer. The eighteen-month premium window is real."*

**The parent Chief Financial Officer**: *"The dual mandate is not negotiable. If you deliver twenty-two IRR without a Strategic Score above eighty, I will still ask why."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Orellana had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Four-Target / Bridge / Pro-Rata / Reserve-Cut / Secondary / Write-Down / Hold:** Round 1 CatalystAI (USD 12M), CircularChem (USD 14M), NanoCoat (USD 9M), BioProcess follow-on (USD 5M). Round 2 CatalystAI bridge (USD 3M), PolyGreen pro-rata (USD 4M), reserve cut from USD 18M to USD 12M. Round 3 NanoCoat secondary at 3.2x, EcoBarrier write-down, CircularChem eighteen-month hold. IRR 22%. MOIC 2.4x. Strategic 81. Capital Deployed USD 88M. Recommended.

- **Option B — Financial-Only Four-Target / Litigate / No-Pro-Rata / No-Reserve-Cut:** Financial-only target selection. IRR 11%. MOIC 1.4x. Strategic 52. Rejected.

- **Option C — CatalystAI-Heavy Concentration:** CatalystAI at USD 22M. Tail-risk asymmetry. IRR 26% on clearance, IRR 8% under co-founder-dispute crystallisation. Rejected on risk-concentration grounds.

Orellana's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Strategic Score was Round 1 — the four-target thesis-setting commitment that anchored the roadmap alignment and that compounded through the Round-2 pro-rata and the Round-3 strategic-hold commitments.
