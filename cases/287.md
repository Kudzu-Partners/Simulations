## Abstract

The Marketing Coordinator at the case-universe Lima-headquartered Peruvian food startup Sabores del Valle S.A. — approximately S/. 2.4m in first-year revenue, 14 employees, a quinoa-based snack portfolio with granola and cookies already in Wong and Metro, a new quinoa-based energy bar formulated and manufactured but not yet launched, and a launch window centred on the urban Lima university segment — had been asked, in the April 2026 CEO directive, to make three 4P launch decisions across a 15-minute three-screen microsim with S/. 18,000 of launch budget and a four-week timeline. The decisions were straightforward. The formula was cocoa-with-maca or citrus-with-camu-camu. The packaging was basic craft, mid-tier resealable, or premium foil. The price point was economy S/. 4.50, standard S/. 6.50, or premium S/. 8.90. The promotion mix was social-media reach, campus flyers with sampling, or micro-influencer trust. Four levers. Three options per lever. Twelve combinations that students will confidently optimise in isolation, and a coherence checker that will flag most of their answers as mismatches. The case opens at 2:30 p.m. on Thursday, April 23, 2026, at Sabores del Valle's San Borja office as **Marcela Quiroz-Valdivia**, a 27-year-old Marketing Coordinator, prepares the 15-minute sequence.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Select the Screen-1 product and packaging combination — cocoa-with-maca or citrus-with-camu-camu formula paired with a basic craft, mid-tier resealable, or premium foil packaging tier — and articulate why each packaging tier signals a quality level independent of the bar itself, why craft packaging reinforces an authenticity narrative and foil packaging reinforces a performance-premium narrative, and why every packaging choice costs different money from the launch budget.
2. Select the Screen-2 price point — S/. 4.50 economy, S/. 6.50 standard, S/. 8.90 premium — against the overlaid law-of-demand curve and articulate why economy moves more units on thinner margin while premium moves fewer units on fatter margin, why no price point wins by default, and why the right price depends on the packaging choice and the channel choice that precede and follow it.
3. Allocate the Screen-3 promotion mix across social-media reach, campus flyers with sampling conversion, and micro-influencer trust, and articulate why social media wins on awareness but loses on conversion, why sampling wins on conversion but loses on reach, why micro-influencer wins on trust but is moderate on both, and where the AIDA funnel's real bottleneck sits for a quinoa energy bar launched to Lima university students.
4. Run the live coherence check and articulate why a premium-packaging-plus-economy-price combination creates a perceived-value mismatch that drops conversion, why McCarthy's 4Ps are not independent levers but a reinforcing or cancelling system, and why the coherence check is the Screen-3 success criterion rather than any individual-lever choice.
5. Read the results dashboard — revenue, units sold, estimated margin, channel ROI and simulated customer comments — and articulate which P drove the outcome, where the mix incoherence cost units, and what price-channel combination would have worked better, converting the 4P framework from an abstract model into a fifteen-minute practical discipline.

---

## The Protagonist

Quiroz-Valdivia, 27, had been Marketing Coordinator since 2024, had completed her undergraduate degree in Business Administration at the Universidad del Pacífico in 2022, had spent eighteen months as a junior brand executive at a Peruvian consumer-foods firm where she had supported three trade-marketing campaigns, and had joined Sabores del Valle in Q3 2024 as the firm's first marketing hire. The CEO, who was also a co-founder, had assigned her the energy-bar launch on three considerations: her command of the urban university segment from her own recent university experience; her willingness to question the founder's favoured option (premium foil at S/. 8.90) on coherence grounds; and her comfort with the 4P framework as a decision discipline rather than a slide template.

---

## The Decision

Quiroz-Valdivia had to commit, by the 3:15 p.m. founder sync, the three-screen launch mix.

1. **Screen 1 — Product and packaging.** Formula: citrus-with-camu-camu (higher perceived differentiation in the functional-snack segment). Packaging: mid-tier resealable (coherent with standard price tier, not over-investing in perceived value). Cost: S/. 4,200 of the S/. 18,000 envelope.
2. **Screen 2 — Price.** Standard S/. 6.50 (aligned with the resealable packaging tier's perceived-value signal and with the Lima university segment's willingness-to-pay band at the functional-snack category).
3. **Screen 3 — Promotion.** Allocation: S/. 2,800 social media (reach at the awareness stage of AIDA), S/. 7,400 campus flyers with sampling at four universities (conversion at the desire stage), S/. 3,600 micro-influencer (trust at the interest-to-desire transition). Total S/. 13,800.
4. **Coherence check.** Premium packaging excluded (would have created the mismatch with standard price). Economy price excluded (would have created the mismatch with mid-tier packaging). The commitment reads as a coherent standard-tier launch with sampling-weighted promotion.

## Sectoral Background

The case-universe Lima functional-snack category had, across 2022–2025, grown from approximately S/. 180m to S/. 290m, concentrated on three categories (energy bars, protein cookies, functional beverages), and attracted three significant entrants — QualiBar imported from the United States, Fuerza Natural the Arequipa price-fighter, and two smaller domestic artisans. The urban Lima university segment had in the 2024 Ipsos panel registered functional-snack monthly spend of approximately S/. 38 per student, weighted toward weekday-afternoon purchases adjacent to campus.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1960–2025 marketing-mix literature produced three findings the microsim had to respect. First, McCarthy's 4P framework — Product, Price, Place, Promotion, as refined through the 1960–1984 literature — was a reinforcing-system framework, not an independent-lever framework, and coherence across the four levers was the single strongest correlate of first-year launch revenue on comparable 2015–2024 food-startup launches. Second, the AIDA funnel (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action) was a category-specific diagnostic rather than a universal one — for functional snacks, the desire-to-action transition was the documented bottleneck, making sampling-at-point-of-sale the highest-leverage promotion channel. Third, perceived-value coherence between packaging tier and price point was the single most common entry-level launch-planning error, with premium-packaging-at-economy-price combinations producing documented conversion-rate readings approximately 40–55% below coherent combinations.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The energy-bar formulation was finalised in Q1 2026. The packaging tier-of-three and price-point-of-three structure was drawn up by the CEO and the Marketing Coordinator in March. The promotion-channel menu was based on the 2025 Lima university-segment panel data. The April 23 microsim was the decision forum.

### What Quiroz-Valdivia knew

Packaging tier signals perceived value independently of the bar itself; the Lima university segment's willingness-to-pay is concentrated in the S/. 5.50–7.50 band; sampling converts at the point of taste; the AIDA bottleneck for functional snacks is desire-to-action rather than attention-to-interest; the founder's preferred premium-foil-and-premium-price combination sits outside the coherence envelope if the product is not repositioned.

### What she did not know

Whether the citrus-with-camu-camu formula would outperform cocoa-with-maca in a blind tasting at four universities; whether the resealable packaging would be read as 'health' or 'gym' in the Lima university context; whether the standard price point would absorb the one-point margin compression from the micro-influencer allocation; and whether a Week-3 reallocation from flyers to influencer would be needed if the awareness reading fell short.

### What others were saying

**The CEO and Co-founder**: *"I want premium packaging. The bar is premium. The price has to match."*

**The Product Formulator**: *"Citrus-with-camu-camu is the stronger differentiation. Cocoa-with-maca competes with QualiBar directly."*

**The Trade Marketing Partner**: *"Sampling at point-of-sale moves the functional-snack desire-to-action transition. Nothing else does at this budget."*

**The Freelance Influencer Agency**: *"Three micro-influencers at three universities for S/. 3,600. Trust signal, not reach."*

**The Financial Advisor**: *"The S/. 18,000 envelope is binding. Any over-run comes out of the Q3 plan."*

## Options Under Consideration

Quiroz-Valdivia had narrowed the space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — Citrus-with-camu-camu formula, mid-tier resealable packaging, standard S/. 6.50 price, sampling-weighted promotion (Recommended):** coherent 4P mix, AIDA bottleneck addressed, perceived-value consistent. Recommended.
- **Option B — Cocoa-with-maca formula, premium foil packaging, premium S/. 8.90 price, influencer-weighted promotion:** rejected — formula competes head-on with QualiBar, premium price fails the university-segment willingness-to-pay test, and the promotion allocation misses the desire-to-action bottleneck.
- **Option C — Citrus-with-camu-camu formula, basic craft packaging, economy S/. 4.50 price, social-media-heavy promotion:** rejected — craft packaging reads as artisan rather than functional in the segment, economy price signals a cheaper-than-category positioning that the product is not built to sustain, and social media misses the conversion bottleneck.

Her private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the screen most correlated with first-year revenue was Screen 3 — the promotion allocation — because Screen 3 was the screen where the AIDA-funnel bottleneck test most sharply dominated the lever-by-lever optimisation headline.
