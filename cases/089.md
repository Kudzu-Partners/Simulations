## Abstract

Alcaldía de San Marcos was, in the case universe, the municipal government of a mid-sized Colombian city of 800,000 inhabitants in the eastern Andean piedmont, with a 2025 general-fund cash position of COP 480 billion (USD 112M at the prevailing exchange rate), a general-obligation-and-revenue-bond debt stock of COP 420 billion, a debt-to-revenue ratio of 87.5%, a citizen-satisfaction score of 42 on the 100-point Encuesta de Percepción Ciudadana, an equity index of 0.57 (a composite of service-delivery parity across income quintiles and barrios), and a fiscal-health index of 0.65. On Monday, September 7, 2026, the Concejo Municipal approved a three-round strategic commitment inside which the Alcaldesa would commit, each round, six operating levers — water-department budget, transport-department budget, property-tax rate, water-service delivery model, citizen-engagement framework, and new-debt-issuance decision — anchored on four watch metrics: citizen satisfaction, equity, fiscal health, and debt-to-revenue. Valeria Ordóñez, a 51-year-old former Secretaria de Hacienda elected Alcaldesa on November 15, 2025, faced a Round-3 drought event that the regional meteorological authority had flagged in the August 2026 bulletin, a Round-1 water-service-model PPP decision that would politically cost her across the opposition, and a Concejo mandate to close Round 3 with an improved composite on all four watch metrics. The case opens at 7:10 a.m. on Monday, September 14, 2026, at the Alcaldía de San Marcos central offices, as Ordóñez prepares the Round-1 decision sheet.

On the morning of **Monday, September 14, 2026**, **Valeria Ordóñez** arrived at the Alcaldía de San Marcos central offices on Plaza Bolívar at 7:10 a.m., opened the Round-1 decision sheet her Secretario de Planeación and her Secretaria de Hacienda had finalized at 11:48 p.m. the night before, and read the dashboard at the top. Citizen satisfaction 42, equity 0.57, fiscal health 0.65, debt-to-revenue 87.5%, and the note below it: "The city is not broken. The city is at the edge of broken." Twelve days separated her from the September 26 Concejo commitment.

## The Protagonist

Valeria Ordóñez, 51, had joined the Alcaldía de San Marcos in 2013 as Subsecretaria de Presupuesto and had been promoted to Secretaria de Hacienda in 2018. A graduate of the Universidad Nacional de Colombia in economics with an MPA from the Harvard Kennedy School, she had, in her Hacienda tenure, rebuilt the municipal fiscal-forecasting framework on a probabilistic revenue model that had, across 2020–2023, closed three consecutive fiscal years at an actual-versus-forecast deviation below 2.8%, co-authored the 2022 participatory-budgeting white paper that had since circulated across ten Colombian mid-sized cities, and had, in 2024, declined an offer to join the national Ministerio de Hacienda to run for local office. Her November 2025 election had carried a four-year mandate inside which she had committed to a participatory-governance agenda she had termed "El Contrato con 800,000" — the contract with the city's 800,000 inhabitants.

## The Decision

Ordóñez had to commit, by the September 26 Concejo commitment, the Round-1 decision sheet and the Round-2 and Round-3 intent:

1. **Water and Transport Budget and Property-Tax Rate** — whether to commit COP 60 billion to the water department (up from the 50 baseline to fund two broken pump stations) and COP 75 billion to transport (a rapid-bus-corridor upgrade); whether to lift the property-tax rate from 0.6% to 0.8% to fund the service-delivery envelope.
2. **Water-Service Model and Citizen Engagement** — whether to switch the water-service model to a public-private partnership (the single most politically costly move of the year) and commit to a basic-consultation citizen-engagement framework in Round 1, upgrading to participatory budgeting in Round 2.
3. **Debt Issuance and Reserve Discipline** — whether to hold Round-1 new-debt issuance at zero, commit a USD 40M infrastructure-bond in Round 2, and pool reserves in Round 3 under the drought trigger; whether to commit the property-tax rate at 0.8% through all three rounds.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across the three-round envelope. A Round-1 package that lifted water to COP 60B, transport to COP 75B, property-tax to 0.8%, committed to the PPP water-service model, ran basic consultation, and issued no new debt produced, on Ordóñez's working model, a Round-1 citizen-satisfaction lift from 42 to 51 (a nine-point lift in one cycle, the fastest among peer cities), a water-satisfaction lift from 35 to 48, a transport-satisfaction lift from 48 to 55, a flat equity at 0.58, a fiscal-health lift to 0.71, and a property-tax-revenue lift of COP 28 billion. The three-round trajectory then carried the city through a Round-3 drought event at a water-satisfaction hold of 52 (rather than a default 20-point drop), a composite of 81, and a debt-to-revenue fall to 78%.

## Company Background

Alcaldía de San Marcos had been incorporated in 1814 as a colonial-era parish and had, across two hundred twelve years, built the 800,000-inhabitant service-delivery-and-governance footprint the case studied. The 2025 general-fund revenue of COP 550 billion was distributed as property-tax 46%, transfers-from-national-government 28%, commercial-and-industrial-tax 14%, fees-and-licenses 7%, and other 5%. The city's four principal operating departments — water, transport, health-and-education (a shared department with the national authority), and public safety — absorbed 82% of the general-fund expenditure. The water-service delivery had, since 1996, been operated by a wholly-owned municipal utility (Empresa Municipal de Agua, EMA) with coverage of 94% (below the comparable mid-city peer benchmark of 98%) and a 2025 service-satisfaction of 35 (below the comparable peer benchmark of 62).

The Concejo's August 2026 strategy dossier had identified three specific pressures the three-round envelope would inherit. First, the regional meteorological authority had, in the August bulletin, placed a 68% probability on a Round-3 (Q2 2027) drought event of moderate-to-severe intensity, with a modeled water-stress window of six to nine weeks. Second, the water-service model had, across the 2022–2024 audit cycle, revealed two pump stations operating at 48% of rated capacity and a barrio-level coverage gap in three underserved districts — conditions that framed the PPP option as an operational turnaround question rather than an ideological one. Third, the 2026 Concejo opposition had, on September 9, pre-committed to a no-vote on any PPP transition, a no-vote on any property-tax lift above 0.75%, and a conditional no-vote on any new-debt issuance above COP 50 billion.

## Industry and Market Context

The Colombian mid-city municipal-governance landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the three-round envelope sat inside. First, the water-service PPP evidence base had, across a 2018–2024 Colombian and regional commercial-summary sample of fourteen mid-city transitions, documented a service-satisfaction lift of 11–18 points within twelve months at a commercial cost of 6–9% of the water-service budget and a political-cost window of 18–24 months — a pattern that informed the Round-1 PPP commitment. Second, the participatory-budgeting evidence base had, across a 2020–2024 sample of twenty-two Colombian cities, documented an equity-index lift of 0.04–0.07 within one fiscal cycle at a commercial cost below 0.2% of the discretionary budget — a finding that monetized the Round-2 participatory upgrade. Third, the infrastructure-bond evidence base had, across a 2015–2024 Colombian single-A-rated mid-city issuances, closed at spreads of 180–240 basis points above the sovereign and had, in 67% of cases, been absorbed by domestic pension and insurance demand within the offering window — a trajectory that anchored the Round-2 USD 40M commitment.

Empirically, the post-2018 public-management literature had two robust findings the three-round envelope had to respect. First, cities that had sequenced service-delivery investment (Round 1) ahead of participatory engagement (Round 2) had, across a 2019–2024 sample of nineteen Colombian-and-regional peer cities, closed the equity-and-satisfaction composite 2.4-times higher than cities that had sequenced engagement ahead of delivery — the capital-before-conversation pattern. Second, cities that had maintained a contingency fund above 8% of the operating budget through a climate-event window had, across the same sample, preserved service-satisfaction at a 78% rate against a 42% rate for cities that had drawn the fund below 4%.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Concejo had, in the August 2026 strategy committee, approved a three-round composite target of 76, a fiscal-health floor of 0.68 through all rounds, a debt-to-revenue ceiling of 85%, and a Round-3 drought-event-preparedness commitment. The Secretaria de Hacienda had, on August 28, circulated a Round-1 pre-reading that proposed the COP 60B water and COP 75B transport budgets and the 0.8% property-tax lift. The Secretario de Planeación had, on September 2, framed the PPP-water-service-model decision at the Round-1 cut-off. The Secretaria de Gobierno (political-affairs) had, on September 5, outlined the opposition's pre-committed positions and recommended the engagement-before-PPP sequencing — a position the Alcaldesa had held under review.

### What Ordóñez knew

The team had modeled three three-round envelopes. A Capital-Before-Conversation Envelope (Round-1 COP 60B water + COP 75B transport + 0.8% tax + PPP + basic consultation + no new debt; Round-2 COP 55B water + COP 90B transport + 0.8% tax + PPP + participatory budgeting + USD 40M debt; Round-3 COP 75B emergency water + COP 80B transport + 0.8% tax + participatory + no new debt + reserve pooling) projected a Round-3 composite of 81 and a debt-to-revenue of 78%. A Progressive Envelope (Round-1 participatory engagement first, PPP deferred to Round 2, 0.7% tax lift) projected a Round-3 composite of 66 on service-delivery lag. A Fiscal-Austerity Envelope (Round-1 tax held at 0.6%, water-service held at COP 50B baseline, no new debt, no PPP) projected a Round-3 composite of 54 on service-delivery collapse through the drought.

### What she did not know

Whether the drought event would arrive at the modeled 68% probability and moderate intensity, or at a 78%-probability severe intensity that the second-source meteorological model had referenced; whether the PPP-transition operator would close at the August 2026 pre-bid terms or would renegotiate at a 12% higher operating-fee under the scale-of-turnaround assessment; whether the Concejo opposition's no-vote would hold through the Round-2 participatory-budgeting window or would soften under the Round-1 service-delivery proof-points; and whether the national-government transfer schedule would hold through the drought-declaration process or would frontload under the emergency-declaration framework.

### What others were saying

**The Secretaria de Hacienda**: *"Capital-Before-Conversation Envelope. Round-1 tax to 0.8% with service-delivery visible by the third month. Round-2 participatory budgeting after the proof-points are in."*

**The Secretario de Planeación**: *"PPP at Round-1 cut-off. The operational turnaround timeline requires an early commitment."*

**The Secretaria de Gobierno (political affairs)**: *"Opposition will no-vote the PPP. Build participation first. Otherwise the Round-1 lift collapses in the Round-2 municipal-assembly debate."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Ordóñez had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Capital-Before-Conversation:** Round-1 service-delivery + PPP + 0.8% tax + basic consultation; Round-2 participatory budgeting + USD 40M debt; Round-3 emergency-water + reserve pooling + tax hold. Round-3 composite 81. Aligns with Hacienda and Planeación. Recommended.

- **Option B — Progressive:** Round-1 participation first, PPP deferred, 0.7% tax. Round-3 composite 66. Political-capital-preserving. Service-delivery-losing through the drought.

- **Option C — Fiscal-Austerity:** No tax lift, no PPP, no new debt. Round-3 composite 54. Balance-sheet-preserving. Contract-breaking.

Ordóñez's private view, not yet shared with the Concejo, was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the Round-3 composite was Round 2 — specifically, the combined participatory-budgeting upgrade, the USD 40M infrastructure-bond, the transport BRT extension, and the women-and-night-service safety module — a four-lever integration that built the contingency fund (on the property-tax-revenue lift of Round-1) into the buffer that absorbed the Round-3 drought without breaching the fiscal-health floor.
