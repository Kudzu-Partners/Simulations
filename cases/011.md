## Abstract

Norda Industries, a USD 4.2 billion global consumer-goods manufacturer with three product families (NordaHome, NordaCare, NordaPro), entered the second week of March 2026 with three simultaneous shocks: the Port of Long Beach closed indefinitely under a prolonged labor action, its largest Tier-1 surfactant supplier (ChemBase Ltd., 45% of a critical input) had signalled insolvency, and a sanctions package covering a specialty Russian polymer used in NordaPro was imminent. Norda held 14 days of finished-goods inventory at the NordaHome line, 22 days at NordaCare, and 31 days at NordaPro. Its incoming CSCO, Isabela Montoya, had to submit a two-week action plan to the Emergency Operating Committee within 72 hours of her appointment. The plan would span supplier strategy, logistics mode, customer allocation, and financial posture. The company's cash position was USD 50M. The base-case crisis cost estimate stood at USD 15–20M for the first two weeks alone. The case places Montoya inside the first convening of the Emergency Operating Committee and sets up the Round 1 decision in the simulation.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate trade-offs between speed, cost, and resilience in a crisis-driven sourcing decision.
2. Analyze how simultaneous multi-node disruptions compound OTIF (on-time in-full) exposure.
3. Design a customer-allocation heuristic that distinguishes strategic, revenue-tier, and contractual obligations.
4. Diagnose the interaction between logistics choice, inventory depth, and customer retention risk.
5. Recommend a Week 1–2 plan that preserves both service and optionality for the recovery phase.

On the morning of **March 11, 2026**, **Isabela Montoya** sat in the Chief Supply Chain Officer's office at Norda Industries' headquarters in Monterrey, México, and looked at the single sheet of paper her assistant had slid under her door at 6:42 a.m. The sheet carried three lines: *Port of Long Beach — closed. ChemBase Ltd. — insolvency imminent. Russian polymer — sanctions expected Friday.* Below those lines, in her predecessor's handwriting, a single annotation: *"Good luck."* Her first Emergency Operating Committee meeting was at 10:00 a.m.

---

## The Protagonist

Isabela Montoya, 47, had been named CSCO of Norda Industries seven days earlier. A graduate of the Instituto Tecnológico y de Estudios Superiores de Monterrey and of MIT's Sloan MBA, she had run regional supply-chain operations for a Fortune 200 consumer-goods company in Asia-Pacific for nine years, followed by a three-year stint leading a post-COVID resilience redesign for a European personal-care group. She had been hired into Norda on the strength of that resilience work and against the backdrop of a board that had lost confidence in its predecessor after a 2024 near-miss during a Red Sea shipping disruption. Her contract carried a two-year vesting gate tied to OTIF recovery and to a composite resilience score the board had yet to formally define.

---

## The Decision

Montoya had to approve, by 10:00 a.m. on March 11, a two-week action plan spanning four decisions that the simulation framework would treat as her Round 1 inputs:

1. **Supplier strategy** — aggressive diversification, moderate diversification, maintain current, or cost minimization.
2. **Logistics mode** — air freight, mixed mode, ocean standard, or overland rerouting.
3. **Customer allocation** — strategic priority, revenue priority, balanced, or contractual-only.
4. **Financial response** — draw the credit line, defer capex, renegotiate supplier terms, or hold conservative.

The plan would be reviewed for approval and tabled against a six-round, twelve-week outlook that the planning unit had mocked up at 3:00 a.m. and printed for the committee. Montoya's Chair — Rafael Quintero, CEO — had told her on the Sunday before her start date: *"We do not need perfect. We need decided."*

---

## Company Background

Norda Industries had been founded in 1972 as a family-owned Monterrey surfactants producer and had, over five decades, built itself into a global manufacturer of home-care, personal-care, and professional-cleaning products across three brand families: NordaHome (24% of revenue), NordaCare (38%), and NordaPro (38%). 2025 revenue was USD 4.2 billion with an EBITDA margin of 12.8%. The company operated twelve manufacturing plants across the Americas, Europe, and Southeast Asia, and sourced from 430 Tier-1 suppliers across 34 countries.

Norda's supply-chain architecture had been built for efficiency: single-source on 38% of Tier-1 inputs, a consolidated freight-forwarding contract with one global 3PL, and an inventory policy tuned to 14–21 days of finished goods and 18–30 days of raw-materials coverage. The last formal resilience audit, commissioned in 2023, had flagged the ChemBase concentration and the Russian-polymer dependency but had been table-stakes-deprioritized in the 2024 capital plan.

Norda's ten largest customers accounted for 62% of revenue. Four of those had signed master service agreements with OTIF thresholds at 96% and financial penalties at 0.3% of invoice per OTIF percentage-point miss.

## Industry and Market Context

The global consumer-goods supply chain had, in the eighteen months preceding March 2026, transited from pandemic-era disruption to a more structurally volatile regime: four of the six largest West Coast U.S. ports had experienced at least one multi-week closure since 2023; twelve specialty-chemical suppliers serving global consumer brands had filed for insolvency during 2025 on the back of European energy costs and Asian oversupply; sanctions packages affecting specialty inputs had, since the 2022 Ukraine conflict, expanded from four to twenty-three Harmonized System codes.

Three findings from post-2020 resilience research were consequential for Norda's decision. First, companies with aggressive supplier diversification recovered full OTIF approximately 4.6 weeks earlier than those that maintained concentrated sourcing, but at a 3–5% total-landed-cost premium in non-crisis conditions. Second, air-freight activation during Week 1 of a disruption reduced cumulative customer-penalty exposure by approximately 60% but consumed roughly 4% of annual supply-chain opex. Third, strategic customer allocation — prioritizing a narrow set of key accounts over contractual distribution — preserved approximately 80% of long-term revenue retention, whereas pure revenue-priority allocation preserved only 60%.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Port of Long Beach had entered its fifth day of closure on March 11. ChemBase Ltd., Norda's largest surfactant supplier, had circulated to its customers a Chapter 11 advisory on March 9. The Russian-polymer sanctions package had been signalled in a March 10 G7 communiqué and was expected to take effect on Friday, March 14. Norda's S&OP desk estimated that, absent action, NordaHome would run out of its primary surfactant within 8 days, NordaCare within 22 days, and NordaPro within 31 days.

### What Montoya knew

The internal planning team had modeled four scenarios across the four decision levers. Aggressive supplier diversification cost USD 3.0M in qualification and activation in Weeks 1–2 but recovered OTIF 5 points faster. Air freight cost USD 4.0M in Weeks 1–2 but preserved 8 OTIF points. Strategic customer allocation cost USD 0.5M in net revenue but preserved 5 customer-satisfaction points with key accounts. The cash position at entry was USD 50M; the committed credit line was USD 20M at a renegotiated rate of SOFR+275; the Q2 capex plan included USD 12M that was deferrable without operational impact.

### What she did not know

Whether ChemBase would file for Chapter 7 liquidation (versus Chapter 11 reorganization), which would extinguish all pending orders, or Chapter 11 and preserve them. Whether the Oakland and Seattle ports would absorb rerouted volume without their own queue failures. Whether the largest retail customer (18% of NordaHome) would hold the contractual penalty or escalate to formal supplier review. Whether a German alternative supplier (RheinChem GmbH, 6-week qualification, 40% price premium) would accept the fast-track terms Norda's sourcing head had floated informally.

### What others were saying

**Patricia Uribe**, Head of Sourcing, argued for **Aggressive Diversification and Air Freight**: *"The 3% premium is cheap insurance against a 12-week customer defection. Activate RheinChem on Friday. Fly NordaHome on Monday."*

**Alejandro Ramírez**, CFO, argued for **Cost Minimization and Defer Capex**: *"The cash is 50. The credit line is 20. If we burn 15 in Weeks 1 and 2 we enter Week 3 below the covenant trigger. Hold tight on supply, defer the capex, and revisit on Thursday."*

**Sofía Delgadillo**, SVP Commercial, argued for **Strategic Allocation**: *"The top four customers are sixty-two percent. Protect them first, explicitly, and tell them. The others will understand if we do not lie."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

- **Option A — Defensive Aggressive (Uribe plan):** Aggressive diversification; mixed-mode logistics; strategic customer allocation; draw credit line. Week 1–2 cash cost ≈ USD 12M; OTIF preserved ≈ 85%; customer satisfaction preserved ≈ 92. Week 3 cash ≈ USD 38M; credit line drawn USD 5M.

- **Option B — Balanced Middle (Montoya's draft):** Moderate diversification; mixed-mode logistics; balanced allocation; renegotiate supplier terms. Week 1–2 cash cost ≈ USD 8M; OTIF preserved ≈ 78%; customer satisfaction preserved ≈ 88. Week 3 cash ≈ USD 42M.

- **Option C — Financial Hold (Ramírez plan):** Maintain current suppliers; ocean-standard logistics; contractual-only allocation; conservative financial response. Week 1–2 cash cost ≈ USD 5M; OTIF preserved ≈ 62%; customer satisfaction preserved ≈ 74. Week 3 cash ≈ USD 45M; top-four-customer penalty risk severe.

Montoya suspected that Option A was right on the merits and Option C was right on the covenants. Option B would be interpreted by the committee as a failure to decide. The clock on the wall read 8:54 a.m.

---

Montoya closed the laptop, picked up the pencil she had brought from her old office, and circled, for the third time, the two words at the top of the sheet of paper her assistant had left at 6:42 a.m.: *fourteen days.* That was the NordaHome figure. That was the figure the committee would remember. At 9:58 a.m. she picked up the printout and walked to the elevator.
