## Abstract

Hotel La Merced S.A.S. was, in the case universe, a 78-room boutique hotel in the Centro Amurallado of Cartagena de Indias — approximately COP 9.8bn in 2025 revenue, 34 employees, one 1860s colonial-era property on Calle de la Moneda fully renovated in 2019, a four-rate-category structure (Rack, Corporate, OTA, Loyalty), an April 2026 channel mix of 54% OTA (primarily Booking.com), 16% Direct Web, 14% Tour Operator, 10% Corporate and 6% Loyalty, a year-to-date 2025 ADR of COP 812,000 and a year-to-date occupancy of 71% producing a RevPAR of COP 576,000, a year-to-date GOPPAR that tracked 8% below RevPAR growth due to OTA commission compression, and a Q3 2026 cancelled-festival announcement (the annual October Hay Festival Cartagena had moved to a Bogotá venue for one edition) that had landed at the property on April 10. The May 2026 quarterly revenue committee would approve the three-quarter package. The case opens at 3:42 p.m. on Thursday, April 23, 2026, at the hotel's general-manager office as **Camila Bonilla-Narváez**, a 37-year-old General Manager, prepares the three-quarter commitment.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Read the four-rate-category structure (Rack, Corporate, OTA, Loyalty) as a fence system rather than as a price list, articulate the rate fences (corporate-account verification, OTA booking window, Loyalty membership tenure) that keep Rack-rate customers from migrating to OTA rates, and articulate why a collapsed fence system destroys rate integrity across all categories within a quarter.
2. Apply Kenneth Littlewood's 1972 rule — close the lower fare class when the expected marginal revenue from a future higher-paying booking exceeds the certain revenue from the current lower-paying booking — to the Q1 high-season OTA close-out timing, and articulate why the branch that fails to close OTA at 70% occupancy on a Saturday night in high season sells the 8 p.m. walk-in's room to a 3 p.m. discounted Booking.com traveller.
3. Calculate the Q1 overbooking commitment against an 8% historical no-show rate, trace the per-room walked-guest cost at COP 360,000 (alternate accommodation plus brand-damage absorption), and articulate why the 10% overbooking rate is the approximate optimum against an 8% no-show and why zero overbooking and 15% overbooking both fail but in different directions.
4. Read the Q2 shoulder-season RevPAR-versus-GOPPAR divergence as the decisive single moment of revenue-management teaching — pushing rate cuts to lift RevPAR against a 48% forecast occupancy raises RevPAR, shifts mix further toward OTA, raises commissions, and lifts GOPPAR at approximately half the RevPAR lift rate — and articulate why RevPAR fills rooms and GOPPAR fills banks.
5. Design the Q3 cancelled-festival demand-shock response from three candidate postures (panic-discount, hold-rates-and-run-empty, build-compensating-demand), commit the compensating-demand package (gastronomy-weekend, corporate-meetings push, wellness retreat), and articulate why this hotel-revenue-management primer frame is distinct from Casa Palma (sim 018), Hotel Pacifico (sim 006), Hotel Cielo F&B (sim 076), Hotel Mirador Digital (sim 077) and the Montaverde Hospitality Portfolio (sim 075) — 298 at the primer-level RevPAR-and-GOPPAR split for the boutique GM, the others at deeper specialised frames across the hotel-galaxy.

GOPPAR llena bancos. Hay un trimestre donde se ven distintas por primera vez."*

## The Protagonist

Bonilla-Narváez, 37, had been General Manager at Hotel La Merced since 2023, had completed an Administración Hotelera degree at the Universidad Externado de Colombia in 2011 and a Maestría en Dirección Hotelera at ESADE (Barcelona) in 2018, had spent nine years inside the Sol Meliá Colombia portfolio in revenue-management and assistant-GM roles across Barranquilla and Cartagena properties, and had joined La Merced from a two-year stint as Director of Revenue at a Santa Marta boutique group. Her revenue-management instincts, her owning-family chairman had noted in the 2025 performance review, had been shaped by two teachings: a doctoral-seminar exposure to the 1970s yield-management literature during her ESADE year; and an eighteen-month stretch inside Sol Meliá's 2020–2021 pandemic-recovery cycle during which she had watched a Barranquilla sibling property destroy its rate-positioning for two subsequent years through panic-discounting during the March-April 2020 demand-shock. The chairman had assigned her the April three-quarter exercise on three considerations: her comfort protecting rate integrity against visible RevPAR pressure; her willingness to hold on Q3 rather than panic-discount; and her command of the RevPAR-versus-GOPPAR divergence as the revenue-committee teaching moment.

---

## The Decision

Bonilla-Narváez had to commit, by the 5:30 p.m. revenue-committee meeting, the three-quarter package.

1. **Q1 — High Season (Dec-Feb and July-Aug).** ADR forecast at COP 1,080,000 blended; occupancy forecast 85%. Rate fences: Rack at COP 1,280,000 unrestricted; Corporate at COP 960,000 with verified account; OTA at COP 1,040,000 net of 18% Booking.com commission (net ADR COP 852,000); Loyalty at COP 840,000 with 12-month tenure requirement. OTA close-out rule: close OTA at 70% occupancy on Saturday nights; close OTA at 75% occupancy on Sunday-Thursday nights; hold OTA on Friday nights against tour-operator walk-ins. Overbooking commitment: 10% against 8% historical no-show.
2. **Q2 — Shoulder Season (March-May and September).** Occupancy forecast 48% at rate integrity; 62% at COP 720,000 blended (rate cut); 71% at COP 640,000 blended (aggressive cut). RevPAR and GOPPAR trajectories under each. The decision: hold rate at 48%, cut moderately to 62%, or cut aggressively to 71%.
3. **Q3 — October 2026 Cancelled-Festival Demand Shock.** Hay Festival Cartagena moved to a Bogotá venue. October forecast occupancy drops approximately 18 percentage points without intervention (from baseline 78% to 60%). Three candidate responses: panic-discount (drop rates 25%, destroy 2027 rate positioning for a demand bump); hold rates (run empty at approximately 56% occupancy); build compensating demand (gastronomy-weekend with Cartagena chef partners, corporate-meetings push into pharma and tourism-industry associations, wellness retreat package).

Commitments: the Q1 rate-fence schedule and OTA close-out rules; the overbooking commitment; the Q2 posture; the Q3 response package; and a one-page revenue-committee memo that frames the three quarters around the RevPAR-versus-GOPPAR-versus-rate-integrity triangle.

## Sectoral Background

The 2020–2025 Cartagena boutique-hotel category had, in the case universe, grown from approximately COP 340bn to COP 520bn in aggregate revenue across the Centro Amurallado, Getsemaní and Bocagrande sub-markets, concentrated in three operator tiers (international chains including Four Seasons Sofitel and Hilton; regional boutique groups like Casa San Agustín and Casa Pestagua; independent boutique properties like Hotel La Merced). The 2025 Centro-Amurallado boutique panel registered an average ADR of COP 780,000, an average occupancy of 68%, an average RevPAR of COP 530,400, an average GOPPAR of COP 318,240, an average OTA-channel share of 52%, and an average direct-channel share of 21%, with significant variance across operators driven primarily by rate-integrity discipline rather than by physical-product differentiation.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1972–2025 revenue-management literature produced three findings the primer sim had to respect. First, Kenneth Littlewood's 1972 rule, operationalised as an OTA close-out trigger rather than as an analytical curiosity, produced documented Q1 RevPAR readings approximately 6–14% higher than unsequenced regimes on comparable high-season boutique panels. Second, the post-1990 OTA-commission-compression literature, applied to the RevPAR-versus-GOPPAR divergence, produced documented GOPPAR readings approximately 12–22% lower than RevPAR-only regimes at equivalent occupancy and demonstrated why RevPAR alone is an incomplete scoreboard. Third, the rate-integrity tradition — compressed into the *never cut rates to fill shoulder-season rooms at the cost of high-season rate memory* maxim — predicted that panic-discounting during a demand shock destroyed rate positioning for two or three subsequent years on the documented Cartagena panel.

The post-2010 hotel-revenue-management literature added two findings. The compensating-demand discipline, as deployed in documented cancelled-event recoveries (the 2019 Barcelona WorldCon cancellation; the 2021 Edinburgh Fringe reduction; and several Cartagena-specific 2022–2024 event shifts), produced documented occupancy-recovery readings approximately 8–14 points above panic-discount regimes while preserving rate integrity. And the GOPPAR-as-scoreboard movement, driven by the PE-ownership segment's insistence on unit economics rather than on vanity RevPAR, had shifted the boutique-independent segment toward net-ADR and contribution-per-available-room reporting during 2022–2025.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The March 2026 revenue-committee meeting had closed Q1 at RevPAR COP 576,000 against a COP 612,000 budget and GOPPAR COP 346,000 against a COP 392,000 budget, both driven by OTA-commission compression that the March committee had not fully anticipated. The April 10 Hay Festival announcement had moved October into Q3-shock territory. The April 23 three-quarter commit was the May revenue-committee's standing agenda.

### What Bonilla-Narváez knew

The four-rate-category structure and the fence rules; the Q1 occupancy forecast and the Littlewood close-out trigger points; the overbooking mathematics and the walk-cost estimate; the Q2 occupancy-versus-rate trade-off at three postures; the Q3 three-response space; the channel mix and channel costs; and the rate-integrity 24-month-memory effect documented in the 2023 Cartagena panel.

### What she did not know

Whether the Q3 gastronomy-weekend partner chefs would commit to the October dates at the commercial terms proposed; whether the corporate-meetings push into the pharma associations could originate five qualified leads within six weeks; whether the wellness retreat would draw the repeat-guest segment the Loyalty channel served; whether Booking.com would run a countervailing Cartagena promotion during October that would complicate the rate-hold posture; and whether the owning-family chairman would accept a Q3 compensating-demand package that projected occupancy recovery to 72% rather than to 78% baseline.

### What others were saying

**The Chairman (owning family)**: *"No bajemos la tarifa por el festival. La memoria tarifaria vale más que el octubre."*

**The Director of Revenue (internal)**: *"RevPAR Q2 sube 12% si corto la tarifa. GOPPAR Q2 sube 6%. ¿Cuál es la métrica que quieres reportar?"*

**The Director of Sales (corporate and groups)**: *"Cinco cuentas farmacéuticas tienen convenciones en octubre. Tres semanas para cerrar, o se van a Medellín."*

**The Hay Festival Producer (Bogotá venue relocation)**: *"Volveremos a Cartagena en 2027. La audiencia repetida existe."*

**The Booking.com Partner Manager (Cartagena territory)**: *"No puedo garantizar que no haya promoción en octubre. Si la hay, La Merced estará incluida salvo que nos pidas lo contrario."*

## Options Under Consideration

Bonilla-Narváez had narrowed the three-quarter space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — Q1 rate-fence integrity + Littlewood close-outs + 10% overbooking; Q2 moderate cut to 62% occupancy; Q3 compensating-demand build with hold-rate (Recommended).** Rate integrity preserved across the three quarters; RevPAR and GOPPAR both lift; Q3 occupancy recovers to approximately 72% without rate damage; 2027 rate memory protected.
- **Option B — Q1 rate integrity; Q2 aggressive cut to 71%; Q3 panic-discount to recover 18-point occupancy shock.** Rejected — the Q2-plus-Q3 combined rate-cut lands on the rate-integrity 24-month-memory trigger; the 2027 high season rate memory starts at the Q3 2026 discounted anchor rather than at the Q1 2026 rack anchor; documented panel evidence shows 14–22% RevPAR compression through Q1–Q2 2027 under equivalent regimes.
- **Option C — Q1 rate integrity + Littlewood close-outs; Q2 full rate hold at 48% occupancy; Q3 full rate hold and run at approximately 56%.** Defensible on rate-integrity grounds but leaves Q2 gross margin approximately COP 380m below budget and Q3 approximately COP 620m below budget, combined deficit exceeds the annual CapEx budget and triggers a 2027 CapEx cut the chairman has flagged as unacceptable.

Bonilla-Narváez's private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the single decision most correlated with the three-quarter composite outcome was the Q3 compensating-demand build, because the Q3 demand shock was the single largest single-quarter variance in the three-quarter arc and because the rate-integrity discipline compounded across Q1–Q3 in a way the panel data showed was irrecoverable within twelve months if the Q3 response broke rate integrity. The *Habitación con Vista al Margen* primer sits at the boutique-hotel GM primer tier in the 2026 Express catalogue, distinct from Casa Palma Revenue Maximizer (sim 018, 200-room resort, 12-month pricing-and-channel-and-displacement), Hotel Pacifico Pricing Power (sim 006, 240-room resort, five-season pricing-curve against a competitive flash sale), Hotel Cielo F&B (sim 076, F&B-specific operations), Hotel Mirador Digital (sim 077, digital-transformation hotel frame), and Montaverde Hospitality Portfolio (sim 075, portfolio-level multi-property). Primer 298 emphasises the first-pass RevPAR-versus-GOPPAR-versus-rate-integrity triangle for the boutique-scale GM; the other galaxy frames emphasise their specialised layers.
