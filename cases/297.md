## Abstract

Sucursal Torreón of Banco Huizache S.A. was, in the case universe, a neighbourhood-scale retail bank branch operating in the Torreón sector of the La Laguna metropolitan area — approximately MXN 620m in April 2026 deposit book, MXN 445m loan book, eight customer-advisor headcount plus two operations staff and one branch-manager role, three product lines (deposits including demand and term; retail loans including personal and vehicle; SME loans for the neighbourhood-bodega and small-manufacturing segment), a NIM target of 3.8% against a year-to-date actual of 3.6%, a NPL ratio of 2.1% against a tolerance of 3.0%, a loan-to-deposit ratio of 72%, and a regional-supervisor dashboard that read the branch's CAMELS micro-panel (Capital, Asset Quality, Management, Earnings, Liquidity, Sensitivity) at each monthly read. The March 2026 regional directive had three forcing elements: a 150-basis-point policy-rate hike the regional economist flagged as more-likely-than-not for Q3 2026, a regional credit-quality audit scheduled for Q4 2026, and a three-round branch-management exercise the Sucursal Managers' Program had issued for the April cycle. The case opens at 7:12 a.m. on Thursday, April 23, 2026, at the branch's manager's office as **Leopoldo Saavedra-Treviño**, a 41-year-old branch manager, prepares the three-round commitment.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Read the branch's CAMELS micro-panel — Capital, Asset Quality, Management, Earnings, Liquidity, Sensitivity — as the regulator's six-dimensional scorecard rather than as a compliance formality, and articulate why the branch manager's product-pricing and advisor-allocation decisions move each dimension at documented magnitudes that the monthly read picks up three to six months out.
2. Allocate the branch's eight customer advisors across the four task families — Acquisition (new-customer origination), Retention (deposit-base protection), Credit Monitoring (NPL-formation suppression), Cross-Sell (fee-income growth) — and articulate why the allocation is a multi-output production problem in which concentrating all eight on any single family produces a predictable failure in the other three.
3. Price retail-loan segments A (prime income, 1.2% default), B (salaried middle-class, 3.8%), C (small-business owner, 7.4%) and D (informal employment, 14.1%) under the risk-adjusted-return-on-capital discipline, and articulate why Raghuram Rajan's risk-shifting problem predicts that pricing a high-default segment at a low-default rate produces two rounds of high apparent spread followed by a third-round default cascade.
4. Diagnose the Round 2 policy-rate-shock repricing gap — deposits reprice within days because they sit at short tenor; loans reprice within months because they sit at twelve-to-twenty-four-month fixed rates — and design the deposit-versus-loan reprice sequencing that defends NIM without reopening deposit-base attrition; articulate why the branch that extended long fixed-rate loans in Round 1 pays the Round 2 bill.
5. Commit the Round 3 regional credit-quality audit package (NPL ratio, provision adequacy, policy compliance), trace the compounding effect of Round 1 credit-pricing errors on Round 3 NPL readings, and articulate why this bank-branch primer frame is distinct from the BancoMirador Metropolitan Branch (sim 071) frame — 297 at neighbourhood scale with a primer-level CAMELS and repricing-gap arc, 071 at metropolitan scale with a mid-cycle positioning package and a two-hundred-basis-point rate-shock stress test.

## The Protagonist

Saavedra-Treviño, 41, had been Sucursal Torreón's branch manager since 2023, had completed a Licenciatura en Economía at the Universidad Autónoma de Sierra Mojada in 2006 and a Maestría en Banca y Finanzas at the Instituto Superior Chipinque de Monterrey in 2012, had spent eleven years inside the Banco Huizache network across three branches (Gómez Palacio, Saltillo, and Torreón) in credit-analyst, SME-advisor and assistant-manager roles, and had assumed the Torreón manager seat in a 2023 internal rotation. His banking instincts, his regional supervisor had noted in the 2025 performance review, had been shaped by two teachings: a two-year stretch inside the 2018–2020 peso-volatility cycle during which he had watched a sibling branch collapse NIM by ninety basis points through mis-sequenced deposit-and-loan repricing; and an eighteen-month stint on the 2021 regional credit-committee during which he had audited three branches whose Round-1 aggressive segment-D pricing had produced Round-3 NPL readings above 8% and triggered regional intervention. The regional supervisor had assigned him the April three-round exercise on three considerations: his comfort protecting NIM through restraint rather than through spread expansion; his willingness to price segment D at its true default probability; and his command of the CAMELS micro-panel as an integrated scorecard rather than as six unrelated numbers.

---

## The Decision

Saavedra-Treviño had to commit, by the 9:30 a.m. regional-supervisor call, the three-round branch-management package.

1. **Round 1 — Advisor allocation and credit pricing.** Distribute eight advisors across the four task families; commit the four-segment retail-loan pricing schedule (A at 14.2% annualised; B at 19.4%; C at 25.8%; D at 34.6%); commit the deposit-rate package (demand at 3.2%, 90-day term at 5.6%, 180-day term at 6.4%). Initial condition: NIM 3.6%, NPL 2.1%, LDR 72%.
2. **Round 2 — 150bp policy-rate shock.** Regional economist forecasts a 150-basis-point policy-rate hike in Q3 2026. Deposits reprice within 14 days (short-tenor book); loans reprice across 12–24 months (fixed-rate book). Repricing-gap exposure quantified; deposit-and-loan reprice sequencing committed; NIM-defence posture chosen from three sub-options (defensive, matched, aggressive).
3. **Round 3 — Regional credit-quality audit.** Q4 2026 audit by the regional credit committee. NPL ratio, provision-adequacy ratio, credit-policy-compliance percentage. Branches that loaded Round 1 with aggressive segment-D pricing watch NPL spike to approximately 6.4% and trigger provision adequacy-shortfall. Audit package committed; remediation plan drafted if any of three thresholds is breached.

Commitments: the Round 1 allocation and pricing; the Round 2 repricing-gap posture; the Round 3 audit package and remediation plan; and a one-page regional-supervisor memo that frames the three-round commitment in the CAMELS and CLV (Customer Lifetime Value) language the supervisor expects.

## Sectoral Background

The 2020–2025 Mexican regional-retail-banking category had, in the case universe, grown from approximately MXN 1.8tn to MXN 2.6tn in aggregate branch-booked deposits, concentrated in three operator tiers (national banks including Banco Ahuehuete, Banco Cenzontle and Banco Mezquital; regional banks including Banco Huizache and peers; cooperativas and SOFOMes). The La Laguna regional panel of approximately 74 branches registered an average NIM of 3.9%, an average NPL of 2.4%, and an average LDR of 68% in the 2025 annual regional-banking report, with significant variance across branches driven primarily by credit-pricing discipline on segments C and D rather than by deposit-rate competition. The 2018–2025 policy-rate cycle had produced three rate shocks of ≥100 basis points (2022, 2023 and 2024), each of which had tested branch-level repricing-gap management and had separated the top-quartile branches from the median on NIM defence.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1983–2025 retail-banking literature produced three findings the primer sim had to respect. First, the CAMELS scorecard tradition, operationalised as a six-dimensional monthly read rather than as an annual compliance exercise, produced documented regulator-intervention-avoidance rates approximately 3.8× those of single-dimension-focus regimes on comparable regional-bank branch panels. Second, Raghuram Rajan's risk-shifting framework, applied to segment-D retail-loan pricing, predicted the two-round high-spread / third-round default-cascade pattern that had been observed in the 2021 regional credit-committee audit and subsequently in the 2022–2024 Mexican regional-banking panel. Third, the CLV-centric branch-management literature — a post-1995 tradition traceable to the customer-relationship-management movement — produced documented branch-profitability readings approximately 1.6× those of loan-only or deposit-only focus regimes.

The post-2010 banking literature added two findings. The repricing-gap management discipline, operationalised as a sequenced deposit-and-loan reprice rather than as a simultaneous reprice, produced documented NIM-defence readings approximately 22–38 basis points higher than unsequenced regimes under equivalent policy-rate shocks. And the three-round retail-banking teaching simulation, as deployed in the 2023–2025 Eureka Express catalogue, had converged on the eight-advisor / four-segment / three-round sandbox as the standard primer configuration.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The March 2026 regional directive had set the April cycle; the April 2 regional-economist forecast had assigned a 62% probability to the Q3 2026 150bp hike; the April 14 regional credit-committee meeting had confirmed the Q4 audit scope; the April 23 morning read showed NIM at 3.6%, NPL at 2.1%, LDR at 72%. The regional supervisor's April 21 note: *"No corras el segmento D agresivo en la Ronda 1. Hemos visto la cascada dos veces en los últimos tres años."*

### What Saavedra-Treviño knew

The eight-advisor headcount and the four-family allocation options; the four-segment retail-loan pricing space with true default probabilities; the deposit-rate package; the 150bp policy-rate-hike probability for Q3; the repricing-gap mechanics; the Q4 audit scope; and the CLV matrix comparing deposit-customer and loan-only-customer lifetime value.

### What he did not know

Whether the Q3 policy-rate hike would land at 150 basis points or at 125 or at 175; whether the segment-D marketplace would absorb the 34.6% pricing rate Option A committed to or would push back with informal-lender competition; whether the segment-C small-business segment would hold at 7.4% default after the rate shock or would drift upward to 9–11%; whether the Round 2 repricing-gap posture could be held against the retail-deposit-customer preference for 180-day term rather than 90-day term; and whether the Q4 audit's provision-adequacy reading would accept the forward-looking provisions Saavedra-Treviño planned to build in Round 1 or would require retrospective adjustments.

### What others were saying

**The Regional Supervisor**: *"Protege el NIM. Si corres al D agresivo, el Q4 audit te corta la cabeza."*

**The Senior Credit Advisor (15 years in the branch)**: *"El segmento D en Torreón es el bodega-familiar. Tasa justa, monitoreo serio, paga."*

**The Deposit-Base Retention Advisor**: *"Si le das al 90-día 5.6 y el nacional le da 5.4, los míos se quedan. Si bajas a 5.2, se van."*

**The Branch Controller**: *"Brecha de repricing: 28% de depósitos repricean en 14 días, 81% de préstamos repricean en más de 180 días."*

**The Regional Economist**: *"62% probabilidad de 150bp en Q3. La expectativa del mercado ya incorporó 75bp. El shock verdadero son los otros 75."*

## Options Under Consideration

Saavedra-Treviño had narrowed the three-round space to three commitment postures.

- **Option A — Balanced advisor allocation + segment-D priced at true risk + defensive repricing-gap posture + forward-looking provisions (Recommended).** Allocate 3 acquisition / 2 retention / 2 credit-monitoring / 1 cross-sell; price A/B/C/D at 14.2/19.4/25.8/34.6; deposit rates at 3.2/5.6/6.4; Round 2 defensive repricing (hold loan rates until legal reprice window; raise deposit rates in two phases to protect base); Round 3 forward-looking provisions built 40bp above IFRS 9 minimum. NIM target held; NPL stays below 3.0; audit package clean.
- **Option B — Acquisition-heavy allocation + segment-D priced aggressively low at 24% + aggressive Round 2 repricing (match loan rates immediately).** Rejected — Rajan risk-shifting cascade lands in Round 3; NPL spikes to 6.4%; provision shortfall triggers audit intervention; the Round 1 apparent NIM expansion is unwound by the Round 3 provision build.
- **Option C — Retention-heavy allocation + conservative segment-D at 38% + matched Round 2 repricing.** Defensible but leaves the branch loan-book too small by Round 3; NIM held; audit clean; but the branch falls short of the 2026 growth target and the CLV composite reads below the panel median.

Saavedra-Treviño's private view was that Option A was the right commitment and that the single decision most correlated with the Q4 audit outcome was the Round 1 segment-D pricing, because the 2021–2024 regional panel data showed that segment-D pricing variance explained approximately 64% of the branch-level Q4 NPL variance. The *Banco del Barrio* primer sits at the neighbourhood-scale bank-branch tier in the 2026 Express catalogue, distinct from BancoMirador Metropolitan Branch (sim 071, metropolitan-scale three-year positioning package with a 200bp rate-shock mid-cycle stress test) and BancoMontaverde Universal Bank CEO (sim 072, universal-bank-level CEO desk). Primer 297 emphasises the first-line branch-manager sandbox with the neighbourhood-bodega segment-D anchor and the CAMELS micro-panel; 071 emphasises the metropolitan four-way competitive set and the three-year positioning arc; 072 emphasises the universal-bank CEO command.
