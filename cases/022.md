## Abstract

TerraLogic Industries, a €4.1B European industrial-technology group, closed its €1.3B acquisition of Apex Networks, an industrial-IoT platform company, on February 2, 2026. The integration plan promised €180M of run-rate cost synergies by Year 3, a 90% talent-retention floor at the Apex engineering core, and a "no customer left behind" service-continuity commitment. Henrik Larsen, appointed Chief Restructuring Officer on the day of close, had ninety days to present an integration program to the TerraLogic board. The program would be evaluated on four weighted KPIs: Cost Synergies (30%), Talent Retention (25%), Revenue Readiness (25%), and Cultural Integration (20%). The first decision — integration strategy — would bind the structural, talent, and board-framing choices in the rounds that followed. The case places Larsen on the evening before the Round 1 integration-strategy recommendation and sets up the simulation's opening decision.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the four canonical post-merger integration strategies (Absorption, Preservation, Symbiotic, Reverse/Platform) and their KPI consequences.
2. Diagnose the interaction between integration strategy and organizational structure, and explain why a good strategy badly structured underperforms a mediocre strategy well-structured.
3. Analyze the compounding effects of talent and cultural interventions, and identify the three-intervention combinations most likely to stack.
4. Recommend a board narrative whose framing matches the four KPIs actually produced — rather than the ones originally promised.
5. Translate an integration strategy into a testable set of Year-1 milestones.

On the evening of **April 27, 2026**, **Henrik Larsen** sat in his office on the eighteenth floor of TerraLogic's Copenhagen headquarters and read, for the fifth time, the four-line agenda for the next morning's executive committee meeting. The agenda carried a single substantive item: *"Integration Strategy — Board-Ready Recommendation."* Below it, in his own handwriting from the previous afternoon, the annotation: *"You cannot pick all four. Pick one and stand behind it."* The executive committee would convene at 8:30 a.m.

---

## The Protagonist

Henrik Larsen, 51, had joined TerraLogic on February 2 as Chief Restructuring Officer. A graduate of the Copenhagen Business School and of INSEAD, he had run two successful post-merger integrations in the European industrial-software space (Siemens-adjacent, Bosch-adjacent) and one that had, in his own public accounting, failed to hit its synergy target and cost him his prior CEO seat. He had been hired by Ana Vidal, TerraLogic's Chair, on the recommendation of two sitting directors and against the preference of the TerraLogic CFO, who had preferred an internal appointment. Larsen's remit was ninety days to present a strategy, twelve months to execute the first phase, and a vesting schedule tied to composite performance across the four KPIs.

---

## The Decision

Larsen had to submit, by 8:30 a.m. on April 28, a single integration-strategy recommendation — the first of four board-facing decisions the simulation would treat as his Round-1 input:

1. **Integration Strategy (Round 1)** — Full Absorption, Preservation, Symbiotic, or Apex-Led Platform.
2. **Structure (Round 2)** — Functional, Divisional, Matrix, or Network/Platform.
3. **Talent & Culture (Round 3)** — three interventions from a menu of ten.
4. **Board Framing (Round 4)** — Cost Focus, Talent Focus, or Balanced.

The Round-1 strategy would bind the Round-2 structure space (some strategies foreclosed some structures), would shape the Round-3 talent math (absorption destroyed retention; preservation destroyed synergies), and would determine whether the Round-4 framing could be delivered honestly. Vidal had told him, in the private call on Friday: *"I can sell any of the four to the board provided you can tell me, with numbers, which KPI you are willing to trade."*

---

## Company Background

TerraLogic Industries, founded in 1987 in Denmark as an industrial-automation systems integrator, had grown through three European mid-market acquisitions into a €4.1B revenue group across four business areas: *Factory Automation* (38% of revenue), *Logistics Systems* (24%), *Process Industries* (22%), and the newly-acquired *Industrial IoT Platform* (Apex, pro forma 16%). 2025 EBITDA margin was 13.8%. The group employed 11,200 people pre-close and, with Apex, 13,900. The Zurich-listed TerraLogic ADR had reacted favorably to the deal announcement (+3.2% on the day) but had traded flat since close, pending the April 28 strategy communication.

Apex Networks, founded in 2016 in Eindhoven, was a 2,700-person industrial-IoT platform company with a deeply Agile engineering culture, a product organization structured around seven cross-functional "pods," and — in the target-screening work — a leadership cohort explicitly marked as a retention risk under absorption. Its top four architects, collectively the authors of roughly 60% of the platform's core patent portfolio, had signed twelve-month retention bonuses in the days before close. Apex's 2025 revenue was €620M at a growth rate of 34%. Its gross margin was 71%. Its EBITDA margin was −8%, and its 2026 operating-plan breakeven depended on a sales-motion acceleration only TerraLogic's channel footprint could provide.

## Industry and Market Context

The European industrial-IoT platform segment had consolidated aggressively in 2024–2025: six of the ten mid-market platform companies had either been acquired or raised growth-equity rounds that repriced the segment. Three findings from the post-2020 integration literature that Larsen's team had briefed on the Monday of his first week were consequential. First, full-absorption integrations delivered the highest headline synergy yield (22% of target opex on average) but lost, on average, 38% of engineering talent at the target within eighteen months. Second, preservation strategies retained 85% of talent but delivered only 6% of target opex as synergy. Third, symbiotic integrations — selective absorption with structural autonomy for innovation nodes — were the most variable: the top quartile outperformed both extremes, the bottom quartile underperformed both. Fourth, a small but growing category of *reverse integrations*, in which the acquired platform company's operating model became the integrated entity's operating model, delivered the highest three-year composite returns but only when the acquirer's own product organization was willing to be restructured. The fourth pattern described, in unsettling detail, what Vidal had told Larsen she was open to.

## The Situation

### How we got here

TerraLogic's board had approved the Apex acquisition in September 2025 on a two-pillar thesis: (a) Apex's platform would unlock €180M of run-rate synergies by Year 3 through the automation of TerraLogic's three incumbent automation stacks, and (b) TerraLogic's global channel would triple Apex's sales motion. The synergy target had been built bottom-up by the TerraLogic CFO's team on an assumption closer to full absorption. The channel-acceleration case had been built top-down by the Apex founder and the TerraLogic chief commercial officer on an assumption closer to preservation. No one had yet reconciled the two.

### What Larsen knew

The internal analytics team had modeled the four integration strategies across the four KPIs. Full Absorption yielded projected synergies of €210M (above target), retention of 61%, revenue readiness 58, cultural integration 42. Preservation yielded synergies of €85M (below target), retention 91%, revenue readiness 78, cultural integration 64. Symbiotic yielded synergies of €160M, retention 84%, revenue readiness 72, cultural integration 71. Apex-Led Platform yielded synergies of €145M, retention 89%, revenue readiness 70, cultural integration 78 — and required TerraLogic to restructure its own product organization to Apex's pod model, which would take eighteen months and disturb two of TerraLogic's own area leaders.

### What he did not know

Whether the top four Apex architects would stay past the twelve-month cliff under an absorption scenario, or whether two of them would leave together (they had done so twice before). Whether the TerraLogic CFO's bottom-up synergy build was credible under anything other than full absorption. Whether Vidal would, in the room, ratify a platform-led scenario that visibly demoted a legacy TerraLogic area leader the CFO had publicly backed in January.

### What others were saying

**Margrethe Olsen**, TerraLogic CFO, argued for **Full Absorption**: *"The synergy number is the deal's thesis. Preserve Apex and we breach the pro-forma we gave the rating agency. Absorb, realize, move on."*

**Jeroen de Vries**, Apex founder and now TerraLogic Executive Vice President, argued for **Apex-Led Platform**: *"You bought a growth story. Do not integrate it into a maintenance story. Restructure the stack around the pods. The synergies you lose in Year 1 you recover in Year 3 through revenue."*

**Lucía Ferrer**, TerraLogic Chief People Officer, argued for **Symbiotic**: *"We retain 84%, we hit a credible synergy number, and we give the cultural-integration work the eighteen months it actually needs. Absorption kills the people you just paid for. Platform kills the people who hired you."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Larsen had narrowed the space to three options he could defend to the board in a forty-minute Q&A.

- **Option A — Full Absorption (Olsen plan):** TerraLogic operating model, TerraLogic org chart, Apex dissolved into area units within 12 months. Synergies €210M; retention 61%; revenue readiness 58; cultural integration 42. Weighted composite (30/25/25/20): 68.

- **Option B — Symbiotic (Ferrer plan):** Selective absorption of corporate functions; preservation of the Apex product/engineering core as a standalone BU for 24 months with explicit interfaces; a unified commercial motion. Synergies €160M; retention 84%; revenue readiness 72; cultural integration 71. Weighted composite: 74.

- **Option C — Apex-Led Platform (de Vries plan):** Adopt the Apex pod model as the group operating model for product and engineering; restructure two TerraLogic areas onto the platform; accept 18 months of transition noise. Synergies €145M; retention 89%; revenue readiness 70; cultural integration 78. Weighted composite: 76.

Larsen suspected Option C was right on composite and wrong on political tractability; Option B was right on composite-conditional-on-execution and right on tractability; Option A was right on the CFO's number and wrong on every other KPI. At 8:22 a.m. he would walk to the executive-committee room with a single recommendation on a single page.

Larsen closed his laptop, put the one-page recommendation into his folio, and looked one last time at the four KPI chips on the dashboard his assistant had pinned to the glass: *F 30 · M 25 · O 25 · L&G 20.* The composite the board would remember was the one he chose to defend. The meeting was thirty-five minutes away.
