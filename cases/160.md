## Abstract

VOLTEX ELECTRONICS was, in the case universe, a Stockholm-headquartered European consumer-electronics manufacturer with EUR 2.4B in 2025 revenue across five categories (small kitchen appliances, personal-care, audio, smart-home and accessories), 38 active SKUs across 18 European markets, a 2025 gross-margin of 34%, a 2025 Circularity Index of 18 against a European consumer-electronics peer-benchmark of 42 (index captures percentage of materials flowing through recovery, refurbishment or recycling channels), a Reverse-Logistics Capacity reading of 12% of annual outbound volume against a peer-benchmark of 34%, a Cost-Efficiency reading at EUR 14.6 per unit delivered against a peer-benchmark of EUR 13.2, an Environmental-Impact reading of 4.2 (higher is worse) against a peer-benchmark of 3.1, and a Customer-Satisfaction reading of 74 against a peer-benchmark of 76. The 2023–2025 operating model had been anchored on a linear-supply-chain discipline that had delivered cost-efficiency at the point of outbound shipment but had left the EU 2025–2027 regulatory calendar (EU Right-to-Repair Directive, revised Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation, and the extended Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment Directive obligations) unaddressed and had left the circular-premium consumer-segment margin to competitors already operating refurbishment and take-back programmes. The Q1 2026 board had approved a EUR 84M circular-supply-chain envelope and a Chief-Operating-Officer-led five-round commitment cycle against a Circularity Index floor above 42, a Reverse-Logistics Capacity floor above 30%, a Cost-Efficiency ceiling below EUR 14.0 per unit, an Environmental-Impact ceiling below 3.4, a CSAT floor above 78, and a composite Circular-Supply-Chain Score above 72. The case opens at 5:34 a.m. on Monday, April 13, 2026, at the Voltex Electronics Stockholm Kungsholmen office as **Henrik Andersson**, a 50-year-old Chief Operating Officer, prepares the Round-1 reverse-logistics-build commitment. Seventy-four days separate him from the June 26 board operations-and-circularity review.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Round-1 reverse-logistics-build commitment (in-house premium-tier reverse logistics treated as a paid service layer vs. outsourced commodity-cost envelope) and diagnose when a premium-tier envelope dominates a commodity envelope on the Circularity-Index and margin-durability trade-off.
2. Analyze the Round-2 take-back-architecture commitment (omnichannel drop-off-plus-pickup with postal, store and scheduled-pickup routes vs. retailer-only take-back) and recommend the omnichannel envelope against the retailer envelope on the Reverse-Logistics-Capacity basis.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 refurbishment-capacity commitment (two-site in-house facility build with skilled-technician cohort vs. third-party-refurbishment outsource) and recommend the in-house envelope against the outsource envelope on the twelve-month CSAT and margin basis.
4. Respond to the Round-4 design-for-circularity commitment (modular-repairable SKU platform rebuild against the EU Right-to-Repair and Ecodesign standards vs. minimum-compliance envelope) and recommend the platform envelope against the minimum envelope on the Circularity-Index basis.
5. Design the Round-5 regulatory-readiness posture commitment (proactive-compliance envelope with published repair-index labels and open-spare-parts catalogue vs. last-minute-compliance envelope) and diagnose when circular supply chain becomes a margin asset rather than a cost tax.

## The Protagonist

Henrik Andersson, 50, had assumed the Chief-Operating-Officer role at Voltex Electronics on September 15, 2025, from a Tier-1 European appliance group where he had, across twelve years, led two circular-supply-chain rebuilds and had closed a 2023 reverse-logistics-and-refurbishment programme across an 18-market white-goods portfolio that had delivered a Circularity-Index uplift from 22 to 48 and a Reverse-Logistics-Capacity uplift from 14% to 38% inside twenty months. Before that he had spent seven years as a Head of Supply Chain at a Copenhagen-based consumer-electronics group. The Chief Executive Officer had recruited him specifically on two capabilities: his documented discipline of treating reverse-logistics as a premium-tier service layer rather than a sunk-cost line item, and his willingness to defend refurbishment-capacity capex against short-horizon gross-margin pressure. His 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the Circularity Index and 25% to the Reverse-Logistics Capacity floor.

## The Decision

Andersson had to commit, by the May 5 board pre-review, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–5.

1. **Round 1 — Reverse-logistics build.** Three archetypes: in-house-premium-tier (reverse logistics operated as a paid premium-tier service layer with customer pick-up SKU, insured-transport, refurbishment routing and pricing signal, Circularity Index +14, Composite 76), outsourced-commodity (reverse logistics outsourced to postal and third-party logistics on cost-minimising terms — Composite 36, capacity-ceiling), or hybrid (in-house for top-3 categories, outsourced for the remaining two — Composite 58). Working envelope: in-house-premium-tier.
2. **Round 2 — Take-back architecture.** Three archetypes: omnichannel (postal, in-store drop-off across 1,800 retail partners, and scheduled in-home pick-up with pre-paid labelling, Reverse-Logistics Capacity +18, Composite 76), retailer-only (take-back through retail partners only — Composite 38, capacity-ceiling), or postal-only (postal take-back — Composite 54). Working envelope: omnichannel.
3. **Round 3 — Refurbishment capacity.** Three vectors: two-site-in-house (two-site facility build in Kraków and Porto with 220-technician cohort, throughput design for 680,000 units per annum, Composite 78), third-party-outsource (outsourced to a pan-European refurbishment partner — Composite 36, margin and CSAT erosion), or single-site (one-site build in Kraków — Composite 58, capacity-ceiling). Working envelope: two-site-in-house.
4. **Round 4 — Design-for-circularity SKU redesign.** Three vectors: modular-platform (rebuild 22 of 38 SKUs on a modular-repairable-platform architecture aligned to the EU Right-to-Repair and Ecodesign standards, published repair-index label on every SKU, Composite 76), minimum-compliance (compliance on Ecodesign minimums only — Composite 38, regulatory tail-risk and margin forfeit), or phased (rebuild 8 SKUs in Year 1, 14 in Year 2 — Composite 60). Working envelope: modular-platform.
5. **Round 5 — Regulatory-readiness posture.** Three vectors: proactive (published repair-index labels, open-spare-parts catalogue with 10-year commitment, third-party repair-network certification, Composite 78), last-minute (compliance at the regulatory effective date — Composite 38, stranded-SKU risk), or phased-commitment (proactive on top-3 categories, last-minute on the remaining two — Composite 58). Working envelope: proactive.

On Andersson's working model, an In-House-Premium / Omnichannel / Two-Site / Modular-Platform / Proactive envelope delivered Circularity Index 48, Reverse-Logistics Capacity 34%, Cost Efficiency EUR 13.8, Environmental Impact 3.2, CSAT 80, Composite 76. An Outsourced-Commodity / Retailer-Only / Third-Party / Minimum-Compliance / Last-Minute envelope delivered Circularity Index 22, Capacity 14%, Cost Efficiency EUR 14.4, Environmental Impact 4.0, CSAT 68, Composite 28 (regulatory-trigger). A Hybrid / Postal-Only / Single-Site / Phased / Phased-Commitment envelope delivered Composite 56.

## Company Background

Voltex Electronics had been founded in Stockholm in 1982 as a small-kitchen-appliance manufacturer and had, across forty-four years, grown into a European consumer-electronics group with 38 active SKUs across five categories, 1,800 retail-partner relationships across 18 markets, and three manufacturing sites (Stockholm, Kraków and Porto). The 2023–2025 operating model had been anchored on a linear-supply-chain discipline that had delivered EUR 14.6 per-unit cost-efficiency but had left the circular-supply-chain margin and the 2025–2027 EU regulatory obligations unaddressed.

The Q1 2026 board dossier had identified three specific pressures the five-round envelope would inherit. First, the EU Right-to-Repair Directive had entered into force in Q3 2024 with Member-State transposition deadlines across 2025–2026, placing an obligation on manufacturers of the Voltex category profile to provide spare-parts availability for up to 10 years and repair-index labelling on point-of-sale SKUs. Second, the revised Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation had, across 2025, introduced specific product-category circular-design requirements with an effective date inside H2 2027. Third, the December 2025 consumer-segmentation study had documented that 28% of European consumers in the Voltex categories had expressed a willingness to pay a 8–14% premium for modular-repairable, take-back-enabled products, a segment the linear-supply-chain operating model was not positioned to serve.

## Industry and Market Context

The European consumer-electronics circular-supply-chain landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2020–2024 premium-tier-reverse-logistics evidence base had, across 34 European consumer-electronics programmes, documented a Circularity-Index differential of 18–26 points between premium-tier envelopes and commodity-cost envelopes, with the bulk of the effect travelling through customer-friction reduction at the take-back interface. Second, the 2022–2024 omnichannel-take-back literature had, across 38 European consumer-goods portfolios, placed omnichannel envelopes at a Reverse-Logistics-Capacity uplift of 18–26 points against retailer-only envelopes. Third, the 2022–2024 in-house-refurbishment literature had, across 28 consumer-electronics programmes, placed two-site in-house envelopes at a CSAT uplift of 6–10 points against third-party-outsource envelopes and at a margin differential of 3–5 points on the refurbished-unit P&L.

Empirically, the post-2022 circular-supply-chain literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, programmes that had rebuilt their SKU platform on a modular-repairable architecture had, across a 2020–2024 sample of 22 programmes, preserved Circularity Index above 42 in 86% of cases against 18% for programmes operating on minimum-compliance Ecodesign envelopes. Second, programmes that had published repair-index labels and open spare-parts catalogues had sustained CSAT above 78 in 82% of cases against 34% for programmes on last-minute-compliance postures.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the October 2025 operations-review, approved the EUR 84M envelope and the June 26 review calendar. The Head of Supply Chain had, on February 4, delivered the reverse-logistics capacity diagnostic. The Head of Product Platform had, on February 18, delivered the modular-repairable platform blueprint. The Chief Financial Officer had, on March 3, confirmed the EUR 84M envelope against the 2028 Circularity-Index trajectory. The Chief Legal Officer had, on March 17, delivered the EU Right-to-Repair and Ecodesign compliance matrix. The Head of Consumer Insight had, on March 24, delivered the circular-segment willingness-to-pay analysis.

### What Andersson knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. An In-House-Premium / Omnichannel / Two-Site / Modular-Platform / Proactive Envelope projected Composite 76. An Outsourced-Commodity / Retailer-Only / Third-Party / Minimum-Compliance / Last-Minute Envelope projected Composite 28. A Hybrid / Postal-Only / Single-Site / Phased / Phased-Commitment Envelope projected Composite 56.

### What he did not know

Whether the Kraków and Porto refurbishment-facility builds would clear against the pan-European technician-labour-market conditions inside the 14-month delivery window; whether the EU Right-to-Repair Member-State transposition calendar would compress the modular-platform rebuild into an impossible 2026 H2 ship window; whether the 22-SKU modular-platform rebuild would sustain gross-margin above 30% on the refurbished-unit P&L under a 2026 H2 consumer-price-elasticity compression; and whether the 28% circular-premium consumer-segment willingness-to-pay would hold at the 8–14% premium band through the 2026 H2 macro window.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Supply Chain**: *"In-house premium-tier reverse logistics. Commodity-cost envelope is a floor we cannot differentiate on."*

**The Head of Product Platform**: *"Modular-repairable platform across twenty-two SKUs. Minimum-compliance is a stranded-SKU portfolio by 2028."*

**The Head of Consumer Insight**: *"Twenty-eight percent of European consumers. Eight-to-fourteen percent willingness-to-pay. Premium tier is the durable-margin lane."*

**The Chief Executive Officer**: *"In a circular supply chain, the return trip is where the margin lives. Ship the premium tier, ship the platform rebuild."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Andersson had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — In-House-Premium / Omnichannel / Two-Site / Modular-Platform / Proactive:** the recommended envelope. Circularity Index 48. Reverse-Logistics Capacity 34%. Cost Efficiency EUR 13.8. Environmental Impact 3.2. CSAT 80. Composite 76. Recommended.
- **Option B — Outsourced-Commodity / Retailer-Only / Third-Party / Minimum-Compliance / Last-Minute:** the status-quo envelope. Composite 28. Rejected — regulatory-trigger and Circularity floor forfeited.
- **Option C — Hybrid / Postal-Only / Single-Site / Phased / Phased-Commitment:** the partial envelope. Composite 56. Rejected — forfeits the Circularity Index floor and the modular-platform durability.

Andersson's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 4 — the modular-repairable platform rebuild — because it was the round that converted the 22-SKU portfolio from a liability against the 2027 Ecodesign effective date into an asset that anchored the premium-tier reverse-logistics, the two-site refurbishment, and the proactive regulatory posture.
