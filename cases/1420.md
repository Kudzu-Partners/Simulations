# CoEditor.io: The Memo Before Nine

**Case Code:** MKT-2026-1420 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1420 — *Mutual-Gains Negotiation: Beyond Zero-Sum* · **v1.0 · September 2026**

**Time required: 90 minutes**

---

At 15:53 in Madrid on Tuesday, April 28, 2026, 6:53 AM in San Francisco, an email with no commentary reached Inés Rovira, director of deal strategy at CoEditor.io. The subject line read "thought you'd want to see this before the 9 AM." The attachment was a five-paragraph internal briefing marked *Confidential — Procurement Eyes Only*, written by MeridianBank's procurement team for that morning's renewal kickoff with CoEditor. The sender was a former CoEditor account director who had joined the bank's vendor-management group the previous quarter.

The kickoff was set for 9:00 AM Pacific at MeridianBank's San Francisco office. Before then, Rovira had to diagnose the negotiation, design the package the team would walk in with, and defend it to CoEditor's chief revenue officer, Priya Krishnamurthy.

## The Protagonist

Rovira had spent twelve years as a mergers-and-acquisitions lawyer before joining CoEditor in 2023 to run its deal desk. She was the only person on the account team formally trained as a negotiator and the one who usually pushed it off price and onto structure. The lead negotiator was Mateo Salazar, senior director of strategic accounts, who had owned MeridianBank since the first sale in 2024. Salazar was conscientious, technically literate and, under price pressure, inclined to find a way to make it work.

## The Decision

Rovira had to decide how to read the negotiation, which package to take into the room and how to open it, and how to defend that recommendation to Krishnamurthy, with two empty chairs beside her reserved for the chief financial officer and the general counsel. She also had to decide what, if anything, the memo was allowed to change.

## The Company and its Setting

CoEditor.io sold a collaborative document-editing platform to enterprises. It had about US$88 million in annual recurring revenue and some 520 employees in San Francisco, Madrid and Bogotá. MeridianBank, a US-headquartered global bank with about 210,000 employees in 32 countries, was its largest customer: 38,500 seats on a 24-month enterprise licence at an annual contract value of US$6.4 million, about 7% of CoEditor's revenue, expiring June 30, 2026 (see Exhibit 1).

Four months earlier, MeridianBank's chief executive had restructured procurement and hired a chief procurement officer with a public mandate to take 12% out of the technology vendor base. Word in the field was that every vendor renewal now had to come in at list price minus 15% or be re-bid. The procurement lead opposite Salazar that morning was Diane Whitford, vice president of vendor management, twelve weeks into the job: sharp, prepared, cordial and structurally adversarial.

## The Situation

The memo said four things CoEditor had not known an hour earlier. First, the bank had costed a migration away from CoEditor at US$14.2 million one-time plus US$3.1 million a year over a 36-month transition, and could not stand up an alternative before the first quarter of 2027. Second, new supervisory cybersecurity guidance required collaboration platforms that handled material non-public information to hold SOC 2 Type II and FedRAMP Moderate certification by December 31, 2026. CoEditor held SOC 2 Type I, and the bank's chief information security officer had a veto over the renewal. Third, the bank's savings target was 12% off run-rate; procurement planned to open at list minus 25%, or US$4.8 million, and close between 12% and 15% off, and the bank's chief financial officer would accept 10% off if multi-year, scope or reference concessions compensated. Fourth, the bank's chief data officer was looking for a partner for an internal artificial-intelligence platform, with a budget of US$18 million over 24 months, and procurement had been told not to raise it.

Whether the leak was deliberate, accidental or political could not be known from Madrid. The document could be genuine and incomplete, or genuine and planted.

CoEditor's own position was mixed. Its security roadmap already had SOC 2 Type II in flight for the fourth quarter; adding FedRAMP Moderate meant roughly US$600,000 of certification work pulled into the next eighteen months. Krishnamurthy, eight months into her seat, was suspicious of any plan that looked like leaving money on the table, and asked every deal team the same question: what would the customer have given us if we had just held firm on price?

If nothing changed, the renewal would be fought on one line. Whitford would open low, Salazar would try to make it work, and the contract would close somewhere inside the bank's discount band, with nothing else on the table.

## What Rovira Controlled

In the diagnosis, four calls. Her **frame**: position first, defending list price as a price war; mixed, working the price line while watching for other options; or interest first, mapping what lay behind each side's position before designing anything. Her **read of the bank's walk-away strength**, on a five-step scale from very strong, able to leave now, to very weak, dependent on an alternative it could not stand up in time. Her **use of the memo**: build tactics on its contents; treat it only as a private stress test of a package that had to stand on its own, with the general counsel signing off; or ignore it entirely. And the **posture towards Whitford**, on a five-step scale from adversarial to a strong investment in the relationship with firmness on substance.

In the design, five more. The **package** (see Exhibit 2): a status-quo renewal; the procurement-default renewal at a discount; or an integrative three-year package combining a higher price, both certifications, a wider seat scope, a public reference and a right of first negotiation on the data platform. The **opening anchor**, the year-one annual contract value CoEditor would name first, between US$5.0 million and US$8.5 million, with the current US$6.4 million as the starting point on the worksheet. The **framing**: issue by issue, or a package of three linked options. The **disclosure schedule**: open with all of CoEditor's interests; disclose categories first and specifics only after Whitford reciprocated; or disclose nothing. And the **fallbacks**: a single central case, or three concession sequences prepared in advance for Whitford holding firm, engaging or reverting.

In the defence, three. How to answer Krishnamurthy's **money-on-the-table challenge**: with the multi-year revenue trajectory, with the margin uplift, or with both, including the deal desk's estimate that about US$1.5 million conceded on the price line bought about US$13 million created off it. Whether the **general counsel's chair** was addressed, with the memo's chain of custody logged and the package justified on objective criteria alone. And whether the **chief financial officer's chair** was addressed, with the blended discount over three years worked out in advance.

Six readings would follow the recommendation. The **joint value** of the deal, CoEditor's revenue plus the migration costs the bank avoided, stood at US$36.3 million for a straight renewal. **Value capture** was CoEditor's share of any value the package added, at 0% before any package existed. **Defensibility**, how well the package held up under challenge, started at 50 out of 100; the **relationship** between Salazar and Whitford a year out, at 60; **anchor discipline**, the rigour of the opening offer and concession pattern, at 50; and Krishnamurthy's **confidence** in the recommendation, at 40.

## Closing

At 16:05 Madrid time, Rovira read the memo a second time, opened a blank page beside it and called Salazar in San Francisco. He was still at home making coffee, and he had not yet seen it.
