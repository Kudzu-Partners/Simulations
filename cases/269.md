## Abstract

The Director of Analytics at the case-universe Bogotá-headquartered statistical consulting firm, DataVista Analítica S.A.S. — approximately COP 14,200M in 2025 annual revenue, 85 analysts and statisticians, 23 active consulting contracts averaging COP 620M each, EBITDA margin 18% — had been alerted, in the April 2026 internal crisis memorandum, to six distribution misspecifications across the client portfolio that exposed the firm to approximately COP 22,120M in combined regulatory and contractual liability, with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) audit window opening in 60 days under Circular Externa 018. The Q1 2026 internal audit had flagged a recurring failure mode: analysts were fitting models to data without first testing whether the distribution family was correctly specified, producing Normal-calibrated catastrophic-loss models for insurance clients, Gaussian-calibrated probability-of-default models truncated at zero and one for banking clients, Poisson-calibrated adverse-event models for INVIMA healthcare clients whose data showed 2.3x overdispersion, and exponential-calibrated pipeline-failure models for Ecopetrol where Weibull wear-out phase dominated the post-year-seven hazard. The April 2026 commitment asked the Director of Analytics to work through the four rounds, read each red flag through the family-selection lens rather than the goodness-of-fit-number lens, and commit to a remediation plan the DataVista board would stake on the family-selection discipline rather than on the goodness-of-fit headline. The case opens at 2:12 p.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the firm's Bogotá head office as **Valentina Torres-Escobar**, a 38-year-old Director of Analytics, prepares the four-round commitment.

## The Protagonist

Torres-Escobar, 38, had been Director of Analytics since 2024, had spent eleven years across Colombian statistical-consulting practice, a two-year postdoctoral fellowship at Universidad de los Andes in 2013–2015 on heavy-tailed distribution fitting, and a one-year secondment at the SFC Supervisory Statistics unit in 2018–2019 where she worked on the Circular Externa 018 technical annex. In 2023 she co-authored with the DataVista co-founders the firm's internal playbook *Family First*, which framed distribution-model validation as a family-selection-first discipline rather than a goodness-of-fit-number-first discipline. The DataVista CEO had assigned her the four-round commitment on three considerations: her command of the Circular 018 technical annex; her discipline of leading the board read with the family-selection column rather than the goodness-of-fit column; and her willingness to recommend a controlled-release client-communication posture rather than a keep-internal posture.

## The Decision

Torres-Escobar had to commit, by the 4:30 p.m. DataVista CEO pre-brief, the four-round commitment and the remediation-plan recommendation.

1. **Round 1 — Diagnostic Audit.** Six red flags triaged by regulatory urgency and COP exposure. Priority: Seguros Andina (regulatory + COP 8,500M), Bancolombia (regulatory + COP 1,600M contract), INVIMA/Hospital (contract + COP 3,200M labour misallocation), Ecopetrol (regulatory + COP 5,600M remediation), Linio (litigation + COP 2,100M), Aceros del Caribe (ISO certification + COP 1,800M).
2. **Round 2 — Model Remediation Design.** Lognormal fit for Seguros Andina with KS goodness-of-fit; Beta-distribution PD model plus 10,000-iteration Monte Carlo with antithetic variates for Bancolombia; Negative Binomial with time-varying rate for Hospital Universitario San Ignacio. Remediation cost COP 640M across the three priorities.
3. **Round 3 — Stakeholder Management and Resource Allocation.** 42 analyst-weeks against ~90 required. Controlled-release client-communication posture. Contractor augmentation at 12 contractor-weeks (COP 134M, 3-week onboarding built into the 60-day timeline).
4. **Round 4 — Institutional Reform.** QA framework: mandatory distribution-assumption testing pre-delivery; peer-review protocol; continuing-education requirement (40 hours annually); client-facing transparency standards (model-assumption disclosure in every engagement letter); model-governance committee (three internal, two external members).

On Torres-Escobar's working commitment, the remediation-plan recommendation combined the five-priority list (Seguros Andina / Bancolombia / INVIMA / Ecopetrol / Linio), the COP 850M emergency-budget allocation, the controlled-release client-communication posture, and the QA-framework presentation to the board — a four-round plan driven entirely by the family-selection signal rather than by the goodness-of-fit headline. Projected exposure mitigated COP 18,400M; reputation score target 72; clients retained target 11 of 12.

## Industry and Market Context

The 1950–2025 applied-statistics literature produced three findings the commitment had to respect. First, family-selection-first validation dominates goodness-of-fit-first validation on documented misspecification rates across insurance, banking and healthcare studies. Second, heavy-tailed distributions (Lognormal, Pareto, Weibull) outperform Normal-family fits by 38–54% on documented tail-risk-capture across 2015–2024 comparable insurance-claims and pipeline-failure datasets. Third, Negative Binomial and Beta-Binomial models outperform Poisson and Binomial fits by 22–31% on documented overdispersion-corrected likelihood ratios.

The post-2010 professional-services-strategy literature added two findings. Controlled-release client-communication postures on model-misspecification crises produced documented client-retention rates 18–26 percentage points higher than keep-internal postures across 2018–2024 crisis-consulting cases. And QA frameworks featuring mandatory distribution-assumption-testing produced documented recurrence-prevention rates of approximately 72% across comparable regulated-industry consulting portfolios.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The April 2026 internal crisis memorandum flagged the six red flags. The Q1 2026 senior-analyst departures had left knowledge gaps across the insurance, banking and healthcare accounts. The SFC Circular Externa 018 deadline and the ANH investigation set the 60-day window. The DataVista CEO had asked Torres-Escobar to bring the four-round commitment to the April 22 pre-brief.

### What Torres-Escobar knew

Family-selection-first validation dominates goodness-of-fit-first validation; heavy-tailed distributions are the correct family for the Seguros Andina, Ecopetrol and Bancolombia tail-risk models; Negative Binomial and Beta-Binomial models correct overdispersion and intra-class-correlation; controlled-release client communication outperforms keep-internal on retention; and QA frameworks prevent recurrence at a 72% reading.

### What she did not know

Whether the SFC would accept the 60-day remediation window as adequate under Circular 018 or demand a 30-day accelerated window; whether the Grupo Sura insurance engagement would survive the Seguros Andina remediation or be pulled to a competitor; whether the Linio litigation threat would crystallise before or after the controlled-release disclosure; and whether the DataVista board would accept the QA-framework-first presentation sequence or demand the remediation plan first.

### What others were saying

**The Senior Statistical Consultant**: *"Pick the family. Lognormal for Seguros Andina. Beta for Bancolombia PD. Negative Binomial for INVIMA. Weibull for Ecopetrol. Five families, five remediations, one discipline."*

**The Model-Validation Coordinator**: *"Kolmogorov-Smirnov on every remediation. Anderson-Darling as the tail-weighted secondary. Q-Q plots in every client-facing report."*

**The DataVista CEO**: *"Controlled release. Stakeholder-by-stakeholder. The framework is the ticket."*

**The Key-Account Manager (Seguros Andina)**: *"Seguros Andina will accept a controlled-release disclosure if the new model produces a defensible under-reserving correction within thirty days."*

## Options Under Consideration

Torres-Escobar had narrowed the space to three remediation postures.

- **Option A — Four-round remediation plan with five-priority list, COP 850M emergency-budget allocation, controlled-release client-communication posture, and QA-framework presentation (Recommended):** Projected exposure mitigated COP 18,400M; reputation target 72; clients retained 11 of 12. Recommended.
- **Option B — Three-priority remediation with keep-internal client-communication posture:** rejected — underperforms on client-retention by 18–26 percentage points per the professional-services-strategy literature.
- **Option C — Six-priority simultaneous remediation with proactive-disclosure posture:** rejected — overcommits the 42 available analyst-weeks (against 90 required) and triggers a pre-audit SFC referral that the consultancy cannot control.

Her private view was that Option A was the right plan and that the red flag most correlated with the board's eventual acceptance was the Seguros Andina Normal→Lognormal/Pareto misspecification — because it was the flag where the family-selection read diverged most clearly from the goodness-of-fit read that the pre-2024 model validation had followed.
