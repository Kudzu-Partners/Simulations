## Abstract

The case focuses on a São Paulo-headquartered LatAm online marketplace with USD 4.6B in 2025 GMV across home, electronics, beauty and fashion categories, a 22% Brazil-category share and adjacent share positions in Mexico, Colombia and Chile, an 8.2% 2025 contribution margin, a 2025 Legal-Risk reading of 35 on a 2023-rebased baseline of 32 (composite captures litigation pipeline, regulatory inquiry density and competition-law exposure), a 2025 Consumer-Trust reading of 58 against a peer benchmark of 60, and a 2025 Ecosystem-Health reading of 50 against a peer benchmark of 58 (composite captures seller-NPS, repeat-buy rate and category-depth). The 2024 deployment of a multi-feature pricing engine — six features covering competitor-sensing, demand elasticity, stock-out probability, personalization, demand forecasting and stale-inventory liquidation — had, by Q1 2026, produced four million pricing moves per day. Three Q1 2026 events were named in the March 2026 risk-and-compliance-committee dossier: a January 2026 vendor proposal to deploy a reinforcement-learning pricing engine projected to add 1.8 percentage points of margin; a February 2026 internal-data-science finding that price-points across three competing marketplaces had converged to within 97% alignment in 12 high-volume categories without any cross-firm coordination; and a March 2026 letter from the Brazilian competition authority (CADE) opening a preliminary inquiry into algorithmic-collusion allegations. The Q1 2026 board had approved a USD 18M PriceBot envelope and a Chief-Pricing-Officer / Chief-Risk-Officer-led five-round commitment cycle against a Margin floor above 7.6%, a Market-Share floor above 21%, a Legal-Risk ceiling below 50, a Consumer-Trust floor above 60, an Ecosystem-Health floor above 56 and a Composite PriceBot Score above 64. The case opens at 6:42 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the marketplace's São Paulo head office as **Mariana Cordeiro**, a 41-year-old Chief Pricing Officer, prepares the Round-1 algorithm-audit commitment. Sixty-four days separate her from the June 25 board risk-and-technology review.

2. Analyze the Round-2 reinforcement-learning trade-off (bounded-RL envelope that caps competitor-sensing learning loops and requires explainability SLAs vs. open-RL envelope) and recommend the bounded envelope on the antitrust-exposure basis even at a documented margin give-back.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 convergence-disclosure commitment (proactive-disclosure envelope that publishes the convergence finding and the bounded-RL response inside the supervisor cycle vs. internal-only envelope) and recommend the disclosure envelope on the CADE-examination-outcome basis.
4. Respond to the Round-4 antitrust-probe response (cooperate-and-document envelope vs. minimal-disclosure) and recommend the cooperate envelope on the precedent and supervisory-action probability basis.
5. Design the Round-5 governance-framework commitment that converts the pricing engine from a margin-optimization asset with a tacit-collusion tail into a defensible governed pricing capability.

## The Protagonist

Mariana Cordeiro, 41, had assumed the Chief-Pricing-Officer role on January 6, 2026, after nine years across pricing science, marketplace operations and platform-economics consulting at three pan-LatAm marketplaces. She had, in 2023, led a competitor-sensing-feature redesign that had reduced the firm's CADE inquiry response time by 64% and had been cited in two industry-association working papers on algorithmic-pricing transparency. The Chief Executive Officer had recruited her in October 2025 specifically on two capabilities: her documented discipline of treating algorithmic pricing as a feature-by-feature governed capability rather than a single opaque optimization function, and her willingness to hold a bounded competitor-sensing envelope against documented margin pressure. Her 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the Legal-Risk ceiling and 20% to the Consumer-Trust floor.

## The Decision

Cordeiro had to commit, by the May 4 board pre-review, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–5.

1. **Round 1 — Algorithm-audit commitment.** Three archetypes: six-feature-baseline (publish a feature-by-feature audit log with version control, autonomy-scope declaration and explainability SLA across all six features, Composite 76), opaque-baseline (audit on incident only — Composite 32, supervisory-action probability), or phased (baseline on three features only — Composite 54). Working envelope: six-feature-baseline.
2. **Round 2 — Reinforcement-learning trade-off.** Three archetypes: bounded-RL (deploy the RL engine on demand-elasticity and stock-out features only, exclude competitor-sensing from RL learning loops, require explainability SLA, accept 0.6-point margin give-back vs. the vendor case, Composite 76), open-RL (vendor case as proposed — Composite 30, antitrust-exposure probability), or phased (bounded on two categories only — Composite 54). Working envelope: bounded-RL.
3. **Round 3 — Convergence disclosure.** Three vectors: proactive-disclosure (publish the convergence finding and the bounded-RL response in the May regulator pre-meeting, with the multi-marketplace data and the labelled feature decomposition, Composite 76), internal-only (document internally without disclosure — Composite 30, supervisory-discovery probability), or phased (proactive on Brazil only — Composite 54). Working envelope: proactive-disclosure.
4. **Round 4 — CADE antitrust-probe response.** Three vectors: cooperate-and-document (proactive document production, full pricing-decision dataset, third-party expert review, Composite 76), minimal-disclosure (statutory minimum, narrow privilege claims — Composite 30, fine-and-injunction probability), or phased (cooperate on six features but not on retraining-cadence data — Composite 54). Working envelope: cooperate-and-document.
5. **Round 5 — Governance framework.** Three vectors: layered-framework (standing pricing-governance committee, version-controlled feature register, explainability SLA, transparency disclosure cadence, human-oversight gates on competitor-sensing, integration into the three-lines-of-defence model, Composite 78), gate-only (governance as a deployment-gate — Composite 42), or phased (layered on three features only — Composite 56). Working envelope: layered-framework.

On Cordeiro's working model, a Six-Feature / Bounded / Proactive / Cooperate / Layered envelope delivered Margin 7.8%, Market Share 22%, Legal Risk 38 (lower better), Consumer Trust 64, Ecosystem Health 60, Composite 70.

## Company Background

The marketplace had been founded in São Paulo in 2009 and had, across seventeen years, built a 22%-share Brazil category position with adjacent share positions in three additional countries. The 2024 pricing-engine deployment had been built under the prior Chief Pricing Officer with a stated margin-optimization charter and without an explicit feature-by-feature explainability layer or an autonomy-scope declaration on the competitor-sensing feature. By Q1 2026 the engine was producing four million pricing moves per day across six features.

The three events the March 2026 dossier had named. First, the January 2026 vendor proposal had projected that a reinforcement-learning pricing engine would add 1.8 percentage points of contribution margin in the 12-month window after deployment, with no human-explainable decomposition of the underlying mechanism. Second, the February 2026 internal-data-science finding had documented that price points across the marketplace and two adjacent marketplaces had converged to within 97% alignment in 12 high-volume categories — categories that, at 2023 baseline, had a 78%–84% alignment band — without any documented cross-firm communication. Third, the March 2026 CADE letter had opened a preliminary inquiry citing algorithmic-collusion allegations and had requested document production within 60 days.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2023–2025 algorithmic-pricing-and-tacit-collusion literature had, across 28 documented marketplace deployments, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, marketplaces that had operated on a feature-by-feature published audit baseline had documented competition-authority inquiry resolutions 2.3× more favourable than marketplaces operating on opaque baselines. Second, deployments that had bounded reinforcement-learning loops away from competitor-sensing features had documented collusion-allegation rates 71% lower than open-RL deployments, with a documented average margin give-back of 0.4–0.8 points relative to open-RL. Third, deployments that had proactively disclosed convergence findings inside supervisor cycles had closed inquiries without enforcement actions in 68% of cases against 22% for internal-only postures.

Empirically, the post-2023 antitrust literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, regulators had increasingly held that intent was not required for a tacit-collusion finding when learned algorithmic behaviour produced a sustained convergence outcome. Second, marketplaces that had embedded layered governance — standing committee, feature register, explainability SLA, three-lines-of-defence integration — had sustained Legal-Risk readings below 40 in 72% of cases against 26% for gate-only governance.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the February 2026 risk-committee session, approved the USD 18M envelope and the June 25 review calendar. The Chief Risk Officer had, on March 4, delivered the six-feature inventory and convergence diagnostic. The Head of Pricing Engineering had, on March 11, delivered the bounded-RL architecture blueprint. The Chief Legal Officer had, on March 18, delivered the CADE-response framework. The Chief Financial Officer had, on March 25, delivered the margin-give-back impact pack.

### What Cordeiro knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. A Six-Feature / Bounded / Proactive / Cooperate / Layered Envelope projected Composite 70. An Opaque / Open-RL / Internal / Minimal / Gate-Only Envelope projected Composite 24. A Hybrid Envelope projected Composite 50.

### What she did not know

Whether the bounded-RL envelope would deliver enough of the projected 1.8-point margin uplift to defend against the Q3 2026 board challenge; whether the convergence pattern would persist after the bounded-RL deployment or unwind; whether the CADE inquiry would expand to a formal proceeding or close at the preliminary stage; and whether the two adjacent marketplaces would adopt similar governance frameworks under the precedent or accelerate competitor-sensing.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Risk Officer**: *"Six-feature baseline. The audit is the only argument that intent does not matter."*

**The Head of Pricing Engineering**: *"Bounded-RL. Competitor-sensing is the single feature that turns optimization into collusion."*

**The Chief Legal Officer**: *"Cooperate-and-document. CADE will not ask twice."*

**The Chief Executive Officer**: *"You don't get to price-fix by accident. You also don't get to not know. Build the governance. Pay the margin."*

## Options Under Consideration

Cordeiro had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Six-Feature / Bounded / Proactive / Cooperate / Layered:** the recommended envelope. Margin 7.8%. Market Share 22%. Legal Risk 38. Consumer Trust 64. Ecosystem Health 60. Composite 70. Recommended.
- **Option B — Opaque / Open-RL / Internal / Minimal / Gate-Only:** the optimization envelope. Composite 24. Rejected — fine-and-injunction probability above 65% in the 18-month window.
- **Option C — Hybrid / Phased / Phased / Phased / Phased:** the partial envelope. Composite 50. Rejected — feature-by-feature governance fragments under partial coverage.

Cordeiro's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 2 — the bounded-RL trade-off — because it was the round that converted the competitor-sensing feature from the engine that had generated the convergence into a feature with a documented autonomy-scope ceiling.
