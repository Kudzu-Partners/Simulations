## Abstract

HealthSpring was, in the case universe, a LATAM consumer health-tech venture founded in December 2025 with a USD 500,000 seed check, a single consumer wellness application in closed beta, a market taxonomy comprising three LATAM incumbents with a combined 11.1 million users, and a founder-and-four-engineers operating team based in Mexico City. On Monday, January 5, 2026, the Board (two seed investors plus the founder) approved a twelve-month no-fundraise no-pivot envelope inside which the Chief Executive Officer would commit, every month, four decisions — marketing budget, acquisition channel, product focus, price point. Lucía Benavides, a 33-year-old former Head of Product at a Bogotá-based digital-health operator, faced a strict cash-runway discipline, a monthly decision rhythm, an incumbent field with deeper pockets, and a Board commitment that a Series-A conversation would open only if the Year-1 LTV-over-CAC closed above 3.0 and the runway at twelve months or longer. The case opens at 6:42 a.m. on Monday, January 12, 2026, at a co-working space on Avenida Insurgentes, as Benavides prepares the Month-1 decision sheet.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Month-1 through Month-3 acquisition-channel, price, and product-focus choices and diagnose when a paid-search-heavy entry dominates on Year-1 revenue versus when a balanced-channel stance dominates on the twelve-month LTV-over-CAC composite.
2. Analyze the Month-4 through Month-6 price-increase-and-retention-focus transition and recommend the price-point revision consistent with a churn trajectory falling from 8% toward 6.2%.
3. Diagnose the Month-7 through Month-9 monetization-and-B2B-pilot choice under a competitor price cut, and design a churn-containment plan that preserves runway through the Month-9 cash-trough.
4. Design the Month-10 through Month-12 close that cuts paid spend, doubles B2B and integrations, and converts a consumer-only unit economic into a mixed consumer-plus-B2B portfolio with a defensible Year-2 plan.
5. Recommend a complete Year-1 envelope, identify the monthly decision most correlated with the Series-A-readiness outcome, and write the cohort-by-cohort narrative the Series-A deck would carry.

On the morning of **Monday, January 12, 2026**, **Lucía Benavides** arrived at the co-working space on Avenida Insurgentes in Mexico City at 6:42 a.m., opened the Month-1 decision sheet on her laptop, and re-read the single paragraph she had pinned to the top of the document across the December planning period: "No pivots. No fundraising. Twelve months. Four decisions a month. The unit economic is the plan." Eleven days separated her from the January 23 Board check-in at which she would commit the first-quarter arc.

---

## The Protagonist

Lucía Benavides, 33, had joined a Bogotá-based digital-health operator in 2018 as Senior Product Manager and had been promoted to Head of Product in 2022. A graduate of the Universidad de los Andes in industrial engineering with a specialization in operations research, she had, in her Bogotá role, shipped a four-product consumer wellness suite and had led the operator's first B2B employer-wellness pilot — a forty-client deal that had carried an annualized contract value of USD 2.8M at the point of her November 2025 departure. Her founding-team recruiting note, sent to the four engineers on November 20, 2025, had read: "Half a million in the bank, twelve months, no Series A allowed. We will know by month four whether our CAC pays back our LTV. If it does, we have a company. If it does not, we close cleanly and go home."

---

## The Decision

Benavides had to commit, by the January 23 Board check-in, the Month-1 decision vector the Board would read as the Q1 arc signal:

1. **Acquisition Channel and Marketing Budget** — whether to concentrate the Month-1 budget on paid search at USD 25,000 per month to buy early-funnel visibility against the three incumbents, or to run a balanced channel mix at a lower effective CAC but slower early-cohort build.
2. **Price Point and Product Focus** — whether to launch at USD 12.99 per month on a free-to-paid conversion funnel with a core-product focus, or to launch at a premium USD 14.99 with a retention-focus on high-intent early adopters.
3. **Monthly Rhythm and Monitoring Set** — whether to monitor CAC, LTV, churn, and runway as the four monthly instruments or to add a fifth-metric active-user count as a leading indicator of the next month's LTV trajectory.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across twelve months. A Month-1 package that ran paid search at USD 25,000, priced at USD 12.99, focused on the core product, and monitored the four-metric set produced, on Benavides's working model, a Month-3 user base of 4,100, a CAC of USD 88, an LTV of USD 162, a ratio of 1.8, a cash balance of USD 298,000, and a runway of fifteen months. A Month-1 package that ran a balanced channel at a lower spend and a premium price produced a slower Month-3 user count but a Month-3 LTV-over-CAC above 2.4 — a trajectory that would reach 3.0 inside Month-6.

## Company Background

HealthSpring had been founded in December 2025 with a single-app wellness concept targeted at 25–45 LATAM urban consumers with a monthly household income above USD 1,800 — a target addressable market of approximately 18 million across Mexico, Colombia, Chile, and Peru that the founder's pre-launch market sizing had identified. The product, in closed beta on the January 12 date, offered a three-module wellness track (sleep, movement, nutrition), a weekly habit dashboard, a wearable-data-integration layer (Apple Health, Google Fit, plus two regional wearable brands), and a Spanish-and-Portuguese dual-language content library of 240 pieces curated from a physician-and-nutritionist panel the founder had retained in November 2025. The Month-1 monthly recurring revenue was USD 0. The Month-1 active-user count was zero.

The competitive landscape comprised three LATAM consumer-health incumbents. Wellnia, headquartered in Buenos Aires, had, in 2025, carried 6.2 million users, an estimated monthly ARPU of USD 8.50, and a Series-C balance sheet. BienEstar, based in Santiago, had carried 3.4 million users, an estimated monthly ARPU of USD 6.20, and a recent Series-B extension. VidaPlus, a Lima-founded challenger, had carried 1.5 million users and had just closed a USD 14M Series-B at a reported USD 72M valuation. The combined 11.1 million active users represented a sector benchmark against which HealthSpring's Year-1 market-share target was framed at a 0.8–2.0% absorption of the addressable base — deliberately below the incumbents' scale but sufficient to establish the unit economic the Series-A conversation would require.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM consumer health-tech landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the Year-1 envelope sat inside. First, the 2024–2025 digital-health venture-funding cycle had, across a commercial-summary dataset of forty-two LATAM consumer-health rounds, produced a Series-A median ticket of USD 6.4M at a median USD 22M pre-money and a Series-A gating rule of a minimum LTV-over-CAC of 3.0 and a minimum twelve-month forward runway at the funding close. Second, the LATAM wearable-integration cohort had, in the 2023–2025 window, emerged as the single most defensible consumer-cohort segment — a commercial-summary analysis of 218 consumer-health-app cohorts had documented a wearable-integrated cohort LTV of USD 340 against a paid-only cohort LTV of USD 142, a 2.4-times multiplier that informed the Month-7-through-Month-12 integration push. Third, the LATAM employer-wellness B2B channel had, across 2024–2025, opened at a commercial contract-value range of USD 24,000–48,000 annualized per mid-market employer, with a commercial-summary finding that a first-year B2B pilot at a USD 38,000 monthly-recurring-revenue anchor contributed more to the Year-2 revenue baseline than any consumer-only monthly cohort the same founder could plausibly build.

Empirically, the post-2020 consumer-subscription literature had two robust findings the Year-1 envelope had to respect. First, founder teams that had, in Month-4 through Month-6, transitioned from a CAC-heavy paid-channel stance to a balanced-channel retention-first stance had, across a 2021–2024 sample of 94 consumer-SaaS founder teams, closed Year-1 with LTV-over-CAC ratios 1.7-times higher than teams that had held paid-channel dominance through Month-9. Second, consumer-SaaS teams that had added a B2B pilot at Month-7 through Month-9 had, across the same sample, preserved Year-2 revenue trajectory through competitor price-cut events at a 62% preservation rate against a 31% preservation rate for consumer-only teams.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Board had, on January 5, 2026, approved the USD 500,000 seed deployment envelope and had committed that a Series-A conversation would open only if the Month-12 scorecard carried an LTV-over-CAC at or above 3.0 and a forward runway at or above twelve months. The founder had, on January 8, closed the four-engineer hiring cycle at a combined USD 38,000 monthly payroll including her own USD 6,000 founder salary. The Month-1 cash-burn envelope had been modeled at USD 62,000 per month across payroll, marketing, cloud, and overhead — a twelve-months-if-nothing-sold ceiling that anchored the runway discipline.

### What Benavides knew

The team had modeled three Year-1 arcs. A Paid-Search-Heavy Arc (USD 25,000 monthly paid search across Months 1–6, USD 12.99 price, core-product focus) projected a Year-1 revenue of USD 780,000, a Year-1 LTV-over-CAC of 2.6, a Month-12 runway of 9 months, and a Series-A probability on the Board's internal scorecard of 38%. A Balanced-Channel Arc (USD 12,000 monthly paid search and USD 8,000 monthly content-and-community across Months 1–6, price raised to USD 14.99 in Month-4, retention focus from Month-4) projected a Year-1 revenue of USD 1,020,000, a Year-1 LTV-over-CAC of 4.1, a Month-12 runway of 14 months, and a Series-A probability of 72%. A Premium-Only Arc (no paid search, content-and-community only, USD 19.99 price point, high-intent retention from Month-1) projected a Year-1 revenue of USD 620,000, a Year-1 LTV-over-CAC of 5.2, a Month-12 runway of 16 months, and a Series-A probability of 44% — high unit economic but insufficient Month-12 scale for the Series-A thesis.

### What she did not know

Whether the Month-7 competitor price-cut event would arrive at the modeled 10% depth or at a deeper 15% that the second-source commercial intelligence had referenced; whether the Month-7-through-Month-9 B2B pilot would convert at the modeled 35% close rate on three mid-market employer conversations or at a lower 22% that a more conservative B2B peer pattern would imply; whether the wearable-integration launch at Month-10 would hold to the planned four-week engineering window or would slip to six weeks and consume the December launch runway; and whether the Series-A conversation that the Board's two funds had previewed would hold through the Q4 2026 market — a macro-uncertainty the Board had explicitly logged.

### What others were saying

**The seed-investor lead**: *"Balanced-Channel Arc. Month-4 price rise. Month-7 B2B pilot. The Month-12 deck writes itself if the cohort chart does."*

**The Head of Engineering (one of the four founding engineers)**: *"The wearable integration is the Year-2 moat. If you delay it past Month-10, you lose the Christmas cohort and the Year-2 revenue anchor."*

**The physician-and-nutritionist panel lead**: *"Retention is the content cadence. Two pieces a week, personalized to the wearable signal. The Month-4 retention curve bends where content meets the wearable."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Benavides had narrowed the space to three Year-1 arcs.

- **Option A — Paid-Search-Heavy:** USD 25,000 monthly paid search, USD 12.99 price, core-product focus, minimal wearable integration. Year-1 revenue USD 780,000. Series-A probability 38%. Fastest Month-3 user count. Weakest Year-1 LTV-over-CAC.

- **Option B — Balanced-Channel:** USD 20,000 monthly balanced (paid search plus content-and-community), price rising from USD 12.99 to USD 14.99 at Month-4, retention focus from Month-4, B2B pilot at Month-7, wearable integration at Month-10. Year-1 revenue USD 1,020,000. Series-A probability 72%. Aligns with seed-investor lead, Head of Engineering, and panel lead.

- **Option C — Premium-Only:** No paid search, content-and-community only, USD 19.99 price point, retention from Month-1. Year-1 revenue USD 620,000. Series-A probability 44%. High unit economic but insufficient Month-12 scale.

Benavides's private view, not yet shared with the Board, was that Option B was the right Year-1 envelope and that the single monthly decision most correlated with the Year-1 outcome was the Month-4 price-and-channel pivot — specifically, the simultaneous lift from USD 12.99 to USD 14.99 and the rebalance of USD 13,000 of monthly paid-search spend into a content-and-community stack that would compound organic acquisition across Months 5 through 9 and carry the channel through the Month-7 B2B pilot without raising the cash-burn ceiling.
