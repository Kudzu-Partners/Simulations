## Abstract

Tempo Records, LLC was, in the case universe, a mid-tier music company headquartered in a secondary-tier creative-industry city, with a roster of 24 signed artists across four genres, a publishing catalog valued at USD 10M, 2025 recorded-music revenue of USD 5.8M, 2025 publishing revenue of USD 2.1M, 2025 live-events revenue of USD 3.4M and 2025 sync-licensing revenue of USD 1.2M, a 2025 roster-health index of 65/100, 2.0M monthly listeners across streaming platforms and USD 5M of cash. The 2024–2025 cycle had left the label at a four-revenue-stream portfolio with three diverging pressures: streaming rates had compressed by 4% across the industry; publishing rates had been cut 10% by a regulatory rate-court decision in Q1 2026; and AI-generated music had begun to flood streaming platforms, producing a measurable catalog-depreciation signal of 3% per quarter on mid-tier roster titles. The Q1 2026 context had compounded the pressure: a mid-tier indie-artist on the Tempo roster had produced an organic viral moment in the second week of April, lifting streaming volumes by a 1.25× multiplier across forty-eight hours, and a film-franchise music supervisor had opened a premium sync-licensing placement window for a Q3 2026 release. The case opens at 6:42 a.m. on Wednesday, April 22, 2026, at the Tempo Records creative-office loft as **Delphine Akinyi-Martinez**, a 43-year-old Chief Executive Officer, prepares the Quarter-1 allocation commitment.

On the morning of **Wednesday, April 22, 2026**, **Delphine Akinyi-Martinez** arrived at the Tempo Records creative-office loft at 6:42 a.m., opened the three-quarter allocation workbook her Head of Artist Development, her Head of Marketing, her Head of Live and her Head of Sync-and-Publishing had finalised at 11:06 p.m. the night before, and re-read the sentence she had written at the front of the 2026 board memo: *"In music, you don't own the charts. You own the decade-long annuity that outlives them."*

## The Protagonist

Akinyi-Martinez, 43, had assumed the Chief Executive Officer role at Tempo Records on January 1, 2023, after fifteen years across A&R, marketing, live-and-touring management, and label leadership at two mid-tier labels and one major-label imprint. She had, in 2019–2021, led the catalog-acquisition of a 780-title indie-publishing roster for a mid-tier label that had compounded from USD 3.4M to USD 9.2M of valuation across three-and-a-half years on a sync-licensing-and-streaming-re-activation thesis. She had, in 2022, closed a film-franchise premium-sync placement that had generated USD 1.8M of one-time revenue and a three-year catalog-visibility halo that had compounded re-listening on a twelve-title bundle. The Tempo board had recruited her specifically on three capabilities: her documented discipline of holding catalog-acquisition spend at zero in stable-cycle quarters to deploy it at viral-moment multipliers, her willingness to rotate artist-development budget into artist-health restoration during AI-disruption-and-festival seasons, and her command of sync-licensing-intensity architecture that had, at the predecessor label, produced 2.4× sync revenue across eighteen months on a constant catalog.

## The Decision

Akinyi-Martinez had to commit, by the 8:00 a.m. label-executive session, the Quarter-1 allocation envelope and the directional intent for Quarters 2–3.

1. **Quarter 1 — Baseline allocation.** Three archetypes. A baseline-dry-powder envelope (USD 400K artist development, USD 200K marketing, zero catalog, USD 500K live, sync-intensity 3; total Q1 spend USD 1.1M; preserve dry powder for Q2 viral-moment deployment). A catalog-front-loaded envelope (USD 1.4M catalog acquisition in Q1; lose the Q2 multiplier). A marketing-forward envelope (USD 800K marketing, sync-intensity 5 in Q1; consume Q3 budget). Working envelope: baseline-dry-powder.
2. **Quarter 2 — Viral-moment response and publishing-rate cut absorption.** Three vectors. An opportunistic-catalog-acquisition envelope (USD 400K marketing, sync-intensity 4, USD 1.2M indie-publishing catalog at the viral multiplier; Q2 profit USD 840K). A marketing-only envelope (USD 600K marketing, sync-intensity 4, no catalog; Q2 profit USD 620K). A defensive envelope (hold Q1 allocation; absorb the publishing-rate cut and the viral moment at baseline). Working envelope: opportunistic-catalog-acquisition.
3. **Quarter 3 — AI-disruption and festival-season rotation.** Three vectors. A festival-and-sync envelope (USD 600K artist-health, USD 1.2M live, sync-intensity 5 on the film-franchise placement, zero catalog; Q3 revenue USD 3.4M, profit USD 1.9M). A defensive envelope (maintain Q1 baseline; absorb AI-disruption catalog depreciation). A catalog-double-down envelope (USD 1.8M additional catalog acquisition against the AI-disruption-discount; defer festival live). Working envelope: festival-and-sync.

On Akinyi-Martinez's working model, a Baseline-Dry-Powder / Opportunistic-Catalog-Acquisition / Festival-and-Sync envelope delivered cumulative cash USD 8.2M, catalog USD 11.3M, roster 72, and total three-quarter profit USD 3.8M — with a Composite Cycle-Read-and-Rotate rating.

## Company Background

Tempo Records, LLC had been founded in 2006 as an independent label for regional acts, had expanded into publishing in 2011, had signed its first festival-touring artist in 2014, and had, across twenty years, built a mid-tier music company organised into four revenue-stream functions (Recorded Music, Publishing, Live Events, Sync Licensing) with a shared A&R, Marketing, Legal and Royalty-Administration backbone. The 2023–2025 cycle had left the label at a four-stream-balanced posture with a 780-title publishing catalog, a 24-artist roster and a live-events book that had absorbed festival-season cyclicality. The Q1 2026 context had compounded three pressures: a regulatory rate-court decision in January 2026 had cut mechanical-publishing rates 10%; AI-generated music had started flooding streaming platforms; and festival-season bookings had opened in March 2026 with a 1.35× uplift signal.

## Industry and Market Context

The 2019–2025 mid-tier-label literature had, across 44 documented three-to-six-quarter budget cycles on USD 8M–USD 40M-revenue labels, produced three findings the envelope had to respect. First, baseline-dry-powder allocations that held catalog spend at zero in stable-cycle quarters had captured viral-moment multipliers of 1.8×–2.6× on catalog acquisitions in 68% of documented cases, against catalog-front-loaded alternatives. Second, sync-licensing-intensity architectures that pushed to level-5 on film-franchise premium placements in festival-season quarters had delivered sync-revenue lifts of 2.1×–3.4× in 62% of documented cases on comparable labels. Third, festival-season live-events allocations at USD 1.0M–USD 1.6M in Q3 cycles had captured live-revenue multipliers of 1.25×–1.45× in 74% of documented cases on mid-tier-label rosters with two-to-four festival-touring artists.

Empirically, the 2022–2025 AI-generated-music literature had two additional findings. First, catalog-depreciation signals from AI-generated flooding had averaged 2.4%–3.8% per quarter on mid-tier-roster titles in 58% of documented cases across the first eighteen months of AI-music proliferation. Second, artist-health restoration budgets at USD 500K–USD 800K in AI-disruption quarters had preserved roster-continuity indices above 70/100 in 72% of documented cases, against defensive-hold postures.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Q4 2025 board had approved the three-quarter allocation framework. The Head of Sync-and-Publishing had, on February 12, delivered the film-franchise premium-sync opportunity memo with a Q3 2026 placement window. The Head of A&R had, on March 4, delivered the artist-health scatter. The Head of Live had, on March 18, delivered the festival-season booking envelope. In the second week of April, a mid-tier indie-artist on the Tempo roster produced an organic viral moment on a short-video platform.

### What Akinyi-Martinez knew

The USD 1.2M indie-publishing catalog acquisition target was a pre-negotiated option that the seller had agreed to hold at the Q1 price through Q2 close. The film-franchise sync placement had a pre-qualified cue-sheet window across five titles on the Tempo roster. The festival-season booking envelope had three signed festivals at USD 380K–USD 520K each.

### What she did not know

Whether the viral moment would extend past 96 hours or extinguish; whether the regulatory publishing-rate cut would stretch to a 15% scope-extension in Q3 2026; whether the AI-generated-music flooding would accelerate beyond the 3% per-quarter depreciation signal; and whether the film-franchise sync placement would close at the list premium or be re-cut to a reduced bundle at 65% of list.

### What others were saying

**The Head of Artist Development**: *"Four hundred K in Q1. Six hundred K in Q3 after the AI flood. Roster stays at seventy plus."*

**The Head of Sync-and-Publishing**: *"Sync three in Q1. Sync five on the film franchise in Q3. The twelve-title bundle gets three years of halo."*

**The Head of Live**: *"One-two million on festivals in Q3. Reliable. Seasonal. Paid on time."*

**The Head of Publishing**: *"One-two million for the indie catalog. The viral multiplier is the option value. We hold Q1 and we deploy Q2."*

**The Board Chair**: *"In music, you don't own the charts. You own the decade-long annuity that outlives them."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Akinyi-Martinez had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Baseline-Dry-Powder / Opportunistic-Catalog-Acquisition / Festival-and-Sync:** the recommended envelope. Cumulative cash USD 8.2M, catalog USD 11.3M, roster 72, total profit USD 3.8M. Recommended.
- **Option B — Catalog-Front-Loaded / Marketing-Only / Defensive:** the up-front-catalog envelope. Cumulative cash USD 6.4M, catalog USD 12.0M, roster 66, total profit USD 2.2M. Rejected — Q2 viral-moment multiplier forfeited and roster-health drag.
- **Option C — Marketing-Forward / Defensive / Catalog-Double-Down:** the defensive-catalog envelope. Cumulative cash USD 5.8M, catalog USD 13.1M, roster 61, total profit USD 1.4M. Rejected — roster-health collapse and festival-and-sync cession.

Her private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the quarter most correlated with the cycle-read-and-rotate posture was Quarter 2 — the viral-moment response with the USD 1.2M catalog acquisition — because it was the quarter that decided whether Tempo deployed its dry powder at the industry's best buying multiplier (Option A) or spread the same USD 1.2M at a baseline multiplier across Q1 and Q3 (Options B and C).
