## Abstract

TERRAVIDA FOODS was, in the case universe, a Medellín-headquartered organic-food company with USD 580M in 2025 revenue, a 2025 EBITDA margin of 18%, a supply base of approximately 2,400 Colombian smallholder farmers across the Antioquia, Eje Cafetero and Valle del Cauca regions, a 2025 Ecosystem Regeneration Index (ERI) reading of 0 against the pan-LATAM regenerative-benchmark band of 45–65, a Farmer-Trust Index of 50, a Supply-Chain Resilience Score of 40, a Brand Score of 55, and a 2025 retail-and-export revenue split of 62% commercial channels, 26% export and 12% premium. The 2023–2025 operating model had been anchored on an organic-certification discipline that had delivered cost-of-goods predictability and an 18% EBITDA run-rate but had left the regenerative-premium window to a small cohort of European food-retail partners already moving from certified-organic to regenerative-certified procurement envelopes at a 12–22% price premium. The Q1 2026 board had approved a five-round CEO-led regenerative-business-model rebuild cycle against an ERI floor above 50, a Farmer-Trust floor above 70, a Resilience Score above 65, a Brand Score above 70, and a composite Regenerative Score above 68. The case opens at 5:42 a.m. on Tuesday, April 14, 2026, at TerraVida's El Poblado office as **Mateo Restrepo**, a 49-year-old CEO, prepares the Round-1 farmer-trust-foundation commitment. Seventy-three days separate him from the June 26 board regenerative-review.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Round-1 farmer-trust-foundation commitment (cooperative co-investment with shared-upside contract vs. top-down operational announcement) and diagnose when a co-investment envelope dominates a command envelope on the Farmer-Trust and regenerative-practice-adoption trade-off.
2. Analyze the Round-2 regenerative-practice-conversion commitment (phased-pilot across 400 lead-farmer cohort with supervised protocol vs. full-portfolio launch) and recommend the phased envelope against the full envelope on the Ecosystem Regeneration Index and adoption-rate basis.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 premium-channel-pricing commitment (European regenerative-retail-partner co-branded envelope at 18% premium vs. commodity channel) and recommend the premium envelope against the commodity envelope on the margin-durability basis.
4. Respond to the Round-4 brand-repositioning commitment (operating-model-first regenerative communications grounded in farmer-outcome metrics vs. values-statement press release) and recommend the operating-model envelope against the signalling envelope on the credibility and hiring-yield basis.
5. Design the Round-5 resilience-measurement commitment (farmer-level ERI reporting with third-party audit vs. aggregate-level self-reporting) and diagnose when the resilience metric becomes an operating discipline rather than a reporting artefact.

## The Protagonist

Mateo Restrepo, 49, had assumed the Chief-Executive-Officer role at TerraVida Foods on March 4, 2025, from a Tier-1 LATAM food-and-beverage group where he had, across eleven years, led two supply-chain transformations across Colombia and Ecuador, and had closed a 2021 farmer-cooperative rebuild in a 1,200-farmer coffee supply chain that delivered a Farmer-Trust Index uplift from 42 to 78 inside eighteen months. Before that he had spent six years as a Director of Sustainability at a Paris-based global food retailer. The board had recruited him specifically on two capabilities: his documented discipline of anchoring supplier transformations on cooperative co-investment rather than procurement-price signalling, and his willingness to defer commodity-channel volume to protect the premium-channel margin envelope. His 2026 variable compensation tied 30% to the ERI outcome and 25% to the Farmer-Trust floor.

## The Decision

Restrepo had to commit, by the May 7 board pre-review, the Round-1 envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2–5.

1. **Round 1 — Farmer-trust foundation.** Three archetypes: cooperative-co-investment (USD 8M in shared-upside three-year contracts across 2,400 farmers, 50% of premium captured above commodity-price floor flows back to farmers, on-the-ground field-officer cadence, Farmer-Trust +22 points, Composite 74), top-down-announcement (operational announcement plus commodity-price floor adjustment — Composite 32, trust-erosion risk), or partial-pilot (co-investment on 800-farmer cohort only — Composite 54). Working envelope: cooperative-co-investment.
2. **Round 2 — Regenerative-practice conversion.** Three archetypes: phased-pilot (400-lead-farmer cohort with supervised regenerative-practice conversion over nine months, ERI +28 on pilot cohort, cross-pollination to full cohort by Year 2, Composite 72), full-portfolio-launch (all 2,400 farmers asked to convert in Year 1 — Composite 42, adoption-drop risk), or voluntary-opt-in (protocol published, adoption elective — Composite 56). Working envelope: phased-pilot.
3. **Round 3 — Premium-channel pricing.** Three vectors: premium-channel-co-branded (European regenerative-retail-partner envelope, co-branded SKU, 18% price premium, minimum offtake on 60% of regenerative-certified volume, Composite 76), commodity-channel (sell into commodity organic channel with 4% premium — Composite 42, margin dilution), or mixed (50% commodity / 50% premium — Composite 58). Working envelope: premium-channel-co-branded.
4. **Round 4 — Brand repositioning.** Three vectors: operating-model-framing (communications anchored on farmer-outcome metrics, ERI disclosure, audited co-investment accounts — Composite 74), values-statement (press release on regenerative commitment without farmer-outcome metrics — Composite 34, greenwashing risk), or quiet-operation (no repositioning communications — Composite 56). Working envelope: operating-model-framing.
5. **Round 5 — Resilience measurement.** Three vectors: farmer-level-ERI (farmer-level ERI reporting on a quarterly cadence with third-party audit, Resilience Score +26 points, Composite 78), aggregate-self-reporting (aggregate-level self-reported ERI annually — Composite 42, credibility gap), or sample-audit (annual 100-farmer sample audit without farmer-level reporting — Composite 58). Working envelope: farmer-level-ERI.

On Restrepo's working model, a Cooperative-Co-Investment / Phased-Pilot / Premium-Channel / Operating-Model-Framing / Farmer-Level-ERI envelope delivered ERI 56, Farmer Trust 74, Resilience Score 68, Brand Score 72, Composite 74. A Top-Down / Full-Portfolio / Commodity / Values-Statement / Aggregate-Self-Reporting envelope delivered ERI 14, Farmer Trust 34, Resilience 38, Brand 42, Composite 32. A Partial-Pilot / Voluntary-Opt-In / Mixed / Quiet-Operation / Sample-Audit envelope delivered ERI 32, Farmer Trust 54, Resilience 52, Brand 54, Composite 52.

## Company Background

TerraVida Foods had been founded in Medellín in 2008 as a single-SKU organic-coffee exporter and had, across eighteen years, grown into a USD 580M organic-food group with eleven categories (coffee, cacao, tropical-fruit puree, plantain, avocado, quinoa, tropical vegetables, sugar, dairy, honey, and packaged breakfast mixes), 2,400 smallholder farmers across three Colombian regions, and three manufacturing facilities in Medellín, Pereira and Cali. The 2023–2025 operating model had been anchored on organic-certification discipline that had delivered an 18% EBITDA margin and a USD 580M revenue run-rate but had seen the 2024 Q2 farmer-satisfaction survey place the Farmer-Trust Index at 50 against a pan-LATAM cooperative-agriculture benchmark of 65.

The Q1 2026 board dossier had identified three specific pressures the five-round envelope would inherit. First, the November 2025 European-retail-partner briefing had signalled that three of TerraVida's top-five retail accounts would shift 40% of their organic-food procurement to regenerative-certified envelopes by 2028 at a 12–22% price-premium — a commercial window the commodity-organic channel would not access. Second, the Q4 2025 Colombian-smallholder-income-volatility report had documented that 34% of TerraVida-supplying farmers had registered income-shock events inside the 2023–2025 horizon, a pattern that fed directly into the Farmer-Trust reading. Third, the Q1 2026 Chief Agronomy Officer's field review had flagged that the bulk of the ERI gap was anchored on soil-health and water-retention practice that could only be converted under a supervised phased-pilot design, not a portfolio-wide directive.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM regenerative-agriculture landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2018–2024 cooperative-co-investment evidence base had, across 42 smallholder supply chains, documented a Farmer-Trust differential of 18–26 points between co-investment envelopes and top-down envelopes, with the bulk of the effect routing through shared-upside contracts. Second, the 2020–2024 phased-pilot regenerative-conversion literature had, across 38 tropical supply chains, placed phased-pilot envelopes at a twelve-month ERI uplift of 24–32 points against full-portfolio-launch envelopes — with an adoption-rate differential of 42 points. Third, the 2022–2024 premium-retail regenerative-certification literature had, across 28 European retail partnerships, placed co-branded envelopes at a price-premium capture of 14–22 points against commodity channels.

Empirically, the post-2022 regenerative literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, regenerative programmes that had anchored on farmer-level ERI reporting with third-party audit had, across a 2019–2024 sample of 26 programmes, preserved Resilience Score above 65 in 84% of cases against 26% for programmes on aggregate self-reporting. Second, regenerative programmes that had framed external communications around farmer-outcome metrics rather than values statements had sustained Brand Score above 70 in 78% of cases against 24% for programmes that had issued values-statement releases without operating-model disclosure.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The board had, in the October 2025 sustainability review, approved the regenerative mandate and the June 26 review calendar. The Chief Agronomy Officer had, on February 4, delivered the soil-health and water-retention diagnostic. The Head of Commercial had, on February 18, delivered the European-retail-partner premium envelope. The Chief Financial Officer had, on March 2, modelled the USD 8M co-investment envelope against the 18% EBITDA floor. The Head of Farmer Relations had, on March 14, delivered the Farmer-Trust baseline. The Chief Communications Officer had, on March 21, delivered the operating-model-framing draft.

### What Restrepo knew

The team had modelled three five-round envelopes. A Cooperative-Co-Investment / Phased-Pilot / Premium-Channel / Operating-Model-Framing / Farmer-Level-ERI Envelope projected Composite 74. A Top-Down / Full-Portfolio / Commodity / Values-Statement / Aggregate-Self-Reporting Envelope projected Composite 32. A Partial-Pilot / Voluntary-Opt-In / Mixed / Quiet-Operation / Sample-Audit Envelope projected Composite 52.

### What he did not know

Whether the 400-lead-farmer cohort would, across the first nine months of the regenerative-practice conversion, sustain the supervised protocol under a 2026 Colombian dry-season shock; whether the European retail-partner co-branded envelope would survive a 2026 H2 consumer-price-elasticity compression; whether the Farmer-Trust reading would hold across the commodity-price volatility band embedded in the 2026 Colombian coffee and cacao futures markets; and whether the farmer-level ERI reporting would be sustainable across 2,400 farmers without expanding field-officer headcount beyond the envelope.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Agronomy Officer**: *"Phased-pilot with the four hundred lead farmers. A full-portfolio launch at 2,400 will collapse by month six."*

**The Head of Farmer Relations**: *"Cooperative-co-investment with shared upside. They will not convert on our operational memo."*

**The Head of Commercial**: *"Premium-channel co-branded at eighteen percent. Commodity organic at four percent is not regenerative."*

**The Chief Executive**: *"Regeneration runs through the farmer, not the press release."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Restrepo had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — Cooperative-Co-Investment / Phased-Pilot / Premium-Channel / Operating-Model-Framing / Farmer-Level-ERI:** the recommended envelope. ERI 56. Farmer Trust 74. Resilience 68. Brand 72. Composite 74. Recommended.
- **Option B — Top-Down / Full-Portfolio / Commodity / Values-Statement / Aggregate-Self-Reporting:** the status-quo envelope. Composite 32. Rejected — Farmer-Trust and ERI erosion.
- **Option C — Partial-Pilot / Voluntary-Opt-In / Mixed / Quiet-Operation / Sample-Audit:** the partial envelope. Composite 52. Rejected — forfeits the ERI floor and the premium-channel margin.

Restrepo's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final Composite was Round 1 — the cooperative-co-investment foundation — because it was the round that unlocked farmer-adoption of the regenerative-practice conversion and preserved the Farmer-Trust reading under which every downstream round operated.
