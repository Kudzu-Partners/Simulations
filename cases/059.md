## Abstract

Banco Ibérico Financial Group was, in the case universe, a Madrid-headquartered Spanish financial-services group with retail-banking, corporate-banking, and asset-management operations across Spain, Portugal, and selected Latin-American markets, 2025 group revenue of EUR 14.8B, and fourteen in-production AI systems spanning fraud detection, loan approval, hiring screening, customer-service routing, anti-money-laundering tiering, and collections. On Monday, June 2, 2025, in the wake of the EU AI Act's February 2025 entry into force and the August 2, 2026 compliance deadline for high-risk systems, the Board approved the creation of a Chief AI Governance Officer (CAIGO) position. Dr. Carmen Risueño, previously Head of Model Risk, was appointed to the role, with a team of four and a first-year budget of EUR 800K. The case opens at 7:30 a.m. on Monday, June 9, 2025, as Risueño prepares the Round-1 governance-inventory decision for the June 23 Board Audit Committee. Five rounds would unfold across a fourteen-month window, ending at the August 2026 EU AI Act compliance deadline and concurrent with a formal AESIA (Agencia Española de Supervisión de la Inteligencia Artificial) investigation on the loan-approval audit.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the EU AI Act risk-tier classification across fourteen in-production AI systems and diagnose the two business-unit-leader pushback cases on the loan-approval systems.
2. Analyze the proxy-discrimination pattern in a fraud-detection model that flags immigrant postal codes without using postal code directly and recommend a fairness-aware retraining protocol.
3. Diagnose the board-disclosure decision on the 16,800-credit-file loan-approval audit and recommend an AESIA self-disclosure timeline.
4. Design a governance architecture selecting eight of twelve components (Ethics Committee, ISO 42001, Impact Assessment, Fairness Testing, SHAP Explainability, Incident Response, Speak-Up Channel, External Advisory Panel, Transparency Report, Developer Certification, Opportunity Register, Continuous Monitoring).
5. Recommend a complete Round-1 package and identify the single Round-5 AESIA-investigation response decision most likely to preserve the cooperative-disclosure classification.

On the morning of **Monday, June 9, 2025**, **Dr. Carmen Risueño** sat at the desk of her new office on the fourteenth floor of the Banco Ibérico headquarters on Paseo de la Castellana and opened the governance-inventory template her team had drafted the previous Friday. The EU AI Act compliance deadline was fourteen months away. The Board Audit Committee meeting was two weeks away. The governance-maturity score in the internal diagnostic the Board had commissioned was 1 out of 10.

---

## The Protagonist

Dr. Carmen Risueño, 48, had been at Banco Ibérico for fourteen years, most recently as Head of Model Risk from 2022 to June 2025, where she had, across three annual cycles, led the internal validation and monitoring of twenty-eight statistical and machine-learning models across retail banking. A computer-science graduate of Universidad Politécnica de Madrid with a doctorate in machine learning from ETH Zürich, she had published, in 2019, a co-authored paper on fairness-aware credit-scoring models that had been cited in two subsequent European Commission working papers during the drafting of the EU AI Act. The CAIGO mandate — which the Board had approved on May 27, 2025 — was, on the internal organization chart, dotted-line to the Chief Executive and solid-line to the Chair of the Board Audit Committee.

---

## The Decision

Risueño had to commit, by 9:00 a.m. on Monday, June 23, the Round-1 governance-inventory package the Board Audit Committee would treat as the fourteen-month compliance baseline:

1. **Governance Inventory** — for each of the fourteen in-production AI systems, a classification as High-Risk, Limited-Risk, or Minimal-Risk under the EU AI Act Article 6 risk-tier framework.
2. **Business-Unit-Leader Response** — posture on the two loan-approval-system classifications on which the Retail Banking and SME Banking business-unit heads had pushed back.
3. **Board-Escalation Package** — accompanying memo and audit-calendar proposal.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across five rounds. A classification of eight high-risk systems (including the two contested loan-approval systems), three limited-risk, and three minimal-risk — the classification the internal model-risk team's technical review supported — committed the CAIGO team to a Round-4 governance-architecture spend at the high end of the EUR 800K budget but pre-empted the Round-5 AESIA investigation's exposure by a factor of four on the regulatory-penalty expected-value math.

## Company Background

Banco Ibérico Financial Group had been founded through the 2001 merger of two Spanish regional banks and had, through two decades of acquisition-led expansion, built the group the case studied. 2025 group revenue was EUR 14.8B, with net income of EUR 3.2B, Tier-1 capital at 13.8%, and a group-wide employee count of 42,400. The group's machine-learning and AI deployment had, between 2019 and 2024, scaled from four models to fourteen in-production systems; the 2023 internal digital-transformation program had specifically prioritized the fraud-detection model (deployed across all retail and SME channels in Q3 2023) and the loan-approval systems (Retail Banking deployed Q2 2022, SME Banking deployed Q4 2023) as strategic priorities.

The AESIA — the Spanish AI supervisory authority — had been established under the 2023 Spanish Royal Decree 729/2023 as the national designated authority under the EU AI Act. AESIA had, since early 2025, corresponded with all Spanish financial-services groups on the Article 6 high-risk-system inventory. The June 2025 diagnostic the Board had commissioned — which placed governance maturity at 1 out of 10 — had been delivered on May 14, 2025, and had flagged three specific exposures: (a) no formal AI risk-management system; (b) no documented impact-assessment process; (c) no continuous monitoring or post-market-surveillance for any of the fourteen systems.

## Industry and Market Context

The European financial-services AI-governance landscape in 2025 was characterized by three structural features the Round-1 decision sat inside. First, the EU AI Act had entered into force on February 2, 2025; the prohibited-practices and AI-literacy obligations had been immediately binding; the high-risk-system obligations (Annex III) had an August 2, 2026 compliance deadline, including risk-management, data-governance, technical-documentation, transparency, human-oversight, accuracy-robustness-cybersecurity, quality-management, conformity-assessment, and post-market-monitoring. Second, the penalty regime established by Article 99 set the ceiling at EUR 35M or 7% of total worldwide turnover for prohibited-practices violations, EUR 15M or 3% for most high-risk-system violations, and EUR 7.5M or 1% for supplying incorrect information; in financial-services parallels (GDPR, MiFID II), cooperative disclosure had historically reduced the effective penalty by 60%–80% of the ceiling. Third, the algorithmic-fairness literature had converged on a finding: proxy discrimination — the use of features that correlate with protected characteristics without using the protected characteristic directly — had been the largest single source of post-deployment discrimination complaints in European financial services between 2021 and 2024, with fraud-detection and loan-approval systems producing 61% of the incident sample.

Empirically, the AI-governance-architecture research had two robust findings. First, governance architectures that included Board AI Ethics Committee with external members, ISO 42001-aligned AI Risk Management System, mandatory Impact Assessment, Fairness Testing, SHAP-based Explainability, and Continuous Model Monitoring had, in the 2023–2025 European financial-services cohort, passed initial regulatory scrutiny in 92% of observed cases; architectures missing any three of these six components had passed in 41%. Second, self-disclosure to a supervisor within thirty business days of an internally-detected model-risk incident had reduced penalty exposure, across seven observed European cases, by a measured factor of 4.2× on average.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Board Audit Committee had received the May 14, 2025 diagnostic and had, on May 27, 2025, approved the CAIGO position with a specific instruction: "EU AI Act compliance by the August 2026 deadline, by any reasonable means." Risueño had accepted the appointment on June 2 and had, in her first week, conducted one-on-one interviews with the Chief Risk Officer, the Chief Technology Officer, the Heads of Retail Banking and SME Banking, and the Chair of the Audit Committee. The four-person CAIGO team — one lead data-scientist, one legal-and-compliance specialist, one model-risk analyst, and one coordinator — had, in the week before the case opens, drafted the fourteen-system inventory and the proposed classifications.

### What Risueño knew

The internal team had drafted three Round-1 governance-inventory packages. A Conservative Package (six high-risk, four limited-risk, four minimal-risk — reflecting the two business-unit-leader-pushback loan-approval systems being reclassified to limited-risk) projected Round-5 AESIA-investigation-penalty exposure EUR 18M–22M. A Balanced Package (eight high-risk, three limited-risk, three minimal-risk — reflecting the technical risk tiers without business-unit accommodation) projected Round-5 exposure EUR 4M–8M under cooperative-disclosure, EUR 28M–35M under a non-cooperative path. An Aggressive Package (nine high-risk, three limited-risk, two minimal-risk — reflecting a stricter reading of a borderline customer-service-routing system) projected similar Round-5 exposure to the Balanced Package but higher Round-4 governance-architecture cost (EUR 1.1M against the EUR 800K budget).

### What she did not know

Whether the Immigrant-Community Association of Catalonia (disguised here as AIC) — which, on the internal-regulatory calendar, was anticipated to file a formal fairness complaint on the fraud-detection model in the Round-2 window — would do so in June or delay to September; whether the loan-approval-system audit that the model-risk team would run in Round 3 would produce an impacted-customer count in the 12,000–18,000 range (base case on the internal model) or in the 22,000–28,000 range (downside case) that would materially change the Board's disclosure-versus-containment posture; whether the CEO — who had, in the Round-4 expected timeline, commissioned a competitive-positioning review that would press the CAIGO to relax the fairness-retraining schedule — would sign or reject the AESIA-disclosure pack at Round 5; and whether Banco Castellano, the competitor named in the internal-strategy deck, would launch the "instant AI credit decisions" product inside the Round-5 window.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Risk Officer**: *"Balanced Package. Eight high-risk. Do not accommodate the business-unit-leader pushback on the loan-approval systems. The pushback is a comfort-seeking signal, not a technical position."*

**Head of Retail Banking**: *"The retail loan-approval system is not Annex III high-risk. It does not evaluate creditworthiness in the regulatory-test sense. I will make my case to the Audit Committee."*

**The Chair of the Audit Committee**: *"The classification is the CAIGO's call. The Audit Committee supports the technical recommendation. Document the business-unit-leader-pushback in the memo. Do not put it in the classification."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Risueño had narrowed the space to three Round-1 packages.

- **Option A — Conservative Accommodation:** Six high-risk, four limited-risk, four minimal-risk. Round-5 AESIA-investigation penalty exposure EUR 18M–22M. Accommodates business-unit-leader pushback. Under-classifies on technical risk tiers.

- **Option B — Balanced Technical:** Eight high-risk, three limited-risk, three minimal-risk. Round-5 exposure under cooperative-disclosure EUR 4M–8M. Aligns with the technical risk tiers and the Chief Risk Officer's position.

- **Option C — Aggressive Over-Classification:** Nine high-risk, three limited-risk, two minimal-risk. Round-5 exposure under cooperative-disclosure EUR 4M–8M. Higher Round-4 governance-architecture cost at EUR 1.1M.

Risueño's private view, not yet shared with the Head of Retail Banking, was that Option B was the right Round-1 package and that the single subsequent decision most correlated with the Round-5 outcome was the Round-3 disclosure-versus-containment posture on the 16,800-credit-file loan-approval audit — specifically, the Board's approval of full AESIA self-disclosure within thirty days of the audit-finding-date.
