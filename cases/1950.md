# Pragati's Office of Research Translation: Nine Crore and Four Inventions

**Case Code:** ORG-2026-1950 · **Companion simulation:** Express 1950 — *Identity at the Crossroads* · **v1.0 · August 2026**

---

On the morning of Friday, May 15, 2026, Anirudh Deshpande found two letters and four folders waiting at the Office of Research Translation. The first was from the Dean of Research: the office was to show a threefold increase in commercialization outcomes within eighteen months, on a flat budget of ₹9 crore (Indian rupees; one crore, ten million), or be restructured. The second was from Kaveri Biologics, which held a biotechnology licence signed in 2019 — the single agreement producing roughly ₹2.2 crore a year in running royalties and 80% of every rupee the office had ever earned. Kaveri wanted the royalty reopened and said it was prepared to walk. The four folders were invention dossiers asking for ₹16 crore between them.

Deshpande had eighteen months and ₹9 crore. In that time he had to decide which of the four inventions the office would back and on what terms, settle the Kaveri renegotiation, and answer a question his own staff had begun asking out loud: what the Office of Research Translation was for.

## The Protagonist

Deshpande, 46, was a materials scientist who had left the laboratory in 2016 to join the office as licensing manager and had directed it since 2021. He had negotiated the Kaveri agreement himself; it was the only deal anyone outside Pragati had heard of, and the reason its revenue rested on one patent. His appointment came up for renewal in front of the Dean who had written the memo; his standing with faculty rested on ten years of not being the man who sold their work.

## The Decision

Deshpande had to place ₹9 crore against ₹16 crore of stated need, name the form each commercialization relationship would take, resolve the Kaveri licence, and hold one account of the office's purpose in front of three audiences that wanted different things from it. Two of the four dossiers carried funding decisions due inside the current cycle, and one of the inventors had said she would not wait at all.

## The Company and its Setting

The Office of Research Translation opened in 2014 inside Pragati Institute of Technology, a public-private research university founded in Pune in 1962. Pragati ran about ₹410 crore of sponsored research a year across 620 research faculty and filed roughly 90 patents a year. Eleven people worked in the office. In twelve years it had taken in 240 disclosed inventions, licensed 18, and earned ₹14 crore, four fifths from Kaveri.

The research faculty valued open publication and inventions that reached the public; the Dean and the institute's industry partners counted licences signed, spin-outs incorporated and royalties banked. Both sat on the review committee. Conditions outside were thin: early-stage risk capital willing to wait out a materials-development cycle was scarce in the region, and the proof-of-concept funds and specialist patent firms a comparable office in Boston would have used were largely absent.

## The Situation

The review would score four numbers, and the planning unit had fixed where each stood on May 15 (see **Exhibit 1**). The first was a commercialization index that set the office's historical rate of licences, options and spin-outs at 100; the memo asked for about 300. Two internal indices, kept since 2022, followed: one scored how consistently the office's public commitments described a single office rather than two, and stood at 50 out of 100; the other, from an annual faculty survey on willingness to disclose and cooperate, stood at 55 out of 100. The fourth number was money — projected running royalty, ₹2.2 crore a year, all of it Kaveri's.

The four folders were the ones the office had graded fundable that spring (see **Exhibit 2**): a clean-energy materials programme on a four-to-five-year horizon; a software tool licensable inside six months, which two senior faculty had called easy money in a senate meeting; a medical device carrying a strategic investor's offer, conditional on a global exclusive licence and a seat on the board that set the programme's research agenda; and the prosecution and filing budget for the other 236 disclosures, which decided whether an inventor got a filing or a letter of regret.

Professor Sharmila Rane held the clean-energy patent. In April she had told the office that if it commercialized her work "the wrong way" she would submit the paper to a journal and let the patent lapse into the public domain. The manuscript was written. Deshpande had one meeting with her scheduled and no agreement.

If the office changed nothing, the arithmetic was not in dispute. The commercialization index would stand near 100 at the review, nothing new having been licensed. Kaveri would take the lower royalty it had asked for or leave, and either way the ₹2.2 crore would fall. The 236 disclosures without a filing decision would keep lapsing. The memo named the consequence.

## What Deshpande Controlled

He controlled four things, in sequence.

**The account of the office.** Four descriptions were on offer: one that held the academic and the commercial expectation together and managed the friction; one that was a commercial engine, whose revenue, spin-outs and exclusivity were the condition of survival; one that was first a steward of open inquiry, whose commercial work stopped where the mission began; and one that treated the mandate as a reporting problem, reaching the number by whatever route produced it. He also named the claim that best answered both memo and description: a pair, one long-horizon and one near-term, sequenced by time to first revenue; the clean-energy programme alone; the software tool alone; or the device deal alone. And he set the share of the office's effort spent keeping Rane inside the process, at any level from none to all of it.

**The form of each relationship.** On the device: the investor's terms, a global exclusive licence and the board seat, for ₹5 crore; a negotiated version, exclusivity limited by field of use and an observer seat, for less cash; a non-exclusive licence open to any manufacturer, which the investor was unlikely to accept; or no device deal this cycle. On the clean-energy programme: a spin-out, upside and control inside Pragati, funding and management on the office; a sponsored-research collaboration, publication open for a modest royalty; an exclusive licence to an established energy company, paying soonest; or no funding this cycle. On the software: a non-exclusive licence to several vendors; an exclusive licence to one; a spin-out run by the students and faculty who wrote it; or no deal.

**The money and the Kaveri position.** He would split ₹9 crore across the four lines in half-crore steps, totalling exactly ₹9 crore, nothing carried forward. On Kaveri: hold the existing terms and accept that the licensee might leave; concede a lower royalty and keep them; concede modestly and add field-of-use sublicensing; or let the licence lapse and rebuild the revenue base from the new portfolio.

**The review.** He would give the Dean, a senate representative and the investor one position in the same words: an office that commercialized in service of the mission and said where the two pulled apart; one that defended the revenue and the deals and told faculty the numbers were fixed; one that defended the mission and told the Dean commercialization would wait for acceptable terms; or one that gave each audience the account that suited it and left its position unstated. He would close out Rane by co-designing the agreement so that publication rights, co-authorship and a public-access clause survived it; by invoking the intellectual-property policy and requiring her to assign the patent; by proceeding with the plan as drafted and taking the paper as it came; or by letting her publish, giving up the patent and keeping the relationship. And he would name the ramp committed for the eighteen months, from no increase at all to the full threefold.

## Closing

At a quarter past nine Deshpande wrote the four lines — clean energy, device, software, pipeline — and ruled two columns beside them, one for the money and one for the form of the deal. Rane's meeting was on Tuesday, the review eighteen months out. The first column had to add to ₹9 crore and the second to a single account of what this office was.
