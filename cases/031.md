## Abstract

Nexus Technologies, a São Paulo-headquartered enterprise SaaS company with 512 employees and R$ 480M in annualized revenue, carried six warning lights on its people dashboard at the start of 2026: a Glassdoor score of 3.4, an eNPS of 18, a voluntary turnover rate of 18.4%, an employer-brand score of 55, a capability composite of 62 against a sector median of 74, and — the item only four people in the company knew — a cash runway of fourteen months against a board-mandated twenty-four-month floor. Helena Aragão, the new Chief People Officer, arrived on April 7, 2026, with a four-quarter budget of R$ 50M, a single mandate ("fix the people operating system before the funding cliff fires"), and a decision that had to land on the CEO's Monday desk: the Q1 talent-acquisition package, expressed as four choices — employer value proposition, selection depth, onboarding design, and the share of the R$ 2.4M Q1 budget routed to high-quality channels. Each choice compounded into Quarters 2 through 4. The case ends at the eve of her first executive committee presentation.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Diagnose a people-system failure across talent acquisition, capability, and retention using a four-pillar dashboard.
2. Evaluate the trade-off between selection depth (time-to-fill) and hire quality under competitor pressure on the same talent pool.
3. Analyze the compounding effect of Quarter-1 employer-brand investment on Quarter-3 retention and Quarter-4 survivor engagement during a workforce reduction.
4. Design a quarter-by-quarter people operating system that treats capability as a cumulative asset, not a training line item.
5. Recommend a Q1 package for Nexus and articulate the falsifiable assumptions that would force a re-tune at Q2.

On the evening of **Sunday, April 12, 2026**, **Helena Aragão** sat in her home office in Pinheiros and looked, for the fourth time, at the single-page brief her Head of People Analytics had emailed her at 6:41 p.m. The brief carried six numbers in red and one number in black. In red: *Glassdoor 3.4. eNPS 18. Voluntary turnover 18.4%. Brand 55. Capability 62. Time-to-fill (engineering) 48 days.* In black, at the bottom: *Runway 14 months. Board floor 24. Four people know.* She had until 8:30 a.m. Monday to present the Quarter 1 people package to the Executive Committee. The all-hands was Thursday.

---

## The Protagonist

Helena Aragão, 46, had been Chief People Officer of Nexus Technologies for six days. She had spent eleven years at a global enterprise-software company where she had built the CHRO function across three Latin American regions, and four years before that as Head of Talent at a Brazilian growth-stage fintech that was now publicly listed. She held an undergraduate degree in psychology from PUC-SP and an executive MBA from Fundação Dom Cabral. Her reputation inside the Brazilian tech community was for refusing to treat "people stuff" as a soft function: in her last role she had co-authored, with the CFO, the internal paper that retired the company's headcount-planning model in favor of a capability-coverage model. Her contract at Nexus included a clause tying a portion of her long-term compensation to a composite people-KPI index evaluated at the end of the four quarters.

---

## The Decision

Aragão had to set, by 8:30 a.m. Monday, the Quarter 1 package: four decisions that the simulation would treat as Round 1 inputs.

1. **Employer Value Proposition.** Four framings were on the table: Innovation Leader, Best Workplace, High Growth/High Reward, Purpose-Driven. Each drew a different candidate mix and moved the employer-brand score differently.
2. **Selection Depth.** Three levels: Fast Screen (two interviews), Moderate (four interviews), Deep (six interviews plus work sample). Fast Screen minimized time-to-fill but degraded hire quality at the 90-day checkpoint; Deep did the opposite and risked losing candidates to GLORYLABS and two other competitors who had qualified offers inside 14 days.
3. **Onboarding Design.** Three models: 30-Day Standard, 60-Day Structured, 90-Day Immersive. The 60-Day Structured model was the only one that had been empirically tied, in the internal 2025 cohort study, to both a six-month retention lift and a first-year productivity delta above the Nexus mean.
4. **High-Quality Channel Budget.** The share of the Q1 R$ 2.4M acquisition budget routed to referrals, university partnerships, and executive-search for senior roles — versus generic job-board spend. The internal model priced the high-quality-channel yield at roughly 2.3x the generic channel on a 12-month retention basis.

The four decisions would compound. A strong Q1 brand lift lowered the marginal cost of Q2 capability hires; a degraded brand compounded the opposite way into Q3 retention and Q4 survivor engagement if the runway event fired.

## Company Background

Nexus Technologies was founded in 2018 by two former employees of a regional payments company to build a vertical-SaaS platform for mid-market Brazilian industrial distributors. By the end of 2025 the company had 512 employees across engineering (172), product (44), sales (96), customer success (78), marketing (36), operations (52), and corporate (34), with 88% in São Paulo and the remainder split between Belo Horizonte and a small Buenos Aires office. Annualized revenue for December 2025 was R$ 480M with a gross margin of 71% and an operating loss of R$ 58M. Net revenue retention was 112%. The customer base of 680 paying accounts included three of the ten largest Brazilian industrial distributors.

Nexus had raised R$ 280M across Series A (2021), B (2023), and C (2024) from a mix of São Paulo-based venture firms, a São Paulo-based corporate-venture arm, and a single U.S.-based growth fund that led the Series C. Cash at April 1, 2026, was R$ 112M, implying a run-rate burn of approximately R$ 8M per month and a runway of fourteen months. The board's long-standing floor for acceptable runway was twenty-four months. Between January and March 2026 the CFO had modeled, privately, a 20% workforce reduction that would push runway to twenty-four months at a recurring-cost level acceptable to the Series C lead.

The people function at Nexus had, until Aragão's arrival, been run by an interim Head of People who reported into the COO. There was no standing People Analytics capability (the data existed in a scattered set of spreadsheets), no defined Employer Value Proposition, no enterprise learning platform, and no compensation benchmark process beyond an annual pulse the CFO ran in February.

## Industry and Market Context

The Brazilian enterprise-SaaS labor market entered 2026 under three simultaneous pressures. First, engineering compensation had compounded at approximately 14% per year for the 2–4-year tenure segment — the segment from which Nexus had lost 31% of its software engineers in 2025. Second, a small set of regional competitors — most visibly GLORYLABS and two others — had adopted a 14-day offer cycle that compressed the recruiting calendar industry-wide and punished firms with multi-round processes. Third, the post-2023 normalization of hybrid work had, in the Brazilian market, stabilized at 2–3 days in office as the median expectation for senior-IC engineers; Nexus, on a 4-day-in-office policy, was at the strict end of its peer set.

Capability benchmarks available to Aragão showed a sector median composite of 74 across six capability areas (AI/ML, enterprise sales, data governance, product management, people management, and customer success). Nexus ranked below median on four of the six, with AI/ML (54) and people management (48) as the two weakest. Seventy percent of the empirical engagement variance in Brazilian tech firms was, in the Fundação Dom Cabral 2025 study, attributable to direct-manager quality — the pillar on which Nexus was weakest.

## The Situation

### How we got here

Nexus had hired aggressively through 2022 and 2023, running a 12-month growth cycle that prioritized headcount over onboarding rigor. The result by late 2025 was a brand score that trailed competitor NovaScale by 22 points and a Glassdoor score that had crossed below 3.5 in November. The board, in its December 2025 meeting, had approved the creation of a CPO role at the C-level. Aragão had been the preferred candidate from the first day of the search.

### What Aragão knew

The internal analytics team had modeled three Q1 packages against a 12-month forward KPI set. An Innovation Leader EVP combined with Moderate selection depth, 60-Day Structured onboarding, and 55% of the Q1 budget into high-quality channels lifted the brand from 55 to 62 and dropped engineering time-to-fill from 48 to 34 days. A Best Workplace EVP with Deep selection depth, 90-Day Immersive onboarding, and 70% into high-quality channels lifted the brand to 66 and time-to-fill to 56 days — at the cost of a projected 8% of senior-engineering candidates lost to competitor offers. A High Growth/High Reward EVP with Fast Screen, 30-Day onboarding, and 40% into high-quality channels lifted the brand to 59 at a time-to-fill of 24 days but cost the 90-day retention two percentage points.

### What she did not know

Whether the U.S.-based Series C lead would accelerate the workforce-reduction conversation from the expected Q3 conversation to a Q2 conversation; whether the three engineers on the currently-open Staff-level requisition would accept offers from GLORYLABS already in hand; and whether the cohort of 2-to-4-year-tenure software engineers (the segment carrying 31% turnover) would read any Q1 announcement as a "new era" worth staying for, or as a cosmetic rebrand worth ignoring.

### What others were saying

**Rafael Nogueira**, CEO and co-founder, argued for the **Innovation Leader EVP**: "We are the most technically ambitious company in Brazilian enterprise SaaS. The brand story has to match the product. Pick Innovation Leader. Make Q1 loud."

**Carolina Ramos**, CFO, argued for the **conservative pace**: "We cannot afford a 90-Day Immersive onboarding until we know what the runway conversation looks like. Moderate selection. 60-Day onboarding. 55% into high-quality channels at most. Hold the line."

**Lucas de Almeida**, VP of Engineering, argued for **Deep selection**: "Every bad hire costs me four months and a team. Pick Deep. Lose the candidates you have to lose. The best ones wait."

---

## Options Under Consideration

Aragão had narrowed the space to three concrete Q1 packages.

- **Option A — Innovation Leader + Moderate + 60-Day + 55% (the Nogueira-Ramos midpoint):** EVP Innovation Leader, four-interview process, 60-Day Structured onboarding, 55% of R$ 2.4M into referrals and university partnerships. Projected end-Q1 brand score 62. Time-to-fill 34 days. 90-day retention +3pp versus 2025 cohort. Capability score unchanged in Q1.

- **Option B — Best Workplace + Deep + 90-Day + 70% (the de Almeida plan):** EVP Best Workplace, six-interview process plus work sample, 90-Day Immersive onboarding, 70% of R$ 2.4M into referrals and executive search. Projected end-Q1 brand score 66. Time-to-fill 56 days. 90-day retention +6pp. Risk: 8% of senior-engineering candidates lost to competitor speed.

- **Option C — High Growth/High Reward + Fast Screen + 30-Day + 40% (the speed play):** EVP High Growth/High Reward, two-interview process, 30-Day Standard onboarding, 40% of R$ 2.4M into high-quality channels. Projected end-Q1 brand score 59. Time-to-fill 24 days. 90-day retention −2pp. Compensation-expectation inflation risk from the Q2 cohort onwards.

Aragão's own instinct, not yet shared with the Executive Committee, was that Option A was defensible to the CEO and Option B was defensible to the VP of Engineering — and that the real question was not which option scored highest in Q1 but which option most raised the probability that the Q4 workforce reduction, if it fired, would land on a company with Trust intact. She had not yet told any of the three advocates that she had seen the runway number.

---

She closed the laptop at 10:47 p.m. The CEO would be in the office at 7:30. The Thursday all-hands was the first moment the 512 employees would hear her voice. She had written, in her notebook, a single line that she intended to quote in the presentation: *"Headcount is a number. Capability is a system."* The presentation was, for now, seventeen words long.
