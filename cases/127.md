## Abstract

LUMINARY SOFTWARE was, in the case universe, a Series-C venture-backed SaaS operator headquartered in San Francisco, with USD 210M in committed capital, a 2025 Annual Recurring Revenue run-rate of USD 48M against a plan of USD 50.4M (a Q3 miss of minus five percent against plan), a net-revenue-retention rate of 118%, and a gross-margin trajectory compressed by a rising AI-inference-cost line that had, across 2023–2025, moved the cost-of-goods-sold ratio from 18% to 26% on the ARR book. On Monday, February 2, 2026, the Board had, at its Q4 review, requested a three-format communication architecture for the week of February 23: an Executive Summary for the Board itself, an All-Hands for the 340 employees, and a Message House for a scheduled interview with a Tier-1 business publication whose reporter had, in a two-year book, placed three prior pieces on LUMINARY SOFTWARE. The case opens at 5:46 a.m. on Tuesday, February 17, 2026, at the LUMINARY SOFTWARE San Francisco headquarters as **Silvina Keller**, a 44-year-old Vice President of Corporate Communications, prepares the Round-1 board-executive-summary commitment — six days separate her from the February 23 Board meeting and eleven days separate her from the March 3 Tier-1 business-publication interview.

On the morning of **Tuesday, February 17, 2026**, **Silvina Keller** arrived at the Luminary Software San Francisco headquarters on Folsom Street at 5:46 a.m., opened the Round-1 board-executive-summary sheet her Director of Internal Communications and her Head of External Communications had finalised at 11:18 p.m. the night before, and re-read the sentence she had taped to her office whiteboard since her 2017 first-crisis-communications cycle: *"Same numbers, three rooms, one truth. That's storytelling."* Six days separated her from the February 23 Board meeting.

## The Protagonist

Silvina Keller, 44, had joined Luminary Software in September 2022 as Vice President of Corporate Communications from a Series-D SaaS operator where she had, across six years, run internal and external communications through two major product launches, a 2020 internal-restructuring cycle, and a 2022 founder-transition. Before that, she had spent nine years as a senior communications lead at a Global-Systemically-Important financial-services operator through two earnings-miss cycles and a 2017 reputational-risk window. The Chief Executive Officer had recruited her specifically on the strength of two capabilities: her experience running three-format communication architectures (board, employees, media) inside a single miss-and-squeeze cycle, and her reputation for refusing to ship a Message House that had not cleared an ethics-floor test. Her 2026 long-term incentive plan tied 25% to the composite communication-effectiveness score across the three rooms and 10% to the ethics-floor test.

---

## The Decision

Keller had to commit, by the February 22 Chief-Executive-Officer pre-review, the Round-1 executive-summary envelope and the directional intent for Rounds 2 and 3.

1. **Round 1 — Board Executive Summary** — how to frame the Q3 miss, the AI-cost-margin squeeze and the Predict product delay. Three vectors: a Situation-Complication-Question-Answer framing that opens with the 2023–2025 ARR growth story, places the Q3 miss and the AI-cost squeeze as the complication, frames the question as whether LUMINARY SOFTWARE can re-accelerate, and answers with a Predict-launch delay plus a cost-architecture commitment; a data-dump framing that walks the numbers line-by-line; or a defensive framing that opens with the Q3 miss. The working envelope seated SCQA against a data-dump alternative.
2. **Round 2 — All-Hands (340 employees)** — how to set three all-hands levers: tone (plain-speaking versus corporate-speak versus overly-optimistic), response to the foreseeable layoffs question (no layoffs under consideration but we are re-forecasting, we are committed to no layoffs, or no comment), and response to the foreseeable Predict-delay question (acknowledge the delay and commit to a revised launch, acknowledge without commitment, or deflect). The working envelope seated plain-speaking-plus-commitment-without-guarantees against a corporate-speak-plus-silence alternative.
3. **Round 3 — Media Message House (with an URGENT media-leak event)** — how to build a three-pillar Message House with bridges and hostile-question responses. Inside the Round-3 window, an URGENT event was expected to land: a Tier-1 reporter leaks a "restructuring" rumour forty minutes before the all-hands. Three vectors: acknowledge the context (yes, we are reviewing cost architecture under the AI-inference-cost line) and hold the truth (no layoffs under consideration); dodge and reroute to the Message House pillars; or refuse to address the rumour.

On Keller's working model, an SCQA / Plain-Speaking-plus-Commitment-Without-Guarantees / Acknowledge-Context-plus-Hold-Truth envelope delivered CES 82, Ethics 91, Credibility 84, Trust 78, Trust held across all three rooms. A Data-Dump / Corporate-Speak / Dodge-and-Reroute envelope delivered CES 48, Ethics 62, Credibility 52, Trust 38, Trust collapsed in the employee room and forfeited on the media room.

## Company Background

Luminary Software had been founded in 2018 as a three-engineer developer-tools start-up and had, across eight years, grown into a Series-C SaaS operator with USD 48M in 2025 ARR, 340 employees, and a flagship product line — Predict — that had, across 2023–2025, been under build for a Q2 2026 general-availability launch that the Q4 2025 Board review had, on a cost-curve and engineering-quality review, moved to Q4 2026. The Q3 2025 revenue miss had been driven by two factors: a rising AI-inference-cost line that had compressed gross margin from 72% to 64% across the 2024–2025 window, and a mid-market-customer-cohort churn spike that had subtracted USD 2.1M from the ARR trajectory against plan.

The Q4 2025 Board dossier had identified three specific pressures the three-round envelope would inherit. First, the Q4 2025 employee-engagement-survey had, at 58, placed the communication-trust sub-score below the 72 baseline the Chief Executive Officer had held at the 2024 Series-C fundraise. Second, the Q1 2026 Tier-1 business-publication two-year book had placed LUMINARY SOFTWARE inside the "growth-at-all-costs-versus-disciplined-growth" reporter narrative. Third, the Q4 2025 board-register had pre-committed to a February 23 Board-meeting disclosure of the Predict-delay — a disclosure that framed the Round-2 all-hands Predict-delay-question response.

## Industry and Market Context

The corporate-communications landscape in 2026 was characterised by three structural features the envelope sat inside. First, the 2019–2024 earnings-miss-communication evidence base had, across 68 growth-stage-SaaS miss-and-squeeze cycles, documented a Credibility differential of 14–22 points between SCQA framings and data-dump framings — a pattern that anchored the Round-1 commitment. Second, the 2020–2024 all-hands literature had, across 52 plain-speaking-plus-commitment-without-guarantees communications, placed CES preservation at a 76% rate against corporate-speak-plus-silence — a pattern that framed the Round-2 commitment. Third, the 2018–2024 media-leak-response evidence base had, across 38 leak-inside-forty-minutes-of-a-scheduled-event scenarios, placed acknowledge-and-hold responses at an Ethics-floor preservation rate of 82% against dodge-and-reroute responses — a pattern that framed the Round-3 URGENT event response.

Empirically, the post-2020 business-storytelling literature had two findings the envelope had to respect. First, communications that had framed a Q3 miss through a Situation-Complication-Question-Answer lens had, across a 2019–2024 sample of 28 comparable SaaS cycles, preserved Trust above the prior-quarter baseline in 78% of cases against 29% for communications that had framed the miss through a data-dump lens. Second, communications that had paired a plain-speaking tone with a commitment-without-guarantees response on layoffs questions had, across the same sample, preserved employee-engagement-survey trust sub-scores in 76% of cases against 22% for communications that had corporate-speak-plus-silence-ed the question.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Chief Executive Officer had, on February 2, approved the three-format communication architecture. The Chief Financial Officer had, on February 9, delivered the Q3 miss-and-squeeze memorandum. The Chief Product Officer had, on February 11, pre-committed to the Q4 2026 Predict launch. The Chief People Officer had, on February 13, flagged the Q4 2025 employee-engagement survey's trust sub-score. The Tier-1 business-publication reporter had, on February 15, emailed to confirm the scheduled March 3 interview.

### What Keller knew

The team had modelled three three-round envelopes. An SCQA / Plain-Speaking-plus-Commitment-Without-Guarantees / Acknowledge-Context-plus-Hold-Truth Envelope projected CES 82, Ethics 91, Credibility 84, Trust 78. A Data-Dump / Corporate-Speak / Dodge-and-Reroute Envelope projected CES 48, Ethics 62, Credibility 52, Trust 38. An SCQA / Plain-Speaking / Refuse-to-Address Envelope projected CES 74, Ethics 78, Credibility 72, Trust 64 — a trajectory that held the Board room but forfeited the media room.

### What she did not know

Whether the Tier-1 reporter's "restructuring" rumour sourcing would ripen into a published piece inside the media-message-house window; whether the employee-engagement-survey trust sub-score would, on the plain-speaking commitment, recover to the 72 baseline inside the Q1 2026 pulse; whether the Predict-delay disclosure would trigger a customer-cohort churn signal inside the Q2 2026 renewal window; and whether the Board would, at the February 23 meeting, request a fourth-format communication architecture for the Tier-1 investor-letter cycle.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Executive Officer**: *"Same numbers, three rooms, one truth. Commit."*

**The Chief Financial Officer**: *"SCQA on the miss. Do not walk the numbers line-by-line in the Board room."*

**The Chief Product Officer**: *"Acknowledge the Predict delay. Commit to Q4 2026. No hedge."*

**The Chief People Officer**: *"Plain-speaking on layoffs. Re-forecasting is not a layoff."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Keller had narrowed the space to three envelopes.

- **Option A — SCQA / Plain-Speaking-plus-Commitment-Without-Guarantees / Acknowledge-Context-plus-Hold-Truth:** the recommended envelope. CES 82. Ethics 91. Credibility 84. Trust 78. Recommended.

- **Option B — Data-Dump / Corporate-Speak / Dodge-and-Reroute:** the defensive envelope. CES 48. Ethics 62. Rejected.

- **Option C — SCQA / Plain-Speaking / Refuse-to-Address:** the partial envelope. CES 74. Ethics 78. Rejected as it forfeits the media room.

Keller's private view was that Option A was the right envelope and that the single round most correlated with the final composite score was Round 3 — the acknowledge-context-plus-hold-truth response that, forty minutes before the all-hands, preserved the ethics-floor test and held the trust position across the Board, the employees and the media rooms.
