## Abstract

ElectroAndina was, in the case universe, a LATAM consumer-electronics retail chain with 182 stores across Colombia, Peru, and Chile, a ten-year-old e-commerce platform, a 2025 revenue of USD 420M, a 20% share of the addressable Andean consumer-electronics market, and an 18% online penetration. On Monday, February 2, 2026, the Chief Executive Officer convened a three-round strategic planning cycle inside which the Chief Retail Officer would commit, each round, six operating levers — pricing index, category focus, safety-stock posture, store-investment level, online-investment level, and vendor strategy. Esteban Rueda, a 46-year-old former VP of Merchandising promoted to Chief Retail Officer on January 8, 2026, faced a new competitor-store opening in Round 2, an online-demand spike on television and large-appliance categories, a gross-margin return on inventory investment baseline of 250, and a Composite Retail Performance Index (CRPI) scorecard at zero. The case opens at 7:20 a.m. on Tuesday, February 3, 2026, at the ElectroAndina merchandise operations center in Bogotá, as Rueda prepares the Round-1 lever sheet.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate the Round-1 pricing-cut-to-buy-share decision against the margin cost and diagnose when a 3%-price-cut dominates on the three-round CRPI composite.
2. Analyze the Round-2 category-focus rotation to televisions and large appliances under an online-demand spike and a competitor four-store opening, and recommend a safety-stock and online-investment posture.
3. Diagnose the Round-3 vendor strategy transition from co-marketing to exclusives to private-label and recommend the pricing anchor that the private-label margin enables.
4. Design a three-round channel-investment sequence that lifts online penetration from 18% to the 30%-range and preserves the store network's role in the omnichannel mix.
5. Recommend a complete three-round lever sequence, identify the single round most correlated with the Round-3 CRPI outcome, and write the shareholder-facing paragraph the CRPI score would anchor.

On the morning of **Tuesday, February 3, 2026**, **Esteban Rueda** arrived at the ElectroAndina merchandise operations center on Carrera 11 in Bogotá at 7:20 a.m., opened the Round-1 lever sheet his category-director team had finalized at 11:45 p.m. the night before, and read the CRPI dashboard at the top of the sheet. Current GMROI sat at 250. Inventory turnover sat at 5.0 times annually. The markdown rate sat at 18%. The sell-through sat at 70%. The customer satisfaction sat at 70. Twelve days separated him from the February 15 Board pre-read at which he would commit the Round-1 envelope.

---

## The Protagonist

Esteban Rueda, 46, had joined ElectroAndina in 2013 as Senior Buyer for the smartphone category and had been promoted to VP of Merchandising in 2019. A graduate of the Universidad Nacional de Colombia with an MBA from INCAE, he had, in his VP role, rebuilt the smartphone category to a 30% share of ElectroAndina revenue and had, in 2024, launched the company's first private-label small-appliance line — a twenty-four-SKU range carried at a 42% gross margin against the 28% gross margin of the comparable branded line. His January 8, 2026 promotion to Chief Retail Officer had carried a Board commitment that the three-round CRPI trajectory would determine the 2027 executive-compensation envelope. His first all-hands note on January 14 had read: "The category focus, the safety stock, the vendor choice — they are not the strategy. The sequence is. Round one buys the share, round two absorbs the spike, round three monetizes the base."

---

## The Decision

Rueda had to commit, by the February 15 Board pre-read, the Round-1 operating envelope and the Round-2 and Round-3 intent:

1. **Pricing Index and Category Focus** — whether to cut pricing by 3% against the sector index to buy share and rotate category focus to the fast-turn smartphone engine, or to hold pricing and rotate category focus to the margin-denser appliance category.
2. **Safety Stock and Inventory Investment** — whether to run safety stock at the standard posture in Round 1 and lift to the high posture in Round 2 ahead of the television demand spike, or to run high safety stock from Round 1.
3. **Store, Online, and Vendor Strategy** — whether to run store investment at level 5 and online at level 6 in Round 1, shifting to level 6 and level 9 respectively by Round 3; whether to rotate vendor strategy from co-marketing through exclusives to private-label across the three rounds.

The three sub-decisions would interact and would compound across the three-round envelope. A Round-1 package that cut pricing 3%, focused on smartphones, ran standard safety stock, invested at store-5 and online-6, and ran vendor co-marketing produced, on Rueda's working model, a Round-1 revenue of USD 22M in the monitoring period, a gross margin of 31%, a share of 21%, an online penetration of 21%, and a CRPI of 62. The three-round trajectory then carried the company to a CRPI of 81, an online penetration of 31%, and a gross margin of 33% under the vendor-rotation and investment sequence.

## Company Background

ElectroAndina had been founded in 1996 as a single-store consumer-electronics operator in Bogotá and had, across thirty years, built the 182-store Andean footprint the case studied. The 2025 revenue of USD 420M was distributed as Colombia 58%, Peru 24%, Chile 18%. The category mix was smartphones 28%, televisions 18%, large appliances 16%, laptops and tablets 14%, small appliances 10%, audio 8%, gaming 6%. The e-commerce platform, launched in 2016, had, across nine years, climbed to an 18% share of company revenue — below the 26% sector benchmark for LATAM consumer-electronics peers. The company's vendor network comprised forty-two principal suppliers across nine brand families and two private-label manufacturing partnerships.

The Board's January strategy dossier had identified three specific pressures the three-round envelope would inherit. First, a regional competitor had, in Q4 2025, announced a four-store Colombian expansion for Q2 2026 that would land during the Round-2 decision window. Second, the Andean consumer had, across the 2024–2025 window, shifted television and large-appliance purchase intent to online channels — Q4 2025 online penetration in televisions had reached 34% against the 18% company average. Third, the 2024 private-label small-appliance launch had, in its fourteen-month track, validated a private-label margin thesis that the Board intended to extend across a broader category footprint in 2026–2027.

## Industry and Market Context

The LATAM consumer-electronics retail landscape in 2026 was characterized by three structural features the three-round envelope sat inside. First, the Andean online-penetration trajectory had, across 2022–2025, climbed from 9% to 18% at the sector level, with electronics, appliances, and entertainment categories running 4–7 points ahead of the composite; a 2026 sector forecast placed the composite at 24% by year-end, creating a structural demand pull on the online-investment lever. Second, the private-label margin multiplier had, across a 2021–2024 LATAM consumer-electronics commercial-summary sample of seventy-two private-label launches, produced a median gross-margin uplift of 12–16 points against branded equivalents, with a median second-year loyalty premium of 8 Net Promoter points — a finding that anchored the Round-3 vendor rotation. Third, the safety-stock-to-stockout relationship had, across the 2023–2024 GMROI-tracking sample of thirty-eight LATAM consumer-electronics chains, documented a 0.72 correlation between a high-safety-stock posture in a demand-spike window and a next-season GMROI improvement of 30+ points — a pattern that informed the Round-2 safety-stock lift.

Empirically, the post-2018 omnichannel retail literature had two robust findings the three-round envelope had to respect. First, chains that had sequenced a vendor rotation from co-marketing through exclusives to private-label across a two-to-four-round horizon had, across a 2019–2024 sample of twenty-eight LATAM retail peers, lifted the blended gross margin by 3–5 points while preserving share — against a 1.2-point gross-margin uplift for chains that had held vendor strategy static. Second, chains that had raised the online-investment level to the level-9 range inside a three-round window had, across the same sample, absorbed competitor physical-store openings with share-softening below 0.8 points, against a 2.1-point share-softening for chains that had held online investment flat.

## The Situation

### How we got here

The Board had, in the December 2025 strategy off-site, approved a three-year CRPI target of 82 and an online-penetration target of 30% by end-2027. The competitor-intelligence office had, on January 10, 2026, confirmed the four-store Colombian expansion. The Chief Merchandising Officer had, on January 14, circulated a Round-1 pre-reading that proposed the 3%-price-cut smartphone-focus envelope. The Chief Digital Officer had, on January 18, pushed back with a Round-1 online-investment-at-level-8 proposal to pre-empt the demand spike. The Chief Supply Chain Officer had, on January 22, signed off on the standard-safety-stock Round-1 posture with a Round-2 lift to high.

### What Rueda knew

The team had modeled three three-round envelopes. A Share-Buy Envelope (Round-1 -3% pricing, smartphone focus, standard safety stock, online-6, co-marketing; Round-2 0% pricing, television focus, high safety stock, online-8, exclusives; Round-3 +2% pricing, balanced category, standard safety stock, online-9, private-label) projected a Round-3 revenue of USD 31M in the monitoring period, a gross margin of 33%, a share of 23%, an online penetration of 31%, a GMROI of 312, and a CRPI of 81. A Margin-First Envelope (Round-1 0% pricing, appliance focus, standard safety stock, online-7, exclusives; Round-2 +1% pricing, balanced, standard, online-8, private-label; Round-3 +2% pricing, balanced, standard, online-9, private-label) projected a Round-3 revenue of USD 27M, a gross margin of 34%, a share of 21%, an online penetration of 28%, and a CRPI of 74. A Defensive Envelope (Round-1 0% pricing, smartphone focus, high safety stock, online-6, co-marketing; Round-2 0%, balanced, high, online-7, co-marketing; Round-3 0%, balanced, high, online-7, exclusives) projected a Round-3 CRPI of 67 — margin-preserving but growth-losing.

### What he did not know

Whether the competitor four-store opening would land at the reported Q2 2026 schedule or would slip to Q3 and compress the Round-2 share-defense window; whether the television demand spike would hold through Round 3 or would normalize once the promotional pricing cycle rolled off; whether the private-label manufacturing partner would hold the 42%-margin commitment at the broader category scope or would renegotiate on volume; and whether the Chile operation's new 14% VAT-regime would compress blended margin by the modeled 1.2 points or by a deeper 2.0 points.

### What others were saying

**The Chief Merchandising Officer**: *"Share-Buy Envelope. Smartphones first — the fast turn. Televisions second — the spike. Balanced third — the price anchor."*

**The Chief Digital Officer**: *"Online investment to level 8 in Round 1. Do not wait. The spike lands before the Round-2 decision window closes."*

**The Chief Supply Chain Officer**: *"Safety stock lifts in Round 2, not Round 1. Holding cost in Round 1 is a margin donation."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

Rueda had narrowed the space to three three-round envelopes.

- **Option A — Share-Buy:** Round-1 -3% pricing, smartphone focus; Round-2 television focus, high safety stock, exclusives; Round-3 +2% pricing, balanced, private-label. Round-3 CRPI 81. Aligns with CMO and CDO. Recommended.

- **Option B — Margin-First:** Round-1 0% pricing, appliance focus, exclusives; rotates to private-label by Round-2. Round-3 CRPI 74. Higher Round-1 margin, lower three-round revenue.

- **Option C — Defensive:** Round-1 0% pricing, high safety stock from Round 1, co-marketing held through Round 2. Round-3 CRPI 67. Margin-preserving. Growth-losing.

Rueda's private view, not yet shared with the Board, was that Option A was the right three-round envelope and that the single round most correlated with the Round-3 CRPI outcome was the Round-2 vendor rotation to exclusives — specifically, the simultaneous category rotation to televisions and large appliances, the safety-stock lift to high, the online-investment lift to level 8, and the Condor-and-Samsung exclusives negotiation that would anchor the Round-3 private-label pricing power.
