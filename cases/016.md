## Abstract

Lumi Skincare, a six-year-old direct-to-consumer premium skincare brand headquartered in Austin, Texas, with USD 92M of 2025 revenue, entered Q2 2026 having just closed a Series C of USD 48M and having committed to its investors that it would deliver USD 150M of 2026 revenue with a blended return-on-ad-spend (ROAS) of no less than 2.6x and a customer acquisition cost (CAC) of no more than USD 38. Its incoming CMO, Renata Alcántara, had been given a Q2 working media budget of USD 6M and had decided — against the objection of her VP Performance — to run the quarter as four consecutive four-week campaigns, each re-planned weekly in a "war room." Week 1 would allocate USD 500K across six digital channels. Three competitors had, in the preceding month, materially stepped up spend in the same performance auction. The case opens on the Sunday evening before the first Monday war-room meeting, with three internally coherent Week-1 plans on the table.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case discussion, participants will be able to:

1. Evaluate a weekly budget allocation across six digital channels with different CAC, ROAS, LTV, and brand-lift profiles.
2. Analyze the trade-off between performance and brand investment under a binding ROAS floor.
3. Design an attribution stance that guides channel allocation under MMM and last-click disagreement.
4. Diagnose the interaction between auction dynamics, competitor spend, and organic trajectory.
5. Recommend a Week-1 plan and articulate the learning agenda that the next three weeks will resolve.

On the evening of **Sunday, May 3, 2026**, **Renata Alcántara** sat at a corner booth of a café on South Congress in Austin with two laptops open, a printed channel-performance pack of the previous six weeks, and a single unopened notebook. On the notebook's cover, in black marker, she had written one sentence the week before: *"Do not let performance eat the brand."*

---

## The Protagonist

Renata Alcántara, 41, had joined Lumi Skincare as Chief Marketing Officer in March 2026. Born and educated in México City (ITESM, MBA at NYU Stern), she had spent six years at a large advertising holding company as a strategy director, followed by four years as VP of Brand at a U.S. consumer-electronics D2C, where she had run the shift from pure-performance marketing into brand-performance equilibrium. She was known in D2C circles for three things: a thesis-first approach to channel planning, a tolerance for short-run ROAS dips in service of LTV expansion, and a personal rule against re-targeting warm customers more than 1.3x per week.

Her contract carried an equity vesting gate tied to 2026 revenue, 2026 blended ROAS, and 2026 organic-traffic share.

---

## The Decision

By **08:00 on Monday, May 4, 2026**, Alcántara had to walk into the first weekly war-room meeting and present a Week-1 plan comprising:

1. **Channel allocation** across six channels: paid search; paid social (Meta + TikTok); influencer; CTV/YouTube; email/CRM; affiliate + retention tech.
2. **Campaign stance**: *ROAS-Max (2.9x floor, CAC ceiling USD 32), Brand-Build (2.2x floor, brand-lift target +3 pts), Balanced (2.6x floor), Defensive (match-competitor, 2.4x floor).*
3. **Attribution default**: last-click with an MMM adjustment at month-end; media-mix modeling live; matched-market holdouts.

The total Week-1 envelope was USD 500K. The Q2 envelope was USD 6M.

---

## Company Background

Lumi Skincare had been founded in 2019 by two engineers and a dermatologist who had built a line of "minimum-effective-dose" skincare around four hero SKUs. By 2025 the brand had expanded to nineteen SKUs, a U.S.-centric revenue base (81%), a LATAM expansion (14%), and a small but growing European D2C business (5%). 2025 revenue was USD 92M with a blended contribution margin of 38% and an EBITDA margin of 5.4%. The brand's contribution margin at the SKU level ranged from 32% (newer serums) to 62% (hero cleanser).

Customer cohorts behaved well for a premium D2C skincare: 90-day repeat rate 34%, 365-day LTV USD 184, gross-margin-on-repeat 46%, refund rate 2.1%. Organic traffic accounted for 28% of site sessions; branded search 11%; direct 14%; the rest was paid or referral. Email list size at end-2025 was 1.42M subscribers with a Q1 2026 average open rate of 27% and a click-through of 3.2%.

The Series C closed on April 14, 2026 at a post-money valuation of USD 440M and was anchored by two strategic growth investors — one with a prior exit in the beauty category — who had, in the term sheet, included a "quarterly growth and efficiency review" clause.

## Industry and Market Context

The U.S. D2C premium skincare market sat in 2026 inside three structural shifts: (i) Meta and TikTok paid CPMs had risen 24% year-over-year as retailers and travel recovered post-2023; (ii) CTV had crossed the threshold of measurable direct-response, with addressable YouTube delivering ROAS inside the same order of magnitude as paid social for many premium DTC brands; (iii) affiliate economics had migrated from creator-led to performance-tech-led, with post-click attribution windows tightening under signal-loss.

Research on D2C growth efficiency had converged on three propositions: (i) the marginal dollar on last-click paid social was often negative in ROI once incrementality was adjusted for; (ii) brand spend (CTV, influencer with long-cycle creators, podcast) had lagged ROAS but accretive LTV; (iii) retention tech, properly sequenced, could move 90-day repeat by 3–6 points with a three-month implementation lag.

Competitive pressure on Lumi specifically had, in the preceding four weeks, intensified: three comparable D2C brands had increased paid social spend by 40–60%; one had launched a Q2 CTV push; auction CPMs in the skincare category had risen 18% week-over-week for two consecutive weeks.

---

## The Situation

### What Alcántara knew

The performance team's six-week pack placed last-click ROAS by channel at: paid search 3.8x, paid social 2.4x, influencer 1.9x (90-day delayed), CTV 1.1x (30-day last-click; higher on MMM), email/CRM 8.6x (on a cost base that did not include content and list growth), affiliate 4.2x. The MMM last run on April 22 had placed influencer at 3.1x and CTV at 2.2x when view-through and brand-lift were modeled. CAC by channel ranged from USD 18 (email/CRM; largely repeat) to USD 81 (CTV).

### What she did not know

Whether the competitor CTV push would convert into auction pressure on paid social or would divert competitor performance budget away from it. Whether the ten influencers scheduled for week-of-May-11 would deliver on-time (two had been late in Q1). Whether the Series C investors' "quarterly review" would treat a Week-1 Brand-Build stance as signal or as noise.

### What others were saying

**Kenji Maruyama**, VP Performance, argued for **ROAS-Max**. *"Paid search 160K, paid social 210K, email 20K, affiliate 70K, influencer 40K, CTV 0. We hit 2.9x. We keep the investor room quiet."*

**Jordan Pruitt**, VP Brand, argued for **Brand-Build**. *"CTV 140K, influencer 120K, paid social 100K, paid search 80K, email 40K, affiliate 20K. ROAS 2.3x Week 1, but brand lift and 90-day repeat up in three weeks."*

**Claudia Reyes**, Head of Growth, argued for **Balanced**. *"Search 120K, social 150K, influencer 80K, CTV 70K, email 40K, affiliate 40K. 2.6x Week 1, 2.7x by Week 4, organic share up 1.5 pts."*

---

## Options Under Consideration

- **Option A — ROAS-Max (Maruyama plan):** Search USD 160K, social USD 210K, email USD 20K, affiliate USD 70K, influencer USD 40K, CTV USD 0. Week-1 ROAS 2.9x; blended CAC USD 31; brand-lift target not funded; organic share flat.

- **Option B — Brand-Build (Pruitt plan):** CTV USD 140K, influencer USD 120K, social USD 100K, search USD 80K, email USD 40K, affiliate USD 20K. Week-1 ROAS 2.3x; brand-lift modeled +3.2 pts over four weeks; organic-traffic share +1 pt; investor optics uncertain.

- **Option C — Balanced (Reyes plan):** Search USD 120K, social USD 150K, influencer USD 80K, CTV USD 70K, email USD 40K, affiliate USD 40K. Week-1 ROAS 2.6x; CAC USD 36; organic share +1.5 pts over four weeks; moderate brand lift.

Alcántara suspected, but had not written down, that the correct answer involved sequencing: a ROAS-leaning Week 1 to establish credibility with the investor room, followed by a brand-leaning Week 3–4 to protect the 2027 brand equity.
